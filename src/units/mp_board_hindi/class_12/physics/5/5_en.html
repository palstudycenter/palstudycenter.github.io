📘 Unit 5 - Electromagnetic Waves
Q: What are electromagnetic waves? Write their characteristics.
When an electric field and a magnetic field, mutually perpendicular to each other and also perpendicular to the direction of propagation of the wave, transport energy through space, this is called an electromagnetic wave.

These waves are produced when a charge undergoes accelerated motion.
Characteristics
  1. These waves are transverse in nature.
  2. These waves are unaffected by electric and magnetic fields.
  3. They do not require any physical medium for their propagation.
  4. The oscillations of the electric and magnetic fields are in the same phase.
  5. These waves travel at the speed of light in free space.
  6. These waves exhibit the properties of reflection, refraction, interference, diffraction and polarization.
Q: Who discovered electromagnetic waves?
Maxwell.
Q: How do the electric and magnetic fields propagate through space?
In space, the electric and magnetic fields propagate by oscillating perpendicular to each other and perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.
Q: What oscillates in an electromagnetic wave?
In an electromagnetic wave, the electric field and the magnetic field oscillate perpendicular to each other's plane, and also perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.
Q: How are electromagnetic waves produced?
Electromagnetic waves are produced by a time-varying magnetic field or a time-varying electric field.
Q: What type of charge can produce electromagnetic waves?
An accelerated charge can produce electromagnetic waves.
Q: Are electromagnetic waves deflected by an electric field or a magnetic field?
No.
Q: Do electromagnetic waves carry energy and momentum?
Yes, electromagnetic waves carry both energy and momentum.
Q: Are electromagnetic waves transverse or longitudinal?
These waves are transverse in nature.
Q: Explain why electromagnetic waves are transverse in nature.
In an electromagnetic wave, both the electric field and the magnetic field oscillate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation. Hence, electromagnetic waves are transverse in nature.
Q: What formula gives the velocity of electromagnetic waves in vacuum?
The velocity of electromagnetic waves in vacuum is given by the following formula—
\[ c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}} \]
where,
  • \(c\) = velocity of electromagnetic waves in vacuum
  • \(\mu_0\) = magnetic permeability of vacuum
  • \(\varepsilon_0\) = electric permittivity of vacuum
Q: Write the relation between the amplitudes of the electric field and the magnetic field.
In an electromagnetic wave, the relation between the amplitudes of the electric field (E) and the magnetic field (B) is as follows—
\[ \frac{E_0}{B_0} = c \]
where—
  • \(E_0\) = amplitude of the electric field
  • \(B_0\) = amplitude of the magnetic field
  • \(c\) = speed of light in vacuum
Q: Because of which property of electromagnetic waves is it believed that light waves are electromagnetic waves?
The velocity of light waves is equal to the velocity of electromagnetic waves.
Difference between electromagnetic waves and sound waves

Q: What is meant by the electromagnetic spectrum? Explain.
The overall distribution of electromagnetic waves based on their wavelength or frequency is called the electromagnetic spectrum.

It consists of the following radiations.
Radiation Discoverer Wavelength Properties Uses
Gamma rays Becquerel and Curie 0.001 Ao to 1 Ao (\(10^{-14}\) m to \(10^{-10}\) m) Very high energy, deep penetrating power, invisible Cancer treatment, study of nuclear reactions
X-rays Roentgen 1 Ao to 100 Ao (\(10^{-13}\) m to \(10^{-8}\) m) Ability to penetrate solid objects, invisible Bone examination, inspection of metals and goods, security screening
Ultraviolet rays Ritter 100 Ao to 4000 Ao (\(10^{-8}\) m to \(4\times10^{-7}\) m) Germicidal, affect the skin Water purification, vitamin D production, medical use
Visible light Newton 4000 Ao to 7900 Ao (\(4\times10^{-7}\) m to \(7\times10^{-7}\) m) Light visible to the human eye, mixture of colours For vision, optical instruments, communication (fibre optics)
Infrared rays Herschel 7900 Ao to \(10^{7}\) Ao (\(4\times10^{-7}\) m to \(10^{-3}\) m) Produce a heating effect, invisible Heaters, night-vision cameras, remote controls
Microwaves Maxwell \(10^{7}\) Ao to \(10^{10}\) Ao (\(10^{-3}\) m to 0.30 m) High-frequency waves, can generate heat Radar, satellite communication, microwave ovens
Radio waves Hertz \(10^{10}\) Ao to \(10^{14}\) Ao (0.30 m to several kilometres) Longest wavelength, able to pass through the atmosphere Radio broadcasting, TV, mobile networks, satellite communication

Q: What is the source of gamma rays, and write its use. (2020)
Gamma rays are produced by transitions in the atomic nucleus and by the decay of certain elementary particles. Their wavelength ranges from 0.001 Ao to 1 Ao.

Uses:
  1. Providing information about the structure of the atomic nucleus.
  2. Treatment of cancer.
Q: Why are the bulbs of lamps emitting ultraviolet rays made of quartz?
Because ultraviolet rays pass through quartz but not through glass. Glass absorbs ultraviolet rays.
Q: Which radiation is used for examining fingerprints?
Ultraviolet radiation is used for examining fingerprints.
Q: What are X-rays? Write two uses of X-rays. (2019)
Rays produced by the sudden deceleration of high-speed electrons striking a target of high atomic number are called X-rays. Their wavelength ranges from 1 Ao to 100 Ao.

Uses:
  1. In surgery.
  2. For studying the internal structure of atomic shells.
Q: In which region of the electromagnetic wave spectrum does a metre wavelength fall?
In the region of X-rays.
Q: Why is dim red light kept in a photographic darkroom?
Red light has the lowest frequency. Hence, its low-energy photons do not affect photographic films. For this reason, dim red light is kept in a photographic darkroom.
Q: Which waves are used as signals in fog, and why?
Infrared rays are used as signals in fog, because owing to their longer wavelength their scattering is very low, giving them greater penetrating power so that these rays can travel long distances even through fog and mist.
Q: What are microwaves? Where are they used?
Electromagnetic waves with a wavelength between 1 mm and 100 mm are called microwaves.

Use: These waves are used in telecommunication.

Their frequency range is from 0-1 metre to 1 mm.

Q: What is meant by the atmosphere? Describe its different layers.
The atmosphere is a vast layer of gases that surrounds the Earth on all sides. It stays attached to the Earth because of the Earth's gravity. It is this envelope of gases that makes life on Earth possible.


The atmosphere is mainly divided into five layers.
  1. Troposphere - This is the lowest layer of the atmosphere, extending to a height of about 12 kilometres from the Earth's surface. Most of the living creatures and water vapour present on Earth are found in this layer. In this layer, temperature decreases with increasing height. Weather-related events: all weather-related phenomena such as clouds, rain, fog and storms occur in this layer.
  2. Stratosphere - This layer extends from just above the troposphere up to a height of 12 to 30 kilometres.
  3. Ozone layer - This layer extends from just above the stratosphere up to a height of 30 to 50 kilometres. The main constituent of this layer is ozone gas. This layer absorbs the harmful ultraviolet rays coming from the sun.
  4. Mesosphere - This layer extends from just above the ozone layer up to a height of 50 to 80 kilometres. In this layer, temperature decreases with increasing height.
  5. Ionosphere - This layer extends from just above the mesosphere up to a height of 80 to 300 kilometres. This layer has extremely high temperature. Hence this layer is comparatively rarefied. Electrically charged particles are found in this layer. These particles help in radio communication, because they reflect radio waves sent from the Earth back to the Earth.
Q: Where is the ozone layer located in the atmosphere? What is its importance?
The ozone layer is located at a distance of about 30 km to 50 km from the Earth's surface. It is made up of ozone molecules. It acts as a protective shield for all living beings on Earth.

Importance -
  1. It absorbs harmful radiations.
  2. It prevents ultraviolet radiation coming from the sun and other bodies from reaching the Earth.
Q: The ozone layer is essential for the existence of life on Earth, why?
The ultraviolet radiation coming from the sun is harmful to living organisms. This layer absorbs this radiation coming from the sun and does not let it reach the Earth's surface. Therefore, the ozone layer is essential for the existence of life on Earth.
Q: What is the ionosphere? Explain the role of the ionosphere in the propagation of radio waves.
This layer extends from 80 kilometres to 300 kilometres above the Earth's surface and contains free electrons and positively charged ions. This layer is called the ionosphere.

As we move towards the upper layers of the ionosphere, the refractive index keeps decreasing. Therefore, the incident radio waves move away from the normal after reflection. At a certain angle of incidence, the incident ray is completely reflected and returns to the Earth's surface. In this way, the ionosphere plays a role in the propagation of radio waves.
Q: On what factors does the state of ionisation of the atmosphere depend?
The state of ionisation of the atmosphere depends on the ultraviolet rays coming from the sun and other celestial bodies, and on the height of the atmosphere above the Earth's surface.
Q: How does the Earth's atmosphere behave towards radiations coming from the sun?
The behaviour of the Earth's atmosphere towards radiation coming from the sun is as follows—
  1. Most of the gamma rays, X-rays and long-wavelength infrared radiation is absorbed by the ionosphere.
  2. Ultraviolet radiation is absorbed by the ozone layer.
  3. Only a very small amount of short-wavelength infrared radiation reaches the Earth's surface through the atmosphere, which causes the Earth's surface to become warm.
  4. The atmosphere is transparent to visible light and microwaves.
Q: From where does the Earth receive electromagnetic radiation? Explain how the atmosphere behaves towards radiations emitted by the Earth.
The main source of electromagnetic radiation on Earth is the sun, that is, this radiation is received from the sun.

For radiations of various wavelengths transmitted from the Earth, the atmosphere behaves as described below.
  1. Infrared radiation transmitted from the Earth is reflected back to the Earth by the lower layers of the atmosphere.
  2. Microwaves transmitted from the Earth's surface pass through the atmosphere and are reflected into space without any deviation.
  3. Short radio waves transmitted from the Earth's surface are deflected by the ionosphere, but pass through the atmosphere. On the other hand, long radio waves with a wavelength greater than 10 metres undergo total internal reflection and are reflected back to the Earth.
Q: Optical and radio telescopes are built on the Earth itself, but X-ray astronomy is only possible from artificial satellites orbiting the Earth, why?
The Earth's atmosphere lets visible light and radio waves pass through, but absorbs X-rays. Hence, X-ray astronomy is possible only from artificial satellites orbiting the Earth.
(VIMP) Q: What is the greenhouse effect?
During the day, long-wavelength rays from the sun reach the Earth's surface through the atmosphere. The Earth's surface absorbs this energy and becomes warm. At night, the warm Earth emits this infrared radiation (heat) back into space in order to cool down.

However, the greenhouse gases present in the atmosphere (such as carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour) absorb this infrared radiation and do not let it escape into space. These gases spread this heat all around, causing the temperature of the atmosphere and the Earth's surface to rise even at night. This is called the greenhouse effect.
Q: If the Earth did not have an atmosphere, would its surface temperature be the same as it is now? Or, how does the absence of the atmosphere affect the surface temperature?
If the Earth did not have an atmosphere, its average surface temperature would be lower than the present temperature, because in the presence of the atmosphere, infrared radiation emitted from the Earth's surface is reflected back by the atmosphere onto the Earth's surface, which raises the surface temperature.
Q: What is displacement current?
When the electric flux in vacuum or in some medium changes with time, this is equivalent to an electric current. This current is called the displacement current.
Q: How does the propagation of radio waves take place? Explain.
Depending on their frequency, radio waves can propagate from one place to another on the Earth in three ways.
  1. By the ground wave (GW) propagation method - In this, amplitude-modulated radio waves are sent directly from one place to another along the Earth's surface. Due to the curvature of the Earth, the waves get slightly deviated. Only radio waves up to a frequency of 1500 KHz can be propagated by this method. This is called the medium wave band.
  2. By space wave - In this method of propagation, radio waves are sent from the transmitting antenna making a small angle with the Earth's surface. These waves reach the receiving antenna after being reflected from the troposphere. Only radio waves up to a frequency of 2000 kilohertz can be transmitted by this method. Since the troposphere is at a very low height, it is not possible to transmit radio waves over long distances by this method.
  3. By sky wave - In this method of propagation, radio waves are sent from the transmitting antenna in an almost vertical direction. These waves reach the receiving antenna after being reflected from various layers of the ionosphere. The frequency of radio waves ranges from 1500 kilohertz to 40 megahertz. This method is used for long-distance transmission.
Q: What is a geostationary satellite? Write its height above the Earth's surface.
An artificial satellite that revolves around the Earth from west to east in its orbit in the equatorial plane, and whose period of revolution is equal to the Earth's period of rotation, that is, a period of 24 hours, is called a synchronous satellite.

Viewed from the equatorial plane, this satellite appears stationary. Hence it is also called a geostationary satellite. Its height above the Earth's surface is 36000 km.
Q: How do microwaves propagate?
Microwaves are first sent from a transmitting antenna located on Earth to an artificial satellite. The artificial satellite amplifies them and reflects them back to another location on Earth, where they are received by a receiving antenna.
Q: Artificial satellites are used for long-distance TV broadcasting, why?
TV signals are transmitted in the form of microwaves, so they are not reflected by the ionosphere. Hence, these rays are sent from a transmitting antenna located on Earth to artificial satellites first. The artificial satellite amplifies them and reflects them back to another location on Earth, where they are received by a receiving antenna. Hence, artificial satellites are used for long-distance TV broadcasting.
Q: What are polar satellites?
Polar satellites are those satellites that orbit the Earth in a polar orbit that passes over the Earth's poles.
Q: What is meant by remote sensing? Write its applications.
Remote sensing is a technique by which information about an object's size, colour, nature, location, temperature, etc. is gathered without touching it.

When a remote sensing satellite passes over a particular area, it takes pictures of that area, which provide information about that area.

Uses
  1. In weather forecasting.
  2. In geological surveys.
  3. It is used in preparing maps of barren land.
  4. It is used to locate the place where an underground nuclear explosion has occurred.
Q: Polar satellites are used for remote sensing. Why?
Polar satellites are used for remote sensing because their height above the Earth's surface is about 1000-1200 km and their period of revolution is about 1.5 to 2 hours.

Thus, a polar satellite completes about 12-15 revolutions around the Earth in its orbit in one day, so that by obtaining daily images of a particular area, we can get information about the daily changes taking place in that area.
Q: For a TV antenna of height h, show that it can be used to transmit a signal on the Earth's surface up to a distance \(d = \sqrt{2hR}\), where R is the radius of the Earth.
Let O be the centre and R be the radius of the Earth. PQ is a TV antenna. Two tangent lines PM and PN are drawn from the top of the tower. It is clear from the figure that the signal can be sent directly to points P and Q.

By the Pythagoras theorem in the right-angled triangle \(\Delta PMO\)—
\[ (PM)^2+(OM)^2=(OP)^2 \]
\[ d^2+R^2=(h+R)^2 \]
\[ d^2+R^2=h^2+R^2+2hR \]
\[ d^2=h^2+2hR \]
Since \(h\ll R\), so \(h^2\to0\)
\[ d^2=2hR \]
\[ \boxed{d=\sqrt{2hR}} \]
