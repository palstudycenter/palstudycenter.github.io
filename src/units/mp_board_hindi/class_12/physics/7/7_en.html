📘 Unit 7 - Electron Emission
Q- What is electron emission? How many types are there? (Question Bank)
The phenomenon of emission of electrons from a metal surface is called electron emission.

It is of the following four types.
  1. Thermionic emission
  2. Photoelectric emission
  3. Field emission
  4. Secondary emission.
Q - What is thermionic emission? (2011, 15, 19, Question Bank)
The process in which, on heating a metal, free electrons start being emitted from its surface is called thermionic emission.
Q - What properties should a metal used for thermionic emission have? (2015 Supplementary, Question Bank)
A metal used for thermionic emission should have the following properties—
  1. The work function of the metal used should be low.
  2. The melting point of the metal used should be high, so that it does not melt on heating.
Q - What is the photoelectric effect?
When light of frequency higher than the threshold frequency falls on the surface of a metal, electrons are emitted from the surface of that metal. This phenomenon is called the photoelectric effect.
Q What are photoelectrons?
Electrons which are emitted from a metal surface due to the photoelectric effect, when light of suitable frequency falls on the surface, are called photoelectrons.
Q Which phenomenon proves that light exhibits particle properties?
The photoelectric effect proves that light also has a particle nature (photon).
Q Explain Einstein's photoelectric equation. (2009/16/19/23)
Albert Einstein gave this equation in 1905 to explain the photoelectric effect.

When light of suitable frequency falls on the surface of a metal, the photons of light give their energy to the electrons. Part of this energy is spent in removing the electron out of the metal (work function \(\phi\)) and the remaining part becomes the kinetic energy of the electron.

Einstein's photoelectric equation
$$ h\nu = \phi + \frac{1}{2}mc^2 $$
$$ h\nu - h\nu_0 = \frac{1}{2}mc^2 \quad (\because \phi = h\nu_0) $$
$$ h(\nu - \nu_0) = \frac{1}{2}mc^2 $$
This is Einstein's photoelectric equation.

Here —
  • m = mass of the electron
  • v = velocity of the electron
  • h = Planck's constant
  • \(\nu\) = frequency of the incident light
  • \(\nu_0\) = threshold frequency of the incident light
(In the source notes, the kinetic energy term in this formula is written as \(mc^2\), whereas normally the electron's velocity \(v\) is used, giving \(\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\) — this is an inconsistency in the original document, retained here exactly as-is.)
Q Explain Einstein's interpretation based on the photoelectric equation.
Photoelectric equation
$$ h\nu = \phi + \frac{1}{2}mc^2 $$
Einstein's interpretation:
  1. Concept of the photon — Light energy has a particle form, called a photon. Each photon has energy \(h\nu\).
  2. Work function \(\phi\) — A minimum amount of energy is required to remove an electron from a metal; this is called the work function. If the photon energy \(h\nu\) is less than the work function, electrons are not emitted.
  3. Maximum kinetic energy — Whatever extra energy remains becomes the kinetic energy of the electron. This shows that the higher the frequency, the greater the speed of the electron.
  4. Effect of intensity — Increasing the intensity of light only increases the number of electrons emitted, not their energy.
Q -Write three experimental observations of the photoelectric effect. (2024)
  1. Photoelectric emission is an instantaneous process, with no time lag.
  2. Photoelectric current (number of photoelectrons) is directly proportional to the intensity of the incident radiation.
  3. The kinetic energy of photoelectrons is directly proportional to the frequency of the incident radiation.
Q - What is meant by threshold frequency in the photoelectric effect? (2011/14/22/23/24)
For a given metal surface, the minimum frequency of incident light below which no electrons are emitted from that metal surface, regardless of the intensity of light, is called the threshold frequency. It is denoted by \(\nu_0\).
Q - What is the work function? Or, what is the threshold energy? (2011/14/20/23)
The energy spent in bringing a free electron from inside a metal to its surface is called the work function or threshold energy of that metal. Work function is a specific property of a given metal. Its value is different for different metals. It is denoted by the letter \(\phi\).

Work function (threshold energy) —
$$ \phi = h\nu_0 $$
Where—
  • \(\nu_0\) = threshold frequency
  • h = Planck's constant
Q The work functions of lithium and copper are 2.3 eV and 4 eV respectively. Which metal would be useful for working with visible light?
Answer - Lithium metal would be useful, because its work function is lower.
Q What is the reason that for a given metal, photoelectric emission does not occur for light beyond a certain wavelength?
Answer- Work function
$$ \phi = h\nu_0 = \frac{hc}{\lambda_0} $$
If the wavelength of light is greater than the threshold wavelength, the energy of the radiation will be less than the work function of the metal. As a result, photoelectric emission will not be possible.
Q - The threshold wavelength of a metal surface is 5000 Å. Will this surface emit photoelectrons for red light? (2017)
Since the threshold wavelength of the metal surface is 5000 Å, while the wavelength of red light is greater than this, the wavelength of the incident light must be less than 5000 Å for electron emission from the metal surface to occur. Hence, this metal surface will not emit photoelectrons for red light.
Q- What is photoelectric emission?
When ultraviolet radiation is made incident on a metal surface, electrons start being emitted from that metal surface; this is called photoelectric emission.
Q - Write the laws of photoelectric emission. (2013/17/19)
Lenard and Millikan, based on the photoelectric effect, formulated the following laws of photoelectric emission -
  1. Electron emission from a surface occurs immediately after light falls on it.
  2. If the frequency of the incident light is less than a certain minimum value, photoelectrons are not emitted. This minimum value is called the threshold frequency.
  3. The maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons is directly proportional to the frequency of the incident light.
  4. The rate of photoelectron emission from a metal surface is directly proportional to the intensity of the incident light.
Q - What is a photon?
According to quantum theory, small bundles (packets) of radiation energy are called photons.
Q - Write the expressions for the energy and momentum of a photon. (2018/22)
Energy of a photon \(E = h\nu\)

Momentum of a photon \(p = mc\)
Q- Write some properties of a photon. (2024)
The properties of a photon are as follows—
  1. In vacuum, a photon travels at the speed of light.
  2. The rest mass of a photon is zero.
  3. Photons travel in a straight line.
  4. Photons have no charge, so they are electrically neutral.
Q- What is a photovoltaic cell or photocell? Draw its labelled diagram and write its uses.
A photovoltaic cell is a device that converts light energy directly into electrical energy.

It works on the principle of the photoelectric effect. As soon as light falls on it, an electric potential is generated in it.
Uses —
  1. In solar panels.
  2. In light sensors.
  3. In calculators, etc.
Q What is the stopping (retarding) potential? (2015/17/20/22/23/24)
The negative potential of the collector plate (anode A) of a photocell at which the photoelectric current becomes zero is called the stopping potential or retarding potential.
Q What effect does the frequency of the incident radiation have on the stopping potential? (2020)
The intensity of the incident light (radiation) has no effect on the stopping potential, and it increases as the frequency is increased.
Q What are de Broglie or matter waves? Write their characteristics (properties). (2015/21/22)
A wave is associated with every moving particle; this wave is called the de Broglie wave or matter wave.

Two characteristics (properties) of matter waves are as follows.
  1. A matter wave is associated with every moving particle.
  2. Matter waves are not electromagnetic waves.
Q- Write de Broglie's wave equation and prove it. (2009/12)
According to Louis de Broglie, a wave is associated with every moving particle.

De Broglie's wave equation
$$ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mc} $$
Where—
  • \(\lambda\) = wavelength of the particle
  • h = Planck's constant
  • p = momentum
  • m = mass of the particle
  • v = velocity of the particle
Derivation —

According to Planck, for a photon —
$$ E = h\nu $$
According to Einstein —
$$ E = mc^2 $$
Equating both:
$$ h\nu = mc^2 $$
$$ h\left(\frac{c}{\lambda}\right) = mc^2 \quad (\because \nu = c/\lambda) $$
$$ \frac{h}{\lambda} = mc $$
$$ \lambda = \frac{h}{mc} $$
$$ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} $$
This is de Broglie's wave equation, which proves the wave nature of particles.

(In the source notes, this derivation uses the speed-of-light symbol \(c\) for the particle's velocity, whereas the next question writes the same formula as \(\lambda=h/mv\) — this is an inconsistency in the original document, retained here exactly as-is.)
Q - Why is the wave nature of matter not observed in everyday life? 2016 Or, why is the de Broglie wave theory not observed in everyday life? (Question Bank)
According to de Broglie, the wavelength associated with a moving mass of matter travelling with velocity is given below.
$$ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} $$
Since the mass of matter associated with objects in everyday life is quite large and the wavelength is very small, this theory is not observed in everyday life.
Q - Explain the dual nature of radiation. (2015, 25 Question Bank)
Radiation (light) behaves like a wave in some phenomena and behaves like a particle in others. This nature of radiation is called the dual nature of radiation.
