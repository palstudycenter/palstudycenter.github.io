📘 Magnetism
Question: What is called magnetism?
The property by which a magnet attracts magnetic materials is called Magnetism.
Question: What is called a magnet?
The substance that attracts magnetic materials is called a Magnet.
Question: What are called magnetic materials?
Substances that are attracted by a magnet, or in which magnetism can be induced, are called Magnetic Materials.

Examples: Iron, Nickel, and Cobalt.
Question: Write the difference between a magnet and a magnetic material.
Magnet Magnetic Material
It has permanent north and south poles. It does not have permanent poles.
Its magnetic properties are permanent. Its magnetic properties are generally temporary.
It attracts other magnetic materials on its own. It is attracted and displays magnetic properties only when brought under the influence of a magnet.
Examples: Bar magnet, horseshoe magnet. Examples: Iron, nickel, cobalt.
Question: What are called natural magnets and artificial magnets?
1. Natural Magnet:

A magnet obtained naturally from nature is called a natural magnet. Its shape is irregular, and it is relatively less powerful.


Example: Lodestone

2. Artificial Magnet:

A magnet made by humans is called an artificial magnet. These can be made in various shapes as required, and they are more powerful than natural magnets.
Question: How many types of artificial magnets are there?
Artificial magnets are mainly of two types—
  1. Permanent Magnet
  2. Temporary Magnet
Question: What are called permanent magnets and temporary magnets?
1. Temporary Magnet:

Magnets whose magnetism is not permanent are called temporary magnets. These are generally made of soft iron. Their magnetism can be induced easily and also disappears quickly.

2. Permanent Magnet:

Magnets whose magnetism persists for a long time are called permanent magnets. These are usually made of hardened steel, nickel, and cobalt. Their magnetism does not disappear quickly.
Question: Permanent magnets are made of steel, while a transformer's core is made of soft iron. Why?
  1. Permanent magnets are made of steel because, once induced, magnetism in steel persists for a long time.
  2. A transformer's core is made of soft iron because magnetism in it exists only as long as current flows, and it disappears as soon as the current is switched off.
Question: What are called the poles of a magnet?
The points at the ends of a magnet where the magnetic attraction is maximum are called Magnetic Poles.

Every magnet has two poles—
  1. North Pole
  2. South Pole
Question: Write the properties and characteristics of a magnet.
The main properties and characteristics of a magnet are as follows—
  1. A magnet attracts magnetic materials towards itself.
  2. When suspended freely, it always comes to rest in the north–south direction.
  3. The poles of a magnet cannot be separated in any way. No matter how many pieces a magnet is broken into, each piece will have both a north pole and a south pole.
  4. An iron object can be made magnetic by repeatedly rubbing it with a magnet in the same direction.
  5. Repeatedly dropping a magnet, striking it with a hammer, or heating it excessively can destroy or reduce its magnetism.
Question: State the molecular theory of magnetism.
Molecular Theory of Magnetism:

According to the molecular theory of magnetism—
  1. Every magnetic material is made up of countless tiny minute magnets, called Molecular Magnets.
  2. Every molecular magnet has two poles—
    • North Pole
    • South Pole
  3. The north and south poles of any molecular magnet cannot be separated from each other.
  4. In the normal state, all these molecular magnets are arranged irregularly in various directions, so the material does not display magnetic properties.
  5. When the material is placed in a magnetic field, all its molecular magnets get arranged in the same direction, causing the material to behave like a magnet.
Question: What is meant by the demagnetisation of a magnet?
When a magnet is repeatedly dropped, struck with a hammer, or heated excessively, its magnetism is destroyed or reduced. This process is called Demagnetisation.
Question: What is called magnetic induction?
When a magnetic material develops magnetism, without being touched, on being brought near a magnet or placed in its magnetic field, this process is called Magnetic Induction.
Question: What is called pole strength?
The ability of a magnetic pole to attract magnetic materials towards itself is called the Pole Strength of that pole.

  • It is denoted by \(m\).
  • It is a Scalar Quantity.
  • Its SI unit is ampere × metre (A·m).
Question: State Coulomb's inverse square law.
According to this law, the force of attraction or repulsion acting between two magnetic poles is directly proportional to the product of the pole strengths of the two poles and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
Let two magnetic poles with strengths \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) be situated at a distance \(r\) from each other; then, according to Coulomb's law, the force acting between them is given by the following:
\[ F\propto m_1m_2 \]
\[ F\propto\frac{1}{r^2} \]
Combining both proportionalities,
\[ F\propto\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2} \]
\[ \boxed{F=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\times\frac{m_1m_2}{r^2}} \]
where \( \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \) is a proportionality constant.
Q. What is the value of \( \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \)?
The value of \( \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \) is \( 10^{-7} \, \mathrm{N/A^2} \).
Question: What is \( \mu_0 \)? Write its value.

\(\mu_0\) is called the magnetic permeability of free space.

Its value is given below.

$$ \mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\, \mathrm{N/A^2} $$
Question: What is called a magnetic field?
The region around a magnet up to which the effect of its magnetic force can be felt is called the Magnetic Field.
Question: What do you understand by the intensity of a magnetic field? Write its S.I. unit.
The force acting on a unit north pole placed at a point in a magnetic field is called the intensity of the magnetic field at that point. It is denoted by \(B\).

If a force \(F\) acts on a pole strength \(m\), then
\[ \boxed{B=\frac{F}{m}} \]
  • It is a Vector Quantity.
  • Its SI unit is newton/(ampere × metre) \(\left(\mathrm{N/(A\cdot m)}\right)\) or Tesla.
Question: What is called magnetic moment?
The product of the pole strength of a magnet and the effective distance between its poles is called the Magnetic Moment.“It is represented by \(M\).”

Let \(NS\) be a bar magnet whose pole strength is \(m\) and effective length is \(2l\); then its magnetic moment will be—
\[ \boxed{M=m\times2l} \]
Its SI unit is ampere × metre² (A·m²).
Q. Find the intensity of the magnetic field at an axial (longitudinal) position of a bar magnet.
Consider a bar magnet \(NS\), whose magnetic pole strength is \(m\), and the distance between its poles is \(2l\). We have to find the magnetic field at point \(P\), which is at a distance \(r\) from the center \(O\) of the bar magnet and lies on its axial position.
According to the figure: \[ SP = r + l \] \[ NP = r - l \]
Now, the magnetic field at point \(P\) due to the north pole \(N\) is:
$$ B_1 = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{m}{(NP)^2} $$ $$ B_1 = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{m}{(r-l)^2}\;(\overrightarrow{NP}) $$
Again, the magnetic field at point \(P\) due to the south pole \(S\) is:
$$ B_2 = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{m}{(SP)^2} $$ $$ B_2 = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{m}{(r+l)^2}\;(\overrightarrow{PS}) $$
Therefore, the resultant magnetic field is:
$$ B = B_1 - B_2 $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 m}{4\pi(r-l)^2} - \frac{\mu_0 m}{4\pi(r+l)^2} $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 m}{4\pi} \left[ \frac{1}{(r-l)^2} - \frac{1}{(r+l)^2} \right] $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 m}{4\pi} \left[ \frac{(r+l)^2-(r-l)^2} {(r^2-l^2)^2} \right] $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 m}{4\pi} \left[ \frac{(r^2+l^2+2rl)-(r^2+l^2-2rl)} {(r^2-l^2)^2} \right] $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 m}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{4rl}{(r^2-l^2)^2} $$
If the bar magnet is very small \((l^2 \ll r^2)\), then \(l^2\) can be neglected.
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0 m}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{4rl}{(r^2-0)^2} $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{4mrl}{r^4} $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{2(2ml)}{r^3} $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{2M}{r^3} \quad (\text{∵ } M=2ml) $$
Q. Find the intensity of the magnetic field at the equatorial position (perpendicular to the magnetic axis) of a bar magnet.
Consider a bar magnet \(NS\), whose north and south poles have pole strength \(m\), and the distance between the poles is \(2l\). We have to find the magnetic field at point \(P\), which is at a distance \(r\) from the center \(O\) of the bar magnet and lies on its equatorial position.
Consider a unit north pole at point \(P\).
Now, in right-angled \(NOS\), by Pythagoras' theorem, \[ SP^2 = OP^2 + OA^2 \] \[ SP^2 = r^2 + l^2 \] \[ SP = \sqrt{r^2 + l^2} = NP \] Also, \[ \cos\theta = \frac{OS}{SP} \] \[ \cos\theta = \frac{l}{\sqrt{r^2 + l^2}} \]
Now, the magnetic field at point \(P\) due to the north pole \(N\) is:
$$ B_1 = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{m}{(NP)^2} $$ $$ B_1 = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{m}{(r-l)^2}\;(\overrightarrow{NP}) $$
Again, the magnetic field at point \(P\) due to the south pole \(S\) is:
$$ B_2 = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{m}{(SP)^2} $$ $$ B_2 = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{m}{(r+l)^2}\;(\overrightarrow{PS}) $$
Since \(B_1 = B_2\), their components along the magnetic axis cancel each other, while the perpendicular components act in the same direction and add up. Therefore, the resultant magnetic field is:
$$ B = B_1 \cos\theta + B_2 \cos\theta $$
$$ B = 2B_1 \cos\theta $$
$$ B = 2 \cdot \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{m}{(r^2 + l^2)} \cdot \frac{l}{\sqrt{r^2 + l^2}} $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{2ml}{(r^2 + l^2)^{3/2}} $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{M}{(r^2 + l^2)^{3/2}} \quad (\text{∵ } M = 2ml) $$
If the bar magnet is very small \((l^2 \ll r^2)\), then \(l^2\) can be neglected.
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{M}{(r^2 + 0)^{3/2}} $$
$$ B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{M}{r^3} $$
At the equatorial position, the direction of the magnetic field is opposite to the direction of the magnetic dipole moment \(M\).
Q. Derive the formula for the torque acting on a bar magnet placed in a uniform magnetic field.
Consider a bar magnet \(NS\), whose pole strength is \(m\) and effective length is \(2l\). It is placed in a uniform magnetic field \(B\), making an angle \(\theta\) with the direction of the magnetic field. Then equal and opposite forces act on its two poles, which form a couple and tend to rotate the magnet to bring it in the direction of the magnetic field. Therefore, the torque produced by this couple is given by:
\[ \tau = \text{One force} \times \text{Perpendicular distance between the two forces} \]
$$ \tau = mB \times NA $$
Now, in the right-angled triangle \( \triangle NAS \):
$$ \sin\theta = \frac{NA}{NS} $$ $$ \sin\theta = \frac{NA}{2l} $$ $$ NA = 2l\sin\theta $$
Substituting the value in the above equation:
$$ \tau = mB \times 2l\sin\theta $$
$$ \tau = (m \times 2l)B\sin\theta $$
$$ \tau = MB\sin\theta \quad (\text{∵ } M=2ml) $$
where \(M\) is the magnetic dipole moment of the bar magnet.
Case I: If \(\theta = 0^\circ\), then
$$ \tau = MB\sin0^\circ $$ $$ \tau = MB \times 0 $$ $$ \tau_{\min}=0 $$
Case II: If \(\theta = 90^\circ\), then
$$ \tau = MB\sin90^\circ $$ $$ \tau = MB \times 1 $$ $$ \tau_{\max}=MB $$

Question: What are called magnetic field lines? Write their properties.
Magnetic Field Lines:

The imaginary lines drawn in a magnetic field, such that the tangent drawn at any point on them represents the direction of the magnetic field at that point, are called Magnetic Field Lines.

Properties of magnetic field lines:
  1. Magnetic field lines emerge from the north pole (N) outside the magnet and enter the south pole (S), while inside the magnet they travel from the south pole towards the north pole. Hence, they form Closed Curves.
  2. The tangent to a field line at any point indicates the direction of the magnetic field at that point.
  3. Where the field lines are more dense (close together), the magnetic field is stronger, and where they are sparse, the magnetic field is weaker.
  4. Two magnetic field lines never intersect each other, because at any point the magnetic field can have only one direction.
  5. In a uniform magnetic field, the magnetic field lines are parallel to each other and equally spaced.
Question: Why do magnetic field lines not have starting and ending points?
Magnetic field lines are Closed Curves. They travel from the north pole to the south pole outside the magnet, and from the south pole to the north pole inside the magnet. Therefore, they have no starting or ending point.
Question: Why do two magnetic field lines never intersect each other?
If two magnetic field lines were to intersect each other, the point of intersection would have two directions of the magnetic field, whereas at any point the magnetic field can have only one direction. Therefore, two magnetic field lines never intersect each other.
Question: Why do like poles repel each other?
Between like poles, the magnetic field lines run in directions opposite to each other and do not join up. As a result, a repulsive force is produced between them. Hence, like poles repel each other.
Question: Why do unlike poles attract each other?
Between unlike poles, the magnetic field lines connect continuously from one pole to the other. This produces an attractive force between them. Hence, unlike poles attract each other.
Question: If, at some location, the magnetic field lines are parallel to each other and equally spaced, what information does this give about the magnetic field?
If, at some location, the magnetic field lines are parallel to each other and equally spaced, then a Uniform Magnetic Field exists there. That is, the direction and intensity of the magnetic field are the same at every point of that region.
Question: Write the difference between electric field lines and magnetic field lines.
Electric Field Lines Magnetic Field Lines
These emerge from a positive charge and terminate on a negative charge. These are closed curves and have no beginning or end.
These have a beginning and an end. These have no beginning and no end.
These represent the direction of the electric field. These represent the direction of the magnetic field.
Two electric field lines also never intersect each other. Two magnetic field lines also never intersect each other.
Question: What is called a magnetic neutral point?
The point where the intensities of the magnetic field produced by a magnet and the Earth's magnetic field are equal and opposite in direction, so that the resultant magnetic field becomes zero, is called the Magnetic Neutral Point.
Question: State Gauss's law relating to magnetism.
Gauss's Law for Magnetism:

According to this law, the total magnetic flux passing through any closed surface is always zero.
\[ \boxed{\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{A}=0} \]
This is because magnetic field lines always form Closed Curves, and an isolated magnetic pole does not exist independently.
Question: What is called a current-carrying solenoid? Explain by drawing a labelled diagram.
When electric current is passed through a coil made by winding a conducting wire into a cylindrical shape with many turns, it is called a Current Carrying Solenoid.

When current is passed through it, it behaves like a bar magnet, and a north pole (N) forms at one end while a south pole (S) forms at the other end.
