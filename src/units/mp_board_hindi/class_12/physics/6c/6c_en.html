📘 Unit 6(C) - Prism
Q: What is a prism?
A prism is a transparent medium bounded by two triangular and three rectangular surfaces.
Q: Explain, with the help of a diagram, the refraction of light through a prism. (Question Bank)
Or draw a labelled diagram of the refraction of light through a prism.
Suppose a light ray OP falls on point P of the refracting face AB of prism ABC, making an angle of incidence \(i\) with the normal NP, and bends towards the normal, travelling further while making an angle of refraction \(r_1\). This ray then falls on point Q of the second refracting surface AC of the prism, making an angle of incidence \(r_2\) with the normal NQ, and, bending away from the normal, emerges in the direction QR making an angle of emergence e.
Q: What is meant by the angle of deviation of a prism? (Question Bank)
The angle formed between the incident ray and the emergent ray is called the angle of deviation. It is denoted by \(\delta\).
Q: What is the angle of minimum deviation?
Initially, as the angle of incidence is increased, the angle of deviation goes on decreasing. For one particular value of the angle of incidence, the angle of deviation is minimum. After that, on further increasing the angle of incidence, the angle of deviation, instead of decreasing, starts increasing. This minimum value of the angle of deviation is called the angle of minimum deviation. It is denoted by \(\delta_m\).
Q: Write the conditions for minimum deviation.
Under the condition of minimum deviation —
  1. the refracted ray is parallel to the base
  2. the angle of incidence and the angle of emergence are equal, i.e. \(i = r\)
  3. both angles of refraction are equal, i.e. \(r_1 = r_2\)
Question: Derive the formula for the refractive index of the material of a prism. (2025)
OR Prove that $$ \mu = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)} $$
Proof
Consider a prism ABC. A ray of light OP is incident at point P on the refracting face AB of the prism, making an angle of incidence \(i\) with the normal NP. It bends towards the normal and travels inside the prism making an angle of refraction \(r_1\). This ray is incident at point Q on the second refracting face AC, making an angle \(r_2\) with the normal NQ, and bends away from the normal, emerging in the direction QR with an angle of emergence \(e\).

When the prism is in the position of minimum deviation, then in this particular condition—

  1. The refracted ray PQ is parallel to the base BC.That is, \(PQ \parallel BC\).
  2. The angle of deviation becomes minimum.That is, \(\delta = \delta_m\).
  3. The angle of incidence and the angle of emergence are equal.That is, \(i_1 = i_2 = i\).
  4. The two angles of refraction are equal.That is, \(r_1 = r_2 = r\).

In quadrilateral APNQ, the angles \(\angle APN\) and \(\angle AQN\) are both \(90^\circ\). Therefore,

\(\angle A + \angle PNQ = 180^\circ\)

\(\angle PNQ = 180^\circ - A\)

In triangle PNQ—
$$ r_1 + r_2 + \angle PNQ = 180^\circ $$ $$ r + r + 180^\circ - A = 180^\circ $$ $$ 2r - A = 0 $$ $$ 2r = A $$ $$ r = \frac{A}{2} $$
Now, the total minimum angle of deviation \(\delta_m\) is the sum of the deviations produced at the two refracting faces—
$$ \delta_m = \delta_1 + \delta_2 $$ $$ \delta_m = (i_1 - r_1) + (i_2 - r_2) $$ $$ \delta_m = (i - r) + (i - r) $$ $$ \delta_m = 2i - 2r $$ $$ \delta_m = 2i - A $$ $$ 2i = \delta_m + A $$ $$ i = \frac{\delta_m + A}{2} $$
Now, let \(\mu\) be the refractive index of the material of the prism with respect to air. According to Snell's law—
$$ \mu=\frac{\sin i}{\sin r} $$
$$\boxed{\mu=\frac{\sin(\frac{\delta_m + A}{2})}{\sin(\frac{A}{2})}} $$
Hence, this is the formula for the refractive index of the material of a prism.
Q: Prove that for a thin prism \(\delta_m = A(\mu - 1)\)
Proof:

We know that the refractive index of the prism material is—
$$ \mu=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\delta_m + A}{2}\right)} {\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)} $$
For a thin prism, \(A\) and \(\delta_m\) are very small. Therefore, for small angles, \(\sin\theta \approx \theta\). Hence,
$$ \mu \approx \frac{\frac{\delta_m + A}{2}} {\frac{A}{2}} $$
$$ \mu = \frac{\delta_m + A}{A} $$
$$ \mu A = \delta_m + A $$
$$ \delta_m = \mu A - A $$
$$ \boxed{\delta_m = A(\mu - 1)} $$
Question: On which factors does the angle of minimum deviation depend?
Answer:
We knowthat
\(\delta_m = A(\mu - 1)\)
Therefore, The angle of minimum deviation depends on the following factors—
  1. Angle of the prism - The angle of minimum deviation increases when the angle of the prism is increased.
  2. Material of the prism - The angle of minimum deviation increases when the refractive index of the prism material is higher.
  3. Colour of light - The angle of minimum deviation is maximum for violet light and minimum for red light.
Q: How does dispersion of light take place through a prism? Explain.
Or why does dispersion of light occur after passing through a prism?
When sunlight passes through a prism, it splits into seven colours; this is called dispersion.
Reason — Sunlight is made up of seven colours. The refractive index of the prism material is different for different colours. Hence, according to the formula \(d_m = A(u - 1)\), while passing through the prism, rays of different colours undergo different deviations, and this is why sunlight splits into seven colours when passing through a prism.
Q: What is a spectrum?
The group of colours obtained due to the phenomenon of dispersion of light is called a spectrum.
The order of colours in the spectrum of white light is as follows:
Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange and Red.
Q: Why does no dispersion occur in the light refracted through a rectangular glass slab?
After refraction at the two parallel surfaces of the slab, the emergent rays are parallel to the incident rays. Dispersion of white light does occur inside the slab, but when the rays of different colours emerge from the slab, they are parallel to one another, and so all the colours recombine to form white light again.
Q: Define angular dispersion and derive a formula for it.
The angle between the rays of the two extreme colours emerging from a prism is called angular dispersion. It is denoted by \(\theta\).

If the deviation of violet light is \(\delta_v\) and the deviation of red light is \(\delta_r\), then the angular dispersion between these colours is—
$$ \theta = \delta_v - \delta_r $$
Derivation of the Formula —
Let the angle of the prism be \(A\), and let the refractive indices of the prism material for violet and red light be \(\mu_v\) and \(\mu_r\), respectively. Then—

The angle of deviation for violet light is—
$$ \delta_v = A(\mu_v - 1) $$
The angle of deviation for red light is—
$$ \delta_r = A(\mu_r - 1) $$
Therefore, the angular dispersion is—
$$ \theta = \delta_v - \delta_r $$ ``` $$ \theta = A(\mu_v - 1) - A(\mu_r - 1) $$ $$ \theta = A[(\mu_v - 1) - (\mu_r - 1)] $$ $$ \theta = A[\mu_v - 1 - \mu_r + 1] $$ $$ \boxed{\theta = A(\mu_v - \mu_r)} $$
Question: What is meant by dispersive power? Derive the formula for it.
The ratio of the angular dispersion between any two colours to the deviation of the mean ray is called the dispersive power of a prism for those colours. It is represented by \(\omega\).

Let the angles of deviation for violet, red, and mean yellow light be \(\delta_v\), \(\delta_r\), and \(\delta_y\), respectively. Therefore, the dispersive power is—
$$ \omega = \frac{\delta_v - \delta_r}{\delta_y} $$
Derivation of the Formula —
Let the angle of the prism be \(A\), and let the refractive indices of the prism material for violet, red, and yellow light be \(\mu_v\), \(\mu_r\), and \(\mu_y\), respectively. Then—

The angle of deviation for violet light is—
$$ \delta_v = A(\mu_v - 1) $$
The angle of deviation for red light is—
$$ \delta_r = A(\mu_r - 1) $$
The angle of deviation for yellow light is—
$$ \delta_y = A(\mu_y - 1) $$
Therefore, the dispersive power is—
$$ \omega = \frac{\delta_v - \delta_r}{\delta_y} $$ $$ \omega = \frac{A(\mu_v - 1) - A(\mu_r - 1)} {A(\mu_y - 1)} $$ $$ \omega = \frac{A[\mu_v - 1 - \mu_r + 1]} {A(\mu_y - 1)} $$ $$ \boxed{\omega = \frac{\mu_v - \mu_r}{\mu_y - 1}} $$
Q: What is scattering of light?
When light strikes fine particles whose size is small compared to the wavelength of light, the light is scattered in various directions; this is called scattering of light.
Question: State Rayleigh's law.
According to Rayleigh's law, the intensity \(I\) of scattered light is inversely proportional to the fourth power of its wavelength \(\lambda\).

$$ I \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4} $$
$$ I = \frac{K}{\lambda^4} $$
where \(K\) is a constant.

Red light has the longest wavelength in the visible spectrum; therefore, its scattering intensity is minimum.

Blue light has a shorter wavelength than red light; therefore, its scattering intensity is greater than that of red light.
Q: Why does the sun appear red at sunrise and sunset?
At sunrise and sunset, light rays have to travel a greater distance to reach us from the sun. Since red light has a longer wavelength, it is scattered very little by the molecules of the atmosphere, whereas all the other colours get scattered almost completely. Hence, the sun appears red at sunrise and sunset.
Q: Why does the sky appear blue?
According to Rayleigh, light of shorter wavelength is scattered more. We know that the wavelength of blue light is less than that of red light. So, when sunlight falls on the molecules of the atmosphere, blue light is scattered more than the other colours. Hence, the sky appears blue.
Q: Why are danger signals red in colour? (2022)
Red light has the longest wavelength, so red light is scattered the least and can travel the farthest. This allows the signal to be seen from far away, which is why danger signals are red in colour.
Q: What is a rainbow? How many types are there?
The semicircular display of seven colours formed by the refraction, reflection, and dispersion of sunlight in raindrops is called a rainbow.

There are two types of rainbows—

Primary rainbow - When rays travelling parallel to the earth fall on water drops above their centres, the rays undergo refraction twice and total internal reflection once; the rainbow formed due to this is called the primary rainbow.

The angular width of the primary rainbow is \(2^\circ\), with red on the outer edge and violet on the inner edge.

Secondary rainbow - When rays travelling parallel to the earth fall on water drops below their centres, the rays undergo refraction twice and total internal reflection twice; the rainbow formed due to this is called the secondary rainbow.

The angular width of the secondary rainbow is \(2^\circ\), with violet on the outer edge and red on the inner edge.

Question: A ray of light incident on an equilateral glass prism undergoes a minimum deviation of \(30^\circ\).
  1. Calculate the speed of light through the prism.
  2. Find the angle of incidence on the first face such that the emergent ray travels along the second face.
Solution:

Given:
For an equilateral prism,
$$ A = 60^\circ $$
Minimum deviation,
$$ \delta_m = 30^\circ $$
Speed of light in vacuum,
$$ c = 3\times10^8\ m/s $$
1. Speed of light through the prism:

The formula for the refractive index of the prism at minimum deviation is—
$$ \mu = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A+\delta_m}{2}\right)} {\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)} $$
Substituting the given values—
$$ \mu = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{60^\circ+30^\circ}{2}\right)} {\sin 30^\circ} $$
$$ \mu = \frac{\sin45^\circ}{\sin30^\circ} $$
$$ \mu = \frac{\frac{1}{\sqrt2}}{\frac{1}{2}} $$
$$ \mu = \sqrt2 $$
The formula for the speed of light in the medium is—
$$ v = \frac{c}{\mu} $$
Therefore,
$$ v = \frac{3\times10^8}{\sqrt2} $$
$$ \boxed{v \approx 2.12\times10^8\ m/s} $$

2. Angle of incidence on the first face:

According to Snell's law—
$$ \sin i_c = \frac{1}{\mu} $$
$$ \sin i_c = \frac{1}{\sqrt2} $$
$$ i_c = 45^\circ $$
When the emergent ray travels along the second face, the angle of incidence \(r_2\) at the second face is equal to the critical angle \(i_c\). Therefore,
$$ r_2 = 45^\circ $$
For a prism,
$$ r_1+r_2=A $$
$$ r_1+45^\circ=60^\circ $$
$$ r_1=15^\circ $$
Now, applying Snell's law at the first face—
$$ \sin i = \mu\sin r_1 $$
$$ \sin i = \sqrt2\sin15^\circ $$
$$ \sin i = \sqrt2\frac{\sqrt3-1}{2\sqrt2} $$
$$ \sin i = \frac{\sqrt3-1}{2} $$
$$ \sin i \approx 0.366 $$
$$ \boxed{i\approx21.5^\circ} $$
