Electric Current
Question - What is meant by electric current? Write its unit. (M.P. 2021)

Answer -

The flow of charge through a cross-section of a conductor per unit time is called electric current. It is denoted by I.

If Q charge flows through the conductor in t seconds, then the electric current is —

\[ I=\frac{Q}{t} \]

The S.I. unit of electric current is Ampere.

Question - What is an Ampere?

Answer -

Ampere is the S.I. unit of electric current.

If 1 Coulomb of charge flows through the cross-section of a conductor in 1 second, then the magnitude of the current flowing in that conductor is 1 Ampere.

\[ 1\ Ampere=\frac{1\ Coulomb}{1\ Second} \]

Question - What is current density? Write its unit. (M.P. 2024)

Answer -

The electric current flowing per unit cross-sectional area of a conductor at a point is called current density. It is denoted by J.

If I is the current flowing in the conductor and A is the cross-sectional area of the conductor, then

\[ J=\frac{I}{A} \]

The S.I. unit of current density is —

\[ Ampere\; per\; metre^2 \]

Or

\[ A/m^2 \]

Current density is a vector quantity.

Question - What do you understand by Drift Velocity? (2026)

Answer -

When a potential difference is applied across the ends of a conductor, the free electrons inside it start moving from the negative end to the positive end of the conductor.

This average velocity of free electrons is called Drift Velocity.

It is denoted by Vd.

Question - Write Ohm's Law.

Answer -

At constant temperature, the potential difference (V) developed across the ends of a conductor is directly proportional to the current (I) flowing through it.

That is,

\[ V \propto I \]

Or

\[ V = RI \]

Where R is the proportionality constant, known as the resistance of the conductor.

This relationship is called Ohm's Law.

Question - Write the limitations of Ohm's Law.

Conditions (Limitations) of Ohm's Law

  1. This law is true only for metallic conductors.
  2. The temperature, length, and other physical conditions of the conductor must remain constant.
  3. This law does not apply to diodes, transistors, thermistors, electrolytes, and gaseous conductors.
  4. At very high temperatures or high potential differences, this law does not remain true.

Question - What is the resistance of a conductor?

The opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of electric current is called Resistance. It is denoted by R.

Its SI unit is Ohm (Ω).

Question - What are Ohmic and Non-Ohmic resistances? (2026)

Ohmic Resistance :

Resistors that obey Ohm's Law are called Ohmic resistors.

  • In these, the ratio of V to I remains constant.
  • Their V–I graph is a straight line.

Examples :

  • Copper wire
  • Nichrome wire
  • Metallic conductors

Non-Ohmic Resistance :

Resistors that do not obey Ohm's Law are called Non-Ohmic resistors.

  • In these, the ratio of V to I does not remain constant.
  • Their V–I graph is not a straight line but a curve.

Examples :

  • Diode
  • Transistor
  • Bulb filament (at high temperatures)
Question - What are the factors affecting the resistance of a conductor?

The resistance of a conductor depends on the following factors —

  1. Length of the conductor : The longer the conductor, the higher its resistance.
  2. Cross-sectional area (thickness) of the conductor : The thicker the conductor, the lower its resistance.
  3. Material of the conductor : Different materials have different resistances.
  4. Temperature : Generally, the resistance of a conductor increases with an increase in temperature.
Question - What is specific resistance?

The resistance of a conductor of unit length and unit cross-sectional area made of a certain material is called Specific Resistance. It is denoted by ρ (rho).

Its value is given by the following formula —

\[ \rho=\frac{RA}{L} \]

Its SI unit is Ohm-meter (Ω·m).

Question - What is conductance?

The ability of a solution to conduct electric current is called Conductance. It is denoted by G.

The value of conductance is equal to the reciprocal of resistance.

\[ G=\frac{1}{R} \]

Its SI unit is Ohm-1 (Ω-1) or Siemens (S).

The value of specific conductance is equal to the reciprocal of specific resistance.

\[ \kappa=\frac{1}{\rho} \]

Where ρ is specific resistance.

Its SI unit is

Question - If the length of a conductor wire is stretched and doubled, by how many times will its resistance increase? Calculate. (M.P. 2013, 2017)

Let the initial resistance of the conductor wire be R, length L, and cross-sectional area A.

Formula for resistance,

\[ R=\rho \frac{L}{A} \]

When the wire is stretched and its length is doubled, then

\[ L' = 2L \]

The volume of the wire remains constant, so

\[ AL = A'L' \]

\[ A'=\frac{AL}{2L} =\frac{A}{2} \]

New resistance,

\[ R'=\rho \frac{L'}{A'} \]

\[ R'=\rho \frac{2L}{A/2} \]

\[ R'=4\rho \frac{L}{A} \]

\[ R'=4R \]

Therefore, the resistance of the conductor wire increases by 4 times.

Question - What will be the resistance of a conductor wire if its length is halved and its cross-sectional area is doubled? (M.P. 2023)

Let the initial resistance of the conductor wire be R, length L, and cross-sectional area A.

Formula for resistance,

\[ R=\rho \frac{L}{A} \]

As per the question,

\[ L'=\frac{L}{2} \]

And

\[ A'=2A \]

New resistance,

\[ R'=\rho \frac{L'}{A'} \]

\[ R'=\rho \frac{L/2}{2A} \]

\[ R'=\rho \frac{L}{4A} \]

\[ R'=\frac{R}{4} \]

Therefore, the new resistance will be 1/4 (one-fourth) of the initial resistance.

Question : What is meant by series combination of resistors? Derive the formula for equivalent resistance in series combination.

When the second end of one resistor is connected to the first end of the next resistor, this type of combination is called Series Combination.

Expression for Equivalent Resistance :

Let R₁, R₂ and R₃ resistors be connected in series, and a current I be flowing through them.

By Ohm's Law,

\[ V_1 = IR_1 \]

\[ V_2 = IR_2 \]

\[ V_3 = IR_3 \]

If the equivalent resistance of the combination is R, then the total potential difference is

\[ V = IR \]

But we know that,

\[ V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3 \]

Therefore,

\[ IR = IR_1 + IR_2 + IR_3 \]

Dividing both sides by I,

\[ R = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 \]

Thus, in a series combination, the equivalent resistance is equal to the sum of all individual resistances.

Question : What is meant by parallel combination of resistors? Derive the formula for equivalent resistance in parallel combination.

When one end of all resistors is connected to one point and the other ends to another point, this type of combination is called Parallel Combination.

Expression for Equivalent Resistance :

Let three resistors R₁, R₂ and R₃ be connected in parallel, and a potential difference V be applied across their ends.

By Ohm's Law,

\[ I_1=\frac{V}{R_1} \]

\[ I_2=\frac{V}{R_2} \]

\[ I_3=\frac{V}{R_3} \]

If the equivalent resistance of this combination is R, then the total current is

\[ I=\frac{V}{R} \]

In parallel combination, the total current is,

\[ I=I_1+I_2+I_3 \]

Therefore,

\[ \frac{V}{R} = \frac{V}{R_1} + \frac{V}{R_2} + \frac{V}{R_3} \]

Dividing by V,

\[ \boxed{ \frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} } \]

Thus, in a parallel combination, the reciprocal of the equivalent resistance is equal to the sum of the reciprocals of all individual resistances.

Question : Write Kirchhoff's laws (laws related to current distribution).

Kirchhoff's First Law (Current Law) :

The algebraic sum of all electric currents meeting at any junction in an electrical circuit is zero.

\[ \sum I = 0 \]

Currents flowing towards the junction are taken as positive, and currents flowing away from the junction are taken as negative.

If currents \(I_1\) and \(I_2\) are entering a junction, and \(I_3\), \(I_4\) and \(I_5\) are leaving the junction, then

\[ I_1 + I_2 - I_3 - I_4 - I_5 = 0 \]

\[ I_1 + I_2 = I_3 + I_4 + I_5 \]

Thus, the sum of all currents entering any junction is equal to the sum of all currents leaving that junction.

Kirchhoff's First Law is based on the principle of conservation of charge.

Kirchhoff's Second Law (Voltage Law) :

In any closed electrical circuit, the algebraic sum of the products of currents flowing in various sections and their corresponding resistances (IR) is equal to the algebraic sum of all electromotive forces (EMF) applied in that circuit.

\[ \sum IR = \sum E \]

For the closed loop (1) as per the diagram,

\[ I_1R_1 - I_2R_2 = E_1 - E_2 \]

And for the closed loop (2),

\[ I_2R_2 + (I_1 + I_2)R_3 = E_2 \]

This law is based on the principle of conservation of energy and applies only to closed circuits.

Question : What is the principle of Wheatstone bridge? Establish it.

Principle :

If four resistors are connected in the four arms of a quadrilateral, and a cell is connected across one diagonal and a galvanometer across the other diagonal, and the four resistors are chosen in such a way that the bridge is in a balanced state (i.e., no current flows through the galvanometer), then the ratio of the resistances of two adjacent arms is equal to the ratio of the resistances of the remaining two adjacent arms.

If four resistors P, Q, R and S are connected in the arms AB, BC, AD and CD of a quadrilateral, and a cell is connected across diagonal AC and a galvanometer G across diagonal BD, and the bridge is in a balanced state, i.e., the galvanometer shows no deflection, then according to the principle of Wheatstone bridge,

\[ \frac{P}{Q}=\frac{R}{S} \]

Derivation of the formula :

Assume that current I flows from cell E in the circuit. At point A, the current divides into two parts, I₁ and I₂.

In the balanced state of the bridge, no current flows through the galvanometer.

According to Kirchhoff's second law, for the closed loop ABDA,

\[ I_1P-I_2R=0 \]

\[ I_1P=I_2R \qquad ...(1) \]

Similarly, for the closed loop BCDB,

\[ I_1Q-I_2S=0 \]

\[ I_1Q=I_2S \qquad ...(2) \]

Dividing equation (1) by equation (2),

\[ \frac{I_1P}{I_1Q} = \frac{I_2R}{I_2S} \]

\[ \frac{P}{Q} = \frac{R}{S} \]

This is the principle of the Wheatstone bridge.

Question : When will the sensitivity of the Wheatstone bridge be maximum?

The sensitivity of the Wheatstone bridge is maximum when the resistances of all four arms of the bridge are approximately equal and the galvanometer has low resistance.

Question : What will be the effect on the balanced state if the positions of the cell and galvanometer are interchanged in a Wheatstone bridge experiment?

In a Wheatstone bridge, even if the positions of the cell and galvanometer are interchanged, there is no change in the balanced state. The balance point of the bridge remains unchanged.

Question : What is meant by the electromotive force and potential difference of a cell?

Electromotive Force (EMF):
The energy provided by a cell to move a unit charge throughout the entire circuit is called the electromotive force of that cell.

If W work is done to move Q charge, then

\[ E=\frac{W}{Q} \]

Its SI unit is Volt (V).


Potential Difference:
The work done to move a unit charge between two points in a conductor is called the potential difference between those points.

If W work is done to move Q charge from one point to another, then

\[ V=\frac{W}{Q} \]

Its SI unit is also Volt (V).

Question : What do you understand by the internal resistance of a cell?

The opposition offered to the flow of current due to the electrolyte and electrodes present inside a cell is called the internal resistance of the cell.

It is denoted by r and its unit is Ohm (Ω).

Question : On what factors does the internal resistance depend and how? (M.P. 2009, 2019, 2022)

The internal resistance of a cell depends on the following factors —

  1. Distance between electrodes : Internal resistance increases with increasing distance between electrodes and decreases with decreasing distance.
  2. Area of electrodes immersed : Internal resistance decreases with increasing immersed area of electrodes and increases with decreasing area.
  3. Nature of the electrolyte : The value of internal resistance varies for different electrolytes.
  4. Concentration of the electrolyte : Internal resistance decreases with increasing concentration and increases with decreasing concentration.
  5. Temperature : Internal resistance decreases with increasing temperature because the mobility of ions increases.

Question - Why do the headlights of a motor vehicle dim when starting it?

Answer -

When starting a motor vehicle, the starter motor draws a very high current from the battery. This causes a drop in the potential difference across the battery terminals.

Due to the reduced potential difference, the headlights receive less electrical power, which causes their brightness to dim for some time.

Question - Why do bulbs dim when a heater is turned on?

Answer -

A heater is a high-power appliance that draws a significant amount of current from the electrical circuit.

Due to the high current flow, the voltage drop across the wires increases, resulting in the bulbs receiving a comparatively lower potential difference.

Consequently, the brightness of the bulbs decreases, and they appear dim.

Question - Why is it difficult to start a car engine in winter?

Answer -

In winters, at low temperatures, the rate of chemical reactions in the battery decreases, reducing the battery's electromotive force and its capacity to deliver current.

Additionally, the viscosity of the engine's lubricating oil increases, requiring more force to turn the engine.

For these reasons, it is difficult to start a car engine in winter.

Question - What is the combination of cells?

Answer -

When two or more cells are connected in a specific order to obtain the required electromotive force (EMF) or current, it is called the combination of cells.

The combination of cells is mainly of two types —

  1. Series Combination
  2. Parallel Combination
Question - How are cells connected in parallel? Derive the expression for the current flowing in the external circuit. When is this combination advantageous?

Answer -

When all positive terminals of several cells are connected together and all negative terminals are connected together, this combination is called a parallel combination.

Let's assume that each cell has an electromotive force (emf) E and internal resistance r, and n such identical cells are connected in parallel. The external resistance is R.

When connected in parallel, the equivalent electromotive force of the battery is

\[ E_p = E \]

And the equivalent internal resistance is

\[ r_p=\frac{r}{n} \]

Therefore, by Ohm's law, the current flowing in the external circuit is

\[ I=\frac{E}{R+\frac{r}{n}} \]

Or

\[ I=\frac{nE}{nR+r} \]

This is the expression for the current flowing in the external circuit.

Utility of Parallel Combination :

  1. When high current is required.
  2. When the value of external resistance (R) is very small.
  3. When the internal resistance of the battery needs to be reduced.
  4. In this combination, the operating life of the cells increases.

Therefore, parallel combination is advantageous when the external resistance is low and high current is required.

Question - How are cells connected in series? Derive the expression for the current flowing in the external circuit. When is this combination advantageous?

Answer -

When the positive terminal of one cell is connected to the negative terminal of the next cell, and so on, this type of combination is called a Series Combination.

Let's assume that each cell has an electromotive force E and internal resistance r, and n such identical cells are connected in series. The external resistance is R.

When connected in series, the total electromotive force of the battery is

\[ E_s=nE \]

And the total internal resistance is

\[ r_s=nr \]

Therefore, according to Ohm's law, the current flowing in the external circuit is

\[ I=\frac{nE}{R+nr} \]

This is the expression for the current flowing in the external circuit.

Utility of Series Combination :

  1. When a higher potential difference (Voltage) is required.
  2. When the external resistance R is much greater than the total internal resistance nr.
  3. When low current and high voltage are required.

Therefore, series combination is advantageous when the external resistance is high compared to the internal resistance, and a higher potential difference is required.
