📘 Unit 8(B) - Nucleus
Q- What is meant by the nucleus?
The entire positive charge and mass of an atom is concentrated in an extremely tiny region of radius about \(10^{-15}\) metre, which is called the nucleus.
Q: What are stable and unstable nuclei?
Stable nucleus - A nucleus in which the number of protons and neutrons is approximately equal is a stable nucleus.

Unstable nucleus - A nucleus in which the number of neutrons is greater than the number of protons is an unstable nucleus.
Q - What is meant by radioactivity?
When certain unstable atoms spontaneously emit radiation from their nucleus, this natural phenomenon is called radioactivity.
Q: What are radioactive substances?
Substances whose nuclei are unstable and which spontaneously emit α, β and γ radiation are called radioactive substances.

Examples: Elements such as uranium, thorium, radium and polonium are radioactive.
Q: Why do substances exhibit the property of radioactivity?
To become more stable, unstable nuclei release their energy in the form of rays or particles, and this is why substances exhibit the property of radioactivity.
Q: Who discovered radioactivity?
Radioactivity was discovered in 1896 by the French scientist Henri Becquerel.
Q: What is the unit for measuring radioactivity?
The unit for measuring radioactivity is the curie or the becquerel.
Q - How many types of radioactive radiation are there?
Answer - There are three types of radioactive radiation.
  1. α particles
  2. β− particles
  3. γ− radiation
Q: What are α, β and γ?
  1. α (alpha) - α rays are positively charged particles and are made up of helium nuclei \((_2^4He)\). Because their mass is large, their penetrating power is low.
  2. β⁻ (beta) rays - β⁻ rays are negatively charged particles and are a stream of fast-moving electrons. Their penetrating power is greater than that of α rays.
  3. γ (gamma) rays - γ rays are electromagnetic waves or radiation, carrying neither positive nor negative charge. Their penetrating power is the greatest.
Q: Arrange α, β and γ in order of their energy, penetrating power, harmful effects and ionising power.
Answer.
  • Energy: \(\alpha<\beta<\gamma\)
  • Penetrating power: \(\alpha<\beta<\gamma\)
  • Harmful effect: \(\alpha<\beta<\gamma\)
  • Ionising power: \(\gamma<\beta<\alpha\)
Q: If a sheet of paper is placed in the path of the α, β and γ rays emitted by a radioactive substance, which one is most likely to be stopped?
Answer- α rays are most likely to be stopped, because their mass is large.
Q: Write the laws of α, β and γ radioactive radiation.
  1. Law of α-radiation - When an α-particle is emitted from a radioactive nucleus, the mass number of the nucleus decreases by 4 and the atomic number decreases by 2.
  2. Law of β-radiation - When a β⁻-particle is emitted from a nucleus, the atomic number of the nucleus increases by 1, while the mass number remains unchanged.
  3. Law of γ-radiation - In the emission of γ-radiation, there is no change in the mass number or atomic number of the nucleus; only the energy state of the nucleus changes.
Q: An atom represented by \(_{Z}^{A}X\) emits \(n\alpha\)-particles. How many protons will remain and what will be the atomic number of the new atom?
Given: Atomic number = \(Z\)

Law of α-particle emission: The atomic number \((Z)\) decreases by 2.

After disintegration: Each α-particle emitted decreases the atomic number by 2. Hence, emission of \(n\alpha\)-particles will decrease the atomic number by \(2n\).

Therefore, the atomic number of the new atom \(=Z-2n\)

Remaining protons \(=Z-2n\)
Q: A radioactive element with mass number 218 and atomic number 84 emits a β⁻-particle. What will be the mass number and atomic number of the element after disintegration?
Given: Mass number: \(A=218\), Atomic number: \(Z=84\)

Law of β⁻-radiation:
  • The mass number \((A)\) remains unchanged.
  • In β⁻ emission, the atomic number \((Z)\) increases by 1.
After disintegration: Mass number: \(A=218\), Atomic number: \(Z=84+1=85\)
Q - State the law of radioactive decay (the exponential law). (2020)
According to this law, at any instant, the rate of decay of radioactive nuclei is proportional to the number of undisintegrated nuclei present at that instant. That is,
$$ \frac{dN}{dt}\propto -N $$
where,
  • \(\dfrac{dN}{dt}\) = rate of decay
  • \(N\) = number of undisintegrated nuclei present at that instant
Q : Derive the equation of radioactive decay \(N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}\).
Answer :

According to the law of radioactive decay, at any instant, the rate of decay of radioactive nuclei is proportional to the number of undisintegrated nuclei present at that instant. That is,
$$ \frac{dN}{dt}\propto -N $$
$$ \frac{dN}{dt}=-\lambda N $$
where \(\lambda\) = decay constant.

Separating the variables,
$$ \frac{dN}{N}=-\lambda\,dt $$
Integrating,
$$ \ln N=-\lambda t+C $$
Taking the antilog,
$$ N=e^{-\lambda t+C} $$
$$ N=e^{C}e^{-\lambda t}\qquad\text{---(1)} $$
When \(t=0\), then \(N=N_0\)

Hence \(e^{C}=N_0\)

Substituting the above value into equation (1),
$$ \boxed{N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}} $$
This is the required derivation of the radioactive decay equation.
Q: What is meant by the decay constant of a radioactive substance?
The decay constant of a radioactive substance is the fixed number that indicates how many of the total undisintegrated nuclei decay per unit time. It is denoted by \(\lambda\).

The unit of \(\lambda\) is second\(^{-1}\).
Q: What is meant by the average life (mean life) of a radioactive substance?
Answer :

The average time for which the nuclei of a radioactive substance exist before decaying is called the average life (mean life) of that radioactive substance. It is denoted by \(\tau\).

If \(\lambda\) is the decay constant, then the average life of a radioactive substance is
$$ \boxed{\tau=\frac{1}{\lambda}} $$
Its unit is the second.
Q: What is meant by the half-life of a radioactive substance? Derive the formula for the half-life of a radioactive substance.
The time in which half of the nuclei of a radioactive substance decay is called the half-life. It is denoted by \(T_{1/2}\).

Derivation of the formula -

The law of radioactive decay is—
$$ N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}\qquad\text{---(1)} $$
where,
  • \(N_0\) = initial number of nuclei
  • \(N\) = number of nuclei remaining after time \(t\)
  • \(\lambda\) = decay constant
At the half-life, \(t=T_{1/2}\), so \(N=\dfrac{N_0}{2}\)

Substituting this into equation (1),
$$ \frac{N_0}{2}=N_0e^{-\lambda T_{1/2}} $$
$$ \frac{1}{2}=e^{-\lambda T_{1/2}} $$
Taking the log,
$$ \ln\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=-\lambda T_{1/2} $$
$$ -\ln 2=-\lambda T_{1/2} $$
$$ T_{1/2}=\frac{\ln 2}{\lambda} $$
Since \(\ln 2=0.693\), therefore
$$ \boxed{T_{1/2}=\frac{0.693}{\lambda}} $$
This is the established formula for the half-life of a radioactive substance.
Q: What are isotopes? Write any two isotopes of hydrogen. (2023)
Answer - Atoms of the same element that have the same atomic number but different mass numbers are called isotopes. Their chemical properties are nearly identical.

Example- For instance, hydrogen has the following three isotopes.
  1. Protium \((_1^1H)\)
  2. Deuterium \((_1^2H)\)
  3. Tritium \((_1^3H)\)
Q: What are isobars? Give an example. (2023/25)
Atoms of different elements that have the same mass number but different atomic numbers are called isobars. Their chemical properties are different.

Example: \(_{18}^{40}Ar\) and \(_{19}^{40}K\)
Q: What is the difference between isotopes and isobars?
  • Isotopes → same atomic number, different mass number.
  • Isobars → same mass number, different atomic number.
Q – What is the nuclear force? Write its properties. (2015/24/26)
The extremely strong attractive force that binds protons and neutrons together within the nucleus is called the nuclear force.

Properties of the nuclear force :
  1. This force is more powerful than the electric force.
  2. It is effective only up to nuclear distances.
  3. This force acts equally between proton–proton, neutron–neutron, and proton–neutron pairs.
  4. It is attractive at short distances, while it becomes repulsive at extremely short distances.
Q: What is meant by mass defect?
When protons and neutrons combine to form a nucleus, the total mass of the resulting nucleus is less than the sum of the masses of its constituent protons and neutrons. This decrease in mass is called mass defect, denoted by \(\Delta m\).
$$ \Delta m=\text{mass of protons}+\text{mass of neutrons}-\text{mass of nucleus} $$
$$ \Delta m=Zm_p+(A-Z)m_n-M $$
where,
  • \(A\) = mass number of the nucleus
  • \(Z\) = atomic number
  • \(m_p\) = mass of a proton
  • \(m_n\) = mass of a neutron
  • \(M\) = mass of the nucleus
Q: Write the cause of mass defect.
When protons and neutrons combine to form a nucleus, some energy is released, and this causes the mass defect in the nucleus.
Q: What is the binding energy of a nucleus?
The energy released when protons and neutrons combine to form a nucleus is called the nuclear binding energy.
Q: What is meant by binding energy per nucleon?
The energy obtained by dividing the total binding energy of a nucleus by the total number of nucleons present in it is called the binding energy per nucleon.

Significance - The greater its value, the more stable the nucleus is.
Q - What do you understand by nuclear fission? (2020/24)
When a heavy nucleus is bombarded by a slow neutron, the nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei of nearly equal size, and a large amount of energy is released. This process is called nuclear fission.

Example - When a slow-moving neutron strikes a \(_{92}^{235}U\) nucleus, it splits into two nuclei, \(_{56}^{144}Ba\) and \(_{36}^{89}Kr\), and releases three new neutrons along with a very large amount of energy (about 190 MeV).
The fission reaction of the \(_{92}^{235}U\) nucleus is expressed by the following equation-
$$ _{92}^{235}U+\,_0^1n\;\rightarrow\;_{56}^{144}Ba+\,_{36}^{89}Kr+3\,_0^1n $$

(In the source, the mass number of krypton in this example was originally written as "289", which is clearly a typing error — according to the equation given below, the correct value is 89, so 89 has been written here.)

Q: What happens when a slow neutron approaches a \(_{92}^{235}U\) nucleus?
The \(_{92}^{235}U\) nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei, releasing 3 new neutrons and energy.
Q: Only a neutron is suitable for causing nuclear fission. Why?
A neutron carries no electric charge, so it is not repelled by the positively charged protons of the nucleus. Because of this, a neutron can easily enter the nucleus, make it unstable, and cause it to undergo fission.
Q: State the source of the energy released in the process of nuclear fission.
The source of the energy released in the process of nuclear fission is mass defect. At the time of fission, a part of the mass is converted into energy, which is released as nuclear energy.
Q - What is meant by a nuclear chain reaction? Give an example.
A nuclear chain reaction is the process in which the neutrons produced by one nuclear fission induce the fission of other nuclei, causing the reaction to proceed continuously in succession.

Example - When \(_{92}^{235}U\) is bombarded with slow-moving neutrons, each uranium nucleus splits into two nearly equal fragments, and along with this, a large amount of energy and three neutrons are produced.

These neutrons also cause fission in other uranium nuclei. This continuous process of nuclear fission is called the chain reaction.
Q - How many types of nuclear chain reactions are there?
There are the following two types of nuclear chain reactions.
  1. Controlled chain reaction - In this type of chain reaction, the reaction is kept under control so that energy is released slowly and safely. It is used in a nuclear reactor to generate electricity.
  2. Uncontrolled chain reaction - In this type of chain reaction, there is no control at all, causing a very large amount of energy to be released suddenly. It is used in atomic bombs.
Q - What is a nuclear reactor? Draw its labelled diagram.
A nuclear reactor is a device in which the chain reaction of nuclear fission is carried out in a controlled manner, so that the energy obtained from it can be used for electricity generation and other purposes.

Labelled diagram
Working of a nuclear reactor-
  1. In a nuclear reactor, a controlled chain reaction is carried out by bombarding \(_{92}^{235}U\) with slow-moving neutrons.
  2. The heat obtained from the energy produced is carried away by a coolant to generate steam.
  3. This steam turns a turbine, which generates electrical energy through a generator.
Q: What are thermal neutrons?
In the controlled chain of nuclear fission, fast-moving neutrons are slowed down with the help of moderators. These slow-moving neutrons are called thermal neutrons.
Q: What is a moderator?
A moderator is a substance that slows down the fast neutrons produced in a nuclear reactor and turns them into slow-moving neutrons, so that the chain reaction of nuclear fission can proceed in a controlled manner.
Q: Name any two moderators used in nuclear reactors.
The following moderators are used to slow down the neutrons in a nuclear reactor.
  1. Heavy water
  2. Graphite
  3. Beryllium oxide
Q: Heavy water is a suitable moderator in a nuclear reactor. Why?
Answer- The mass of the nucleus of heavy water is of the same order as the mass of a neutron. Hence, neutrons slow down after colliding with heavy water.
Q: On what principle does an atomic bomb work?
An atomic bomb works on the principle of the uncontrolled chain reaction of nuclear fission.
Q: What happens when a neutron approaches a proton?
The neutron and proton combine to form a deuteron nucleus, releasing energy.
Q: Write the applications of nuclear fission.
The major applications of nuclear fission are as follows—
  1. Nuclear reactor - Controlled fission is carried out for research and energy production.
  2. Atomic bomb - The rapid release of a very large amount of energy is used in destructive weapons.
Q: What is nuclear fusion? Give an example.
When two or more extremely light nuclei fuse together at high temperature and high pressure to form a single nucleus, this process is called nuclear fusion. A huge amount of energy is released in this process.

Example - Two deuterium \((_1^2H)\) nuclei combine to form a helium \((_2^4He)\) nucleus.
$$ _1^2H+\,_1^2H\;\rightarrow\;_2^4He+21.6\text{ MeV} $$
Q: State the source of the energy released in the process of nuclear fusion.
In nuclear fusion, the mass of the nucleus produced is less than the sum of the masses of the fusing nuclei. This decrease in mass is converted into energy according to Einstein's mass-energy equivalence relation
$$ \Delta E=\Delta mc^2 $$
and this energy is released in the process.
Q – Why is nuclear fusion possible only at very high temperature and very high pressure?
In nuclear fusion, two positively charged nuclei combine, but since they carry like charges, they strongly repel each other. To overcome this repulsion—
  1. A very high temperature is required, so that the kinetic energy of the nuclei increases enough for them to come sufficiently close to each other.
  2. A very high pressure is required, so that the nuclei are densely packed together, increasing the likelihood of collision.
Q: Why has nuclear fusion not yet been used on Earth as an experimental and controlled energy source?
Nuclear fusion is possible only at very high temperature. Because it occurs at such a high temperature, controlling it is not possible.
Q: On what principle does a hydrogen bomb work?
A hydrogen bomb works on the principle of nuclear fusion.
Q: By what principle is energy produced in the Sun?
Energy is produced in the Sun by the principle of nuclear fusion.
Q: Write the difference between nuclear fission and nuclear fusion.
Nuclear fission Nuclear fusion
A heavy nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei. Two lighter nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus.
Possible even at ordinary temperature. Possible only at extremely high temperature (≈10⁸ K).
Energy per fission is about 200 MeV. Energy per fusion is about 24 MeV.
Weapon based on this process → atomic bomb. Weapon based on this process → hydrogen bomb.
