📘 Unit 3(C) - Magnetic Materials
Q: How many types of materials are there based on their magnetic behaviour?
Based on magnetic behaviour, materials are of three types –
  1. Paramagnetic materials
  2. Ferromagnetic materials
  3. Diamagnetic materials
Q: What are paramagnetic materials? Write their properties.
Materials that are weakly attracted by a magnetic field are called paramagnetic materials.

Examples: Aluminium (Al), Chromium (Cr), Platinum (Pt), Magnesium (Mg).

Properties:
  • Their magnetic moment is positive but very small.
  • Their magnetic effect disappears when the magnetic field is removed.
  • Their relative magnetic permeability \(\mu_r\) is slightly greater than one (>1).
  • Their magnetic susceptibility \(\chi\) is positive and small.
Q: What are ferromagnetic materials? Write their properties.
Materials that are strongly attracted by a magnetic field and acquire permanent magnetism are called ferromagnetic materials.

Examples: Iron (Fe), Cobalt (Co), Nickel (Ni).

Properties:
  • Their magnetic moment is very large.
  • Magnetism remains in them even after the magnetic field is removed (property of hysteresis).
  • Their relative magnetic permeability \(\mu_r\) is very large (>1).
  • Their magnetic susceptibility \(\chi\) is positive and very high.
  • They are used to make permanent magnets.
Q: What are diamagnetic materials? Write their properties.
Materials that are weakly repelled by a magnetic field are called diamagnetic materials.

Examples: Copper (Cu), Silver (Ag), Gold (Au), Bismuth (Bi).

Properties:
  • Their magnetic moment is negative and very small.
  • Their effect disappears completely when the magnetic field is removed.
  • Their relative magnetic permeability \(\mu_r\) is slightly less than one (<1).
  • Their magnetic susceptibility \(\chi\) is negative and small.

Q: What is Curie temperature?
The temperature at which a ferromagnetic material loses its ferromagnetic property and becomes paramagnetic is called the Curie temperature.

Examples:
  • Iron → 770°C
  • Nickel → 360°C
  • Cobalt → 1150°C
Q: What is meant by residual magnetism, retentivity, hysteresis loss and eddy-current loss?
  • Residual magnetism: The magnetism remaining in a material after the magnetic field is removed is called residual magnetism.
  • Retentivity: The property due to which a material retains some amount of magnetism even after the magnetic field is removed is called retentivity.
  • Hysteresis loss: The energy lost as heat by a magnetic material while its magnetism is repeatedly reversed is called hysteresis loss.
  • Eddy-current loss: The energy loss due to heat generated by the current induced (eddy current) inside a conductor in a changing magnetic field is called eddy-current loss.
Q: Define the following — magnetic axis, magnetic meridian, magnetic equator, magnetic pole, geographic axis, geographic meridian.
  • Magnetic axis: The imaginary line joining the Earth's magnetic north and south poles is called the magnetic axis.
  • Magnetic meridian: The imaginary vertical plane on the Earth's surface that contains the magnetic axis is called the magnetic meridian.
  • Magnetic equator: The line on the Earth's surface where a magnetic needle stays horizontal and the dip is zero is called the magnetic equator.
  • Magnetic pole: The places on the Earth's surface where the dip of a magnetic needle is \(90^\circ\) are called magnetic poles.
  • Geographic axis: The imaginary line joining the Earth's north and south poles is called the geographic axis. This is also the Earth's axis of rotation.
  • Geographic meridian: The imaginary vertical plane on the Earth's surface that contains the geographic axis is called the geographic meridian.
Q: What are the angle of dip and the angle of declination?
  • Angle of dip: The angle that the resultant intensity of the Earth's magnetic field makes with the horizontal at a place is called the angle of dip. It is denoted by \(\theta\).
  • Angle of declination: The angle between the magnetic meridian and the geographic meridian at a place is called the angle of declination. It is \(0^\circ\) at the equator and \(90^\circ\) at the magnetic poles.
Q: Establish the relation between the Earth's geomagnetic elements.
Let the Earth's total magnetic field at a place be \(I\). This magnetic field makes an angle \(\theta\) with the horizontal plane, then—

Horizontal component,
$$ H = I\cos\theta \quad \text{....(1)} $$
Vertical component,
$$ V = I\sin\theta \quad \text{....(2)} $$
Dividing equation (2) by (1)—
$$ \frac{V}{H} = \frac{I\sin\theta}{I\cos\theta} $$ $$ \frac{V}{H} = \tan\theta $$
Squaring and adding equations (1) and (2)—
$$ H^2 + V^2 = I^2\cos^2\theta + I^2\sin^2\theta $$ $$ H^2 + V^2 = I^2(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) $$ $$ H^2 + V^2 = I^2 $$
Q: Explain the following — isogonic lines, agonic lines, isoclinic lines, aclinic lines, isodynamic lines.
  • Isogonic lines: Lines joining places where the angle of declination has the same value are called isogonic lines.
  • Agonic lines: Lines joining places with zero angle of declination are called agonic lines.
  • Isoclinic lines: Lines joining places with the same angle of dip are called isoclinic lines.
  • Aclinic lines: Lines joining places with zero angle of dip are called aclinic lines (the magnetic equator).
  • Isodynamic lines: Lines joining places where the horizontal component \(H\) of the magnetic field has the same value are called isodynamic lines.
