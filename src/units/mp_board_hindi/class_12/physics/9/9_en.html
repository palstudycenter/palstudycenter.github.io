Q: What is a semiconductor? Name any two semiconductor materials.
Answer - Materials in which electric current does not flow easily at low temperature, but flows easily when the temperature is increased, are called semiconductors.

Their electrical conductivity lies between that of a conductor and an insulator.

Examples: Silicon, germanium, etc.
Q: Name any two semiconductor materials.
The names of two semiconductor materials are as follows.
  1. Silicon
  2. Germanium
Q: What effect does increasing the temperature of a conductor and a semiconductor have on their electrical conductivity? (M.P. 2024)
Answer - Increasing the temperature of a conductor decreases the conductor's electrical conductivity, while it increases the electrical conductivity of semiconductors.
Q: How many types of semiconductors are there?
Semiconductors are mainly of two types—
  1. Intrinsic (pure) semiconductor - No impurity is added to these. When the temperature increases, electron-hole pairs are generated and both carry current.
    Examples — Silicon (Si), Germanium (Ge)
  2. Extrinsic (impure) semiconductor - A semiconductor obtained by adding a small amount of impurity to an intrinsic semiconductor is called an extrinsic (impure) semiconductor.
These are of two types—

N-type — Electrons are the majority carriers.

P-type — Holes are the majority carriers.

Q: What is doping?
Doping is the process in which a very small amount of a suitable impurity is added to a pure semiconductor (such as silicon or germanium) to increase its electrical conductivity.
Q: How many types of extrinsic semiconductors are there? Or, how are P-type and N-type semiconductors obtained from a pure semiconductor? Explain their formation. Or, define N-type and P-type semiconductors. (M.P. 2019/21/26)
Answer - Extrinsic semiconductors are of two types.
  1. N-type semiconductor - A semiconductor in which electrons are the majority carriers is called an N-type semiconductor.
When a pentavalent impurity (such as phosphorus, arsenic, antimony) is added to pure germanium, four of the five electrons of the impurity atom form bonds and one electron remains free.

This free electron easily carries the electric current. Hence such a semiconductor is called an N-type semiconductor or donor semiconductor.
Electron → majority carrier

Hole → minority carrier
  1. P-type semiconductor - A semiconductor in which holes are the majority carriers is called a P-type semiconductor.
When a trivalent impurity (such as boron, aluminium, gallium) is added to pure germanium, the three electrons of the impurity atom form bonds and one bond remains incomplete. This incomplete bond is called a hole.

This hole behaves like a positive charge carrier and helps in carrying the electric current. Hence such a semiconductor is called a P-type semiconductor or acceptor semiconductor.
Hole → majority carrier

Electron → minority carrier

Q: Name the majority charge carriers of P-type and N-type semiconductors. (M.P. 2025)
Answer - In P-type semiconductors the charge carriers are holes, and in N-type semiconductors the majority charge carriers are electrons.
Q: What is a P-N junction diode?
A device formed by the junction of a P-type and an N-type semiconductor, which shows unidirectional conductivity, is called a P-N junction diode.
Properties:
  1. It conducts current in forward bias.
  2. It blocks current in reverse bias.
Q: Explain the flow of current in a P-N junction diode under forward bias.
In a P-N junction diode, when the P-type is connected to the positive (+) terminal of the battery and the N-type is connected to the negative (–) terminal, it is called forward bias.
Working - In forward bias, due to the effect of the electric field of the external battery, the holes of the P-type move towards the junction and the electrons of the N-type are also attracted towards the junction.

At the junction, the combination of holes and electrons reduces the barrier potential. When the barrier potential is sufficiently reduced, electrons flow from N to P and holes flow from P to N. As a result, current begins to flow through the diode.
Q: Explain the flow of current in a P-N junction diode under reverse bias.
When the P-type is connected to the negative (–) terminal of the battery and the N-type is connected to the positive (+) terminal, it is called reverse bias.
Working - In reverse bias, due to the effect of the electric field of the external battery, the holes of the P-type are pulled away from the junction and the electrons of the N-type also stay away from the junction. Because of this, the depletion layer becomes stronger and the path for the flow of current gets blocked. Under normal conditions the current remains almost zero. Current can flow only at a very high voltage (the breakdown voltage).
Q: What is a rectifier?
The process of converting alternating current into direct current is called rectification, and the device used in this process is called a rectifier.

It is of two types -
  1. Half-wave rectifier
  2. Full-wave rectifier
Q: Explain the action of a P-N junction diode as a half-wave rectifier with the help of a circuit diagram and the input-output signal diagram. (2026)
Working - When an AC voltage is applied to the primary coil of the transformer, an AC voltage is also induced in the secondary coil. The AC voltage has two half-cycles, the first positive and the second negative.

During the positive half-cycle, end A of the secondary coil is positive with respect to end B. At this time the diode is in forward bias. Current flows through the diode into the load R, and an output voltage is obtained across the load.

During the negative half-cycle, end A becomes negative with respect to end B. At this time the diode is in reverse bias. No current flows through the diode and the load, and the output voltage across the load is zero.

In this way, the P-N diode passes current to the load only during the positive half-cycle. Hence it is called a half-wave rectifier.
Q: Explain the action of a P-N junction diode as a full-wave rectifier with the help of a circuit diagram and the input-output signal diagram.
When an AC voltage is applied to the primary coil of the transformer, an AC voltage is also induced in the secondary coil. In full-wave rectification, two or four diodes are used so that both half-cycles are delivered to the load.

During the positive half-cycle, the diodes are in forward bias and current flows through the load R. At this time the output voltage across the load is positive.

During the negative half-cycle also, the diodes are connected in such a way that current flows through the load R in the same direction. At this time too the output voltage across the load is positive.

In this way, full-wave rectification uses both half-cycles to obtain a continuous, positive current in the load. Hence it is called a full-wave rectifier.
