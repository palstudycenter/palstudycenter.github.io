Q.1 If \(P(A)=\frac35,\; P(B)=\frac15\) and A and B are independent events, find \(P(A\cap B)\).

Solution

\[ P(A)=\frac35 \]

\[ P(B)=\frac15 \]

\[ \text{Since A and B are independent events,} \]

\[ P(A\cap B)=P(A)\times P(B) \]

\[ =\frac35\times\frac15 \]

\[ =\frac3{25} \]

\[ \therefore P(A\cap B)=\frac3{25} \]

Q.2 Two cards are drawn at random and without replacement from a pack of 52 playing cards. Find the probability that both cards are black.

Solution

\[ \text{Total black cards}=26 \]

\[ P(\text{First card is black}) = \frac{26}{52} = \frac12 \]

\[ \text{After drawing one black card,} \]

\[ \text{Remaining black cards}=25,\qquad \text{Remaining cards}=51 \]

\[ P(\text{Second card is black}\mid\text{First card is black}) = \frac{25}{51} \]

\[ P(\text{Both cards are black}) = \frac{26}{52}\times\frac{25}{51} \]

\[ = \frac12\times\frac{25}{51} \]

\[ = \frac{25}{102} \]

\[ \therefore \text{Required Probability}=\frac{25}{102} \]

Q.3 A box contains 15 oranges, out of which 12 are good and 3 are bad. Three oranges are selected without replacement. Find the probability that the box will be approved for sale.

Solution

\[ \text{A box is approved only when all three selected oranges are good.} \]

\[ P(\text{First orange is good}) = \frac{12}{15} \]

\[ P(\text{Second orange is good}\mid\text{First is good}) = \frac{11}{14} \]

\[ P(\text{Third orange is good}\mid\text{First two are good}) = \frac{10}{13} \]

\[ P(\text{Box approved}) = \frac{12}{15}\times\frac{11}{14}\times\frac{10}{13} \]

\[ = \frac{44}{91} \]

\[ \therefore \text{Probability of approval} = \frac{44}{91} \]

Q.4 A fair coin and an unbiased die are tossed. Let \(A=\) Event that a head appears on the coin and \(B=\) Event that 3 appears on the die. Check whether A and B are independent.

Solution

\[ P(A)=\frac12 \]

\[ P(B)=\frac16 \]

\[ P(A\cap B) = \frac1{12} \]

\[ P(A)\times P(B) = \frac12\times\frac16 = \frac1{12} \]

\[ P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B) \]

\[ \therefore \text{A and B are independent events.} \]

Q.5 A die marked 1, 2, 3 in red and 4, 5, 6 in green is tossed. Let \(A=\) Event that the number is even and \(B=\) Event that the number is red. Check whether A and B are independent.

Solution

\[ S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\} \]

\[ A=\{2,4,6\} \]

\[ B=\{1,2,3\} \]

\[ P(A)=\frac36=\frac12 \]

\[ P(B)=\frac36=\frac12 \]

\[ A\cap B=\{2\} \]

\[ P(A\cap B)=\frac16 \]

\[ P(A)P(B) = \frac12\times\frac12 = \frac14 \]

\[ P(A\cap B)\ne P(A)P(B) \]

\[ \therefore \text{A and B are not independent events.} \]

Q.6 Let E and F be events such that \(P(F)=\frac{3}{10}\), \(P(E\cap F)=\frac15\) and \(P(E)=\frac35\). Are E and F independent?

Solution

\[ P(E)=\frac35 \]

\[ P(F)=\frac3{10} \]

\[ P(E\cap F)=\frac15 \]

\[ P(E)P(F) = \frac35\times\frac3{10} = \frac9{50} \]

\[ P(E\cap F)=\frac15=\frac{10}{50} \]

\[ P(E\cap F)\ne P(E)P(F) \]

\[ \therefore \text{E and F are not independent events.} \]

Q.7 Given \(P(A)=\frac12,\; P(A\cup B)=\frac35\) and \(P(B)=p\). Find the value of \(p\) if A and B are: (i) Mutually Exclusive (ii) Independent

Solution

(i) A and B are Mutually Exclusive

\[ P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B) \]

\[ \frac35=\frac12+p \]

\[ p=\frac35-\frac12 =\frac{6-5}{10} =\frac1{10} \]

\[ \therefore p=\frac1{10} \]

(ii) A and B are Independent

\[ P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A)P(B) \]

\[ \frac35=\frac12+p-\frac{p}{2} \]

\[ \frac35=\frac12+\frac{p}{2} \]

\[ \frac{6}{10}=\frac{5}{10}+\frac{p}{2} \]

\[ \frac{p}{2}=\frac1{10} \]

\[ p=\frac15 \]

\[ \therefore p=\frac15 \]

Q.8 Given \(P(A)=0.3,\; P(B)=0.4\) and A, B are independent events, find: (i) \(P(A\cap B)\) (ii) \(P(A\cup B)\) (iii) \(P(A|B)\) (iv) \(P(B|A)\)

Solution

\[ P(A)=0.3,\qquad P(B)=0.4 \]

\[ \text{Since A and B are independent,} \]

\[ (i)\; P(A\cap B) =P(A)P(B) =(0.3)(0.4) =0.12 \]

\[ (ii)\; P(A\cup B) =P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B) \]

\[ =0.3+0.4-0.12 =0.58 \]

\[ (iii)\; P(A|B) =\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)} =\frac{0.12}{0.4} =0.3 \]

\[ (iv)\; P(B|A) =\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(A)} =\frac{0.12}{0.3} =0.4 \]

\[ \therefore \text{Answers: } 0.12,\;0.58,\;0.3,\;0.4 \]

Q.9 Given \(P(A)=\frac14,\; P(B)=\frac12,\; P(A\cap B)=\frac18\). Find \(P(A'\cap B')\).

Solution

\[ P(A\cup B) =P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B) \]

\[ =\frac14+\frac12-\frac18 =\frac58 \]

\[ P(A'\cap B') =1-P(A\cup B) \]

\[ =1-\frac58 =\frac38 \]

\[ \therefore P(A'\cap B') =\frac38 \]

Q.10 Given \(P(A)=\frac12,\; P(B)=\frac{7}{12}\) and \(P(A\cap B)=\frac14\). Check whether A and B are independent.

Solution

\[ P(A)=\frac12 \]

\[ P(B)=\frac{7}{12} \]

\[ P(A\cap B)=\frac14 \]

\[ P(A)\times P(B) = \frac12\times\frac{7}{12} = \frac7{24} \]

\[ P(A\cap B) = \frac14 = \frac6{24} \]

\[ P(A\cap B)\ne P(A)P(B) \]

\[ \therefore \text{A and B are not independent events.} \]

Q.11 Given \(P(A)=0.3,\; P(B)=0.6\) and A, B are independent events, find: (i) \(P(A\cap B)\) (ii) \(P(A\cap B')\) (iii) \(P(A\cup B)\) (iv) Probability that neither A nor B occurs.

Solution

\[ P(A)=0.3,\qquad P(B)=0.6 \]

\[ (i)\; P(A\cap B) =P(A)P(B) =(0.3)(0.6) =0.18 \]

\[ (ii)\; P(A\cap B') =P(A)\,[1-P(B)] \]

\[ =0.3(1-0.6) =0.3(0.4) =0.12 \]

\[ (iii)\; P(A\cup B) =P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B) \]

\[ =0.3+0.6-0.18 =0.72 \]

\[ (iv)\; P(\text{Neither A nor B}) =1-P(A\cup B) \]

\[ =1-0.72 =0.28 \]

\[ \therefore \text{Answers: } 0.18,\;0.12,\;0.72,\;0.28 \]

Q.12 A die is tossed three times. Find the probability of getting an odd number at least once.

Solution

\[ P(\text{Not getting an odd number in one throw}) = \frac36 = \frac12 \]

\[ P(\text{Not getting an odd number in all three throws}) = \left(\frac12\right)^3 = \frac18 \]

\[ P(\text{Getting at least one odd number}) = 1-\frac18 \]

\[ = \frac78 \]

\[ \therefore \text{Required Probability} = \frac78 \]

Q.13 A box contains 10 black and 8 red balls. Two balls are drawn successively with replacement. Find the probability that: (i) both balls are red, (ii) first ball is black and second is red, (iii) one ball is black and the other is red.

Solution

\[ \text{Total balls}=18 \]

\[ P(\text{Red})=\frac{8}{18}=\frac49 \]

\[ P(\text{Black})=\frac{10}{18}=\frac59 \]

\[ \text{(i) Both balls are red} \]

\[ P(RR) = \frac49\times\frac49 = \frac{16}{81} \]

\[ \text{(ii) First black and second red} \]

\[ P(BR) = \frac59\times\frac49 = \frac{20}{81} \]

\[ \text{(iii) One black and one red} \]

\[ P(BR)+P(RB) = \frac59\times\frac49 + \frac49\times\frac59 \]

\[ = \frac{20}{81}+\frac{20}{81} = \frac{40}{81} \]

\[ \therefore \text{Answers: } \frac{16}{81},\; \frac{20}{81},\; \frac{40}{81} \]

Q.14 The probability that A solves a problem is \(\frac12\) and the probability that B solves it is \(\frac13\). If both try independently, find the probability that: (i) the problem is solved, (ii) exactly one of them solves the problem.

Solution

\[ P(A)=\frac12,\qquad P(B)=\frac13 \]

\[ \text{(i) Probability that the problem is solved} \]

\[ P(\text{Not solved}) = \left(1-\frac12\right) \left(1-\frac13\right) = \frac12\times\frac23 = \frac13 \]

\[ P(\text{Solved}) = 1-\frac13 = \frac23 \]

\[ \text{(ii) Exactly one solves} \]

\[ = P(A\cap B') + P(A'\cap B) \]

\[ = \left(\frac12\right)\left(\frac23\right) + \left(\frac12\right)\left(\frac13\right) \]

\[ = \frac13+\frac16 = \frac12 \]

\[ \therefore \text{Answers: } \frac23,\; \frac12 \]

Q.15 Examine whether the following pairs of events are independent:
(i) \(E=\) Spade, \(F=\) Ace
(ii) \(E=\) Black card, \(F=\) King
(iii) \(E=\) King or Queen, \(F=\) Queen or Jack

Solution

(i)

\[ P(E)=\frac{13}{52}=\frac14,\qquad P(F)=\frac4{52}=\frac1{13} \]

\[ P(E\cap F)=\frac1{52} \]

\[ P(E)P(F) = \frac14\times\frac1{13} = \frac1{52} \]

\[ \therefore \text{E and F are independent.} \]

(ii)

\[ P(E)=\frac{26}{52}=\frac12,\qquad P(F)=\frac4{52}=\frac1{13} \]

\[ P(E\cap F)=\frac2{52}=\frac1{26} \]

\[ P(E)P(F) = \frac12\times\frac1{13} = \frac1{26} \]

\[ \therefore \text{E and F are independent.} \]

(iii)

\[ P(E)=\frac8{52}=\frac2{13},\qquad P(F)=\frac8{52}=\frac2{13} \]

\[ P(E\cap F)=\frac4{52}=\frac1{13} \]

\[ P(E)P(F) = \frac2{13}\times\frac2{13} = \frac4{169} \]

\[ P(E\cap F)\ne P(E)P(F) \]

\[ \therefore \text{E and F are not independent.} \]

Q.16 Suppose \(P(H)=0.60,\; P(E)=0.40\) and \(P(H\cap E)=0.20\). Find:
(i) Probability that neither H nor E occurs.
(ii) \(P(E|H)\)
(iii) \(P(H|E)\)

Solution

\[ P(H)=0.60,\qquad P(E)=0.40,\qquad P(H\cap E)=0.20 \]

\[ \text{(i) Probability that neither H nor E occurs} \]

\[ P(H\cup E) = P(H)+P(E)-P(H\cap E) \]

\[ = 0.60+0.40-0.20 = 0.80 \]

\[ P(\text{Neither }H\text{ nor }E) = 1-P(H\cup E) \]

\[ = 1-0.80 = 0.20 \]

\[ \text{(ii) }P(E|H) = \frac{P(H\cap E)}{P(H)} \]

\[ = \frac{0.20}{0.60} = \frac13 \]

\[ \text{(iii) }P(H|E) = \frac{P(H\cap E)}{P(E)} \]

\[ = \frac{0.20}{0.40} = \frac12 \]

\[ \therefore \text{Answers: } 0.20,\; \frac13,\; \frac12 \]

Q.17 Two dice are thrown simultaneously. Find the probability that both dice show an even prime number.

Solution

\[ \text{The only even prime number is }2. \]

\[ P(\text{Getting }2\text{ on one die}) = \frac16 \]

\[ P(\text{Getting }2\text{ on both dice}) = \frac16\times\frac16 \]

\[ = \frac1{36} \]

\[ \therefore \text{Required Probability} = \frac1{36} \]

Q.18 If A and B are independent events, show that \(A'\) and \(B'\) are also independent.

Solution

\[ \text{Since A and B are independent,} \]

\[ P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B) \]

\[ P(A')=1-P(A),\qquad P(B')=1-P(B) \]

\[ P(A'\cap B') = 1-P(A\cup B) \]

\[ = 1-\left[P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)\right] \]

\[ = 1-P(A)-P(B)+P(A)P(B) \]

\[ = [1-P(A)][1-P(B)] \]

\[ = P(A')P(B') \]

\[ \therefore A' \text{ and } B' \text{ are independent events.} \]
