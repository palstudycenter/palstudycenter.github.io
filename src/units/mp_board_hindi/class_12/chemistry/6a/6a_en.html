📘 Unit 6 -(Haloalkanes)
Question: What are haloalkanes?
Organic compounds in which one or more hydrogen atoms of an alkane are replaced by halogen atoms (F, Cl, Br or I) are called haloalkanes.

Based on the number of halogen atoms, haloalkanes are mainly of the following types:

  1. Monohaloalkanes – These contain one halogen atom. Their general formula is RX.
  2. Dihaloalkanes – These contain two halogen atoms. Their general formula is RX₂.
  3. Trihaloalkanes – These contain three halogen atoms. Their general formula is RX₃.
  4. Polyhaloalkanes – These contain more than three halogen atoms. Their general formula is RXₙ.
Where,
R = alkyl group
X = halogen atom (F, Cl, Br or I)
Question: How many types of monohaloalkanes are there?
Based on the nature of the carbon atom, monohaloalkanes are of three types:
  1. Primary haloalkane (1°) – The carbon atom to which the halogen is attached is bonded to only one other carbon atom.

    Example:

    CH₃–CH₂–Cl

    Ethyl chloride
  2. Secondary haloalkane (2°) – The carbon atom to which the halogen is attached is bonded to two other carbon atoms.

    Example:

    (CH3)2CH–Cl

    Isopropyl chloride
  3. Tertiary haloalkane (3°) – The carbon atom to which the halogen is attached is bonded to three other carbon atoms.

    Example:

    (CH₃)₃C–Cl

    Tert-butyl chloride
Question: Write the general methods of preparation of haloalkanes.
The main methods of preparing haloalkanes are as follows:
  1. Halogenation of alkanes
    In the presence of light or heat, when an alkane reacts with chlorine or bromine, haloalkanes are obtained.
    CH₄ + Cl₂ Light ⟶ CH₃Cl + HCl
  2. Addition of hydrogen halide to alkenes
    Addition of HCl, HBr or HI to alkenes gives haloalkanes. In an unsymmetrical alkene, the addition generally follows Markovnikov's rule.
    CH₂=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃–CH₂Br
  3. Preparation of haloalkanes from alcohols
    Reacting alcohols with various halogenating reagents gives haloalkanes.
    (a) With hydrogen halide
    C₂H₅OH + HCl → C₂H₅Cl + H₂O
    (b) With phosphorus trichloride
    3C₂H₅OH + PCl₃ → 3C₂H₅Cl + H₃PO₃
    (c) With phosphorus pentachloride
    C₂H₅OH + PCl₅ → C₂H₅Cl + POCl₃ + HCl
    (d) With thionyl chloride
    C₂H₅OH + SOCl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + SO₂ + HCl
  4. Hunsdiecker Reaction
    Heating silver salts of fatty acids with bromine or chlorine in a suitable solvent gives a haloalkane with one carbon atom less than the original acid.
    CH₃COOAg + Br₂ → CH₃Br + CO₂ + AgBr
Question: Write the physical properties of haloalkanes.
The main physical properties of haloalkanes are as follows:
  1. Physical state: Lower members are generally gases or volatile liquids, while members with higher molecular mass can be liquids or solids.
  2. Colour and odour: Most pure haloalkanes are colourless and may have a characteristic odour.
  3. Boiling point: In general, the boiling point increases as molecular mass increases.
  4. Density: Many chloro-, bromo- and iodoalkanes have a higher density than water, although this is not a universal rule for all haloalkanes.
  5. Solubility in water: Most haloalkanes are only very slightly soluble in water.
Question: Why are alkyl halides immiscible with water?
Although the C–X bond in alkyl halides is polar, they are generally only very slightly soluble in water. The main reason for this is that strong hydrogen bonds exist between water molecules. The attractive forces formed between alkyl halides and water are not strong enough to sufficiently break the strong hydrogen bonds of water. Therefore, most alkyl halides are immiscible with, or very slightly soluble in, water.
Question: Into how many groups are the chemical properties of alkyl halides divided?
The main chemical reactions of alkyl halides can be divided into three groups:
  1. Nucleophilic substitution reaction
  2. Elimination reaction
  3. Reaction with metals
Question: What are nucleophilic substitution reactions? Explain SN¹ and SN² with examples.
The reaction in which the halogen atom of an alkyl halide is replaced by a nucleophile (Nu⁻) is called a nucleophilic substitution reaction.
R–X + Nu⁻ → R–Nu + X⁻
Because the C–X bond is polar, a partial positive charge ($\delta^+$) develops on the carbon attached to the halogen. The nucleophile attacks this electron-deficient carbon and the halide ion is displaced.
1. SN¹ reaction
SN¹ is a unimolecular nucleophilic substitution reaction. Its rate depends only on the concentration of the substrate.
Rate ∝ [R–X]
This reaction mainly occurs in two steps:

First step: Heterolytic cleavage of the C–X bond occurs, forming a carbocation. This is the slow step.

R–X → R⁺ + X⁻

Second step: The nucleophile attacks the carbocation and the substitution product is formed.

R⁺ + Nu⁻ → R–Nu
2. SN² reaction
SN² is a bimolecular nucleophilic substitution reaction. Its rate depends on the concentration of both the substrate and the nucleophile.
Rate ∝ [R–X][Nu⁻]
This reaction occurs in a single step. The nucleophile attacks the carbon atom from the side opposite to the halogen, and a transition state is formed.
Nu⁻ + R–X → [Nu···R···X] → R–Nu + X⁻
Question: Write the difference between the SN¹ and SN² reactions.
SN¹ reaction SN² reaction
1. It occurs in two steps. 1. It occurs in a single step.
2. Its rate depends only on the concentration of the substrate. 2. Its rate depends on the concentration of both the substrate and the nucleophile.
3. A carbocation is formed first. 3. No carbocation is formed.
4. Tertiary alkyl halides generally react easily in this reaction. 4. Primary alkyl halides generally react easily in this reaction.
Question: Give some examples of nucleophilic substitution reactions.
Alkyl halides ($R-X$) react with various nucleophilic reagents ($Nu^-$) to form new compounds. The main examples are as follows:
  1. Reaction with aqueous alkalis
    Reacting an alkyl halide with aqueous $KOH$ or $NaOH$ gives an alcohol.
    C₂H₅Br + KOH(aq) → C₂H₅OH + KBr
  2. Reaction with sodium alkoxide
    This is an important $S_N2$ reaction.
    C₂H₅I + NaOC₂H₅ → C₂H₅OC₂H₅ + NaI
    It is used in the Williamson ether synthesis.
  3. Reaction with silver oxide ($Ag₂O$)
    2C₂H₅I + Ag₂O → C₂H₅OC₂H₅ + 2AgI
  4. Reaction with sodium alkynide
    This is an $S_N2$ reaction.
    HC≡CNa + CH₃I → HC≡C–CH₃ + NaI
  5. Reaction with potassium cyanide (KCN)
    R–X + KCN → R–C≡N + KX
    This mainly gives an alkyl cyanide.
  6. Reaction with silver nitrite ($AgNO₂$)
    R–X + AgNO₂ → R–NO₂ + AgX
    This mainly gives a nitroalkane.
  7. Reaction with the silver salt of a fatty acid
    RCOOAg + R'X → RCOOR' + AgX
    This reaction gives an ester.
Question: When a haloalkane reacts with KCN, the main product formed is alkyl cyanide, whereas its reaction with AgCN gives isocyanide as the major product. Explain. (2023)

KCN is an ionic compound. In solution it gives $K^+$ and $CN^-$ ions. In the $CN^-$ ion, both carbon and nitrogen can act as nucleophilic centres, but under normal conditions attack from the carbon end predominates. Therefore, an alkyl halide mainly gives an alkyl cyanide.

R–X + KCN → R–C≡N + KX

AgCN is mainly covalent in nature, and the $CN$ group is attached to silver in such a way that attack from the nitrogen end is more favourable. Hence alkyl isocyanide is the major product.

R–X + AgCN → R–N≡C + AgX
Question: What is an elimination reaction? State the Saytzeff rule related to it. (2019)

When two atoms/groups, in the form of a small molecule or group, are removed from an organic compound and an unsaturated compound is formed in their place, such a reaction is called an elimination reaction.

When a haloalkane containing a $\beta$-hydrogen is heated with alcoholic $KOH$, elimination of hydrogen from the $\beta$-carbon and halogen from the $\alpha$-carbon occurs, forming an alkene.

CH₃CH₂Cl + KOH(alc.) → CH₂=CH₂ + KCl + H₂O
Saytzeff Rule

When more than one alkene can possibly be formed in an elimination reaction, the more stable and more substituted alkene is generally formed as the major product. This is called the Saytzeff rule.

CH₃CHBrCH₂CH₃ + KOH(alc.) → CH₃CH=CHCH₃ + KBr + H₂O

In this example, but-2-ene is the major product.

Question: Write the reactions of alkyl halides with metals.
Alkyl halides react with certain metals to form organometallic compounds.
  1. Reaction with magnesium
    In the presence of dry ether, an alkyl halide reacts with magnesium to form a Grignard reagent.
    CH₃Cl + Mg Dry ether ⟶ CH₃MgCl
  2. Wurtz reaction
    Reacting an alkyl halide with sodium in dry ether gives a higher alkane.
    2CH₃Cl + 2Na → CH₃–CH₃ + 2NaCl
  3. Frankland reaction
    Reaction of an alkyl halide with zinc gives a dialkyl zinc compound.
    2R–X + 2Zn → R₂Zn + ZnX₂
  4. Formation of zinc alkyl
    Dialkyl zinc compounds are traditionally regarded as related to the Frankland reagent.
    2CH₃Cl + 2Zn → (CH₃)₂Zn + ZnCl₂
  5. Reaction with lead-sodium alloy
    Reaction of ethyl halide with lead-sodium alloy can be used to prepare tetraethyl lead.
    4C₂H₅Cl + 4Na + Pb → (C₂H₅)₄Pb + 4NaCl
  6. Reaction with lithium
    An alkyl halide reacts with lithium to form an alkyl lithium.
    R–X + 2Li → R–Li + LiX

Question: Write any three effects of dichloromethane (methylene chloride) on humans. (2025)
Excessive exposure to dichloromethane can have the following effects:
  1. Exposure to large amounts of vapour can cause headache, dizziness and nausea.
  2. At high concentrations, its vapours can cause irritation of the respiratory tract and respiratory distress.
  3. Prolonged skin contact can cause irritation and dryness of the skin.
Question: What is haloform? How many types are there?

Organic compounds in which three halogen atoms are attached to the same carbon atom are called haloforms. Their general formula is CHX₃.

where X = F, Cl, Br or I.

Haloforms are of four types—

  1. Fluoroform — CHF₃
  2. Chloroform — CHCl₃
  3. Bromoform — CHBr₃
  4. Iodoform — CHI₃
Question: What is the haloform reaction? Write its use.
When a methyl ketone ($RCOCH₃$) reacts with a halogen ($X₂$) and an alkali ($NaOH/KOH$), a haloform ($CHX₃$) and a carboxylate salt are formed. This is called the haloform reaction.
RCOCH₃ + 3X₂ + 4NaOH → RCOONa + CHX₃ + 3NaX + 3H₂O

It is used to identify the methyl ketone group ($–COCH₃$).

Question: What is chloroform?

Chloroform is a colourless, volatile organic compound with a characteristic odour. Its chemical formula is CHCl₃.

Its IUPAC name is trichloromethane.

Question: Write the haloform method of preparing chloroform.

In this reaction, acetone \((CH₃COCH₃)\) is reacted with chlorine and sodium hydroxide to obtain chloroform \((CHCl₃)\).

Reaction:

CH₃COCH₃ + 3Cl₂ + 4NaOH → CHCl₃ + CH₃COONa + 3NaCl + 3H₂O

The chloroform obtained is separated from the mixture and purified.

Question: Write the laboratory method of preparing chloroform.

Principle: In the laboratory, chloroform is obtained by reacting ethyl alcohol with bleaching powder and water.

Equations:

1. Reaction of bleaching powder with water:

CaOCl₂ + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + Cl₂

2. Oxidation of ethyl alcohol:

CH₃CH₂OH + Cl₂→ CH₃CHO + 2HCl

3. Formation of chloral by chlorination:

CH₃CHO + 3Cl₂ → CCl₃CHO + 3HCl

5. Reaction of chloral with calcium hydroxide:

2CCl₃CHO + Ca(OH)₂ → 2CHCl₃ + Ca(HCOO)₂

In this way chloroform (CHCl₃) is obtained.

Method: Ethyl alcohol and bleaching powder are taken in a distillation flask, and water is added to it. On slowly heating the mixture, the chlorine produced from the bleaching powder reacts with the ethyl alcohol to form chloral. Reaction of chloral with calcium hydroxide gives chloroform (CHCl₃). The chloroform obtained is separated by distillation and purified.

Question: Write the physical properties of chloroform (CHCl₃).
  • It is a colourless and volatile liquid.
  • It has a characteristic sweet odour.
  • Its density is about 1.49 g/cm³ (around 20°C).
  • It is slightly soluble in water.
  • It is soluble in organic solvents such as alcohol and ether.
  • It is a good solvent for many organic substances.
Question: Write the chemical properties of chloroform (CHCl₃).
  1. Oxidation:
    In the presence of light and air, chloroform slowly forms phosgene.
    2CHCl₃ + O₂ Light ⟶ 2COCl₂ + 2HCl
  2. Reduction:
    In the presence of a suitable reducing agent, chloroform can give methylene chloride or other further reduced products.
  3. Reaction with alkali:
    With hot aqueous KOH, chloroform forms potassium formate.
    CHCl₃ + 4KOH → HCOOK + 3KCl + 2H₂O
  4. Carbylamine reaction:
    When a primary amine is heated with chloroform and alcoholic KOH, an extremely foul-smelling substance, alkyl isocyanide, is formed. This is called the carbylamine reaction.
    CH₃NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3KOH → CH₃NC + 3KCl + 3H₂O
    It is used to test for primary amines.
  5. Chlorination:
    Chloroform reacts with chlorine to form carbon tetrachloride.
    CHCl₃ + Cl₂ → CCl₄ + HCl
  6. Nitration:
    Chloroform reacts with nitric acid to form picric acid.
    CHCl₃ + HNO₃ → CCl₃NO₂ + H₂O
  7. Reimer-Tiemann reaction:

    When phenol is heated with chloroform and aqueous NaOH, o-hydroxybenzaldehyde (salicylaldehyde) is obtained as the main product. This reaction is called the Reimer-Tiemann reaction.

Question: Why is chloroform stored filled up to the neck in brown bottles? Or, how is chloroform stored safely? (2020)

In the presence of light and air, chloroform slowly oxidises to form phosgene (COCl₂), which is an extremely toxic gas.

2CHCl₃ + O₂ Light ⟶ 2COCl₂ + 2HCl

Hence chloroform is protected from light and kept in a well-sealed bottle. A small amount of ethanol is added to commercial chloroform as a stabiliser.

Question: Write the uses of chloroform.
  • As a solvent for organic compounds.
  • As a solvent in laboratory and industrial chemical processes.
  • As a raw material in the manufacture of certain pharmaceutical and chemical substances.
  • Used in chlorination and other organic syntheses.
Note: It was historically used as an anaesthetic, but due to its toxicity it is not used in modern medicine.
Question: What is iodoform?

Iodoform is a yellow crystalline organic compound with the chemical formula CHI₃.

Its IUPAC name is triiodomethane.

It is distinctly identified by its characteristic odour and yellow colour.

Question: What do you understand by the iodoform test? Or, what happens when acetone is heated with an alkaline solution of iodine?
When acetone is reacted with an alkaline solution of iodine ($I₂$) and NaOH, a bright yellow precipitate of iodoform (CHI₃) is obtained.
CH₃COCH₃ + 3I₂ + 4NaOH → CH₃COONa + CHI₃ + 3NaI + 3H₂O

The formation of the yellow iodoform precipitate is called the iodoform test.

Question: Write the laboratory method of preparation, properties, and uses of iodoform.
Principle
Iodoform is obtained by reacting acetone with iodine and an alkali.
CH₃COCH₃ + 3I₂ + 4NaOH → CH₃COONa + CHI₃ + 3NaI + 3H₂O
Method

Iodine and alkali are used in the laboratory preparation of iodoform. This process should be carried out only in a trained laboratory with proper safety precautions.

Physical properties
  • It is a bright yellow solid.
  • It has a characteristic pungent/medicinal odour.
  • It is only very slightly soluble in water.
  • It is soluble in certain organic solvents.
  • Its melting point is about 119°C.
Uses
  • For the iodoform test in organic chemistry.
  • Historically, in certain medicinal/antiseptic uses.
Question: What is carbon tetrachloride? Write the method of preparation, properties, and uses of carbon tetrachloride.

Carbon tetrachloride is a colourless, volatile organic compound with a characteristic odour. Its chemical formula is CCl₄.

Its IUPAC name is tetrachloromethane.

Method of preparation

(1) Chlorination of methane

CH₄ + 4Cl₂ → CCl₄ + 4HCl

(2) Chlorination of carbon disulphide

CS₂ + 3Cl₂ → CCl₄ + S₂Cl₂
Properties
  • It is a colourless and volatile liquid.
  • It is non-flammable.
  • It is insoluble in water.
  • It is soluble in organic solvents.
  • Its boiling point is about 76.7°C.
  • It is a toxic substance.
Uses (2024)
  • Historically as an organic solvent.
  • In the manufacture of certain chemical substances.
  • Historically it was also used in fire extinguishers.
Note: Due to its toxicity and environmental effects, many of its earlier uses are now restricted or banned.
Question: Write any three effects of tetrachloromethane (carbon tetrachloride) on humans.
  1. It is toxic to the liver, and excessive exposure can cause liver damage.
  2. It can damage the kidneys.
  3. Exposure to large amounts can affect the central nervous system, causing effects such as headache, dizziness and weakness.
Question: What is Freon?
Chlorofluoro derivatives of methane and ethane are generally called chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) or Freons.

The names and formulas of various Freons are as follows.

  • Freon-11 — CCl₃F
  • Freon-12 — CCl₂F₂
  • Freon-22 — CHClF₂
  • Freon-113 — C₂Cl₃F₃

Method of preparing Freon:

Freon-12 ($CF₂Cl₂$) was historically prepared by the fluorination of carbon tetrachloride.

3CCl₄ + 2SbF₃ → 3CF₂Cl₂ + 2SbC

The CF₂Cl₂ obtained is called Freon-12.

Properties of Freon:

  1. It is a colourless and almost odourless gas.
  2. It is only very slightly soluble in water.
  3. It is relatively stable under normal conditions.
  4. It is not easily flammable.

Uses of Freon:

  1. As a coolant in refrigerators.
  2. As a coolant in air-conditioning equipment.
  3. Formerly as a propellant in aerosol sprays.
Question: What effect does Freon have on the environment?

Certain Freon compounds reach the atmosphere and damage the ozone layer. This allows more of the sun's harmful ultraviolet rays to reach the earth. Some Freons also contribute to the greenhouse effect and global warming. Therefore, the use of harmful Freons has been regulated to protect the environment.

Question: Write the full name of DDT. How is it prepared? Write its uses. Or, what happens when chlorobenzene is heated with chloral in the presence of concentrated H₂SO₄? (2013/14/16/20/24)

The full name of DDT is dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane.

Method of preparing DDT

Reacting benzene and chloral in the presence of concentrated sulphuric acid gives DDT.

Equation:

C₆H₅Cl + CCl₃CHO Conc. H₂SO₄ ⟶ C₁₄H₉Cl₅ + H₂O
Uses
  • DDT was historically used as a powerful insecticide.
  • It has been used to control mosquitoes and other harmful insects.
  • It has had widespread historical use in agriculture.

Note: DDT persists in the environment for a long time and can bioaccumulate. Therefore, its use has been banned or strictly regulated in many countries.

Question: Write the full name of BHC. How is it prepared? Write its uses.

The full name of BHC is benzene hexachloride.

Method of preparing BHC

Addition of chlorine to benzene in the presence of light gives hexachlorocyclohexane (HCH).

C₆H₆ + 3Cl₂ UV Light ⟶ C₆H₆Cl₆

This reaction gives various stereoisomers of HCH. The γ-isomer is called lindane.

Other names
  • HCH (hexachlorocyclohexane)
  • Lindane — γ-HCH
  • Gammexane
Uses
  • BHC was historically used as an insecticide.
  • Its γ-isomer (lindane) has been used in certain specific pest control applications.

Note: Due to the health and environmental effects of BHC/HCH, and lindane in particular, their use is banned or strictly regulated in many places.
