📘 7(A) — Alcohols
Question - What are alcohols? Give their classification.

Organic compounds in which a hydroxyl group (–OH) is directly attached to a saturated carbon atom (sp³-C) are called alcohols.

General formula: R–OH

Examples -

CH₃OH (methyl alcohol)

C₂H₅OH (ethyl alcohol)

Classification of alcohols

Based on the number of hydroxyl groups

(1) Monohydric alcohols - Alcohols that contain only one –OH group.

Based on the nature of the carbon bearing the –OH group, these are of three types—

Primary alcohol (1°) - The –OH is attached to a carbon that is attached to only one other carbon.

CH₃–CH₂–OH → Ethanol

Secondary alcohol (2°) - The –OH is attached to a carbon that is attached to two other carbons.

(CH₃)₂CH–OH → Isopropanol

Tertiary alcohol (3°) - The –OH is attached to a carbon that is attached to three other carbons.

(CH₃)₃C–OH → tert-Butanol

(2) Dihydric alcohols - Alcohols that contain two –OH groups.

HO–CH₂–CH₂–OH → Ethylene glycol

(3) Trihydric alcohols - Alcohols that contain three –OH groups.

HO–CH₂–CHOH–CH₂–OH → Glycerol

Question - Explain the nomenclature of alcohols.

1. Common naming system - In this system, the name of the alcohol is formed from the alkyl group and the word 'alcohol'.

Alkyl + alcohol

Examples -

CH₃OH → Methyl alcohol
C₂H₅OH → Ethyl alcohol

2. IUPAC nomenclature - The following steps are followed in the IUPAC nomenclature of alcohols—

1. Select the longest carbon chain containing the –OH group.

2. Number the chain from the end that gives the –OH group the lowest locant.

3. The final –e of the parent hydrocarbon name is removed and –ol is added.

4. When more than one –OH group is present, suffixes such as diol, triol etc. are used.

Examples -

CH₃OH → Methanol
C₂H₅OH → Ethanol

Question - What different types of isomerism are found in alcohol compounds? Give examples.

Alcohol compounds mainly exhibit the following types of isomerism—

1. Chain isomerism - Chain isomerism occurs when the structure of the carbon chain differs.

C₄H₉OH → CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₂OH
CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH₂OH

2. Position isomerism - The carbon chain is the same, but the position of the –OH group differs.

CH₃–CH₂–CH₂OH → Propan-1-ol
CH₃–CHOH–CH₃ → Propan-2-ol

3. Functional group isomerism - This isomerism occurs when the molecular formula is the same but the functional groups differ.

C₂H₆O:   CH₃CH₂OH → Ethanol
CH₃–O–CH₃ → Dimethyl ether

Thus, alcohols mainly exhibit chain, position, and functional group isomerism.

Question - Write the general methods of preparing alcohols.

The general methods of preparing alcohols are as follows—

1. From alkenes

Alcohols are obtained by the addition of water to alkenes.

2. From alkyl halides

Alcohols are obtained by reacting alkyl halides with aqueous KOH.

3. By reduction of aldehydes and ketones

Reduction of aldehydes gives primary alcohols, and reduction of ketones gives secondary alcohols.

4. By reduction of esters

Reduction of esters gives two alcohols.

5. By fermentation of glucose

Ethanol is obtained by fermenting glucose in the presence of yeast.

6. From Grignard reagents

Various alcohols are obtained by the reaction of a Grignard reagent (RMgX) with formaldehyde, aldehydes, or ketones, followed by hydrolysis.

7. Hydroboration–oxidation reaction

Alcohols are obtained by first hydroborating an alkene with BH₃ and then oxidizing it with H₂O₂ in an alkaline medium.

Question - Write the physical properties of alcohols.

1. The lower members are generally colorless liquids, while the higher members can be solids.

2. Alcohols of low molecular mass are soluble in water. Solubility in water decreases as molecular mass increases.

3. The boiling points of alcohols are higher than those of hydrocarbons of comparable molecular mass, because alcohols form hydrogen bonds.

4. The melting point and boiling point of alcohols generally increase as molecular mass increases.

5. Alcohols are polar compounds, and hydrogen bonds are found between their molecules.

6. Lower alcohols have a characteristic odor, while higher alcohols have a relatively less intense odor.

Question - Why is the boiling point of propanol higher than that of the corresponding alkane?

Intermolecular hydrogen bonds are found between molecules of propanol. Because of this, propanol molecules undergo association. Hence, more energy is required to separate the molecules. Therefore, its boiling point is higher than that of the corresponding alkane.

Question - Why is the boiling point of alcohols higher than that of ethers?

Alcohol molecules are associated with each other through hydrogen bonds. Hence, more energy is required to separate these molecules.

Ether molecules do not form hydrogen bonds with each other. Hence, their boiling points are lower than those of alcohols.

Question: What are the methods used to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols?

The following are the major methods used to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols—

  1. Lucas test
  2. Victor Meyer test
  3. Oxidation test
  4. Dehydrogenation test
Question: What is Lucas reagent? Describe the Lucas test.

Lucas reagent: A mixture of concentrated HCl and anhydrous ZnCl₂ is called Lucas reagent.

Lucas test:

When Lucas reagent is added to an alcohol, turbidity due to the formation of an alkyl halide is produced.

Reaction:

  • Tertiary (3°) alcohol — turbidity is produced immediately.
  • Secondary (2°) alcohol — turbidity is produced after a few minutes.
  • Primary (1°) alcohol — turbidity is produced very slowly at room temperature.

Thus, the Lucas test is used to identify primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols.

Question: How are primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols distinguished by the Victor Meyer method?

The Victor Meyer method is a chemical test used to distinguish between primary (1°), secondary (2°), and tertiary (3°) alcohols.

In this method, the alcohol is first converted into an iodoalkane by reaction with PI₃, and then into a nitroalkane by reaction with AgNO₂. Reacting the nitroalkane with nitrous acid and alkali produces different colors.

  • Primary (1°) alcohol: gives a red color.
  • Secondary (2°) alcohol: gives a blue color.
  • Tertiary (3°) alcohol: produces no color.

Thus, the nature of the alcohol can be identified based on the color produced.

Question: Explain the differentiation between primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols by the oxidation method. Give appropriate chemical equations.

(3) Oxidation: Different alcohols give different products on oxidation. On this basis, primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols can be distinguished.

Primary alcohol (1°): Primary alcohols are readily oxidized by K₂Cr₂O₇ to give first an aldehyde and then a carboxylic acid.

Secondary alcohol (2°): Secondary alcohols give a ketone on oxidation by K₂Cr₂O₇. Ketones are not easily oxidized further by K₂Cr₂O₇ under ordinary conditions.

Tertiary alcohol (3°): Tertiary alcohols are not easily oxidized by K₂Cr₂O₇ under ordinary conditions.

Question: What is meant by dehydrogenation of alcohols? Write the dehydrogenation reactions of primary, secondary, and tertiary alcohols.

Dehydrogenation: When the vapor of an alcohol is heated to about 570 K, it loses a molecule of hydrogen. This process is called dehydrogenation. Different alcohols give different products on dehydrogenation.

Primary alcohol: Primary alcohols give an aldehyde on dehydrogenation.

Secondary alcohol: Secondary alcohols give a ketone on dehydrogenation.

Tertiary alcohol: Instead of normal dehydrogenation, tertiary alcohols undergo dehydration at high temperature to form an alkene.

Question: Write the tests for alcohol.

The following tests are performed to identify alcohol—

1. Ceric ammonium nitrate test: Mixing alcohol with a solution of ceric ammonium nitrate gives a red-brown color. This is a test for the presence of alcohol.

2. Ester test: Heating alcohol with acetic acid and concentrated H₂SO₄ forms an ester, giving a sweet, fragrant odor.

Question: What is wood spirit?

Methanol (CH₃OH) obtained by the destructive distillation of wood is called wood spirit.

Question: Write the modern method of preparing methanol along with its properties and uses.

Modern method:

Methanol is obtained by passing a mixture of carbon monoxide and hydrogen over a ZnO/Cr₂O₃ catalyst at high pressure and temperature.

Physical properties:

  • It is a colorless, flammable liquid, soluble in water.
  • Its boiling point is about 338 K.
  • It has a characteristic odor.
  • It is poisonous.
  • Its consumption can cause blindness and death.

Uses:

  • In the manufacture of formaldehyde and formalin.
  • To denature ethyl alcohol.
  • As a solvent for paints, varnishes, and shellac.
  • In the manufacture of methyl salicylate and methyl anthranilate.
  • In the manufacture of dyes and medicines.
  • In the manufacture of plastics (Perspex).
  • As a fuel along with petrol.
  • As an antifreeze in car radiators, etc. in cold countries.
Question: What is fermentation? Write its uses.

Fermentation: The biochemical process in which sugar is converted into alcohol or acid with the help of enzymes is called fermentation.

Uses:

  • In the manufacture of alcoholic beverages such as wine and beer.
  • In the bread and bakery industry, to leaven dough using CO₂.
  • In making curd (yogurt).
  • In making pickles and vinegar.
Question: What is molasses (jaggery syrup)?

The thick, sweet, and sticky substance left over after making sugar from sugarcane juice is called molasses (jaggery syrup). It mainly contains sugar and is used to make ethyl alcohol.

Question: How is ethyl alcohol obtained from molasses (jaggery syrup)?

The main method of obtaining ethyl alcohol from jaggery (or molasses) is fermentation. This process is completed in the following steps.

(i) Dilution:

Jaggery is taken in a tank and water is added to it, bringing the sugar content to about 8–10%.

(ii) Nutrient mixture:

Ammonium sulfate and ammonium phosphate are added to the dilute solution. These salts act as nutrients for the yeast.

(iii) Addition of dilute H₂SO₄:

A few drops of dilute H₂SO₄ are added to the solution to prevent the growth of unwanted bacteria.

(iv) Adding yeast:

Yeast is now added and the mixture is kept at a temperature of about 25–30°C.

(v) Fermentation:

During fermentation, the enzyme invertase converts sucrose into glucose and fructose.

Sucrose → Glucose + Fructose

After this, the enzyme zymase converts glucose into ethyl alcohol and carbon dioxide.

(vi) Distillation:

When fermentation is complete and CO₂ stops being released, the resulting fermented liquid is purified by distillation. This gives rectified spirit containing about 90–95% C₂H₅OH.

Question: How is ethanol obtained from starch? Explain briefly. Give the reaction equations.

Ethanol is prepared industrially from starchy materials such as potatoes and barley. The starchy material is heated with superheated steam at about 335 K to form a paste, and malt is added to it.

The enzyme diastase present in malt converts starch into maltose.

Starch → Maltose

The product obtained is cooled and yeast is added to it. The enzyme maltase present in yeast converts maltose into glucose.

Maltose → Glucose

Finally, the enzyme zymase ferments glucose to form ethanol and carbon dioxide.

Glucose → Ethanol + Carbon dioxide

Question: Write the properties and uses of ethanol.

Properties:

  • It is a colorless, transparent, and volatile liquid.
  • It has a characteristic pungent smell and a burning taste.
  • It is completely soluble in water, ether, and benzene.
  • Its boiling point is about 78.1°C.
  • It forms hydrogen bonds with water.

Uses:

  • As alcohol in beverages.
  • As a solvent in varnishes, paints, medicines, and perfumes.
  • As a fuel when mixed with gasoline.
  • In disinfectants and pharmaceutical preparations.
  • In the manufacture of acetic acid, chloroform, iodoform, and ether.
  • As a reagent and preservative in laboratories.
Question: What is an azeotrope?

A constant-boiling mixture of a solution of ethanol and water is called an azeotrope. The ethanol-water azeotrope consists of about 95% ethanol and 5% water.

Question: What do you understand by absolute alcohol?

Nearly completely pure ethyl alcohol is called absolute alcohol. It is obtained by removing water from the azeotrope.

Question: What do you understand by denatured spirit?

To prevent the misuse of ethyl alcohol, a small amount of methanol or another suitable substance is added to it, rendering it unfit for drinking. The alcohol obtained in this way is called denatured spirit.
