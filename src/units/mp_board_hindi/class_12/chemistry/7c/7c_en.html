📘 Ethers — Questions and Answers
Question: What are ethers?

Organic compounds in which two alkyl or aryl groups are joined to the same oxygen atom are called ethers. The general formula of an ether is R–O–R′.

They may also be regarded as the anhydride of an alcohol or as a dialkyl derivative of water.

Alcohol → Ether

Question: How many types of ethers are there?

Ethers are mainly of two types—

1. Simple (symmetrical) ethers: When the two alkyl or aryl groups attached to the oxygen atom are the same, they are called simple ethers.

R–O–R
CH₃–O–CH₃ — Dimethyl ether
C₆H₅–O–C₆H₅ — Diphenyl ether

2. Mixed (unsymmetrical) ethers: When the two groups attached to the oxygen atom are different, they are called mixed ethers.

CH₃–O–C₂H₅ — Ethyl methyl ether
Question: Explain the nomenclature of ethers.

There are two main systems for naming ethers—

(i) Common (trivial) system: In this method, the names of the two alkyl or aryl groups attached to the oxygen are written followed by the word ether.

CH₃–O–CH₃ — Dimethyl ether
CH₃–O–C₂H₅ — Ethyl methyl ether

(ii) IUPAC system: In this system, an ether is regarded as an alkoxyalkane. The smaller alkyl group is treated as the alkoxy group, and the larger group is treated as part of the alkane.

CH₃–O–CH₃ — Methoxymethane
CH₃–O–C₂H₅ — Methoxyethane
Question: What different types of isomerism are found in ethers?

Ethers mainly exhibit functional isomerism and metamerism.

1. Functional isomerism: When compounds with the same molecular formula have different functional groups, it is called functional isomerism.

Alcohols and ethers exhibit functional isomerism.

C₂H₆O
CH₃–CH₂–OH — Ethyl alcohol
CH₃–O–CH₃ — Dimethyl ether

2. Metamerism: When compounds have the same functional group, but the distribution of the carbon groups attached on either side of the functional group differs, it is called metamerism.

For example, the two ethers with formula C₄H₁₀O—

CH₃CH₂–O–CH₂CH₃ — Diethyl ether
CH₃–O–CH₂CH₂CH₃ — Methyl propyl ether

Question: Write the physical properties of ethers.

The main physical properties of ethers are as follows—

  1. Ethers are generally colorless, volatile liquids.
  2. They generally have a characteristic odor.
  3. The boiling points of ethers are lower than those of alcohols.
  4. Ethers are sparingly soluble in water, while they dissolve well in most organic solvents.
  5. The density of ethers is generally lower than that of water.
  6. Ethers are highly inflammable.
  7. Ether molecules do not form hydrogen bonds with one another. Hence their boiling points are lower than those of alcohols of similar molecular mass.
  8. Ethers of low molecular mass are generally more volatile.
Important: Because ether molecules do not form hydrogen bonds with one another, their boiling points are lower compared to the corresponding alcohols.
Question: Lower ethers are soluble in water, why?

Ethers form hydrogen bonds with water molecules, which is why they are soluble in water.

As the molecular mass increases, the solubility of ethers in water decreases.

Question: Ethers are volatile, why?

Ether molecules do not form hydrogen bonds with one another, so they do not remain associated.

Hence they are more volatile than the corresponding alcohol.

Question: Describe the laboratory method of preparing diethyl ether under the following points.

Principle — Diethyl ether is prepared by the dehydration of ethyl alcohol. In this process, the reaction in the presence of sulfuric acid is understood to occur in two steps.

Equations —

(i) First step: Ethyl hydrogen sulfate is formed.

(ii) Second step: Ethyl hydrogen sulfate reacts with ethyl alcohol to form diethyl ether, and sulfuric acid is regenerated.

Overall reaction:

Schematic apparatus sequence: After the ether is formed from the mixture of alcohol and acid, the product is condensed and collected. In an actual laboratory, this process is carried out only under the supervision of a trained teacher, since ether is highly inflammable.

Method — Diethyl ether is obtained by the acidic dehydration of alcohol. In this process, the ether is separated by condensation. This process, based on the principle of a continuous supply of alcohol, is called the continuous etherification method.

Purification — The ether obtained may contain small amounts of alcohol, water, and acidic impurities. Hence, for purification, the acidic impurities are first removed using an alkaline medium, then moisture is removed using a suitable drying agent. Finally, pure and dry ether is obtained by distillation.

Question: What is called the continuous etherification method? Is it truly a continuous process?

Diethyl ether is prepared by heating ethyl alcohol in excess in the presence of concentrated H₂SO₄ at about 140°C. This reaction occurs in two steps.

First step: Ethyl alcohol reacts with sulfuric acid to form ethyl hydrogen sulfate.

Second step: Ethyl hydrogen sulfate reacts with ethyl alcohol to form diethyl ether, and H₂SO₄ is regenerated.

The above equations make it clear that H₂SO₄ is regenerated, which helps convert ethyl alcohol into ether. This sequence keeps repeating. Hence this method is called the continuous etherification method.

Is this really a continuous process?

In reality, ether cannot be produced continuously for an indefinite time by this method. The main reasons for this are as follows—

1. Formation of water: The water formed in the reaction dilutes the sulfuric acid, reducing its reactivity.

2. Reduction of sulfuric acid: At high temperature, some of the sulfuric acid may be reduced by the alcohol and converted into sulfur dioxide (SO₂).

Question: What is called sulfuric ether?

Ethyl ether, i.e. diethyl ether, is also called sulfuric ether, because concentrated H₂SO₄ is used in its preparation.

Question: Write the uses of diethyl ether.

The main uses of diethyl ether are as follows—

  1. It was historically used, mixed with chloroform and alcohols, as an anesthetic.
  2. It is used as a solvent for substances such as resins, fats, oils, and gums.
  3. It is also used as a refrigerant.
  4. Because of its relatively inert nature, it is used as a solvent for Grignard reagents.
Question: How can you distinguish between alcohol and diethyl ether? (2019)

Alcohol and diethyl ether can be distinguished by the following tests—

1. Sodium test:

Alcohol reacts with sodium to give hydrogen gas. This hydrogen gas burns with a pop sound when ignited, whereas diethyl ether generally does not give this test.

2. Iodoform test:

If the alcohol is ethanol (C₂H₅OH), it gives the iodoform test and yellow-colored iodoform (CHI₃) is obtained, whereas diethyl ether does not give the iodoform test.
