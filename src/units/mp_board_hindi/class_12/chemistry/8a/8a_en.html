📘 Chapter — Aldehydes and Ketones
Q: What is the carbonyl group?

The ( >C=O ) group is called the carbonyl group.

Q: What are carbonyl compounds?

Compounds that contain the carbonyl group ( >C=O ) are called carbonyl compounds; for example — aldehydes and ketones.

Q: What are aldehydes and ketones?

Aldehyde — When the two hydrogen atoms on the terminal carbon of an alkane are replaced by a doubly-bonded oxygen atom, the resulting compound is called an aldehyde. Aldehydes contain the (–CHO) group.

\[ CH_4 \xrightarrow{-2H/+O} HCHO \]
\[ CH_3-CH_3 \xrightarrow{-2H/+O} CH_3CHO \]

Ketone — When the two hydrogen atoms on a middle carbon of an alkane are replaced by a doubly-bonded oxygen atom, the resulting compound is called a ketone. Ketones contain the (–CO–) group.

\[ CH_3-CH_2-CH_3 \xrightarrow{-2H/+O} CH_3-CO-CH_3 \]
Q: How many types of ketones are there?

Ketones are of two types.

  1. Simple or symmetrical ketone: When the two alkyl groups attached to the ketone group are identical, it is called a simple or symmetrical ketone.
    Example: CH3-CO-CH3 — dimethyl ketone (acetone)
  2. Mixed or unsymmetrical ketone: When the two alkyl groups attached to the ketone group are different, it is called a mixed or unsymmetrical ketone.
    Example: CH3-CO-C2H5 — methyl ethyl ketone
Q: How are aldehydes and ketones named?

1. In the common system

Aldehyde → The suffix 'aldehyde' is added at the end of the name of the source from which the corresponding acid is obtained.

Example: HCHO — formaldehyde, CH3CHO — acetaldehyde

Ketone → First the name of the smaller alkyl group is written, followed by the larger alkyl group, and the word 'ketone' is added at the end.

Example: CH3CO CH3 — dimethyl ketone

2. In the IUPAC system

Aldehyde → The 'e' of the corresponding alkane is removed and 'al' is added.

Example: HCHO — methanal, CH3CHO — ethanal

Ketone → The 'e' of the corresponding alkane is removed and 'one' is added.

Example: CH3CO CH3 — propanone

Q: Describe the general methods of preparing aldehydes and ketones.
(Or — write only the chemical equations to obtain the following: acetaldehyde/acetone from calcium acetate, acetaldehyde/acetone from acetylene, acetaldehyde from acetyl chloride. Or — what happens when: ethyl alcohol is oxidised by acidified K2Cr2O7, acetyl chloride is reduced by hydrogen in the presence of BaSO₄, calcium formate/acetate is subjected to dry distillation, acetylene reacts with water in the presence of H₂SO₄ and HgSO₄ — the answers to all these questions are contained in the methods given below)

The general methods of preparing aldehydes and ketones are as follows.

1. Oxidation of alcohol

When alcohol is oxidised in the presence of acidified K2Cr2O7, aldehydes and ketones are obtained. Oxidation of primary alcohols gives aldehydes, while secondary alcohols give ketones.

\[ H\text{-}CH_2\text{-}OH + [O] \xrightarrow{\text{acidified } K_2Cr_2O_7} H\text{-}CHO + H_2O \]
\[ CH_3\text{-}CH_2\text{-}OH + [O] \xrightarrow{\text{acidified } K_2Cr_2O_7} CH_3\text{-}CHO + H_2O \]
\[ CH_3\text{-}CH(OH)\text{-}CH_3 + [O] \xrightarrow{\text{acidified } K_2Cr_2O_7} CH_3\text{-}CO\text{-}CH_3 + H_2O \]

2. Dehydrogenation of alcohol

When alcohol is dehydrogenated in the presence of Cu catalyst at 300°C, aldehydes and ketones are obtained. Primary alcohols give aldehydes and secondary alcohols give ketones.

\[ H\text{-}CH_2\text{-}OH \xrightarrow{Cu/300^\circ C} H\text{-}CHO + H_2 \]
\[ CH_3\text{-}CH_2\text{-}OH \xrightarrow{Cu/300^\circ C} CH_3\text{-}CHO + H_2 \]
\[ CH_3\text{-}CH(OH)\text{-}CH_3 \xrightarrow{Cu/300^\circ C} CH_3\text{-}CO\text{-}CH_3 + H_2 \]

3. Oxidation of fatty acid vapour (over hot MnO)

When vapours of fatty acids are passed over hot MnO at 300°C, aldehydes and ketones are obtained.

\[ HCOOH + HCOOH \xrightarrow{MnO/300^\circ C} HCHO + CO_2 + H_2O \]
\[ CH_3COOH + HCOOH \xrightarrow{MnO/300^\circ C} CH_3CHO + CO_2 + H_2O \]
\[ CH_3COOH + CH_3COOH \xrightarrow{MnO/300^\circ C} CH_3COCH_3 + CO_2 + H_2O \]

4. Dry distillation of the calcium salt of a fatty acid

When the calcium salt of a fatty acid is subjected to dry distillation, aldehydes and ketones are obtained. (2009/12/16/18/21/22/24)

\[ (HCOO)_2Ca \xrightarrow{\text{dry distillation}} HCHO + CaCO_3 \]
\[ (CH_3COO)_2Ca + (HCOO)_2Ca \xrightarrow{\text{dry distillation}} 2CH_3CHO + 2CaCO_3 \]
\[ (CH_3COO)_2Ca \xrightarrow{\text{dry distillation}} CH_3COCH_3 + CaCO_3 \]

5. Rosenmund reaction

When acetyl chloride is reduced in the presence of palladium poisoned with BaSO₄, acetaldehyde is obtained. This is called the Rosenmund reaction. Here palladium acts as the catalyst, while BaSO₄ prevents the acetaldehyde from being further reduced to alcohol. (2022/23/25)

\[ CH_3COCl + H_2 \xrightarrow{Pd/BaSO_4} CH_3CHO + HCl \]

6. Stephen reaction

When methyl cyanide is reduced in the presence of Sn and HCl, an intermediate compound is formed, whose hydrolysis gives acetaldehyde. This is called the Stephen reaction.

\[ CH_3CN + 2[H] \xrightarrow{Sn/HCl} CH_3CH=NH\cdot HCl \xrightarrow{+H_2O} CH_3CHO + NH_4Cl \]

7. By hydrolysis of acetylene

When acetylene is hydrolysed in the presence of dilute 42% H₂SO₄ and 1% HgSO₄, acetaldehyde is obtained. (2020/23/25)

\[ CH \equiv CH + H_2O \xrightarrow{42\%\,H_2SO_4/1\%\,HgSO_4} CH_3CHO \]

8. Gattermann-Koch reaction

When benzene in an ethereal solution is treated with carbon monoxide and HCl at high pressure in the presence of anhydrous AlCl₃ or cuprous chloride (in small amount), benzaldehyde is formed. (2012)

\[ C_6H_6 + CO \xrightarrow{\text{anhydrous } AlCl_3/HCl} C_6H_5CHO \]

9. Gattermann aldehyde synthesis

When benzene reacts with HCN and HCl in the presence of anhydrous AlCl₃, an intermediate imino chloride is first formed, whose hydrolysis gives benzaldehyde. (2020/23/25)

\[ C_6H_6 + HCN + HCl \xrightarrow{\text{anhydrous } AlCl_3} C_6H_5CH=NH\cdot Cl \xrightarrow{+H_2O} C_6H_5CHO + NH_4Cl \]

10. Etard reaction

When toluene is oxidised by chromyl chloride in CS₂ or CCl₄, benzaldehyde is formed. This is called the Etard reaction.

\[ C_6H_5CH_3 + 2[O] \xrightarrow{\text{chromyl chloride}/CCl_4} C_6H_5CHO + H_2O \]
Q: Describe the physical properties of aldehydes and ketones.

The physical properties of aldehydes and ketones are as follows.

  1. Physical state: Most aldehydes are liquids at room temperature. Lower ketones are colourless liquids with a pleasant smell. Compounds with higher molecular weight are colourless solids.
  2. Boiling point: The boiling point of aldehydes and ketones is higher than that of hydrocarbons of comparable molecular weight.
  3. Solubility: Lower aldehydes and ketones are soluble in water. As the size of the alkyl group increases, the solubility of these compounds in water decreases.
Q: Why are aldehydes and ketones polar in nature?
(Or — Why do aldehydes and ketones give nucleophilic substitution reactions?)

Aldehydes and ketones contain the carbonyl group (>C=O). Since the oxygen of the carbonyl group is more electronegative, it pulls the electron density of the double bond towards itself, so that it acquires a partial negative charge (δ–), while the carbon acquires a partial positive charge (δ+). This is why the carbonyl group is polar.

\[ >C=O \;\longrightarrow\; >C^{\delta+}-O^{\delta-} \]

A nucleophilic reagent (Nu−) easily attacks the positively charged carbon atom of the carbonyl group. This is why aldehydes and ketones give nucleophilic substitution reactions.

Note: The source notes write "nucleophilic substitution reaction" here; aldehydes/ketones actually give a nucleophilic addition reaction — this is the source's original terminology, and has been kept exactly as-is here.
Q: Explain the order of reactivity among formaldehyde, acetaldehyde and acetone.

Aldehydes and ketones contain the carbonyl group (>C=O). Since the oxygen of the carbonyl group is more electronegative, it pulls the electron density of the double bond towards itself, so that it acquires a partial negative charge (δ–), while the carbon acquires a partial positive charge (δ+). This is why the carbonyl group is polar.

\[ >C=O \;\longrightarrow\; >C^{\delta+}-O^{\delta-} \]

A nucleophilic reagent easily attacks the positively charged carbon atom of the carbonyl group. Now, the methyl group has a positive inductive effect (+I). Therefore, its presence decreases the reactivity of the carbonyl carbon.

Since acetone has two methyl groups, acetaldehyde has only one methyl group, while formaldehyde has no methyl group at all, acetone is the least reactive and formaldehyde is the most reactive.

Order of reactivity: Formaldehyde > Acetaldehyde > Acetone

The chemical properties of aldehydes and ketones are as follows.

[A] Addition reactions

1. Addition of hydrogen: Aldehydes and ketones react with hydrogen to form alcohols.

2. Addition of hydrogen cyanide: Aldehydes and ketones react with hydrogen cyanide to form cyanohydrins.

3. Addition of sodium bisulfite: Aldehydes and ketones react with sodium bisulfite to form the sodium bisulfite adduct.

[B] Substitution reactions

1. Reaction with hydroxylamine: Aldehydes and ketones react with hydroxylamine to form oximes.

2. Reaction with hydrazine: Aldehydes and ketones react with hydrazine to form hydrazones.

3. Reaction with phenylhydrazine: Aldehydes and ketones react with phenylhydrazine to form phenylhydrazones.

4. Reaction with semicarbazide: Aldehydes and ketones react with semicarbazide to form semicarbazones.

5. Reaction with Grignard reagent: Aldehydes and ketones react with the Grignard reagent to form an intermediate compound, whose hydrolysis gives an alcohol.

[C] Reaction with chlorine

Formaldehyde does not react with chlorine. Acetaldehyde reacts with chlorine to form trichloroacetaldehyde, which is called chloral.

\[ CH_3CHO + 3Cl_2 \rightarrow CCl_3CHO + 3HCl \]

Acetone reacts with chlorine to form trichloroacetone.

\[ CH_3COCH_3 + 3Cl_2 \rightarrow CCl_3COCH_3 + 3HCl \]

When chlorine is passed over boiling benzaldehyde, benzoyl chloride is formed.

\[ C_6H_5CHO + Cl_2 \rightarrow C_6H_5COCl + HCl \]

[D] Reduction reactions

1. Reduction by LiAlH₄: On reduction with LiAlH₄, aldehydes form primary alcohols and ketones form secondary alcohols.

\[ HCHO + 2[H] \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} CH_3OH \] \[ CH_3CHO + 2[H] \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} CH_3-CH_2OH \] \[ CH_3COCH_3 + 2[H] \xrightarrow{LiAlH_4} CH_3CH(OH)CH_3 \]

2. Reduction with sodium amalgam and water: On reduction with Na-Hg and HCl acid, aldehydes form primary alcohols and ketones form secondary alcohols.

\[ HCHO + 2[H] \xrightarrow{Na\text{-}Hg/HCl} CH_3OH \] \[ CH_3CHO + 2[H] \xrightarrow{Na\text{-}Hg/HCl} CH_3-CH_2OH \] \[ CH_3COCH_3 + 2[H] \xrightarrow{Na\text{-}Hg/HCl} CH_3CH(OH)CH_3 \]

On reduction with sodium amalgam and water, benzaldehyde gives benzopinacol, which is also called hydrobenzoin.

3. Clemmensen reduction: Aldehydes and ketones are reduced in the presence of zinc amalgam and concentrated HCl to form alkanes. This is called the Clemmensen reduction.

\[ HCHO + 4[H] \xrightarrow{Zn\text{-}Hg/HCl} CH_4 + H_2O \] \[ CH_3CHO + 4[H] \xrightarrow{Zn\text{-}Hg/HCl} CH_3-CH_3 + H_2O \] \[ CH_3COCH_3 + 4[H] \xrightarrow{Zn\text{-}Hg/HCl} CH_3CH_2CH_3 + H_2O \]

4. Wolff-Kishner reduction: When an aldehyde or ketone is heated with hydrazine, and then reduced with a strong base such as KOH in a high-boiling solvent such as glycol, an alkane is obtained.

\[ HCHO + H_2NNH_2 \rightarrow HCH=NNH_2 \xrightarrow{450K/KOH/\text{glycol}} CH_4 + N_2 \] \[ CH_3CHO + H_2NNH_2 \rightarrow CH_3CH=NNH_2 \xrightarrow{450K/KOH/\text{glycol}} CH_3-CH_3 + N_2 \] \[ (CH_3)_2CO + H_2NNH_2 \rightarrow (CH_3)_2C=NNH_2 \xrightarrow{450K/KOH/\text{glycol}} (CH_3)_2CH_2 + N_2 \]

On reduction in a neutral or alkaline solution, acetone gives pinacol.

[E] Oxidation reactions

1. Reaction with Tollens' reagent: The ammoniacal solution of silver nitrate is called Tollens' reagent. It contains active Ag₂O.

\[ AgNO_3 + NH_4OH \rightarrow AgOH + NH_4NO_3 \]

AgOH quickly loses one molecule of water and changes into silver oxide, which is called Tollens' reagent.

\[ 2AgOH \rightarrow Ag_2O + H_2O \quad (\text{Tollens' reagent}) \]

Tollens' reagent oxidises the aldehyde to an acid and is itself reduced to a silver mirror.

\[ HCHO + Ag_2O \rightarrow HCOOH + 2Ag \] \[ CH_3CHO + Ag_2O \rightarrow CH_3COOH + 2Ag \] \[ C_6H_5CHO + Ag_2O \rightarrow C_6H_5COOH + 2Ag \]

Ordinary ketones generally do not react with Tollens' reagent, so no silver mirror is formed.

2. Reaction with Fehling's reagent: Fehling's solution is of two types — Fehling's solution A: a blue solution of copper sulfate (CuSO₄); Fehling's solution B: an alkaline solution of sodium potassium tartrate (Rochelle salt) in NaOH. When the two solutions are mixed, a deep blue solution of CuO is obtained, and this is called Fehling's reagent.

\[ CuSO_4 + 2NaOH \rightarrow Cu(OH)_2 + Na_2SO_4 \] \[ Cu(OH)_2 \rightarrow CuO + H_2O \quad (\text{Fehling's reagent}) \]

When Fehling's solution is heated with an aldehyde, it oxidises the aldehyde to a carboxylic acid and is itself reduced to give a brick-red precipitate of Cu₂O.

\[ HCHO + 2CuO \rightarrow HCOOH + Cu_2O\,(\text{red precipitate}) \] \[ CH_3CHO + 2CuO \rightarrow CH_3COOH + Cu_2O\,(\text{red precipitate}) \]

Benzaldehyde and ketones are not oxidised by Fehling's solution.

3. Oxidation of acetone: Acetone is oxidised by acidified potassium dichromate to form acetic acid.

\[ CH_3COCH_3 + 4[O] \xrightarrow{\text{acidified } K_2Cr_2O_7} CH_3COOH + H_2O + CO_2 \]

[F] Reaction with Schiff's reagent

A solution of fuchsin in sulfur dioxide is called Schiff's reagent. It is colourless.

Schiff's reagent = Fuchsin + SO₂

It is used to distinguish between aldehydes and ketones.

An aldehyde reacts with Schiff's reagent and turns it pink/purple — this is a special test for aldehydes.

\[ \text{Aldehyde} + \text{Schiff's reagent} \rightarrow \text{pink/purple colour} \]

Ketones generally show no colour change with Schiff's reagent.

\[ \text{Ketone} + \text{Schiff's reagent} \rightarrow \text{no colour change} \]

[G] Reactions with ammonia

1. Reaction of formaldehyde with ammonia: Formaldehyde and ammonia undergo a special type of reaction to form urotropine. It is a heterocyclic compound.

\[ 6HCHO + 4NH_3 \longrightarrow (CH_2)_6N_4 + 6H_2O \]

Full name: The chemical name of urotropine is hexamethylenetetramine.

Formula: (CH₂)₆N₄ or (C₆H₁₂N₄)

Structure: In it, four nitrogen atoms are located in a cage-like structure and are joined by –CH₂– groups.

Uses:

  • In making medicines for the treatment of urinary tract diseases and arthritis.
  • As a preservative and disinfectant.
  • In making phenolic resins and explosives (such as RDX).

2. Reaction of acetaldehyde with ammonia: Acetaldehyde reacts with ammonia to form an addition compound, acetaldehyde-ammonia.

3. Reaction of acetone with ammonia: Acetone reacts with ammonia to form an addition compound, acetone-ammonia.

4. Reaction of benzaldehyde with ammonia: Benzaldehyde reacts with ammonia to form hydrobenzamide.

\[ 3C_6H_5CHO + 2NH_3 \rightarrow (C_6H_5CH)_3N_2 + 3H_2O \]

[H] Reactions of the alpha-hydrogen

1. Cannizzaro reaction: When two molecules of an aldehyde lacking an α-hydrogen, such as formaldehyde or benzaldehyde, react with each other in the presence of a strong base (NaOH), one molecule is oxidised to form the sodium salt of a carboxylic acid, while the other is reduced to form an alcohol. This is a redox reaction.

\[ HCHO + HCHO + NaOH \longrightarrow CH_3OH + HCOONa \] \[ C_6H_5CHO + C_6H_5CHO + NaOH \longrightarrow C_6H_5CH_2OH + C_6H_5COONa \]

2. Tishchenko reaction: When two molecules of an aldehyde possessing an α-hydrogen, such as acetaldehyde, react with each other in the presence of aluminium alkoxide, one molecule is oxidised to a carboxylic acid and the other is reduced to an alcohol. The two then condense to form an ester.

\[ CH_3CHO + CH_3CHO \rightarrow CH_3COOC_2H_5 \]

[I] Condensation reaction

(2009/11/12/16/18/20/23)

1. Aldol condensation: When two molecules of an aldehyde or ketone possessing an α-hydrogen condense in the presence of dilute NaOH, a β-hydroxy aldehyde (aldol) or β-hydroxy ketone (ketol) is formed. On heating, these undergo dehydration to form α,β-unsaturated compounds.

\[ 2CH_3CHO \rightarrow CH_3\text{-}CH(OH)\text{-}CH_2\text{-}CHO \xrightarrow{\Delta} CH_3\text{-}CH=CH\text{-}CHO \] \[ 2CH_3COCH_3 \rightarrow CH_3\text{-}C(OH)\text{-}CH_2\text{-}COCH_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} CH_3\text{-}C=CH\text{-}COCH_3 \]

2. Cross aldol condensation: When two different aldehydes or ketones undergo aldol condensation with each other, it is called cross aldol condensation; for example, aldol condensation between acetaldehyde and acetone.

\[ CH_3CHO + CH_3COCH_3 \rightarrow CH_3\text{-}CH(OH)\text{-}CH_2\text{-}COCH_3 \rightarrow CH_3\text{-}CH=CH\text{-}COCH_3 \]

3. Claisen-Schmidt (Claisen) condensation: When benzaldehyde is heated with an aldehyde possessing an α-hydrogen, such as acetaldehyde, in the presence of dilute NaOH, cinnamaldehyde is formed.

\[ C_6H_5CHO + H_2CHCHO \xrightarrow{NaOH} C_6H_5CH=CHCHO + H_2O \]

4. Formation of mesitylene: When three molecules of acetone are heated with concentrated sulfuric acid, trimethylbenzene is formed, which is called mesitylene.

\[ 3CH_3COCH_3 \xrightarrow{\text{conc. } H_2SO_4} C_6H_3(CH_3)_3 + 3H_2O \]

5. Condensation of acetone with chloroform: Acetone condenses with chloroform in the presence of caustic potash to form chlorotone.

\[ (CH_3)_2CO + CHCl_3 \xrightarrow{KOH} (CH_3)_2C(OH)CCl_3 \]

(2008/09/14/17/18/19)

6. Benzoin condensation: Two molecules of benzaldehyde condense with each other in the presence of KCN to form an α-hydroxy ketone, which is called benzoin.

\[ 2C_6H_5CHO \xrightarrow{KCN} C_6H_5CH(OH)COC_6H_5 \]

[J] Polymerisation reactions

The process in which small molecules (monomers) of the same or different kind combine with one another to form a large molecule (polymer) is called a polymerisation reaction.

Polymerisation in formaldehyde:

\[ n\,HCHO \xrightarrow{\text{on evaporating the solution}} (HCHO)_n\,H_2O \] \[ 3\,HCHO \xrightarrow{\text{on keeping at room temperature}} (HCHO)_3 \]

Polymerisation in acetaldehyde:

\[ 3CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{\text{on warming with drops of conc. } H_2SO_4} (CH_3CHO)_3 \] \[ 4CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{\text{conc. } H_2SO_4 \text{ at } 0^\circ C} (CH_3CHO)_4 \]

Acetone does not undergo polymerisation. Benzaldehyde also does not undergo polymerisation.

[K] Reactions of benzaldehyde

1. Perkin reaction: When benzaldehyde is heated with acetic anhydride in the presence of sodium acetate, cinnamic acid is formed.

\[ C_6H_5CHO + (CH_3CO)_2O \xrightarrow{CH_3COONa} C_6H_5CH=CHCOOH + CH_3COOH \]

2. Knoevenagel reaction: When benzaldehyde reacts with malonic ester in the presence of pyridine, cinnamic acid is formed.

\[ C_6H_5CHO + H_2C(COOC_2H_5)_2 \xrightarrow{\text{pyridine}} C_6H_5CH=CHCOOH + CO_2 + H_2O \]

3. Reaction with aniline: The reaction of benzaldehyde with aniline gives benzaldehyde aniline (Schiff's base).

\[ C_6H_5CHO + H_2NC_6H_5 \rightarrow C_6H_5CH=NC_6H_5 + H_2O \]
Q: Name reagents that react with aldehydes but not with ketones.

Reagents that react with aldehydes but not with ketones are as follows.

  1. Tollens' reagent → aldehydes form a silver mirror.
  2. Fehling's reagent → aldehydes give a red precipitate of Cu₂O.
  3. Schiff's reagent → aldehydes give a pink colour.

But ketones do not react with any of these.

Q: Formaldehyde gives the Cannizzaro reaction while acetaldehyde does not — why?

Formaldehyde has no α-hydrogen atom, so it undergoes the Cannizzaro reaction. Acetaldehyde, on the other hand, has an α-hydrogen atom, so it undergoes aldol condensation instead.

Q: Why is benzaldehyde less reactive than acetaldehyde?

In benzaldehyde, the –CHO group is directly attached to the benzene ring. The resonance effect of the aromatic ring reduces the reactivity of the carbonyl group. Hence, benzaldehyde is less reactive than acetaldehyde.

Q: Describe the laboratory method of preparing formaldehyde.

Principle: Formaldehyde is obtained by oxidising methanol in the presence of a platinum catalyst on asbestos.

Equation:

\[ CH_3OH + \tfrac{1}{2}O_2 \longrightarrow HCHO + H_2O \]

Physical properties:

  • It is a colourless gas with a characteristic pungent smell. It is highly soluble in water.

Uses:

  1. Formaldehyde is used with phenol to make bakelite and other types of plastics.
  2. In making urotropine, which is used as a medicine for urinary diseases, arthritis, etc.
  3. In the manufacture of synthetic dyes (such as indigo).
Q: What is formalin? Describe its uses.

A 40% aqueous solution of formaldehyde is called formalin.

Uses: Formalin is used to disinfect and preserve dead bodies.

Q: Describe the laboratory method of preparing acetaldehyde.

Principle: Acetaldehyde is obtained by oxidising ethanol with acidified K2Cr2O7.

Equation:

\[ CH_3CH_2OH + [O] \xrightarrow{\text{acidified } K_2Cr_2O_7} CH_3CHO \]

Physical properties:

  • It is a colourless, volatile liquid. It has a sharp smell, but its aqueous solution has an apple-like fragrance.
  • Its boiling point is 21°C.

Uses:

  1. As a hypnotic (sleep-inducing) drug.
  2. Metaldehyde is used as a solid fuel in lamps.
Q: Describe the laboratory preparation of acetone (dry distillation of calcium acetate).

Principle: Acetone is formed by the dry distillation of calcium acetate.

Equation:

\[ (CH_3COO)_2Ca \xrightarrow{\text{dry distillation}} CH_3COCH_3 + CaCO_3 \]

Physical properties:

  • It is a colourless liquid with a sweetish taste. It is soluble in water as well as in organic solvents.
  • Its boiling point is 56°C.
  • It is a toxic substance.

Uses:

  1. In making synthetic rubber.
  2. In making the artificial perfume 'Adron'.
  3. As a solvent for artificial silk, cellulose, varnish, etc.
  4. In making acetic anhydride, which is used in various kinds of synthetic materials.
Q: Arrange the following compounds in increasing order of their boiling points: CH3CHO, CH3CH2OH, CH3OCH2CH2CH2CH2
\[ CH_3OCH_2CH_2CH_2CH_3 \;<\; CH_3CHO \;<\; CH_3CH_2OH \]
Note: In the source, the second term of this answer was written as "CH₂CH₂OH" with a typographical error (missing the initial CH₃), and with an inconsistency in the carbon chain (number of CH₂ units) of the ether compound; here it has been presented with the spelling corrected to match the compounds given in the original question (CH₃CHO, ethanol, and the alkyl ether), while the chemical order (ether < aldehyde < alcohol) has been kept the same as in the source.
Objective Questions
  1. The formation of a cyanohydrin from a ketone is an example of what?
    Answer: Nucleophilic addition
  2. Which compound gives the Cannizzaro reaction with concentrated alkali solution?
    Answer: Benzaldehyde
  3. Aldehydes and ketones react with which of the following to form oximes?
    Answer: NH₂OH
  4. What does an aromatic aldehyde give with a primary amine?
    Answer: Schiff's base
  5. The reaction of acetaldehyde in an alkaline medium is —
    Answer: Aldol condensation
  6. Which of these does not give the iodoform reaction?
    Answer: CH₃COCH₃ (ketone)
  7. The precipitate obtained from ethanol with Fehling's solution is —
    Answer: Cu₂O
  8. The reduction of aldehydes and ketones to hydrocarbons takes place in the presence of what?
    Answer: Zn/Hg and HCl
  9. What is the nature of the α-hydrogen in an aldehyde?
    Answer: Acidic
  10. What is formed when benzaldehyde is heated with alcoholic KCN?
    Answer: Benzoin
  11. Which compound does not give a silver mirror with ammoniacal AgNO₃?
    Answer: CH₃COOH
  12. Which reagent reacts readily with both aldehydes and ketones?
    Answer: Schiff's reagent
  13. How is a methyl ketone identified?
    Answer: Iodoform test
  14. Which reagent can be used to distinguish between aldehydes and ketones?
    Answer: NaHSO₃ solution
  15. Which compound will give the Cannizzaro reaction?
    Answer: Benzaldehyde
  16. Tollens' reagent is —
    Answer: Ammoniacal silver nitrate
  17. In the reaction of formaldehyde with KOH, methanol and potassium formate are formed — by what name is this known?
    Answer: Cannizzaro reaction
  18. Benzaldehyde is —
    Answer: C₆H₅CHO
  19. What colour does Schiff's reagent give with benzaldehyde?
    Answer: Pink
  20. What is the IUPAC name of glacial acetic acid?
    Answer: Ethanoic acid
  21. A solution of sodium potassium tartrate and alkaline copper sulfate is called —
    Answer: Fehling's solution
  22. Why do carboxylic acids form cyclic dimers?
    Answer: Intermolecular hydrogen bonding
  23. Which compound is formed from the reaction of an aromatic aldehyde with an acid anhydride?
    Answer: Cinnamic acid
  24. Define formalin —
    Answer: 40% aqueous solution of formaldehyde
  25. The formula of formaldehyde —
    Answer: CH₂O
  26. The condensation reaction of benzaldehyde with KCN is called —
    Answer: Benzoin condensation
Note: In the source, the answer to question 6 ("does not give the iodoform reaction") is given as CH₃COCH₃ (acetone), whereas chemically acetone itself gives the iodoform test (it is a methyl ketone); and the answer to question 7 states that ethanol gives a Cu₂O precipitate with Fehling's solution, whereas the Fehling test is specific to aldehydes only, not alcohols. The answer to question 12 ("Schiff's reagent reacts with both") is also inconsistent with the content of section [F] above (where ketones are stated to show no colour change with Schiff's reagent). These are all original inconsistencies of the source document, and have been kept here exactly as-is without any change.
