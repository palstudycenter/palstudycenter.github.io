Q. What are Oxidation and Reduction Reactions? Explain.

Oxidation –

According to the electron transfer concept, when a substance loses electrons, the process is called oxidation.

Example:
Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻
Here zinc loses two electrons; therefore, zinc undergoes oxidation.

Reduction –

According to the electron transfer concept, when a substance gains electrons, the process is called reduction.

Example:
Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu
Here copper ions gain electrons; therefore, reduction takes place.

Q. What do you mean by an Electrochemical Cell? How many types are there?

An electrochemical cell is a device in which electrical energy is produced by a chemical reaction or a chemical reaction is carried out by using electrical energy.

Oxidation-reduction (redox) reactions take place in an electrochemical cell.

Types of Electrochemical Cells:

  1. Galvanic (Voltaic) Cell: It is an electrochemical cell in which electrical energy is produced by a spontaneous chemical reaction.

Examples: Daniell Cell and Dry Cell.

  1. Electrolytic Cell: It is an electrochemical cell in which a chemical reaction is carried out with the help of an external source of electric current.

Example: Electrolysis of Water.

Q. How many types of Galvanic (Voltaic) Cells are there?

Galvanic (Voltaic) cells are of two types—

  1. Primary Cell: In these cells, the chemical reaction is irreversible. They cannot be recharged after use.
    Example: Dry Cell.
  2. Secondary Cell: In these cells, the chemical reaction is reversible. They can be recharged and used repeatedly.
    Example: Lead-Acid Battery.

Q. Describe the Construction and Working of a Daniell Cell.

Construction: A Daniell cell consists of two half-cells—a zinc half-cell and a copper half-cell—connected by a salt bridge.

  1. Zinc Half-Cell (Anode): A zinc rod is dipped into a zinc sulphate (ZnSO₄) solution. It acts as the anode where oxidation takes place.
  2. Copper Half-Cell (Cathode): A copper rod is dipped into a copper sulphate (CuSO₄) solution. It acts as the cathode where reduction takes place.
  3. Salt Bridge: A U-shaped tube filled with agar-agar or gelatin containing KCl or KNO₃ solution. It allows the movement of ions between the two half-cells and maintains electrical neutrality.
  4. Connecting Wire: The zinc and copper electrodes are connected through an external wire and a voltmeter to measure the potential difference.

Working:

  1. Oxidation at the Anode: Zinc loses electrons to form zinc ions.

\[ \mathrm{Zn \rightarrow Zn^{2+} + 2e^-} \]

The released electrons flow through the external circuit to the copper electrode.

  1. Reduction at the Cathode: Copper ions gain electrons and are deposited as copper metal.

\[ \mathrm{Cu^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow Cu} \]

  1. Overall Cell Reaction:

\[ \mathrm{Zn + CuSO_4 \rightarrow ZnSO_4 + Cu} \]

  1. Cell Representation:

\[ \mathrm{Zn(s)\;|\;ZnSO_4(aq)\;||\;CuSO_4(aq)\;|\;Cu(s)} \]

  1. Direction of Current: Electrons flow through the external circuit from zinc (anode) to copper (cathode). Therefore, the conventional current flows from copper to zinc.
Q. What is a Salt Bridge? State its Functions.

A salt bridge is a U-shaped tube filled with an electrolyte solution such as potassium chloride (KCl) or potassium nitrate (KNO₃) mixed with agar-agar or gelatin.

It connects the two half-cells of a galvanic (electrochemical) cell.

Functions:

  1. Maintains electrical neutrality.
  2. Completes the electrical circuit.
  3. Prevents the mixing of the two solutions.
  4. Helps maintain a constant potential difference.
Q. What is a Dry Cell?

A dry cell is a galvanic cell that converts chemical energy into electrical energy. It contains a paste electrolyte instead of a liquid electrolyte; therefore, it is called a dry cell.

Construction:

  1. Zinc Container: It forms the outer casing of the cell and acts as the anode (–).
  2. Carbon Rod: It is placed at the center of the cell and acts as the cathode (+).
  3. Electrolyte: A paste of ammonium chloride (NH₄Cl) and zinc chloride (ZnCl₂), which helps in the movement of ions.
  4. Depolarizer: A mixture of manganese dioxide (MnO₂) and carbon powder that prevents the formation of hydrogen gas.
  5. Top Seal: The cell is sealed with wax to prevent the electrolyte paste from leaking out.

Working:

At the Anode (Oxidation):
Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻

At the Cathode (Reduction):

NH₄Cl → NH₄⁺ + Cl⁻

2NH₄⁺ + 2e⁻ → 2NH₃ + H₂

2MnO₂ + H₂ → Mn₂O₃ + H₂O

MnO₂ removes the hydrogen produced during the reaction, ensuring a continuous supply of electric current.

Uses:

A dry cell is a simple, inexpensive, and portable source of electrical energy. It is widely used in flashlights, remote controls, clocks, radios, toys, and other portable electronic devices.

Q. What is Electrode Potential?

Electrode potential is the potential difference developed between a metal electrode and its ionic solution when they are in contact.

It is a measure of the tendency of an electrode to lose or gain electrons.

Q. What is Standard Electrode Potential?

The electrode potential measured under standard conditions, namely ion concentration of 1 mol L⁻¹, temperature of 25°C (298 K), and pressure of 1 atmosphere, is called the standard electrode potential.

Examples:

Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Cu     E° = +0.34 V

Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Zn     E° = −0.76 V

Thus, copper has a greater tendency to gain electrons, whereas zinc has a greater tendency to lose electrons.

Q. What is the Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE)?

The Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) is a reference electrode used to measure the electrode potentials of other electrodes.

Its electrode potential is assigned a value of 0.00 V at all temperatures.

A Standard Hydrogen Electrode consists of:

  1. A platinum electrode coated with platinum black to increase its surface area.
  2. The electrode is immersed in a solution containing 1 M H⁺ ions (generally 1 M HCl).
  3. Pure hydrogen gas (H₂) is passed over the electrode at a pressure of 1 atmosphere.
  4. The temperature is maintained at 298 K (25°C).

Representation:

Pt(s) ∣ H₂(1 atm) ∣ H⁺(1 M)

Standard Conditions:

  1. Ion concentration = 1 M
  2. Gas pressure = 1 atm
  3. Temperature = 25°C (298 K)
  4. Standard electrode potential, E° = 0.00 V

Uses of SHE:

  1. It is used as a reference electrode for measuring the standard electrode potentials of other electrodes.
  2. It helps determine the direction of current flow in an electrochemical cell.
Q. What is the Electrochemical Series? Write its main characteristics and any two uses.

The Electrochemical Series is a series obtained by arranging elements in the increasing order of their standard reduction potentials (E°).

Most Reactive → Least Reactive

K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au

Characteristics of the Electrochemical Series:

  1. The oxidizing power of elements increases from top to bottom in the series.
  2. The reducing power of elements decreases from top to bottom in the series.
  3. Metals placed above hydrogen liberate hydrogen gas when they react with acids.
  4. A metal placed higher in the series can displace a metal placed lower in the series from its salt solution.

Uses:

  1. It is used to compare the reactivity of metals.
  2. It is used to compare the reducing power of different metals.

Q. Explain the Nernst Equation for a Chemical Cell and write any three of its applications.

The dependence of the E.M.F. of a cell on concentration and temperature was studied by Walther Nernst. He proposed an equation expressing the effect of these factors on electrode potential, known as the Nernst Equation.

It is used to calculate the electrode potential of a reduction half-reaction.

General Nernst Equation:

\[ E = E^\circ - \frac{2.303RT}{nF}\log Q \]

Oxidation Half-Reaction:

\[ \mathrm{Zn \rightarrow Zn^{2+} + 2e^-} \]

\[ E = E^\circ - \frac{2.303RT}{nF} \log\frac{[Zn^{2+}]}{[Zn]} \]

Reduction Half-Reaction:

\[ \mathrm{Cu^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow Cu} \]

\[ E = E^\circ - \frac{2.303RT}{nF} \log\frac{[Cu]}{[Cu^{2+}]} \]

Daniell Cell:

\[ \mathrm{Zn + CuSO_4 \rightarrow ZnSO_4 + Cu} \]

\[ E = E^\circ - \frac{2.303RT}{nF} \log\frac{[Zn^{2+}]}{[Cu^{2+}]} \]

Applications of the Nernst Equation:

  1. To calculate the E.M.F. of an electrochemical cell.
  2. To determine the concentration of ions in solution.
  3. To predict whether a cell reaction will occur spontaneously or not.
Q. What do you understand by Corrosion? Explain the Electrochemical Theory of Rusting.

Corrosion is the process in which a metal is gradually destroyed due to the action of the surrounding environment such as air, moisture, acids, and gases. The corrosion of iron is commonly known as rusting.

Examples: Rusting of iron and the formation of a green coating (patina) on copper.

Electrochemical Theory of Rusting: Rusting is an electrochemical process in which iron is oxidized in the presence of moisture and oxygen.

(1) Anodic Reaction (Oxidation):

\[ Fe \rightarrow Fe^{2+} + 2e^- \]

(2) Cathodic Reaction (Reduction):

\[ O_2 + 2H_2O + 4e^- \rightarrow 4OH^- \]

(3) Formation of Rust:

\[ Fe^{2+} + 2OH^- \rightarrow Fe(OH)_2 \]
\[ 4Fe(OH)_2 + O_2 + 2H_2O \rightarrow 4Fe(OH)_3 \]
\[ Fe(OH)_3 \rightarrow Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O \]

Hydrated ferric oxide \((Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O)\) is called rust.

Q. Write the Factors Affecting Corrosion.

The main factors affecting corrosion are:

  1. Moisture – Rusting occurs faster in the presence of water.
  2. Oxygen – Oxygen accelerates the corrosion process.
  3. Nature of Metal – More reactive metals corrode more easily.
  4. Impurities – Impure metals corrode more rapidly.
  5. Salts and Acids – Salt water and acidic solutions increase the rate of corrosion.
Q. Write the Methods of Preventing Corrosion.

The following methods are commonly used to prevent corrosion:

  1. Painting: Painting the surface of a metal prevents direct contact with air and moisture, thereby preventing corrosion.

Therefore, iron gates, railings, and other iron objects are painted to protect them from rusting.

  1. Galvanization: In this method, a thin layer of zinc is coated on the surface of iron.

Since zinc is more reactive than iron, it oxidizes first and protects the iron from corrosion.

Hence, galvanized iron does not rust easily.

Q. State Ohm's Law.

According to Ohm's Law, the potential difference across a conductor is directly proportional to the electric current flowing through it, provided the temperature remains constant.

\[ V \propto I \]
\[ V = RI \]

where R is the resistance of the conductor.

Q. What is Resistance?

The opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of electric current is called Resistance. It is represented by R.

Its SI unit is ohm (Ω).

Q. What is Specific Resistance (Resistivity)?

The resistance offered by a solution of volume 1 cm³ (1 cc) is called Specific Resistance (Resistivity). It is represented by \(\rho\).

It is given by:

\[ \rho=\frac{RA}{L} \]

Its unit is Ω cm.

Q. What is Conductance?

The ability of a solution to conduct electric current is called Conductance. It is represented by C.

It is equal to the reciprocal of resistance.

\[ C=\frac{1}{R} \]

Its unit is Ω-1.

Q. What is Specific Conductance (Conductivity)?

The conductance of a solution having a volume of 1 cm³ (1 cc) is called Specific Conductance (Conductivity). It is represented by \(\kappa\).

It is equal to the reciprocal of specific resistance.

\[ \kappa=\frac{1}{\rho} \]

Its unit is Ω-1 cm-1.

What is Equivalent Conductance? Write its unit.

Equivalent Conductance:

Equivalent conductance of a solution is the product of its specific conductance and the volume of the solution containing one gram-equivalent of the electrolyte. It is represented by \(\lambda_{eq}\).

\[ \lambda_{eq}= \kappa V \]

Where,

  • \(V\) = Volume of the solution containing one gram-equivalent of the electrolyte.
  • \(\kappa\) = Specific conductance

Unit:

\[ \Omega^{-1}\,cm^{2}\,eq^{-1} \]

Effect of Dilution:

Equivalent conductance increases with dilution.

Relation with Normality:

Let the normality of the solution be \(N\).

\(\because\;\) \(N\) gram-equivalents of electrolyte are present in 1 litre = 1000 mL.

\(\therefore\;\) 1 gram-equivalent of electrolyte is present in \(\frac{1000}{N}\) mL.

Therefore, \[ V=\frac{1000}{N} \]

Hence,

\[ \lambda_{eq}= \kappa V \]

or,

\[ \lambda_{eq}= \kappa\left(\frac{1000}{N}\right) \]
What is Molar Conductance? Write its unit.

Molar Conductance:

Molar conductance of a solution is the product of its specific conductance and the volume of the solution containing one gram-mole of the electrolyte. It is represented by \(\lambda_m\).

\[ \lambda_m=\kappa V \]

Where,

  • \(V\) = Volume of the solution containing one gram-mole of the electrolyte.
  • \(\kappa\) = Specific conductance.

Unit:

\[ \Omega^{-1}\,cm^{2}\,mol^{-1} \]

Effect of Dilution:

Molar conductance increases with dilution.

Relation with Molarity:

Let the molarity of the solution be \(M\).

\(\because\;\) \(M\) gram-moles of electrolyte are present in 1 litre = 1000 mL.

\(\therefore\;\) 1 gram-mole of electrolyte is present in \(\frac{1000}{M}\) mL.

Therefore, \[ V=\frac{1000}{M} \]

Hence,

\[ \lambda_m=\kappa V \]

or,

\[ \lambda_m=\kappa\left(\frac{1000}{M}\right) \]
Write the effect of dilution on different types of conductance.

On increasing dilution of a solution:

  1. Specific conductance (\(\kappa\)) decreases because the number of ions per unit volume decreases.
  2. Equivalent conductance (\(\lambda_{eq}\)) increases because more ions become free and active per gram-equivalent.
  3. Molar conductance (\(\lambda_m\)) increases because more ions become free and active per mole.
State Kohlrausch's Law and write its two applications.

Kohlrausch's Law:

According to Kohlrausch's Law, at infinite dilution, the molar conductance of an electrolyte is equal to the sum of the independent molar conductances of its constituent ions.

\[ \Lambda_m^\circ=x\lambda_c^\circ+y\lambda_a^\circ \]

Where,

  • \(\Lambda_m^\circ\) = Molar conductance at infinite dilution
  • \(\lambda_c^\circ\) = Ionic conductance of the cation at infinite dilution
  • \(\lambda_a^\circ\) = Ionic conductance of the anion at infinite dilution
  • \(x\) = Number of cations
  • \(y\) = Number of anions

Applications:

  1. To determine the molar conductance of weak electrolytes at infinite dilution.
  2. To determine the ionic conductance of individual ions.
State Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis.

Faraday's laws of electrolysis describe the chemical changes produced by the passage of electric current through an electrolyte.

First Law:

The mass of a substance deposited or liberated at an electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity passed through the electrolyte.

\[ m \propto Q \] \[ m = ZQ \]

Where \(Z\) is the electrochemical equivalent.

Second Law:

When the same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytes, the masses of substances deposited are proportional to their chemical equivalents.

\[ \frac{m_1}{m_2}=\frac{W_1}{W_2} \]

Where,

  • \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) = Masses of substances deposited.
  • \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) = Their chemical equivalents.
What is Cell Constant?

Cell constant is the ratio of the distance between the two electrodes to the effective area of the electrodes.

\[ \text{Cell Constant}=\frac{l}{A} \]

Where,

  • \(l\) = Distance between the two electrodes.
  • \(A\) = Effective area of the electrodes.

Unit:

\[ cm^{-1} \]
What is the relationship between Specific Conductance and Cell Constant?

The relationship between specific conductance \((\kappa)\) and the cell constant \(\left(\frac{l}{A}\right)\) is:

\[ \kappa=C\times\frac{l}{A} \]

Where,

  • \(\kappa\) = Specific conductance
  • \(C\) = Conductance
  • \(\frac{l}{A}\) = Cell constant

Thus, if the cell constant is known, the specific conductance of a solution can be determined from its conductance.
