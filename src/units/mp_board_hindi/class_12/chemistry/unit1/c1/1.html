📘 Unit 1 - Solution
What is a solution? 2023

A homogeneous mixture of two or more substances is called a solution.

For example, a solution of salt and water.

In a solution, the substance present in a larger amount is called the solvent, and the substance present in a smaller amount is called the solute.

Classify solutions based on concentration.

Based on concentration, solutions are of five types.

  1. Concentrated solution
  2. Dilute solution
  3. Saturated solution
  4. Unsaturated solution
  5. Supersaturated solution.

The following are the units for expressing the concentration of a solution:

  1. Molarity
  2. Molality
  3. Normality
  4. Formality
  5. Mole fraction
  6. Percentage
  7. ppm Conc.

What is Molarity? Write its symbol, formula and unit.

Answer:

The number of moles of solute present in one litre of solution is called molarity. It is denoted by M.

\[ M=\frac{n}{V(l)} \]
\[ M=\frac{W}{M\times V(l)} \]
\[ M=\frac{W\times1000}{M\times V (ml)} \]

The unit of molarity is \( \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}} \).

What is Molality? Write its symbol, formula and unit.

Answer:

The number of moles of solute present in one kilogram of solvent is called molality. It is denoted by m.

\[ m=\frac{n}{W(kg)} \]
\[ m=\frac{W}{M\times W(kg)} \]
\[ m=\frac{W\times1000}{M\times W(g)} \]

The unit of molality is \( \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}} \).

What is Normality? Write its symbol, formula and unit.

Answer:

The number of gram equivalents of solute present in one litre of solution is called normality. It is denoted by N.

\[ N=\frac{E}{V(l)} \]
\[ N=\frac{W}{E.W.\times V(l)} \]
\[ N=\frac{W\times1000}{E.W.\times V(ml)} \]

The unit of normality is \( \mathrm{eq\,L^{-1}} \) or N.

What is Formality? Write its symbol, formula and unit.

Answer:

The number of gram formula masses of solute present in one litre of solution is called formality. It is denoted by F.

\[ F=\frac{n_f}{V(l)} \]
\[ F=\frac{W}{F.W.\times V(l)} \]
\[ F=\frac{W\times1000}{F.W.\times V(ml)} \]

The unit of formality is \( \mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}} \) or F.

What is Mole Fraction? Write its symbol, formula and unit.

Answer:

The ratio of the number of moles of a component to the total number of moles present in the solution is called mole fraction. It is denoted by \(X\).

\[ X=\frac{\text{Moles of a component}} {\text{Total moles}} \]
\[ X_1=\frac{n_1}{n_1+n_2} \]
\[ X_2=\frac{n_2}{n_1+n_2} \]

The mole fraction has no unit (it is a dimensionless quantity).

What are Colligative Properties? Write their examples.

Answer:

The properties of a solution that depend only on the number of solute particles present in the solution and not on the nature of the solute are called colligative properties.

Examples:

  1. Relative lowering of vapour pressure
  2. Elevation in boiling point
  3. Depression in freezing point

What is meant by vapor pressure, vapor pressure lowering, and relative lowering of vapor pressure?
  1. Vapor Pressure – When a liquid is in equilibrium with its vapor, the pressure exerted by the vapor on the surface of the liquid is called vapor pressure.

The vapor pressure of the pure solvent is denoted by \(P_0\).

  1. Lowering of Vapor Pressure – When a non-volatile solute is added to a pure solvent, the vapor pressure of the resulting solution decreases. This decrease in vapor pressure is called vapor pressure lowering.

If the vapor pressure of the pure solvent is \(P_0\) and the vapor pressure of the solution is \(P\), then

\[ \text{Lowering of vapor pressure} = P_0 - P \]

  1. Relative Lowering of Vapor Pressure – The ratio of the lowering of vapor pressure of a solution to the vapor pressure of the pure solvent is called the relative lowering of vapor pressure.

If the vapor pressure of the pure solvent is \(P_0\) and the vapor pressure of the solution is \(P\), then

\[ \text{Relative lowering of vapor pressure} = \frac{P_0 - P}{P_0} \]

Why does the vapor pressure decrease when a non-volatile solute is added to a solvent?

Particles of a non-volatile solute do not themselves vaporize. When they are added to a solvent, they occupy space between the solvent particles and partially prevent the solvent particles from going into the vapor state from the surface.

As a result, fewer solvent particles can reach the vapor state, and the vapor pressure of the solution becomes lower than that of the pure solvent.

Question: State Raoult's Law and prove it.
Raoult's Law:
According to Raoult's Law, at a constant temperature, the relative lowering of vapor pressure of a solution is equal to the mole fraction of the solute.
$$ \frac{P_0-P_s}{P_0} = \frac{n_1}{n_1+n_2} $$
Where —
P0 = Vapor pressure of pure solvent
Ps = Vapor pressure of solution
n1 = Number of moles of solute
n2 = Number of moles of solvent

Principle:
The vapor pressure of a solution is proportional to the mole fraction of the solvent.
$$ P_s \propto \frac{n_2}{n_1+n_2} $$
$$ P_s = K \left( \frac{n_2}{n_1+n_2} \right) \qquad ...(1) $$
For pure solvent
$$ P_s=P_0 \quad \text{and} \quad n_1=0 $$
From equation (1),
$$ P_0= K\left( \frac{n_2}{0+n_2} \right) $$
$$ P_0=K $$
Therefore,
$$ K=P_0 $$
Substituting the value of K in equation (1),
$$ P_s= P_0 \left( \frac{n_2}{n_1+n_2}
$$ \frac{P_s}{P_0} = \frac{n_2}{n_1+n_2} $$
Subtracting 1 from both sides,
$$ 1-\frac{P_s}{P_0} = 1- \frac{n_2}{n_1+n_2} $$
$$ \frac{P_0-P_s}{P_0} = \frac{n_1+n_2-n_2}{n_1+n_2} $$
$$ \frac{P_0-P_s}{P_0} = \frac{n_1}{n_1+n_2} $$
Hence, it is proved that
$$ \frac{P_0-P_s}{P_0} = \frac{n_1}{n_1+n_2} $$
This is Raoult's Law.
Raoult's Law for dilute solutions
In a dilute solution, the value of moles of solute (n) is very small compared to the moles of solvent (N).
$$ N+n \approx N $$
Therefore
$$ \frac{P_o-P}{P_o} = \frac{n}{N} $$
That is, for a dilute solution, the relative lowering of vapor pressure is equal to the ratio of the number of moles of solute and solvent.

Question: What is an azeotropic mixture? How many types are there?

Answer:
A liquid mixture that boils at a constant temperature and distills at the same temperature without changing its composition is called an azeotropic mixture.

It is of two types.

  1. Minimum boiling azeotrope
  2. Maximum boiling azeotrope

1. Minimum boiling azeotrope:

Solutions that show positive deviation from Raoult's Law are called minimum boiling azeotropes. Their vapor pressure is high.

Example: Solution of acetone and CS2.

2. Maximum boiling azeotrope:

Solutions that show negative deviation from Raoult's Law are called maximum boiling azeotropes. Their vapor pressure is low.

Example: Solution of acetone and chloroform.

Question: Can the components of an azeotropic mixture be separated by distillation?

The components of an azeotropic mixture cannot be separated by simple distillation, because it distills at a constant temperature without changing its composition.

Question: What is osmosis?

The property of solutions in which solvent molecules spontaneously flow from a region of lower solute concentration to a region of higher solute concentration through a semipermeable membrane is called osmosis.

Question: What is a semipermeable membrane?

A membrane that allows only solvent molecules to pass through it but obstructs solute molecules is called a semipermeable membrane.

Examples: Egg membrane, goat's stomach, etc., are biological membranes, and copper ferrocyanide is an artificial semipermeable membrane.

Question: Write the name of an artificial semipermeable membrane.

The name of the artificial semipermeable membrane is Copper Ferrocyanide.

Question: Why does a raisin swell in water, while a grape shrinks when placed in a concentrated sugar solution?

When a raisin is placed in water, the outside water has a lower concentration. Therefore, water enters the raisin through the semipermeable membrane. This process is called endosmosis. As a result, the raisin swells.

When a grape is placed in a concentrated sugar solution, the outside solution has a higher concentration. Therefore, water from inside the grape moves out. This process is called exosmosis. As a result, the grape shrinks.

Question: State the difference between osmosis and diffusion.
Osmosis Diffusion
1. In osmosis, only solvent molecules move. 1. In diffusion, both solvent and solute molecules move.
2. A semipermeable membrane is required for osmosis. 2. No membrane is required for diffusion.
3. Osmosis occurs only in liquids. 3. Diffusion occurs in solids, liquids, and gases.
4. Osmosis is a specific biological process. 4. Diffusion is a general physical process.
Question: Describe an experiment demonstrating the phenomenon of osmosis.

To demonstrate the phenomenon of osmosis, a Thistle Funnel is used.

A animal membrane (semipermeable membrane) is tied over the mouth of a thistle funnel. The thistle funnel is filled with a concentrated sugar solution up to some height. After this, the thistle funnel is immersed in a beaker filled with water in such a way that the membrane remains submerged in water.

After some time, the level of the solution in the thistle funnel tube starts rising.

This phenomenon demonstrates osmosis because water molecules pass through the semipermeable membrane from a less concentrated solution (water) to a more concentrated solution (sugar solution), causing the level of the solution in the tube to rise.

Question: State the biological importance of osmosis.

Osmosis plays a significant role in biological processes. Its main importance is as follows.

  1. Through osmosis, the roots of trees and plants absorb water from the soil, and this water reaches other parts of the plant.
  2. The flow of blood in human veins and arteries is affected by osmotic pressure.
  3. Glucose and other nutrients administered to patients reach the blood through osmosis and are absorbed by the cells.
  4. Osmosis maintains water balance in cells and helps maintain cell turgidity.
  5. Osmosis plays a vital role in the excretion of waste products like urea by kidneys.
Question: What is osmotic pressure?

Osmotic pressure is the minimum pressure that must be applied to a solution to prevent osmosis through a semipermeable membrane. It is denoted by \(\pi\).

Question: Define reverse osmosis.

If a pressure greater than its osmotic pressure is applied to a solution, then solvent molecules start flowing from the solution towards the pure solvent. This process is called reverse osmosis.

Question: What is meant by isotonic, hypertonic, and hypotonic solutions?

1. Isotonic Solution:

Two solutions having the same osmotic pressure are called isotonic solutions. There is no net flow of water between them.

Example: A 0.91% NaCl solution is isotonic with blood.

2. Hypertonic Solution:

If a solution has a higher osmotic pressure compared to another solution, it is called a hypertonic solution.

In such a solution, cells shrink because water moves out of the cell.

Example: A concentrated NaCl solution is hypertonic compared to blood.

3. Hypotonic Solution:

If a solution has a lower osmotic pressure compared to another solution, it is called a hypotonic solution.

In such a solution, cells swell because water enters the cell.

Example: Pure water is hypotonic compared to blood.

Question: State Van't Hoff's law for dilute solutions?

Van't Hoff's Law:

According to Van't Hoff's Law—the osmotic pressure of a dilute solution behaves like the pressure of a gas.

In mathematical form:

\[ \pi V = nRT \] \[ \pi = \left(\frac{n}{V}\right)RT \] \[ \pi = CRT \]

Where \(\frac{n}{V}=C\) (Molar concentration)

Where—

  • \(\pi\) = Osmotic pressure
  • \(V\) = Volume of solution
  • \(n\) = Moles of solute
  • \(R\) = Gas constant
  • \(T\) = Absolute temperature
  • \(C\) = Molar concentration
Question: Prove that osmotic pressure is a colligative property.

Van't Hoff's law applies to dilute solutions, according to which—

\[ \pi V = nRT \]

Or,

\[ \pi = \frac{nRT}{V} \]

Since \(R\), \(T\) and \(V\) are constant, therefore

\[ \pi \propto n \]

That is, osmotic pressure depends only on the number of solute particles (molecules/ions), not on their type or nature.

Therefore, osmotic pressure is a colligative property.

Answer :

Let's assume that W grams of solute are dissolved in V liters of solution and the molecular weight of the solute is M.

Number of moles of solute —

According to Van't Hoff's law —

\[ \pi V=nRT \] \[ \pi V=\frac{W}{M}RT \] \[ M=\frac{WRT}{\pi V} \]

Q. What is meant by boiling point, elevation in boiling point, and molal elevation constant? Explain.

Boiling Point :

The boiling point of a liquid is the temperature at which its vapor pressure becomes equal to the atmospheric pressure and the liquid begins to boil.

Example : The boiling point of pure water is 100°C.

Elevation in Boiling Point :

When a non-volatile solute is added to a pure solvent, the boiling point of the resulting solution increases compared to the boiling point of the pure solvent. This increase in boiling point is called elevation in boiling point. It is denoted by \(\Delta T_b\).

\[ \Delta T_b = T - T_0 \]

Where—

  • \(T\) = Boiling point of solution
  • \(T_0\) = Boiling point of pure solvent

Molal Elevation Constant :

We know that—

\[ \Delta T_b \propto m \] \[ \Delta T_b = K_b m \]

Where \(K_b\) is a constant called the molal elevation constant.

Definition

When 1 mole of a non-volatile solute is dissolved in 1 kilogram of solvent, the increase in the boiling point of the solution is called the molal elevation constant.

It is denoted by \(K_b\).

Unit :

\[ K\,kg\,mol^{-1} \]
Question: What is meant by gram molecular (or gram-mole) elevation constant?

When 1 gram-mole of a solute is dissolved in 100 grams of solvent, the increase in the boiling point of the solution is called the gram molecular elevation constant.

It is denoted by \(K_{100}\).

Question: Why does a solution of non-volatile solute have a higher boiling point than a pure solvent?
Or
What is the reason for boiling point elevation?

When a non-volatile solute is added to a pure solvent, the vapor pressure of the solution decreases.

Therefore, a higher temperature is required to make the vapor pressure of the solution equal to the atmospheric pressure compared to the pure solvent.

For this reason, the boiling point of a solution containing a non-volatile solute is higher than that of the pure solvent.

Question: Calculate the molecular mass of the solute using boiling point elevation.

Answer:

Let \(W_1\) grams of solute be dissolved in \(W_2\) grams of solvent, and the molecular mass of the solute be \(M_1\).

Therefore, molality

\[ m=\frac{1000W_1}{M_1W_2} \]

Now, the formula for boiling point elevation —

\[ \Delta T_b=K_bm \] \[ \Delta T_b= K_b\times \frac{1000W_1}{M_1W_2} \]

Therefore, the molecular mass of the solute —

\[ \boxed{ M_1= \frac{1000K_bW_1} {\Delta T_b\,W_2} } \]

Where —

  • \(K_b\) = Molal elevation constant
  • \(\Delta T_b\) = Elevation in boiling point
Q. What is meant by freezing point, depression in freezing point, and molal depression constant? Explain.

Freezing Point :

Freezing point is the temperature at which the solid and liquid states of a substance are in equilibrium, i.e., the temperature at which the liquid begins to freeze.

The freezing point of the pure solvent is denoted by \(T_0\).

Depression in Freezing Point :

When a non-volatile solute is added to a pure solvent, the freezing point of the resulting solution decreases compared to the freezing point of the pure solvent. This decrease in freezing point is called depression in freezing point. It is denoted by \(\Delta T_f\).

\[ \Delta T_f = T_0 - T \]

Where —

  • \(T_0\) = Freezing point of pure solvent
  • \(T\) = Freezing point of solution

Molal Depression Constant :

We know that —

\[ \Delta T_f \propto m \]

Or,

\[ \Delta T_f = K_f m \]

Where \(K_f\) is a constant called the molal depression constant.

Definition

When 1 mole of a non-volatile solute is dissolved in 1 kilogram of solvent, the decrease in the freezing point of the solution is called the molal depression constant.

It is denoted by \(K_f\).

Unit :

\[ K\,kg\,mol^{-1} \]

Question: Explain the solubility of gases in liquids.

Answer:

The ability of a gas to dissolve in a liquid is called the solubility of gases in liquids.

The solubility of gases in liquids primarily depends on pressure and temperature—

Effect of Pressure:

Increasing pressure increases the solubility of gases in liquids. (For this reason, CO₂ is dissolved in cold beverages under high pressure.)

Effect of Temperature:

Increasing temperature decreases the solubility of gases in liquids, because gas molecules start to escape.

Question: Explain Henry's Law with an example.

Answer:

According to Henry's Law— at a constant temperature, the amount of gas dissolved in a liquid is proportional to the partial pressure of that gas.

That is:

\[ p \propto x \] \[ p = K_H \times x \]

Where—

  • \(p\) = Partial pressure of the gas
  • \(x\) = Mole fraction of the gas dissolved in the liquid
  • \(K_H\) = Henry's constant

Example:

When a soda bottle containing CO₂ is closed, the pressure of CO₂ inside it is high, and CO₂ gas remains more dissolved in water.

When the bottle is opened, the pressure decreases, which causes less CO₂ to dissolve in water, and CO₂ gas starts to escape.

Question: What do you understand by Van't Hoff factor? Write its applications and importance.

Answer:

The Van't Hoff factor (Van’t Hoff factor) is a number that indicates how many particles a solute molecule divides (or associates) into in a solution.

It is denoted by i.

\[ i = \frac{\text{Actual number of particles}}{\text{Expected number of particles}} \]

If a solute dissociates in the solution, then \(i > 1\), and if it associates, then \(i < 1\).

Applications:

  • In accurate calculation of molecular mass (especially for electrolytes)
  • In modified formulas for freezing point depression and boiling point elevation
  • In calculating osmotic pressure
  • In determining the extent of dissociation/association of solute

Importance:

  • It helps in understanding the difference between actual and normal (theoretical) molecular mass.
  • Explains abnormal colligative properties.
  • Useful in understanding the behavior of electrolyte solutions.
