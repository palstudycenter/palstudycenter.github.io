What are carbohydrates? Explain their types and give examples.

Carbohydrates are polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds that yield these on hydrolysis. They are the main source of energy in organisms. General formula: C n ​(H₂O) n

Types of carbohydrates

  1. Monosaccharides - the simplest carbohydrates that cannot be hydrolyzed further.

    Examples: glucose, fructose and galactose.

  2. Disaccharides - carbohydrates in which 2 monosaccharide units are joined together are called disaccharides. They have a sweet taste and are soluble in water.

    Examples:

    Sucrose, maltose, lactose

  3. Oligosaccharides - carbohydrates in which 3 to 10 monosaccharide units are joined together are called oligosaccharides. They have a sweet taste and are soluble in water.

    Example: raffinose

  4. Polysaccharides - carbohydrates in which hundreds to thousands of monosaccharide units are joined together are called polysaccharides.

    These are generally insoluble and tasteless. Their function is to store energy and provide structural support.

    Examples: starch, glycogen, cellulose

Name the products obtained from the hydrolysis of lactose, maltose and sucrose.

The products obtained from the hydrolysis of lactose, maltose and sucrose are as follows.

  1. Lactose → glucose + galactose
  2. Maltose → glucose + glucose
  3. Sucrose → glucose + fructose
Which unit of carbohydrate provides energy to living organisms?

The main unit of carbohydrate that provides energy to living organisms is glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆).

During cellular respiration, glucose is oxidized to produce carbon dioxide, water and energy (ATP).

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP

Name the two final products of cellular respiration.

The two final products of respiration in cells are:

  1. Carbon dioxide (CO2)
  2. Water (H2O)

Besides this, energy is also produced in the form of ATP.

How does ATP function as a source of energy?

ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is called the energy currency of the cell.

It forms ADP (adenosine diphosphate) and Pi (inorganic phosphate), releasing energy.

ATP → ADP + Pi + energy

In this way it provides immediate energy to living cells.

Carbohydrates have the following functions in living organisms.

  1. Glucose provides energy through respiration.
  2. Glycogen is the energy store in animals.
  3. Carbohydrates prevent proteins from being used as an energy source.
  4. Ribose and deoxyribose are parts of RNA and DNA.
Write the functions of carbohydrates in plants.

Carbohydrates have the following functions in plants.

  1. It is stored in plants in the form of starch and functions as a food reserve.
  2. Cellulose, which is a polysaccharide, is the structural component of the plant cell wall.
Explain the digestion of carbohydrates
  1. Mouth: Salivary amylase breaks down starch into maltose.
  2. Small intestine: Pancreatic amylase further breaks down starch into maltose.
  3. Brush border enzymes: Maltase, lactase and sucrase convert disaccharides into monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose).
  4. Absorption: Monosaccharides are absorbed from the intestinal wall into the blood and reach the cells, providing energy.
What are amino acids? Give examples.

Amino acids are organic compounds in which both an amino group (–NH₂) and a carboxyl group (–COOH) are present in the same molecule. They are the basic units of protein.

Examples:

  • Glycine
  • Alanine
What are essential and non-essential amino acids?

Essential amino acids: Amino acids that are not synthesized in the human body and must be obtained from food.

Examples: valine, leucine, lysine, tryptophan.

Non-essential amino acids: Amino acids that are synthesized in the human body.

Examples: glycine, alanine, aspartic acid, glutamic acid

What is a zwitterion? Write its formula. 2022

When the acidic group (-COOH) of an amino acid gives up a hydrogen ion (H+) and the basic group (–NH₂) accepts that same ion, a dipolar ion is formed which is called a zwitterion.

What is meant by amide and peptide bonds? Explain.
  1. Amide bond: When the –COOH group of a carboxylic acid reacts with an amine (–NH₂) group and water (H₂O) is released, the –CONH– bond formed between them is called an amide bond.
    R–COOH+R′–NH₂⟶ R–CONH–R′+H₂O
  2. Peptide bond: When the –COOH group of one amino acid reacts with the –NH₂ group of another amino acid and water (H₂O) is released, the –CONH– bond formed between them is called a peptide bond.
    This bond plays a fundamental role in the formation of proteins.
What are proteins?

Proteins are high molecular weight, nitrogen-containing biological compounds formed by the condensation of many amino acids through peptide bonds. They are essential components of living cells and perform structural and functional roles.

Examples: hemoglobin, keratin, insulin, collagen.

How many types of proteins are there?

Proteins are mainly divided into two types based on their shape and structure —

  1. Fibrous proteins: Their structure is long and fiber-like. They are insoluble in water. Their main function is to provide structural support.

    Examples—

    Keratin (in hair, nails)
    Collagen (in bones and ligaments)
    Myosin (in muscles)

  2. Globular proteins - Their structure is spherical or oval. They are soluble in water. Their main function is to regulate and control biochemical reactions.
    Examples—
    Hemoglobin (oxygen transport)
    Insulin (blood sugar regulation)
    Enzymes (biocatalysts)

What is hemoglobin? What is its function in the body? (2024)

Hemoglobin is a red-colored protein found in red blood cells (RBC) and contains iron. It functions to carry oxygen from the lungs to the tissues and carbon dioxide from the tissues to the lungs.

Functions:

  1. Carrying oxygen from the lungs to the tissues.
  2. Carrying carbon dioxide from the tissues to the lungs.
  3. Helping maintain the pH balance of the blood.
What is oxyhemoglobin? Explain with an equation.

When hemoglobin present in the blood combines with oxygen from the lungs, the temporary compound formed is called oxyhemoglobin.

Hb (deoxyhemoglobin)+O2 ⇌ HbO₂ (oxyhemoglobin)

Which disease is caused by hemoglobin deficiency?

Hemoglobin deficiency causes anemia, due to which fatigue, weakness and a pale complexion occur.

What is an enzyme?

Enzymes are biological catalysts that increase the rate of chemical reactions occurring in living cells, while remaining unchanged themselves.
Most enzymes are proteins, and they play an important role in processes such as digestion, respiration and metabolism.

Write the industrial uses of enzymes.

Enzymes are biocatalysts made of protein, which greatly increase the rate of biochemical reactions and do not undergo permanent change themselves.

Industrial uses of enzymes:

  1. In the textile industry – amylase is used to remove starch from fabrics.
  2. In the food industry – invertase is used to soften toffee, protease to tenderize meat.
  3. In the dairy industry – rennin is used to make cheese.
  4. In the liquor industry – zymase is used to convert sugar into alcohol.
Explain the structure of protein.

Proteins are made up of long chains of amino acids joined by peptide bonds. The structure of protein is understood at four levels:

  1. Primary structure - This is the straight chain of amino acids. It tells which amino acid is joined in which order. This structure determines the properties and function of the protein.
  2. Secondary structure - The amino acid chain arranges itself by folding or coiling due to hydrogen bonds.
    Main types:
    α-helix
    β-pleated sheet
    This makes the protein stable.
  3. Tertiary structure - The entire chain of the protein takes on a 3-dimensional form. This includes hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, hydrophobic interactions and disulfide bonds. This is the active structure of the protein.
  4. Quaternary structure - When more than one polypeptide chain combine to form a functional protein, it is called the quaternary structure.

Example: hemoglobin (which has 4 polypeptide chains).

Which bond helps stabilize the alpha-helix structure of protein?

The stability of the α-helix structure of protein comes from intramolecular hydrogen bonds, which form between the >C=O group of one amino acid and the –NH group of the fourth amino acid in the chain.

What is denaturation of protein?

When the natural structure of a protein is destroyed under the effect of heat, strong acid/base or chemical reagents, and its biological activity is lost, this process is called denaturation of protein.

What is a nucleotide? Write its different components.

Nucleotides are the structural and functional units of nucleic acids.

Each nucleotide is made up of three components —

Phosphate group—

  1. Phosphoric acid (in DNA)
  2. Orthophosphate group (in RNA)

Pentose sugar

  1. Deoxyribose (in DNA)
  2. Ribose (in RNA)

Nitrogenous base

  1. Purine (Adenine, Guanine)
  2. Pyrimidine (Cytosine, Thymine – in DNA, Uracil – in RNA)
What is a nucleic acid? How many types are there?

Nucleic acids are complex biomolecules made up of nucleotides, hence they are also called polynucleotides. They store and transfer genetic information in organisms.

There are two types – DNA and RNA

Functions:

  1. Storage and transfer of genetic information (in DNA)
  2. Protein synthesis (in RNA)
What is DNA? Write its chemical composition and functions. 2018

It is a double helix nucleic acid. It is the genetic material in all organisms. It is called the blueprint of life.

The full form of DNA is deoxyribonucleic acid.

Chemical composition of DNA

  1. Phosphoric acid
  2. Deoxyribose sugar
  3. Nitrogenous bases (A, G, C, T)

Functions of DNA

  1. It transfers traits from generation to generation.
  2. It stores the information for protein synthesis in the form of a code.
What is RNA? Write its full form.

It is a single-stranded nucleic acid. It carries out protein synthesis in all eukaryotes.

Full form of RNA – ribonucleic acid

Chemical composition of RNA

  1. Orthophosphate group
  2. Ribose sugar
  3. Nitrogenous bases (A, G, C, U)

The main function of RNA — protein synthesis in organisms.

What is a vitamin?

Vitamins are organic compounds that are needed in very small amounts for the normal growth, proper functioning and disease prevention of the body. They do not provide energy but are necessary for metabolism.

What role do vitamins play in the human body?

The main roles of vitamins in the human body

  1. Vitamins help in the proper growth of the body, especially essential for the physical and mental growth of children.
  2. Vitamins increase the body's disease resistance, enabling us to fight diseases.
  3. They help in digesting food, absorbing it into the body and using it properly.
  4. Vitamins keep the skin, eyes, bones and teeth strong and healthy.
How many types of vitamins are there?

Vitamins are divided into two classes.

  1. Fat-soluble vitamins such as A/D/E/K
  2. Water-soluble vitamins such as B/C
