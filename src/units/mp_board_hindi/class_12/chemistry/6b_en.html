📘 Chapter — Haloarenes
Question: What are haloarenes?

Aromatic compounds in which a halogen atom is directly attached to the benzene ring are called haloarenes.

Examples:

  • Chlorobenzene — C6H5Cl
  • Bromobenzene — C6H5Br
  • Iodobenzene — C6H5I
Question - What is chlorobenzene?

Answer -

The compound obtained by replacing one hydrogen atom of benzene with a chlorine (Cl) atom is called chlorobenzene.

Molecular formula: C6H5Cl

Question: Write the general methods of preparing chlorobenzene.

1. Halogenation of benzene

When benzene is reacted with chlorine in the presence of FeCl3 catalyst, chlorobenzene is obtained.

\[ C_6H_6 + Cl_2 \xrightarrow{FeCl_3} C_6H_5Cl + HCl \]

2. By the Raschig process

When a mixture of benzene vapour, air and HCl is passed over a CuCl2 catalyst at about 230°C, chlorobenzene is obtained.

\[ C_6H_6+\frac{1}{2}O_2+HCl \xrightarrow[\;230^\circ C\;]{CuCl_2} C_6H_5Cl+H_2O \]

3. From phenol

When phenol is heated with PCl5, chlorobenzene is obtained.

\[ C_6H_5OH+PCl_5 \xrightarrow{\Delta} C_6H_5Cl+POCl_3+HCl \]

4. Hunsdiecker reaction

When silver benzoate is reacted with chlorine, chlorobenzene is obtained.

\[ C_6H_5COOAg+Cl_2 \xrightarrow{CCl_4} C_6H_5Cl+CO_2+AgCl \]

5. Sandmeyer reaction

When benzene diazonium chloride is reacted in the presence of CuCl, chlorobenzene is obtained.

\[ C_6H_5N_2Cl \xrightarrow{CuCl/HCl} C_6H_5Cl+N_2 \]
Question: Write the physical properties of chlorobenzene.
  • It is a colourless to pale yellow liquid with a characteristic odour.
  • It is insoluble in water but soluble in organic solvents such as alcohol, ether, benzene, etc.
  • Its boiling point is about 132°C.
  • Its density is greater than that of water — about 1.106 g/mL.
Question: Write the chemical properties of chlorobenzene.

(A) Nucleophilic substitution reaction

Chlorobenzene reacts with certain nucleophilic reagents (Nu−) to form new compounds.

1. Reaction with aqueous NaOH:

Phenol can be obtained from chlorobenzene at high temperature and pressure.

\[ C_6H_5Cl+NaOH \xrightarrow[\;300\,atm\;]{623\,K} C_6H_5OH+NaCl \]

2. Reaction with CuCN:

Phenyl cyanide is obtained from chlorobenzene.

\[ C_6H_5Cl+CuCN \xrightarrow{\Delta} C_6H_5CN+CuCl \]

3. Reaction with KSH:

Thiophenol is obtained from chlorobenzene.

\[ C_6H_5Cl+KSH \xrightarrow{\Delta} C_6H_5SH+KCl \]

(B) Reduction

Reduction of chlorobenzene gives benzene.

\[ C_6H_5Cl+2[H] \xrightarrow{Ni/Al} C_6H_6+HCl \]

(C) Reaction with metals

1. Reaction with magnesium:

In the presence of dry ether, chlorobenzene reacts with magnesium to form phenylmagnesium chloride.

\[ C_6H_5Cl+Mg \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} C_6H_5MgCl \]

2. Wurtz-Fittig reaction:

When chlorobenzene is heated with methyl chloride and sodium in dry ether, toluene is obtained.

\[ C_6H_5Cl+CH_3Cl+2Na \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} C_6H_5CH_3+2NaCl \]

3. Fittig reaction:

When chlorobenzene is heated with sodium in dry ether, diphenyl is obtained.

\[ 2C_6H_5Cl+2Na \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} C_6H_5-C_6H_5+2NaCl \]

4. Ullmann's diaryl synthesis:

When chlorobenzene is heated with copper powder at about 200°C, diphenyl is obtained.

\[ 2C_6H_5Cl+2Cu \xrightarrow{200^\circ C} C_6H_5-C_6H_5+2CuCl \]
(D) Electrophilic aromatic substitution in chlorobenzene

1. Halogenation

Chlorobenzene reacts with chlorine in the presence of a halogen carrier to form ortho and para dichlorobenzene.

2. Nitration

Chlorobenzene reacts with concentrated nitric acid in the presence of concentrated sulphuric acid to form ortho and para nitrochlorobenzene.

3. Sulphonation

Chlorobenzene reacts with concentrated sulphuric acid to form ortho and para chlorobenzene sulphonic acid.

4. Friedel-Crafts reaction

When chlorobenzene is reacted with methyl chloride in the presence of anhydrous AlCl3, a mixture of ortho and para chlorotoluene is obtained.

Note: The –I effect of chlorine deactivates the ring, but due to the +R effect, substitution in chlorobenzene occurs mainly at the ortho and para positions.
Question: Haloarenes are less reactive than haloalkanes. Why? Explain.

In haloarenes, the lone pair of electrons on the halogen participates in resonance with the benzene ring. As a result, the C–X bond acquires partial double bond character.

Therefore, the C–X bond becomes stronger, and it becomes difficult for a nucleophile (Nu−) to break this bond. Hence, haloarenes are less reactive.

In contrast, no such resonance occurs in haloalkanes. Hence their C–X bond breaks relatively easily, and they readily undergo nucleophilic substitution reactions.

Question: Write the uses of chlorobenzene.

The main uses of chlorobenzene are as follows—

  1. In the manufacture of phenol and aniline.
  2. In the manufacture of DDT.
  3. In the manufacture of picric acid.
