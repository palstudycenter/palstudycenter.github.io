d and f-block elements
What are d-block elements?

Answer: Elements in whose atom the last electron enters the d-orbital are called d-block elements.

These elements are found in the middle part of the periodic table, between s-block and p-block elements.

The general electronic configuration of d-block elements is (n–1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻².

Write any two applications of d-block elements. 2025

Answer - Uses of d-block elements -

  1. Precious metals like gold, platinum, silver, etc., are used to make jewelry.
  2. Silver nitrate is used for making mirrors and as a reagent in laboratories.
Into how many series are d-block elements divided?

d-block elements are divided into four series.

  1. First Transition Series (3d series): Includes elements from atomic number 21 (Sc) to 30 (Zn).
  2. Second Transition Series (4d series): Includes elements from atomic number 39 (Y) to 48 (Cd).
  3. Third Transition Series (5d series): Includes elements from Lanthanum (57) and Hafnium (72) to Mercury (80).
  4. Fourth Transition Series (6d series): Includes elements from Actinium (89) to Lawrencium (103).

Both Cr²⁺ and Mn³⁺ have d⁴ electronic configuration, yet Cr²⁺ is a reducing agent while Mn³⁺ is an oxidizing agent. Why?

Both Cr²⁺ and Mn³⁺ have d⁴ electronic configuration, but -
Cr²⁺ readily loses an electron to form Cr³⁺, which is highly stable due to its half-filled t₂g³ configuration. Hence, it acts as a reducing agent.

Mn³⁺ readily accepts an electron to form Mn²⁺, which is most stable due to its completely half-filled (d⁵) configuration. Hence, it acts as an oxidizing agent.

(i) Atomic radius, (ii) Ionization energy, (iii) Metallic character, (iv) Oxidation states. (2019)

  1. Atomic Radius - Atomic radius decreases from left to right.

Reason - Nuclear charge increases, but shielding decreases as electrons enter d-orbitals.

  1. Ionization Energy - Generally, ionization energy increases.

  2. Metallic Character - Metallic character decreases from left to right.
    Reason - Increase in ionization energy reduces metallic nature.
  3. Oxidation States - Initially (Sc→Mn) oxidation states increase, then (Mn→Zn) decrease.

Reason - Initially, the availability of d-electrons increases, later it decreases.

What are transition elements?

d-block elements whose atoms in common oxidation states have partially filled d-orbitals (d¹ to d⁹) are called transition elements.

Why are d-block elements called transition elements?

d-block elements act as a transition bridge between both s and p blocks. Therefore, they are also called transition elements.

Are all d-block elements transition elements?

No, not all d-block elements are transition elements.

Only those d-block elements are called transition elements in which the (n - 1) d-subshell is partially filled.

Which d-block elements are not transition elements?

The following d-block elements are not transition elements.

  1. Zinc (Zn)
  2. Cadmium (Cd)
  3. Mercury (Hg)
Why are Zinc, Cadmium, and Mercury not transition elements?

Elements in which the (n - 1) d-subshell is partially filled are called transition elements.

Zn, Cd, and Hg have a general electronic configuration of (n – 1)d¹⁰ ns². This means their d-orbitals are completely filled (d¹⁰). Hence, due to the absence of partially filled d-orbitals, they are not transition elements.

Scandium (Sc = 21) and Zinc (Z = 30) are both d-block elements, but Scandium is a transition metal while Zinc is not. Why? (2025)

Scandium (Sc = 21) and Zinc (Z = 30) are both d-block elements.

d-block elements in which the (n - 1) d-subshell is partially filled are called transition elements.

Scandium (Sc = 21) is considered a transition metal because its ion Sc³⁺ has a partially filled d-orbital (3d¹).

However, Zinc (Z = 30) is not considered a transition metal because both its neutral atom and ion Zn²⁺ have completely filled d-orbitals (3d¹⁰).

Why is Mercury in liquid state?

In Mercury (Hg), due to d-covalency and relativistic effects, its electrons are drawn closer to the nucleus. This weakens the metallic bond, and atoms cannot hold each other tightly.

As a result, Mercury (Hg) remains in liquid form even at normal temperatures instead of being solid.

Write the characteristics of transition elements.

Transition elements have the following characteristics:

  1. Transition elements have high melting and boiling points.
  2. Transition elements are paramagnetic.
  3. They form colored compounds.
  4. They usually exhibit variable oxidation states.
  5. Transition elements form alloys.
  6. They act as catalysts in many reactions.
  7. They have the ability to form complex compounds.
Why do transition elements have high melting and boiling points? (2023)

Answer - Transition elements possess both covalent and metallic bonds, due to which the bonding energy in transition elements is very high. Therefore, the melting and boiling points of transition elements are extremely high (above 1000°C).

Why are transition elements paramagnetic?

Answer: Transition elements have unpaired electrons in their d-orbitals. Unpaired electrons generate magnetic moment due to spin motion and are attracted by a magnetic field. Therefore, transition elements are paramagnetic.

Explain the magnetic properties of transition elements based on their electronic configuration. (2015) OR
Which of cuprous and cupric ions is diamagnetic and which is paramagnetic?

Answer - Based on electronic configuration, transition elements mainly exhibit two types of magnetic properties.

(i) Diamagnetism - When all electrons present in a substance are paired, it is diamagnetic.

Example - Cu⁺ (Cuprous ion) - 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶3d¹⁰

In this, all electrons are in a paired state, due to which it is diamagnetic.

(ii) Paramagnetism - When unpaired electrons are present in a substance, it is paramagnetic. A substance with unpaired electrons is paramagnetic.

Example - Cu²⁺ (Cupric ion) -

1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶3d⁹

In this, one unpaired electron is present, due to which it is paramagnetic.

Why do transition metals form colored compounds?

Answer - In transition elements, the (n-1)d orbitals are partially filled. The unpaired electrons present in them absorb the energy of visible light and move to higher energy vacant d-orbitals, leading to d-d transition. Due to this, the light reflected from them is colored instead of white. Therefore, compounds or ions of transition elements are colored.

For example, Cu²⁺ ions are blue.

Why are Cu⁺ compounds colorless, while Cu²⁺ compounds are blue?

Cu⁺ (Cuprous ion = 28) - (1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶3d¹⁰)

In this, all d-orbitals are completely filled, which do not absorb the energy of visible light, and d-d transition does not occur. Due to this, the light reflected from them is white. Hence, Cu⁺ (Cuprous ion) is colorless.

Cu²⁺ (Cupric ion = 27) -

In this, one unpaired electron is present, which absorbs the energy of visible light and moves to higher energy vacant d-orbitals, and d-d transition occurs. Due to this, the light reflected from them is blue instead of white. Hence, Cu²⁺ (Cupric ion) is blue.

TiO₂ is white while TiCl₃ is purple, why?

In TiO₂, titanium is in the +4 oxidation state (Ti⁴⁺ = 18 electrons), with an electronic structure of 3d⁰ 4s⁰. Since the d-orbital is empty, d-d transition is not possible, so it does not absorb visible light and appears white.

In TiCl₃, titanium is in the +3 oxidation state (Ti³⁺ = 19 electrons), with an electronic structure of 3d¹ 4s⁰. Since there is one electron in the d-orbital, d-d transition is possible, which absorbs some part of visible light, so it appears purple.

Explain why cuprous ion is not stable in aqueous solution?

In aqueous solution, two Cu⁺ ions are converted into one Cu²⁺ and Cu (solid copper) through a redox reaction.

2Cu⁺ → Cu²⁺ + Cu (s)

That is, Cu⁺ cannot exist in aqueous solution and is converted to the more stable Cu²⁺.

Why do transition elements exhibit variable oxidation states?

Answer: The energy difference between (n-1)d and ns orbitals in transition elements is very small. Therefore, electrons from both orbitals can participate in bonding. This is why transition elements exhibit various oxidation states.

For example - Mn shows oxidation states from +2 to +7.

Compare the stability of the +2 oxidation state for the elements of the first transition series.

Answer: In the first transition series, the stability of the +2 oxidation state decreases from left to right.

  1. Mn²⁺ is stable due to its half-filled 3d⁵ configuration.
  2. Zn²⁺ is stable due to its completely filled 3d¹⁰ configuration.

Thus, stability depends on half-filled or completely filled d-subshells.

Why does a metal exhibit its highest oxidation state only in oxides or fluorides?

Metals exhibit their highest oxidation state only in oxides and fluorides because O and F can extract the maximum number of electrons from the metal, stabilizing it.

Why does Zn only exhibit the +2 oxidation state?

Answer - The electronic configuration of zinc is [Ar] 3d¹⁰4s².

It has no unpaired electrons in the d-orbital. Therefore, it can only form stable 3d¹⁰ configured Zn²⁺ by losing the two electrons from the 4s² orbital.

Therefore, Zn only exhibits the +2 oxidation state.

Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻

Why do transition elements readily form alloys?

Answer: Transition elements have almost similar atomic sizes and their d-orbitals are partially filled. Therefore, they can easily substitute each other in the crystalline lattice and form homogeneous mixtures. Hence, they readily form alloys.

Transition elements form two types of alloys?

Transition elements form two types of alloys.

  1. Interstitial alloys.
  2. Solid solution alloys.

Why are transition metals good catalysts?

Answer - Transition metals act as good catalysts because -

  1. They have a large surface area.
  2. They can easily change their oxidation states.
  3. They can adsorb reactants on their surface.

This increases the rate of reaction.

For example - In the Haber process for ammonia synthesis, the transition element Fe is used as a catalyst.

Why do transition metals have a tendency to form complex compounds?

Answer - Transition metals form complex compounds because -

  1. Their cation size is small and charge density is high, so they strongly attract ligands.
  2. They have vacant d-orbitals to accept lone pairs of electrons from ligands.

Example [Cu(NH₃)₄]SO₄ → Tetraammine Copper (II) Sulfate

Why do transition elements show high values of enthalpy of atomization? (2025)

Answer - Because their atoms have a large number of unpaired electrons, they exhibit strong interatomic interactions. Therefore, strong bonding between atoms results in high enthalpy of atomization.

What are f-block elements? Write their general electronic configuration and characteristics.

Answer: Elements in which the last electron enters the (n-2)f orbital are called f-block elements.

They are placed separately at the bottom of the periodic table.

The general electronic configuration is (n–2)f¹⁻¹⁴ (n–1)d⁰⁻¹ ns².

Characteristics -

  1. All these elements are rare, so they are also called rare earth metals.
  2. In f-block elements, the atomic radius decreases from left to right. This is called lanthanide and actinide contraction.

Uses of f-block elements -

  1. Used in chemical industries.
  2. Used in nuclear applications.
What are inner transition elements? How many types are there? Explain with reasons.

Answer: f-block elements are called inner transition elements because in them the last electron enters the inner (n-2)f orbital instead of the outer shell.

Their position in the periodic table comes between the transition elements (d-block), but they are shown separately below to maintain the structure of the table.

They are of two types:

  1. Lanthanides (58Ce to 71Lu) → 4f-series
  2. Actinides (90Th to 103Lr) → 5f-series
What are lanthanides? Why are they called lanthanides? Write their electronic configuration and uses.

Answer: Elements from atomic number 58 (Ce) to 71 (Lu) in which electrons fill the 4f-orbital are called lanthanides.

They are called lanthanides because these elements come after Lanthanum (La, Z = 57) and exhibit similar properties.

General electronic configuration: [Xe] 4f¹⁻¹⁴ 5d⁰⁻¹ 6s²

Uses:

  1. For making permanent magnets.
  2. For making alloys.
  3. In TV screens, lamps, and lasers.
  4. As catalysts in petroleum cracking.
What is lanthanide contraction? Write its reason.

Answer: When the ionic and atomic radii continuously decrease with increasing atomic number in the lanthanide series, it is called lanthanide contraction.

Reason for contraction - As the atomic number of lanthanide elements increases, the newly added electron enters the 4f subshell [(n - 2) f] instead of entering the outermost shell.

The shielding effect of 4f electrons is very weak. Due to the charge on the nucleus of the atom, electrons are more attracted towards the nucleus, which contracts the atomic ion.

How does lanthanide contraction affect the properties of lanthanides?

Effects of Lanthanide Contraction –

  1. Difficult separation – Due to similar properties of all lanthanides, it is difficult to separate them.
  2. Similarity in properties – Due to similar radii of Zr and Hf, their properties are also very similar.
  3. Increase in covalency – Due to the small size of M³⁺ ions, covalent character increases.
Why is it difficult to separate lanthanides?

Answer: It is difficult to separate lanthanides because –

  1. Due to lanthanide contraction, their size is almost similar.
  2. Their chemical properties are almost identical.
  3. They generally exhibit the +3 oxidation state.
What are actinides? Why are they called actinides? Write their electronic configuration and uses.

Answer: Elements from atomic number 90 (Th) to 103 (Lr) in which electrons enter the 5f-orbital are called actinides.

They are called actinides because these elements come after Actinium (Ac, Z = 89), the first element of the third transition series, and exhibit similar properties.

The general electronic configuration is [Rn] 5f¹⁻¹⁴ 6d⁰⁻¹ 7s².

  1. Uranium and Plutonium are used as nuclear fuel.
  2. Thorium is used in nuclear power reactors.
  3. For making atomic bombs.
  4. As medicine in cancer treatment.

Q. Actinide contraction is greater from element to element than lanthanide contraction. Why?

Ans. This is due to poor shielding effect by 5f electrons in the actinoids than that of 4f electrons in the lanthanoids.
