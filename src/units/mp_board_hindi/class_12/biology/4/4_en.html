Unit 2(A) - Principles of Inheritance and Variation
Question: What is inheritance?
The process by which traits are transferred from parents to offspring is called inheritance.
Question: What is variation?
The differences found in traits among parents and offspring, or among organisms of the same species, are called variation.
Question: What is genetics?
The branch of biology under which inheritance and variation are studied is called genetics.
Question: Who first used the term "genetics"?
W. Bateson (William Bateson) first used the term "Genetics" in the year 1905.
Question: Who is the father of genetics?
Gregor Johann Mendel is called the father of genetics.
Question: What is a trait (character)?
A heritable feature of an organism that is visible or recognizable is called a trait.

Examples:
  • Height of the plant
  • Colour of the flower
  • Colour of the eyes
Question: What is a gene?
A gene is a specific segment of DNA that controls the genetic information for a particular trait of an organism and is transferred from generation to generation.

Example:
  • The gene controlling height in the pea plant.
Question: Who first used the term "gene"?
Johannsen first used the term "gene" in the year 1909.
Question: What is an allele?
The different forms of the same gene are called alleles.

Example:
  • T = Tall (dominant allele)
  • t = Dwarf (recessive allele)
Question: What are homozygous and heterozygous?
Homozygous:
When both alleles for a trait in an organism are identical, it is called homozygous.

Example:
  • TT (pure tall)
  • tt (pure dwarf)

Heterozygous:
When both alleles for a trait in an organism are different from each other, it is called heterozygous.

Example:
  • Tt (hybrid tall)
Question: Write the difference between homozygous and heterozygous.
Homozygous Heterozygous
Both alleles are identical. Both alleles are different from each other.
This is a pure state. This is a hybrid state.
Can be either dominant or recessive type. It has one dominant and one recessive allele.
Example: TT, tt Example: Tt
Question: What is genotype?
The genetic makeup (combination of alleles) of the genes of an organism is called the genotype. It is not directly visible, but it determines the traits of the organism.

Example:
  • TT, Tt, and tt are genotypes for plant height.
Question: What is phenotype?
The external, visible traits of an organism, which appear due to the combined effect of the genotype and the environment, are called the phenotype.

Example:
  • Tall plant, dwarf plant — are phenotypes.
Question: Write the difference between genotype and phenotype.
Genotype Phenotype
It is the genetic makeup (combination of alleles) of the organism. It is the external, visible traits of the organism.
It is not directly visible. It is directly visible.
It depends only on the genes. It depends on both the genotype and the environment.
Example: TT, Tt, tt Example: Tall plant, dwarf plant
Question: What is a gamete?
Sex cells in which the number of chromosomes is halved and which take part in fertilisation are called gametes.

Example:
  • Male gamete — sperm
  • Female gamete — ovum (egg cell)
Question: What is hybridisation (crossing)? How many types are there?
When controlled fertilisation is carried out between organisms having two different traits, this process is called crossing (hybridisation). The offspring produced from a cross are called hybrids.

Example:
  • Tall plant × dwarf plant

Types of crosses —

1. Monohybrid Cross
When a cross is carried out on the basis of only one trait (one pair of contrasting traits), it is called a monohybrid cross.

Example:
  • TT (tall) × tt (dwarf)

2. Dihybrid Cross
When a cross is carried out on the basis of two pairs of contrasting traits, it is called a dihybrid cross.

Example:
  • RRYY (round, yellow seeds) × rryy (wrinkled, green seeds)

3. Back Cross
When the hybrid of the first filial generation (F₁) is crossed with either one of its parents (dominant or recessive), it is called a back cross.

Example:
  • Tt × TT
  • Tt × tt

4. Test Cross
When the hybrid of the first filial generation (F₁) is crossed with its recessive parent (tt), it is called a test cross.

Example:
  • Tt × tt
Question: What are dominant traits and recessive traits?
Dominant Trait:
A trait that expresses itself even in the hybrid state (such as Tt) is called a dominant trait.

Recessive Trait:
A trait that does not express itself in the hybrid state and appears only in the homozygous state (such as tt) is called a recessive trait.

Example:
  • Pea plants with the Tt genotype are tall.
  • Therefore T (tall) is the dominant trait and t (dwarf) is the recessive trait.
Question: Write the difference between dominant and recessive traits.
Dominant Trait Recessive Trait
It expresses itself in both the hybrid (Tt) and homozygous (TT) states. It expresses itself only in the homozygous (tt) state.
It is represented by a capital letter. It is represented by a small letter.
It suppresses the effect of the recessive trait. It does not express itself in the presence of the dominant trait.
Example: T (tall) Example: t (dwarf)
Question: Give a brief introduction to Mendelism.
  • Gregor Johann Mendel was born on 22 July 1822 in the town of Brno in Austria.
  • He belonged to an ordinary farming family.
  • He later became a priest at a church in Brno.
  • He carried out experiments on pea plants (Pisum sativum) for about 7 years, from 1856 to 1863.
  • In the year 1865, he presented his research findings to the Natural History Society of Brno.
  • He passed away in 1884.
  • About 16 years after his death, in 1900, Hugo de Vries, Carl Correns, and Erich von Tschermak rediscovered his laws.
  • These laws are collectively called Mendelism.
Question: Why did Mendel choose the pea plant for his experiments?
Mendel chose the pea plant for the following reasons —
  • Pea is an annual plant, so several generations can be studied in a short time.
  • It is easy to grow, and more plants can be grown in less space.
  • It produces a large number of flowers and seeds.
  • Its flowers are bisexual, so it generally undergoes self-pollination.
  • It has many clearly contrasting traits.
  • Artificial cross-pollination is also easy to carry out in it.
Question: What were the reasons for Mendel's success?
  • He selected pure-bred (true-breeding) plants.
  • He studied only one trait at a time.
  • He carried out experiments over many generations.
  • He carefully recorded and statistically analysed the results obtained.
Question: What are Mendel's laws of inheritance?
Mendel's three laws of inheritance are —
  1. Law of Dominance
  2. Law of Segregation
  3. Law of Independent Assortment
Question: Explain Mendel's Law of Dominance.
Law of Dominance:
According to this law, when two pure-bred organisms with a pair of contrasting traits are crossed, only the dominant trait appears in the first filial generation (F₁), while the recessive trait remains suppressed.

Example:
A pure tall pea plant (TT) is crossed with a pure dwarf plant (tt).

P generation:
TT (tall) × tt (dwarf)

F₁ generation:
  • Genotype: Tt
  • Phenotype: 100% tall plants
This proves that tallness (T) is the dominant trait and dwarfness (t) is the recessive trait.
Question: Write Mendel's Law of Segregation with an example.
Law of Segregation:
According to this law, the two alleles of a trait separate (segregate) from each other at the time of gamete formation, so that only one allele goes into each gamete. At the time of fertilisation, these alleles pair up again.

Example:
When the hybrid plant of the F₁ generation (Tt) is self-pollinated, both tall and dwarf plants are obtained in the F₂ generation.
  • Genotypic ratio: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt
  • Phenotypic ratio: 3 tall : 1 dwarf
Question: Write Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment with an example.
Law of Independent Assortment:
According to this law, when a cross is made involving two or more traits, the alleles of each trait segregate into the gametes independently of one another. Hence, the inheritance of one trait does not affect the inheritance of another trait.

Example:
In a dihybrid cross of seed shape (round/wrinkled) and seed colour (yellow/green) in the pea plant, both traits are inherited independently.
Question: Write the deviations from Mendelism.
Not all inheritance in organisms fully follows Mendel's laws. Such exceptions are called Non-Mendelian inheritance.

Its main examples are —
  • Incomplete Dominance
  • Codominance
Question: Write the law of incomplete dominance.
Incomplete dominance was discovered by Carl Correns in the four o'clock plant (Mirabilis jalapa).

When pure plants with two contrasting traits are crossed, in the first generation (F₁) neither the dominant trait nor the recessive trait is fully expressed; instead, an intermediate trait between the two is seen.
Question: Write the law of codominance.
When both alleles in a hybrid organism express their effect equally and simultaneously, this phenomenon is called codominance.
Question: What is linkage?
When two or more genes are located close to each other on the same chromosome and are inherited together instead of showing independent assortment, this phenomenon is called linkage.
Question: What is sex determination?
The process by which the sex (male or female) of an organism's offspring is determined is called sex determination.
Question: How does sex determination occur in humans?
In humans, sex determination occurs through the X and Y sex chromosomes.
  • The chromosome combination of a female is XX.
  • The chromosome combination of a male is XY.
  • The ovum (egg cell) contains only the X chromosome.
  • The sperm contains either the X or the Y chromosome.
  • Fertilisation by an X-bearing sperm produces XX (daughter), and fertilisation by a Y-bearing sperm produces XY (son).
Question: What is the basis of sex determination in humans?
The basis of sex determination in humans is the X and Y sex chromosomes.
Question: Who is responsible for the birth of a son or a daughter in humans?
In humans, the father is responsible for the birth of a son or a daughter, because his sperm contain both X and Y types of sex chromosomes, whereas all the mother's ova contain only the X chromosome.
Question: What is the probability of having a son or a daughter in humans?
In humans, the probability of having a son or a daughter is 50% : 50%.
Question: Write the chromosomal theory of sex determination in humans.
According to the chromosomal theory, sex determination in humans depends on whether the sperm carries the X or the Y chromosome at the time of fertilisation. If an X-bearing sperm unites with the ovum, XX (daughter) is produced, and if a Y-bearing sperm unites with it, XY (son) is born.
