📘 7-Human Health and Disease
Question: What is meant by health?
Answer:

According to the W.H.O., a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being of a human being is called health.

Question: What is meant by disease? How many types of diseases are there?
Answer:

Any abnormal change or weakness produced in the normal state of the body is called a disease.

Types of diseases — Diseases occurring in humans can be divided into two categories.

  1. Congenital diseases: Diseases that are present in a human being from birth are called congenital diseases. These diseases can occur due to the following causes—
    • (i) Due to mutation in a gene; e.g., haemophilia, colour blindness, sickle cell anaemia, etc.
    • (ii) Due to chromosomal abnormalities; e.g., Down syndrome, Turner syndrome, Klinefelter syndrome.
    • (iii) Due to environmental factors; e.g., cleft lip, cleft palate.
  2. Acquired diseases: These diseases are not present from birth but arise due to some cause after birth; they are called acquired diseases. Acquired diseases are of two types—
    • (i) Infectious or communicable: Diseases that spread from one person to another are called infectious diseases. E.g., malaria, typhoid, etc. Infectious diseases can be divided into two types—
      • (a) Contagious diseases: Diseases that spread through bodily contact. E.g., ringworm, smallpox, measles, leprosy, etc.
      • (b) Non-contagious diseases: Diseases that spread through physical agents (air, water, food) and vectors. Examples — cholera, malaria, plague, hepatitis, etc.
    • (ii) Non-infectious or non-communicable diseases: Diseases that do not spread from one person to another are called non-infectious diseases. E.g., night blindness and cancer. Non-infectious diseases are mainly of seven types—
      • (a) Degenerative diseases: These diseases occur due to important organs not functioning properly. E.g., epilepsy, heart disease, etc.
      • (b) Deficiency diseases: These diseases occur due to a deficiency of nutrients, vitamins, minerals or hormones. Examples — diabetes, beriberi, goitre, etc.
      • (c) Allergies: When the body becomes hypersensitive to certain foreign substances, allergies occur.
      • (d) Cancer: This is caused by abnormal growth of tissues and is called cancer.
      • (e) Mental disorders: Diseases arising due to mental stress are called mental disorders. Examples — depression, headache, insanity, weakening of memory, etc.
      • (f) Diseases caused by environmental factors: Many diseases arise due to ultraviolet rays, air pollutants, noise, etc. present in the environment. E.g., salt deficiency in the body during the summer season.
      • (g) Diseases caused by bad habits: Many diseases also arise due to the consumption of intoxicants such as smoking, gutkha, tobacco, etc., and an unbalanced lifestyle. Examples — obesity, liver-related diseases, mental disorders.

Question: What is AIDS? Write the name of its pathogen and its symptoms.
Answer:

The full form of AIDS is “Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome”, which means a deficiency in a person's immunity to fight diseases.

This disease is caused by HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus), which damages the body's immune system.

Symptoms of AIDS:

  1. Repeatedly falling prey to common diseases.
  2. Continuous fever in the patient.
  3. Diarrhoea.
  4. Gradual loss of body weight.
  5. Decline in the body's immune capacity.
Question: What are the various routes by which the Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) is transmitted?
Answer:

HIV infection can occur in the following ways—

  1. Through sexual contact with an infected (AIDS-afflicted) person.
  2. Through contaminated blood transfusion and blood products.
  3. Through contaminated intravenous syringes or sharing syringes among drug users.
  4. From an infected pregnant mother to her unborn child.
Question: Write the name of the test used to confirm AIDS.
Answer:
  1. ELISA (Enzyme Linked Immuno Sorbent Assay)
  2. PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)
Question: What is the mechanism by which the AIDS virus (HIV) causes decline of the immune system in an infected person?
Answer:

The causative virus of AIDS is HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus). This virus mainly attacks the body's immune cells — CD4+ T-lymphocytes (T-helper cells), macrophages and dendritic cells.

Mechanism:

  1. HIV enters the cell by binding to the CD4 receptor and a co-receptor (CCR5 or CXCR4) through its surface protein.
  2. On entering the cell, HIV's RNA is converted into DNA by the enzyme reverse transcriptase.
  3. This viral DNA gets integrated into the DNA of the host cell.
  4. Infected CD4+ T-cells are gradually destroyed.
  5. As a result, the body's immune capacity declines and the patient becomes prone even to ordinary infections and cancer.
Question: How is AIDS treated?
OR
How will you prevent HIV?
Answer:

To date, there is no effective cure for AIDS. Hence prevention is the best way to avoid it.

  1. Through safe sexual intercourse using condoms.
  2. By testing blood for HIV before transfusion.
  3. By spreading awareness about AIDS.
  4. By avoiding sexual contact with an infected person.
  5. Staying away from drugs, etc. are ways to avoid it.

AIDS is not a disease that occurs by chance; hence it can be avoided by remaining cautious.

Question: What is influenza? Write the name of its pathogen, its transmission, and its prevention and treatment.
Answer:

This is a viral disease.

Pathogen: Orthomyxovirus.

Transmission: Through droplets released when an infected person coughs or sneezes.

Symptoms: Chills, fever, coughing, sneezing, etc.

Prevention and treatment:

  1. Injections of penicillin and streptopenicillin reduce the severity of this disease.
  2. Cleaning the infected patient's clothes with boiling water.
  3. Using the drug amantadine for its treatment.
  4. A wet cloth compress should be used when the fever is very high.
Question: What is hepatitis? Write the name of its pathogen, its transmission, and its prevention and treatment.
Answer:

This is a viral disease that causes inflammation in the human liver. It is caused by a special type of virus called the hepatitis virus.

Hepatitis is of five types — hepatitis A, B, C, D, E.

Transmission: The viruses of hepatitis A and E usually enter through the mouth, while hepatitis B and D occur due to the following causes—

  1. Through contaminated blood transfusion.
  2. From an infected mother to the foetus through the placenta.
  3. Through sexual contact with an infected partner.
  4. Hepatitis B usually spreads through contaminated blood transfusion.

Symptoms: Vomiting, enlargement of the liver, fever, etc.

Tests for hepatitis:

  1. SBT (Serum Bilirubin Test)
  2. SGPT (Serum Glutamic Pyruvic Transaminase)
  3. ELISA (Enzyme Linked Immuno Sorbent Assay)

Prevention and treatment:

  1. One should eat food high in carbohydrates and low in protein and fat.
  2. Use a condom during sexual intercourse.
  3. Use of interferon drugs.
  4. Getting vaccinated against hepatitis.
Question: What is the common cold? Write the name of its pathogen, its transmission, and its prevention and treatment.
Answer:

This is a viral disease.

Pathogen: Rhinovirus.

Transmission: Through droplets released when an infected person sneezes and coughs, and through objects used by an infected person.

Symptoms: Sneezing, coughing, sore throat, blocked nose, difficulty in breathing, etc.

Prevention and treatment:

  1. Attention should be paid to personal hygiene.
  2. Hands should be washed regularly.
  3. Use of medicines like Aspirin and Antihistamines.
  4. A nasal spray should be used.
Question: What is dengue fever? Write the name of its pathogen, its transmission, and its prevention and treatment.
Answer:

This is a viral disease.

Pathogen: Dengue virus.

Transmission: This disease is spread by a species of mosquito, Aedes aegypti.

Symptoms: High fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, loss of appetite, nausea, muscle pain, etc.

Tests:

  1. ELISA
  2. Haemagglutination (HI test)
  3. P.C.R

Prevention and treatment:

  1. The most important measure to prevent dengue is eliminating mosquito breeding sites.
  2. Spraying the insecticide DDT in dark and damp places.
  3. Besides these, mesh screens should be fitted on doors and windows so that mosquitoes cannot enter the house.

There is no specific antibiotic drug for dengue fever. Paracetamol can be taken during fever.

Question: What is rabies or hydrophobia? Write the name of its pathogen, its transmission, and its prevention and treatment.
Answer:

This is a viral disease.

Pathogen: This disease is caused by the rhabdovirus. Rabies is a serious disease of the central nervous system.

Transmission: This disease is usually transmitted by the bite of a dog or monkey. The virus is present in the saliva of these animals, and infection to another person occurs through the saliva.

Symptoms:

  1. As soon as the virus enters the body, it first causes excitement in the body, and later destroys the cells of the brain and the spinal cord.
  2. Severe pain in the head is a special symptom of this disease. High fever and spasms in the muscles of the throat and chest occur in the body, causing distress to the patient.

Prevention and treatment:

  1. To prevent rabies, puppies and kittens should be vaccinated.
  2. A vaccine should be administered after the bite of an infected dog. Earlier 14 doses of this vaccine were given, but currently only 6 doses are given.
Question: What is swine flu? Write the name of its pathogen, its transmission, and its prevention and treatment.
Answer:

This is a viral disease.

Pathogen: This is a viral infectious disease caused by the H1N1 virus.

Transmission: Swine flu or respiratory influenza is a respiratory disease. Its virus infects the respiratory tract of pigs, as a result of which cough and phlegm form in pigs. Particles of the cough and phlegm enter the human body through respiration.

Symptoms:

  1. The patient has a fever of 100°F or more. Shivering and headache occur in the body.
  2. Appetite decreases. The incubation period of this disease is about 1–4 days.

Prevention and treatment:

  1. Vaccination is the best way to prevent or reduce infection with this disease.
  2. Two medicines — zanamivir or Relenza, and oseltamivir or Tamiflu — are given to the patient for treatment of this disease.
Question: Humans develop resistance following infection by viral diseases, but why does the common cold occur repeatedly even though it is also caused by a virus?
Answer:

In most viral diseases (such as smallpox, measles, etc.), antibodies and memory cells against that particular virus form in the body after infection. These immune cells act quickly on re-infection and protect against the disease.

But in the case of the common cold, it is caused by rhinovirus and some other viruses. These viruses have hundreds of different strains. When infection occurs with one type of virus, the body develops resistance only against that type. The next time, a different type of rhinovirus may enter the body, against which the previously formed antibodies are not effective.

For this reason, the common cold occurs repeatedly, whereas in other viral diseases, resistance is generally acquired for life after one occurrence.

Question: What are bacterial human diseases? Write their names.
Answer:

Many diseases occur in humans due to infection by various types of bacteria present in food, water, air and soil; these are called bacterial diseases. They are as follows—

  1. Diseases caused by bacteria present in water and food. E.g., enteric fever or typhoid.
  2. Diseases caused by airborne bacteria or bacteria present in air. E.g., pneumonia, whooping cough and tuberculosis.
  3. Diseases caused by bacteria present in soil. E.g., tetanus.

(a) Enteric fever or Typhoid:

Pathogen: The pathogen of this disease is the bacterium Salmonella typhi present in food; it is also called continued fever.

Transmission: This disease spreads through contaminated food and water and through the patient's faeces, urine and saliva. Flies play a special role in spreading this disease. They carry bacteria from faeces and contaminate domestic food items. These bacteria, along with food, reach the intestine and damage the intestinal wall.

Symptoms: The patient has body ache and headache. There is also fever up to 101°F, but the pulse rate is relatively slow. In the first week of this fever, which lasts about three weeks, there is fever and headache, and in the second week the patient's fever rises unusually, resulting in a crust forming on the lips and tongue. In severe cases, there can also be bleeding from the intestine.

Prevention and treatment:

  1. Typhoid is tested using the Widal test.
  2. Infants should be given the TAB vaccine.
  3. Cleanliness should be maintained around oneself.
  4. Water should be boiled before drinking.
Question: What measures would you adopt to prevent waterborne diseases?
Answer:

Waterborne diseases such as amoebiasis, cholera, dysentery, typhoid, hepatitis-A, etc. mainly spread through contaminated water. To prevent these, we should adopt the following measures—

1. Purifying water

  1. Drinking water should always be used after boiling, filtering, or purifying with chlorine/a filter.
  2. Water tanks should be cleaned periodically.

2. Maintaining hygiene

  1. Wash hands with soap before eating and after using the toilet.
  2. Do not defecate in the open. Clean toilets regularly.

Question: What is pneumonia? Write the name of its pathogen, its transmission, and its prevention and treatment.

Symptoms: Sudden coldness of the body, chest pain, cough with grey mucous sputum, rise in body temperature, cold, etc.

Question: What is tuberculosis (TB)? Write the name of its pathogen, its transmission, and its prevention and treatment.

Pathogen: Mycobacterium tuberculosis bacterium.

Transmission: Through the spitting, sneezing and coughing of an infected person, and through the food and water of an infected person.

Question: What is tetanus? Write the name of its pathogen, its transmission, and its prevention and treatment.
Answer:

Pathogen: Clostridium tetani bacterium.

Transmission: This bacterium usually enters the body through wounds on the skin.

Symptoms: There is pain in all the joints of the body, resulting in muscle stiffness. Most of the pain occurs in the jaw and neck.

Prevention and treatment:

Question: How does the infection of amoebiasis occur?
Answer:

Amoebiasis is caused by infection with a parasite called Entamoeba histolytica. This disease usually affects the intestines.

Transmission:

  1. By ingesting cysts, this parasite enters the body through impure water, contaminated food or dirty hands.
  2. Its infective forms become active on reaching the intestines (faecal-oral route).
  3. Cysts present in the faeces of an infected person reach a healthy person through flies, filth, or contaminated water/food.
  4. Growth in the intestine — on reaching the intestine, these cysts transform into trophozoites.
  5. The trophozoites damage the inner lining of the intestine, causing problems such as diarrhoea, bloody diarrhoea, and abdominal pain.
Question: Write the name of the causative agent that produces malaria in humans. Also write the main symptoms of this disease and the method of its transmission.
Answer:

Until the 19th century, people believed that malaria was caused by the foul, damp air arising from marshy areas. It was named malaria (mal-aria, meaning "bad air") for this reason.

Pathogen: This disease is caused by the unicellular protozoan parasite Plasmodium.

Transmission: The vector of this disease is the female Anopheles mosquito.

Symptoms: In malaria, before the onset of fever, the patient loses appetite. The mouth becomes dry, and there is pain in the head, muscles and joints, along with nausea.

Prevention and treatment:

  1. Do not allow dirty water to collect around you.
  2. Use mosquito nets to protect against mosquitoes.
  3. Do not let water stagnate in pits nearby.

Life cycle of Plasmodium: When an infected female Anopheles bites a human, Plasmodium enters the human body in the form of sporozoites. Initially, the parasite multiplies in the liver and then attacks the red blood cells, causing the red blood cells to rupture. Along with the rupturing of the red blood cells, a toxic substance called haemozoin is released, which is responsible for the chills and the recurring high fever that appears at intervals of every three to four days.

When a female Anopheles mosquito bites an infected person, the parasites enter its body and their further development takes place there. These parasites multiply extensively in the mosquito and become sporozoites, which accumulate in its salivary glands. When this mosquito bites a human, the sporozoites enter the human body, and thus the process described above begins again.

The malarial parasite requires two hosts — human and mosquito — to complete its life cycle. The female Anopheles is also the vector, i.e., the one that transmits the disease.

For treatment of this disease, antimony compound drugs should be used.

Question: Write the name of the causative agent that produces ascariasis in humans. Also write the main symptoms of this disease and the method of its transmission.

Some diseases in humans, such as taeniasis, ascariasis, filariasis, etc. are caused by helminth worms. Some of the major diseases among these are described below.

Transmission: The eggs of Ascaris are present in the patient's intestine and come out with the faeces. These contaminate soil, water, plants and the milk of domestic animals. Question: Write the name of the causative agent that produces elephantiasis (filariasis) in humans. Also write the main symptoms of this disease and the method of its transmission.

Question: What is cancer? Give a brief description of the methods of identification, diagnosis and treatment of cancer.
Answer:

Abnormal and uncontrolled division of cells is called cancer.

Cancer cells invade and destroy the surrounding tissues. These cells grow abnormally and gradually take the form of lumps, resulting in the formation of a tumour or neoplasm.

Causes of cancer: There are many factors that develop cancer; these factors can be physical, chemical, radiation-related and biological.

Symptoms:

  1. Sudden growth in a wart or mole.
  2. Persistent indigestion or difficulty in swallowing.
  3. Unnecessary bleeding.
  4. Thickening and sudden growth in the breasts.

Identification of cancer: In cervical cancer, breast cancer and intestinal cancer, screening tests can identify the disease even at early stages. In most cancers, examination is done only after symptoms have appeared.

The main diagnostic techniques are as follows—

  1. Cervical smear test.
  2. Histopathological test.
  3. X-ray, CT scan, MRI, Positron Emission Tomography (PET).
  4. Chemical tests — testing of antigens/antibodies.
  5. Direct examination by endoscope.

Treatment of cancer: The following methods are used for the treatment of cancer—

  1. Surgery: Removing the cancer-affected part surgically is still the primary method of cancer treatment.
  2. Radiation therapy: In this method, cancer cells are targeted and destroyed using X-rays or other sources of radioactivity.
  3. Chemical therapy or chemotherapy: Certain chemicals are used to destroy cancer cells and inhibit their growth. This therapy also has many side effects.
Question: What is meant by metastasis? Explain. (VIMP)
Answer:

Metastasis is the process in cancer cells in which some cells break away from the primary (original) tumour and reach other organs of the body through blood or lymph, forming new tumours there. This is the most dangerous characteristic of cancer, as it can cause cancer to spread throughout the body.

Answer:

Opioids:

These are depressant drugs that slow down the functions of our body. They act through our digestive system by binding to opioid receptors present in the central nervous system and slowing down their activity. Morphine is useful in surgery. They act as painkillers.

Cannabinoids:

These are also depressants, taken up by cannabinoid receptors present in our brain. They usually affect the cardiovascular system.

Plant source: These are obtained from cannabis, botanical name Cannabis sativa, from the inflorescence, bracts and resin of the plant.

Manufacturing process: Since these are a group of certain chemical compounds, the inflorescence, bracts, resin, and flower tops of Cannabis sativa are mixed in different proportions to make new combinations, producing marijuana, charas, ganja and bhang. Coca alkaloids:

This is also known as cocaine or coca. It is a stimulant that acts by stimulating the central nervous system. It blocks the transport of the neurotransmitter dopamine. It is taken by inhaling.

Nicotine:

Humans have been using tobacco for more than the last 400 years. It is used in the form of bidis, cigarettes, hookahs, etc., by chewing its leaves, or by grinding it well and inhaling it through the nose.

Tobacco contains nicotine, which stimulates the adrenal gland, causing this gland to increase the secretion of the hormones adrenaline and noradrenaline, which increases excitement, blood pressure, respiratory rate and heart rate.

Question: What is meant by immunity?
Answer:

The ability of the body by which it recognises foreign substances and neutralises, expels or metabolises them without causing damage to its own tissues is called immunity.

Immunity is of two types—

  1. Innate immunity
  2. Acquired immunity

(1) Innate immunity: Immunity that is obtained by offspring from their parents is called innate immunity. There are four types of barriers in innate immunity—

  1. Physical barrier: The skin on our body is the main barrier, which prevents the entry of microorganisms.
  2. Physiological barrier: Acid in the stomach, saliva in the mouth, tears in the eyes, etc., all prevent the growth of pathogens.
  3. Cellular barrier: Leucocytes and lymphocytes in the blood of our body engulf pathogens and destroy them.
  4. Cytokine barrier: Virus-infected cells secrete proteins called interferons, which protect uninfected cells from further viral infection.

(2) Acquired immunity: Immunity obtained after birth is called acquired immunity. This immunity is antigen-specific. Its main characteristic is memory. When a pathogen enters the body for the first time, the response shown by the immune system by forming antibodies is called the primary response.

Memory cells are formed during this response. Hence, when the same pathogen enters the body again, the immune system, with the help of previously formed memory cells, produces a larger quantity of antibodies and mounts a more intense response, which is called the secondary immune response.

Question: What is meant by Active Immunity and Passive Immunity?
(Note: In the source, no explicit question sentence was available before this section; a title has been added for structural purposes.)
Answer:

(1) Active Immunity: When antibodies are formed in the body upon entry of a pathogen or antigen, this type of immunity is called active immunity. These antibodies are formed slowly and take time to become effective.

Natural active immunity is obtained when an infectious organism enters the body through natural infection, and artificial active immunity is obtained when a pathogen or antigen is deliberately introduced into the body through vaccination.

(2) Passive Immunity: Passive immunity is obtained when previously formed antibodies are introduced into the body for its protection. This immunity becomes effective quickly, but it is effective only for a short duration.

During pregnancy, the foetus receives IgG from the mother through the placenta, and during lactation, the mother provides IgA to the infant through colostrum; these are examples of natural passive immunity. Antivenom/antitoxin, given for the treatment of snake bites or fatal diseases such as tetanus, rabies and diphtheria, produces artificial passive immunity.

Question: What is meant by the immune system in the body?
Answer:

All the structures related to disease-resisting capacity are collectively called the immune system. This includes lymphoid organs, tissues, cells and antibodies, etc.

Question: What are lymphoid organs?
Answer:

Organs related to the origin and/or maturation and proliferation of lymphocytes are called lymphoid organs.

  1. Primary lymphoid organs: Organs where immature lymphocytes differentiate into antigen-sensitive lymphocytes are called primary lymphoid organs.

    Examples — bone marrow and thymus.

  2. Secondary lymphoid organs: Organs where lymphocytes interact with the antigen and undergo proliferation and activation are called secondary lymphoid organs.

    Examples — spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches, and appendix. The development and maturation of T-lymphocytes occurs in the bone marrow and thymus.

Question: What is meant by antigen, antibiotic and interferon?
Answer:

Antigen: Foreign substances that activate the immune system upon entering the body are called antigens. These can be bacteria, viruses, toxins or parasites, etc. Antigens induce the formation of antibodies in the body.

Antibiotic: Antibiotics are chemical substances produced by microorganisms that destroy other harmful bacteria or inhibit their growth. They are used in the treatment of bacterial diseases. Examples — penicillin, tetracycline, etc.

Interferon: Interferon is a type of protein produced by virus-infected cells of the body. It functions to protect the surrounding healthy cells from viral infection. Hence interferon is a natural antiviral substance.

Question: Write the difference between antibiotics and interferon.
Answer:
No. Antibiotic Interferon
1. Antibiotics are chemical substances produced by microorganisms (mainly bacteria). Interferons are protein substances produced by the cells of the body.
2. These are effective against bacterial diseases. These are effective against viral diseases.
3. These are given to the body externally as a medicine. These are produced naturally within the body.
4. These were discovered by Alexander Fleming. These were discovered by Isaacs and Lindenmann.
5. Example — penicillin, tetracycline. Example — interferon-α, interferon-β.
Question: What is meant by Vaccination and Immunisation?
Answer:

The process of introducing a very small quantity of an inactivated, attenuated or dead microorganism, or a toxin produced by it, into the body is called vaccination, and the substance introduced into the body is called a vaccine.

A vaccine contains dead, weakened or cultured antigens (pathogens). When a person is vaccinated, these inactivated antigens induce the formation of antibodies in that person's body. These antibodies protect the body from infection for a long time (from 6 months to a lifetime).

The principle of vaccination is based on the property of memory of the immune system. Vaccination also produces memory B and T cells, which help destroy pathogens by quickly recognising them and producing antibodies. This type of immunisation falls under active immunisation.

Sometimes antibodies are needed immediately to protect against infection by fatal pathogens. For this, antibodies or antitoxins are directly introduced into the body through a vaccine. For example, the vaccines given for the bite of a poisonous snake or for tetanus contain already-formed antibodies. This type of immunisation is called passive immunisation. Acquiring disease resistance through vaccination is called vaccination.

Question: What is meant by allergy?
(Note: In the source, no explicit question sentence was available before this section; a title has been added for structural purposes.)
Answer:

The hypersensitivity (excessive reaction) displayed by a person upon coming into contact with certain external particles or substances is called an allergy. The term "allergy" was first used in 1905 by Clemens von Pirquet.

Question: What are allergens?
Answer:

The factors due to which a person shows hypersensitivity are called allergens. Such as — dust particles, cold, heat, fur, feathers, perfume smell, cloth, pollen grains, mites, soap, some specific air, sunlight, foul odour, etc.

Symptoms: The causes of allergy are usually different, but generally the skin, mucous membranes and respiratory tract are more affected by it. Sneezing, coughing, runny nose, difficulty in breathing, etc.

Mechanism: First, the allergen acts like an antigen and initiates the formation of antibodies. These antibodies get attached to the mast cells and basophils of connective tissue. IgE immunoglobulin is found in these antibodies.

Treatment: The use of medicines such as antihistamines, adrenaline and steroids reduces the symptoms of allergy, but as a result of the modern lifestyle, immunity in people has decreased and sensitivity to allergens has increased.
