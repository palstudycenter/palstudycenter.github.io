Q: What is ecology?
Answer:

The science in which the mutual relationships between organisms and their environment (biotic and abiotic components) are studied is called ecology.

Q: What is environment?
Answer:

Environment is the entire set of external conditions that surround an organism on all sides and influence its life, development and activities.

Q: What are environmental factors?
Answer:

Those components of the environment that directly or indirectly affect organisms are called environmental factors.

Environmental factors are mainly divided into two groups—

  1. Biotic factors: These include components such as microorganisms, animals and plants.
  2. Abiotic factors: These include components such as soil, water, temperature, light and radiation.
Q: What is an organism?
Answer:

A living component found in the environment is called an organism.

Q: What is a population?
Answer:

A group of organisms of the same species living in a defined geographical area at a given time, that uses the same resources or competes for them, is called a population.

Example: The human population.

Q: What is population ecology?
Answer:

The branch of ecology in which the mutual interactions between groups of the same species and their environment are studied is called population ecology.

Q: Write the characteristics or features of a population.
Answer:

The characteristics of a population are as follows—

  1. Population size and population density
  2. Birth rate (natality)
  3. Death rate (mortality)
  4. Sex ratio
Q: What is meant by population density?
Answer:

The number of organisms found per unit area is called population density.

$$\text{Population density} = \dfrac{\text{Total number of organisms}}{\text{Area}}$$
Q: Write the factors that affect population growth.
OR
Name the basic processes that decrease and increase the population density in a given habitat during a given period. (2025)
Answer:

The factors that affect population growth are as follows—

  1. Birth rate (natality)
  2. Death rate (mortality)
  3. Immigration
  4. Emigration
Q: What is the birth rate (natality) of a population? (2017/18/19)
Answer:

The average number of offspring produced by a population per unit time is called the birth rate (natality).

Q: What is the death rate (mortality) of a population? (2017/18/19)
Answer:

The average number of individuals dying in a population per unit time is called the death rate (mortality). It is a major factor in determining the size of a population.

Q: What is immigration?
Answer:

Immigration is the process in which organisms or individuals come from other places and settle in an area, causing the population of that area to increase.

Q: What is emigration?
Answer:

Emigration is the process in which organisms or individuals leave an area and settle in some other place, causing the population of that area to decrease.

Q: What is an age pyramid?
Answer:

An age pyramid is a graphical representation in which a population is depicted on the basis of its age structure (pre-reproductive, reproductive and post-reproductive age groups).

An age pyramid shows whether a population is growing, stable, or declining.

Q: What is a community?
Answer:

A group of interacting populations of different species (plants, animals, microorganisms) living in a defined area is called a community.

Example: The algae, fish, frogs, insects, etc. found in a pond together form a community.

Q: What is an ecotone?
Answer:

When two communities are located close to each other, a region exists between them in which the populations of both communities are found. This region is called an ecotone (a zone of tension).

Q: What is an ecological system (ecosystem)?
Answer:

A system formed by more than one community together with their abiotic components is called an ecosystem.

Examples:

  1. Pond ecosystem
  2. Forest ecosystem
  3. Grassland ecosystem
Q: What is a biome?
Answer:

A group of more than one similar type of ecosystem is called a biome.

Examples:

  1. Desert biome
  2. Tropical rainforest biome
  3. Tundra biome
Q: What is the biosphere?
Answer:

All the biomes found on Earth, taken together, are called the biosphere.

Example: The entire life-supporting zone of the Earth (the life-bearing part of the land, water and atmosphere).

Q: Where is the troposphere found?
Answer:

The lowest layer of the atmosphere surrounding the Earth is called the troposphere.

It extends on average to a height of about 8 to 16 km from the Earth's surface. This layer contains the life-sustaining gases, and clouds, rain and other weather-related events occur in it.

Q: What is a habitat?
Answer:

The natural place or environment where an organism lives, obtains food, reproduces and completes its life cycle is called a habitat.

For example—

  1. The habitat of a fish is water.
  2. The habitat of a lion is the forest.
Q: What is a niche?
Answer:

The specific activities carried out by an organism within its habitat are called its niche.

For example—

  1. The way it obtains food
  2. Its use of energy
  3. Its relationship with other organisms
Q: Write the difference between habitat and niche.
Answer:
Habitat Niche
1. The natural place where an organism lives. 1. The specific role played by an organism within its habitat.
2. Relates to where the organism lives, its safety and reproduction. 2. Relates to the organism's food, use of energy and relationships with other organisms.
3. It tells where the organism lives. 3. It tells what the organism does.
4. Example: The habitat of a fish — water. 4. Example: The niche of a fish — the role of a consumer.
Q: What are population interactions? How many types are there?
Answer:

When different populations in an ecosystem affect one another positively, negatively, or neutrally, these are called population interactions.

They are of the following types—

  1. Positive interactions
  2. Negative interactions
  3. Neutral interactions
Q: What are positive interactions?
Answer:

In these, organisms or populations help each other, benefiting one or both of them. These are called positive interactions.

Q: What is symbiosis? Explain with examples.
Answer:

A relationship between organisms of two different species in which one or both organisms benefit, and neither organism is harmed, is called symbiosis.

This relationship can be of the following types—

  1. Mutualism: When a relationship exists between organisms of two different species such that both benefit, it is called mutualism. This relationship may be permanent or long-term.
    Example: Lichen — the alga carries out photosynthesis and provides food to the fungus; the fungus provides shelter to the alga.
  2. Commensalism: When a relationship exists between two organisms or populations such that one benefits while the other is neither benefited nor harmed, it is called commensalism. This relationship may be permanent or temporary.
    Example: Orchid plants growing on trees get support and light, while the tree suffers no harm.
  3. Scavenging: Scavenging is the process in which certain organisms obtain food from the bodies of dead organisms. Such organisms are called scavengers.
    Example: Foxes, jackals, hyenas, vultures, etc.
Q: What are negative interactions?
Answer:

When at least one organism is harmed in a mutual relationship between two organisms or populations, these are called negative interactions.

This relationship is of the following types—

  1. Competition: There is rivalry among organisms for space, water, food, minerals and other resources. Both organisms are harmed in this. Competition between organisms of the same species is called intraspecific competition, and competition between organisms of different species is called interspecific competition.
    Example: Crop and weeds.
  2. Predation: This is a temporary mutual relationship between two species, in which an organism of one species kills and eats an organism of another species as food. In this, one organism benefits and the other is harmed.
    Example: Lion–deer.
  3. Antibiosis: When a relationship exists between two organisms such that a substance secreted by one organism inhibits the growth of, or kills, the other organism, it is called antibiosis.
    Example: Antibiotics such as penicillin and streptomycin inhibit the growth of microorganisms.
  4. Parasitism: When an organism obtains its food from another living organism, it is called parasitism. Parasites are of two types—
    • (a) Ectoparasites: Parasites that obtain their food from the outer surface of the host are called ectoparasites.
      Example: Lice and dodder (Cuscuta).
    • (b) Endoparasites: Parasites that live inside the body of their host and obtain nutrition from it, thereby harming the host.
      Example: Tapeworm present in the intestine of humans or animals.
Q: Write the difference between mutualism and commensalism.
Answer:
Mutualism Commensalism
1. Both organisms benefit. 1. Only one organism benefits; the other is neither benefited nor harmed.
2. The relationship may be permanent or long-term. 2. The relationship may be permanent or temporary.
3. Example: Lichen – alga and fungus. 3. Example: Orchid plants growing on trees.
Q: Write the difference between symbiosis and parasitism.
Answer:
Symbiosis (Mutualism) Parasitism
1. In this, two organisms live together such that both benefit and neither is harmed. 1. In this, a smaller organism (the parasite) obtains food from a larger organism (the host).
2. It is a positive interaction. 2. It is a negative interaction.
3. Example: Lichen (alga + fungus). 3. Example: Tapeworm — in the intestine of humans or animals.
