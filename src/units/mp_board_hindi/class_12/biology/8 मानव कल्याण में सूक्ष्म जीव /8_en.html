Q: What is microbiology? (Question Bank)
Answer:
Microbiology is the branch of biology under which microorganisms that are visible only through a microscope are studied.
Q: What are microorganisms?
Answer:
Organisms that can be seen only with the help of a microscope are called microorganisms.

Examples: Bacteria, fungi, protozoans, etc.

Q: How is curd obtained?
Answer:

Lactobacillus bacteria, i.e. lactic acid bacteria (LAB), are used to convert milk into curd. A small amount of lactic acid bacteria is added to lightly warmed milk as inoculum, which slowly converts the milk into curd at 30-40°C. The inoculum contains millions of LAB.

Vitamin B12 is found in curd.

LAB destroys harmful bacteria present in the body.

Q: Write the full form of LAB.
Answer:
The full form of LAB is Lactic Acid Bacteria.
Q: In which food item will you find lactic acid bacteria? Mention some of their useful applications.
Answer:

Curd contains a very large number of lactic acid bacteria (LAB).

Applications of LAB:

  1. The lactic acid produced by LAB coagulates the milk protein, converting milk into curd, and making the milk protein easily digestible.
  2. LAB synthesizes vitamin B12, which enhances the nutritional value of curd.
  3. LAB checks the growth of disease-causing microbes in the human stomach, thus preventing diseases.

Q: How are dosa and idli obtained?
Answer:

In this, the batter of pulses (dal) and rice flour is fermented by bacteria, which releases CO₂ from the batter. This makes the batter light and fluffy, because fermentation is a type of anaerobic respiration in which incomplete oxidation of sugars occurs and CO₂ is released as a by-product. Streptococcus faecalis bacteria are used for this.

Nowadays, ENO is also used instead of bacteria. This too produces CO₂ when added to the batter, causing fermentation and making the batter soft and fluffy.

Q: How is bread obtained?
Answer:

To make bread, the fungus Saccharomyces cerevisiae is added to wheat flour, and the kneaded dough is left to stand for some time, during which anaerobic respiration occurs and leavening (fermentation) takes place, because yeast (fungus) produces the enzymes amylase, maltase or zymase in the dough. The enzyme amylase converts starch into maltose, and maltose into glucose, which makes the dough and bread spongy.

Q: What are antibiotics?
Answer:

Antibiotics are chemical compounds that are obtained from microorganisms and are used to inhibit the growth of microorganisms or to destroy them.

Example: Penicillin, streptomycin, tetracycline.

The term antibiotic was first used by Waksman in 1942.

Use: Antibiotics are used in diseases such as whooping cough, plague, leprosy and diphtheria.

Q: How does antibiotic resistance develop in bacteria?
Answer:

Antibiotics are generally used to treat diseases related to bacteria and fungi. During treatment, the required dose, method of use, and duration of use vary according to the patient and the severity of the disease. Using an antibiotic for a long period causes drug resistance to develop in the bacteria related to that disease. Tuberculosis is the best example of this.

Q: What is sewage?

Q: What is sewage? In what way can sewage be harmful? (2020)(2023)
Answer:

In large cities and towns, human waste from homes and the dirty water flowing in drains is called sewage.

Sewage contains the following types of impurities—

  1. Suspended solids — sand, silt.
  2. Colloidal matter — faecal matter, bacteria.
  3. Cloth fibres and paper.
  4. Dissolved substances — nitrates, phosphates, ammonia, calcium, etc.

Harmful effects of sewage:

  1. Due to the presence of human excreta, it is a source of many kinds of pathogens. Water-borne infectious diseases can spread from a water supply contaminated by sewage.
  2. Open sewage acts as a breeding ground for many disease vectors and insects.
Q: Write the full form of BOD.
Answer:
The full form of BOD is Biochemical Oxygen Demand.
Q: What is biochemical oxygen demand? (2019, 23)
OR
What is BOD?
Answer:
The amount of oxygen required by microorganisms, at a fixed temperature, to decompose the organic matter present in one litre of water is called the biological or biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) of that water. This means that a higher BOD indicates the presence of more organic matter in the water. More organic matter means more polluted water.
Q: The BOD test was conducted on three water samples, namely, river water, untreated sewage, and secondary effluent discharged from a sewage treatment plant. The samples were labelled A, B and C, but the laboratory assistant did not mention which sample was which. The BOD values of the three samples A, B and C were recorded as 20 mg/L, 8 mg/L, and 400 mg/L respectively. Which sample of water is the most polluted? Can you assign the correct label to each sample, assuming that the river water is relatively clean? (NCERT)
Answer:
  1. Sample A has a BOD of 20 mg/L. This is secondary effluent discharged from a sewage treatment plant. Since this is treated water, its BOD will be lower than that of untreated water (it will have a lower amount of organic matter). Most of the organic matter is broken down during sewage treatment.
  2. Sample B has a BOD of 8 mg/L. Hence, it represents river water. This water is cleaner and clearer than both untreated and treated sewage water.
  3. Sample C has a BOD of 400 mg/L. This means it is the most polluted water and has a high amount of organic matter. It is untreated sewage.
Q: Describe the treatment given to sewage before it is discharged into a natural water source. Why is this treatment necessary?
Answer:

Discharging sewage directly into a river or stream is harmful because it has a high organic matter content, which pollutes the water. It contains pathogenic microbes. It contains harmful heavy metals and inorganic compounds.

Sewage pollutes water bodies. Therefore, large sewage treatment plants have been set up in cities to treat sewage and make water free of pollution.

There are two stages of sewage treatment—

  1. Primary treatment — In this, sewage is treated physically. Filtration and sedimentation methods are used to separate large and small solid particles. They are separated in different stages.

Initially, floating debris is removed by sequential filtration. Following this, grit (i.e. small stones and soil particles) is removed by sedimentation.

All the solids that settle down form the Primary sludge, and the floating part is called the primary effluent. The primary effluent is then taken from the primary settling tank for secondary treatment.

  1. Secondary treatment — In this, the effluent is passed through aeration tanks and is agitated by a mechanism. This allows aerobic bacteria to decompose the organic matter present in the effluent,

as a result of which the number of bacteria increases rapidly and they form a mass called flocs.

Fungal filaments join with bacteria to form flocs. In this way, these bacteria rapidly reduce the organic matter, due to which the BOD of the effluent also begins to fall.

BOD is the amount of oxygen that is used by bacteria to decompose the organic matter present in one litre of water.

We can say that the greater the BOD of water, the greater the amount of organic matter in it, and the dirtier (more polluted) that water will be. When the BOD of the effluent falls, the effluent is sent to a sedimentation tank. This sediment is called activated sludge.

A small part of the activated sludge is sent back to the aeration tank, where it acts as inoculum. The remaining activated sludge is passed into an anaerobic tank. This anaerobic tank is called the Anaerobic Sludge Digester. Anaerobic bacteria act to decompose the sludge. Methane, hydrogen sulphide and CO₂ gases are obtained from this decomposition. This produces biogas (gobar gas).

This gas is used for lighting and cooking. In this way, the treated water contains a very small amount of pollutants, and can now be discharged into any river or stream. Hence, our government should set up more such plants in cities and towns.

Q: How is the BOD of primary effluent reduced during its secondary treatment?
Answer:

In secondary treatment, primary effluent water is passed through, and bacteria decompose the organic matter of the effluent water. BOD depends on the water flowing through the aeration tank. During this process, aerobic microorganisms particularly affect the amount of organic matter in the water. Hence, due to the decrease in the amount of organic matter in the water, BOD also decreases.

Q: What is biogas?
Answer:
Biogas is a group of gases which contains 50-70% methane and other gases (CO₂, H₂, H₂S). The source of energy obtained from biomass, in the form of gas, is called biogas.
Q: How is biogas obtained?
Answer:

Biogas is produced from animal dung. The biogas obtained from dung is called gobar gas or biogas.

Waste biomass is anaerobically decomposed by Methanobacterium. The gas obtained from this contains a large amount of methane.

$C_6H_{12}O_6 \xrightarrow{Methanobacterium} 3CH_4 + 3CO_2$
Q: Draw a clean labelled diagram of a biogas plant. (2024)
Q: Give a description of the gobar gas plant, with a diagram.
Answer:

It has the following parts—

  1. Slurry tank — In this, dung is mixed with water in a small tank to make slurry. This slurry is connected to the digester tank by a pipe.
  2. Digester tank — This is a tank that is 10-15 feet deep, whose walls are made of solid bricks. The slurry is stored in it; here, the dung is decomposed by bacteria; this tank is airtight.
  3. Gas holder — The gas formed in the digester tank floats up and collects in the gas holder. This holder has an outlet pipe, through which the gas requirements of nearby homes are fulfilled.
  4. Sludge tank — The dung remaining after decomposition is removed from the tank through a connected pipe. This dung (sludge) is used as manure, while the gas serves as a source of energy.

  3. The by-product obtained from it can be used as manure.
  4. The pollution caused by dung can be eliminated through it.

Q: What are biopesticides? Write the advantages and disadvantages of biopesticides.
Answer:

Organisms or their products that are used to destroy pests or unwanted organisms are called biological pesticides or biopesticides.

Examples:

  1. Neem — This is a medicinally important plant with insect-repellent properties. It has the ability to kill or restrict around 300 species of common pests.
  2. Datura — This is a wild plant; due to the presence of a strong combination of anticholinergic substances in it, it is used as a fungicide and insecticide.

Benefits of biopesticides:

  1. Harmful insects can be destroyed by them, protecting crops.
  2. They do not pollute the environment and are relatively cheap.
  3. They do not harm non-target insects, and can be produced as needed.

Disadvantages of biopesticides (drawbacks):

  1. Their effect is not as quick and dramatic as chemical pesticides.
  2. They cannot fulfil the nutrient requirement of the crop.
  3. They generally cannot be used together with chemical pesticides.

Q: Describe the role of the virus of the genus Nucleopolyhedrovirus in ecologically sensitive areas.

Answer:

Various biofertilizers increase the fertility of soil in the following ways—

  1. Rhizobium bacteria — This bacterium is a symbiont, present in the roots of plants of the family Leguminosae. It fixes atmospheric nitrogen and converts it into an organic molecule, which the plant uses as a nutrient.
  2. Mycorrhiza — This is a symbiotic fungus that is present as a symbiont in the roots of gymnosperm plants. It is of two types.

Endomycorrhiza — This goes inside the epidermis up to the cortex, but remains present outside the endodermis. It goes inside the plant.

  1. Cyanobacteria or blue-green algae — Cyanobacteria such as Anabaena, Nostoc, etc. increase soil fertility by converting free nitrogen into nitrates. This is why cyanobacteria are used in paddy fields.

Biofertilizers can be used in place of chemical fertilizers, and biological control methods can be used in place of chemical pesticides. These biofertilizers and biocontrol agents are an integral part of organic farming and are environment friendly.

  1. The symbiotic bacterium 'Rhizobium', found in the root nodules of leguminous plants, fixes atmospheric nitrogen. The spores of this bacterium are used as a biofertilizer in the soil.

(B) Use of microorganisms as biocontrol agents:

  1. Spores of the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis are used as a biocontrol agent. These spores, available in the market, can be used to control the larvae of many kinds of insects.

The gene responsible for producing the toxic substance (Cry protein) produced by this bacterium has been incorporated into the genome of many crop plants to make them insect-resistant.

  1. The fungus Trichoderma is used to control many kinds of plant pathogens.
  2. Baculoviruses of the genus Nucleopolyhedrovirus are used for the biological control of many kinds of insects and other arthropods. They do not harm any kind of non-target organism, since they are species-specific.

Q: Prove with examples that microorganisms release gases during metabolism. (NCERT)
Answer:

The following examples clearly show that microorganisms release gases during metabolism—

  1. Anaerobic methanogens such as Methanobacterium release gases such as methane, hydrogen sulphide and carbon dioxide during their metabolism (these gases are components of biogas).

Q: Find out the role of microorganisms in the following—
(a) Single cell protein (SCP)
(b) Soil.
Answer:

(a) Role of microorganisms as single cell protein (SCP): Single cell protein is mainly obtained from microorganisms. The biomass obtained from the growth of microorganisms, which is a rich source of nutrients such as protein, vitamins and minerals, is called SCP. Generally, SCP is obtained from the following microorganisms—

  1. Methylophilus methylotrophus — This was the first source of SCP obtained.
  2. Fusarium spp. — Quorn protein.
  3. Arthrospira maxima (available in the market under the name Spirulina) — A rich source of protein.
  4. Chlorella — Green algae.
  5. Edible fungi — Agaricus, Morchella, etc.

(b) Role of microorganisms in soil: Various kinds of microorganisms found in the soil play different roles in the soil.

  1. Decomposers — Many kinds of microorganisms decompose the dead organic matter of the soil, converting it into simple inorganic substances, which can be reused by plants. Hence, decomposers increase the fertility of the soil. In this way, they also assist in biogeochemical cycles. Bacteria, including actinomycetes and fungi, are the main decomposers.
  2. Nitrogen fixers — These organisms fix free nitrogen present in the atmosphere into usable nitrogen (green plants cannot use free nitrogen). The nitrogen fixed by these organisms increases soil fertility. The important nitrogen fixers are—
    Symbiotic Rhizobium bacteria.
    Free-living bacteria — Azotobacter, Azospirillum.
    Blue-green algae — Anabaena, Nostoc.
  3. Nitrifying bacteria — Nitrifying bacteria convert ammonia into nitrate via nitrite. Plants prefer to absorb nitrate.
    Example — Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter.
  4. Denitrifying bacteria — These bacteria convert nitrates and release free nitrogen into the soil; as a result, the fertility of the soil decreases.
    Example — Pseudomonas denitrificans.
  5. Mycorrhiza — Fungi that form mycorrhiza increase soil fertility.
  6. Pathogens — Many kinds of pathogens are also found in the soil. They are responsible for soil-borne diseases in plants.
  7. Rhizosphere — Many microorganisms are found around the roots of plants. Some of these produce growth-promoting substances, while some, such as Trichoderma, protect plants from many fungal pathogens.
