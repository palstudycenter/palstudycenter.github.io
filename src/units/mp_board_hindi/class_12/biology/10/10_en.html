📘 10-Biotechnology and Its Applications
Q: In which fields is biotechnology currently being used?
Answer:

At present, biotechnology is being used in many important fields, such as—

  1. Medicine and healthcare – vaccines, insulin, hormones, antibiotics, gene therapy, diagnostic techniques (PCR)
  2. Agriculture – high-yield and disease-resistant crops, GM crops, tissue culture
  3. Industry – production of enzymes, biofuels, organic acids, medicines
  4. Environmental protection – wastewater treatment, biodegradation, bioremediation
  5. Animal husbandry and fisheries – improved breeds, vaccines, artificial insemination
  6. Forensic science – DNA fingerprinting, identification of criminals
  7. Food and dairy industry – production of curd, cheese, bread, probiotics
Q: Write any two objectives of biotechnology.
Answer:

The objectives of biotechnology are as follows—

  1. The objective of biotechnology is the diagnosis and prevention of incurable diseases through the most modern treatment methods, so that the general public can gain health-related benefits.
  2. To make the most nutritious food products available to the general public through biotechnology.
Q: What is a genetically modified organism (GMO)? Write its benefits and usefulness. (2020/22/23/25)
OR
What is a GMO?
OR
What are transgenic animals?
Answer:

Organisms (plants, animals, or microorganisms) into whose genome a desired gene from some other organism has been inserted by recombinant DNA technology are called genetically modified organisms (GMOs). This change is permanent and can also be transferred to subsequent generations.

Benefits —

  1. Higher yield is obtained in crops.
  2. Resistance to pests and diseases increases, resulting in reduced use of pesticides.
  3. The nutritional value of food increases (such as vitamins, protein).
  4. Production cost is reduced.
  5. Chemical pollution of the environment is reduced.

Usefulness —

  1. In the development of improved and GM crops in agriculture.
  2. In making hormones, vaccines, and medicinal proteins in medicine.
  3. To understand gene action in research.
  4. In the production of enzymes and biofuels in industrial biotechnology.
  5. For bioremediation in environmental protection.
Q: What is meant by transgenics?
Answer:

The process of obtaining a genetically modified organism, or transgenic organism (GMO), is called transgenics.

Q: Write the full form of GMO.
Answer:

The full form of GMO is Genetically Modified Organism.

Q: How is a GMO different from a hybrid?
Answer:

A GMO differs from a hybrid because in a hybrid, the entire genome is crossed, whereas in a GMO, only a few desired foreign genes are present in an organism.

Q: What are genetically modified crops? Write examples of these. (2013, 16)
OR
What are GM crops?
Answer:

Genetically modified (GM) crops are crops whose genes are altered through biotechnology so that they acquire traits such as high yield, pest/disease resistance, or better quality.

These crops are prepared by the transgenesis method with the help of recombinant DNA technology.

Examples: Maize, soybean, tomato, and rapeseed oil.

Q: Write the benefits of GM crops. (2014/15/23)
Answer:

The benefits of genetically modified crops are as follows—

  1. Genetically modified crops produce the desired phenotypic traits in crop plants.
  2. Transgenic crops synthesize products with specific biochemical pathways.
  3. These crops help in preventing the expression of pre-existing genes.
  4. These crops have higher nutritional value.
  5. Post-harvest losses are greatly reduced.
Q: What is genetically modified food?
OR
What is GM food?
Answer:

Genetically modified food is food obtained from plants or animals in whose genome desired changes have been made with the help of biotechnology.

Through these changes, the production capacity, nutritional value, disease/pest resistance, or storage capacity of the food is increased.

Examples:

  1. Golden rice (vitamin-A enriched rice)
  2. Food products obtained from Bt cotton
Q: What is golden rice? (2022, 22/24)
Answer:

Golden rice is a genetically modified (GM) variety of rice into which genes necessary for the production of β-carotene (beta-carotene) have been inserted. β-carotene is a precursor of vitamin A, and it gives the rice grains a golden colour.

Q: How is golden rice useful in preventing blindness in children?
Answer:

A deficiency of vitamin A in children can cause night blindness and permanent blindness. Eating golden rice converts β-carotene into vitamin A in the body. This removes the deficiency of vitamin A and helps in preventing blindness.

Q: What are Bt crops? Explain with an example.
Answer:

Bt crops are genetically modified (GM) crops into which the cry gene of the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) has been inserted. This gene produces an insecticidal protein (Bt toxin), which kills certain specific insects.

Examples:

  1. Bt cotton → protection from the pink bollworm
  2. Bt maize → protection from the stem borer insect

Benefits:

  1. Reduced use of pesticides
  2. Increase in crop production
  3. Safer for the environment
Q: What are cry proteins? Write their uses.
Answer:

Cry proteins are insecticidal proteins that have the ability to kill certain specific insects. These proteins destroy the cells of the insect's gut, causing the death of the insect.

Use - Cry proteins produced by the Bt bacterium are used in Bt crops (such as Bt cotton), which makes the crops insect-resistant.

Q: Write the name of the organism that produces cry proteins.
Answer:

The organism that produces cry proteins is Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt).

Q: Many proteins are secreted in an inactive form. How is this beneficial for the organism producing the toxin?
Answer:

Many proteins and toxins produced by microorganisms are secreted in an inactive form so that they do not harm the producing organism itself. These proteins/toxins become active only under specific environmental conditions, such as a suitable pH, temperature, or the presence of enzymes.

For example, the Bt toxin (cry protein) produced by Bacillus thuringiensis is found in an inactive form within the bacterium. This toxin becomes active only upon entering the alkaline pH of the insect's digestive tract, and it destroys the cells of the insect's gut.

Q: Explain the various stages of the production of human insulin.
OR
How is insulin manufactured artificially?
Answer:

Human insulin is produced by recombinant DNA technology. Its main stages are as follows—

  1. The gene responsible for insulin is isolated from the β-cells of the human pancreas.
  2. A plasmid is extracted from a bacterium, which acts as a vector.
  3. With the help of restriction enzymes, the insulin gene is joined to the plasmid. This forms a recombinant plasmid.
  4. The recombinant plasmid is inserted into a bacterial cell.
  5. The transgenic bacteria are grown in a large bioreactor, where they produce insulin (or its chains).
  6. Insulin is separated from the bacteria and purified.
  7. Active human insulin is prepared by joining the A and B chains.

Insulin made by this method is pure, safe, and extremely effective in the treatment of diabetes.

Q: What is gene therapy? Explain with the example of adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency.
Answer:

Gene therapy is a technique in which a defective gene in a person's cells is removed and replaced with a normal (functional) gene, so that a genetic disease can be treated.

For example, gene therapy in the treatment of ADA deficiency is the first successful gene therapy.

Q: What is ADA?
Answer:

ADA is an enzyme that is necessary for the proper development of the immune system.

Full form of ADA: Adenosine deaminase

Disease caused by ADA deficiency: Due to ADA deficiency, T-lymphocytes do not develop, as a result of which the body's immune strength becomes extremely weak. This disease is also called SCID (Severe Combined Immunodeficiency).

Q: Write the procedure of gene therapy in ADA deficiency.
Answer:

The procedure of gene therapy in ADA deficiency is as follows—

  1. Lymphocytes are removed from the patient's body from the bone marrow or blood.
  2. In the laboratory, a normal ADA gene is inserted into these cells with the help of a vector (often a virus).
  3. The modified (transgenic) cells are cultured.
  4. These cells are re-introduced into the patient's body.
  5. These cells now start producing the ADA enzyme, and the immune capacity improves.
Q: Write the types of gene therapy.
Answer:

Gene therapy is mainly divided into two types—

  1. Somatic gene therapy -

    In this, a normal gene is inserted into the patient's somatic cells (such as blood or bone marrow cells). Its effect is limited only to that individual and is not passed on to the next generation.
    Example: SCID (treatment of ADA deficiency)

  2. Germ-line gene therapy -

    In this, gene alteration is done in germ cells (egg/sperm) or in an early embryo. Its effect can also be transferred to subsequent generations. For ethical and legal reasons, this is generally not permitted in humans.

Q: When and on whom was gene therapy first used?
Answer:

Gene therapy was first used in 1990 on a four-year-old girl to correct adenosine deaminase (ADA) deficiency.

Q: Write the full form of ELISA. Which disease can be identified by it?
Answer:

The full form of ELISA is Enzyme Linked Immuno Sorbent Assay.

ELISA is a test method by which AIDS is identified by checking for the presence of antibodies/antigens against HIV in the blood.

Q: What is meant by immunomodulation? (2018)
Answer:

When the body's immune system is altered by medicines, this event is called immunomodulation.

Q: What are stem cells?
Answer:

Stem cells are undifferentiated cells that have the ability to self-renew, and which can differentiate into various types of specialized cells (such as blood, nerve, muscle, etc.).

Q: What is stem cell technology? Write its uses.
OR
Write three uses of stem cell technology.
Answer:

Stem cell technology is a biotechnology method in which stem cells are used to repair, reconstruct, and replace damaged or diseased tissues/organs.

Uses -

  1. Treatment of diseases – in cancer, blood disorders (leukemia), diabetes, Parkinson's disease, etc.
  2. Tissue and organ reconstruction – repair of heart, nerve, skin, and bone tissues.
  3. Bone marrow transplant – in the treatment of blood cancer.
  4. Accident/burn treatment – in creating new skin for burnt tissues.
Q: What is biosafety?
Answer:

Biosafety is all the rules, precautions, and procedures whose objective is to protect human health, animals, plants, and the environment from potential harm arising from the use of biotechnology, laboratories, and genetically modified organisms (GMOs).

Examples:

  1. Rules for the testing and approval of GM crops
  2. Safety levels in laboratories (BSL-1 to BSL-4)
  3. Safe disposal of biological waste
Q: What do you understand by biopatent? State its uses and misuses. (2009,10,12,14,16,24)
Answer:

A biopatent is the legal right given to the inventor of a biological product, process, or genetic innovation (such as a gene, microorganism, or drug), by which he/she retains exclusive rights over its manufacture and use.

Uses of biopatents

  1. Biological inventions receive legal protection.
  2. Research and innovation are encouraged.
  3. New drugs, vaccines, and bio-products are developed.
  4. The inventor gains economic benefit.

Misuses of biopatents

  1. Monopolies are established over biological resources.
  2. Wrongful patenting of traditional knowledge (biopiracy).
  3. The prices of medicines increase.
  4. Developing countries may be exploited.
Q: Briefly explain biopiracy. (2009/10/12/18/20/21/23/25)
Answer:

The commercial use of the biological resources or traditional knowledge of a country/community without permission is called biopiracy.

Example - The attempt by foreign companies to obtain a patent on the medicinal properties of neem in India. This is considered a leading example of biopiracy.

Q: What is forensic science?
Answer:

Forensic science is the science in which scientific techniques are used to investigate crimes and to present evidence in court.

Q: Explain the method of DNA fingerprinting in forensic science. (2021)
Answer:

DNA fingerprinting is a technique in which a person's identity is established by analysing the specific pattern of their DNA.

Method

  1. DNA is extracted from blood, hair, saliva, etc. found at the crime scene.
  2. The DNA is cut with restriction enzymes.
  3. The resulting fragments are separated by gel electrophoresis.
  4. The obtained DNA pattern is compared with the DNA of the suspect.
