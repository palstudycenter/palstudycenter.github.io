Q: What is biotechnology?
Answer:
The use of the technology of organisms, living systems or cellular components for human welfare is called biotechnology.
Q: Write the principles of biotechnology.
Answer:

Modern biotechnology has drawn its major contribution from the following techniques—

  1. Genetic Engineering
  2. Chemical Engineering
Q: What is chemical engineering?
Answer:
Under chemical engineering, biotechnological products (such as antibiotics, vaccines and enzymes) are obtained in bulk quantity by growing only the desired microbes or prokaryotic cells in a sterile environment.
Q: What is genetic engineering?
Answer:

The technique of altering the structure of the genetic material (DNA) as required is called gene manipulation or genetic engineering.

The following tasks are carried out in this—

  1. Artificial synthesis of genetic material.
  2. Joining two different genes or DNA segments to create new DNA.
  3. Repair of DNA.
  4. Regulating gene action by removing, adding or replacing certain nucleotides of DNA to synthesize new DNA molecules of the desired structure.
Q: Who is regarded as the father of genetic engineering?
Answer:
Paul Berg is regarded as the father of genetic engineering.
Q: What is recombinant DNA? (2019 Supplementary)
Answer:
DNA to which a foreign DNA segment has been added with the aim of obtaining a desired phenotype is called recombinant DNA.
Q: What is recombinant DNA technology? Write its various steps. (2023/25)
Answer:

The technique of constructing a recombinant DNA molecule is called recombinant DNA technology.

A recombinant DNA molecule is usually constructed by joining two or more DNA segments from different organisms. In the recombinant DNA molecule so formed, a gene of one organism remains joined with the regulatory sequences of another organism. Such a gene is called a chimeric gene.

The main steps of genetic transformation in an organism are as follows—

  1. Identifying the DNA containing the desired gene.
  2. Transfer of the desired gene or DNA segment into the host.
  3. Maintaining the transferred DNA in the host and passing it on to the progeny.

Q: What are restriction enzymes?
Answer:
Restriction enzymes are special enzymes that recognize a specific nucleotide sequence on DNA and cut the DNA at that very site. These enzymes are generally found in bacteria and function to destroy foreign (invading) DNA, such as the DNA of viruses.
Q: How many types of restriction enzymes are there?
Answer: Restriction enzymes are of two types—
  1. Restriction Exonuclease
  2. Restriction Endonuclease
Q: What is a restriction exonuclease?
Answer:

Restriction exonucleases are enzymes that cut nucleotides one by one, in sequence, from the ends (5′ or 3′ end) of a DNA or RNA chain.

Uses—

  1. It helps in the degradation of nucleic acids.
  2. In genetic engineering it is used to modify or remove the ends of DNA.
Q: What is a restriction endonuclease? How does it work? (2019/24)
Answer:

Restriction endonucleases are special enzymes that recognize a specific nucleotide sequence (recognition site) within (inside) the DNA and cut both strands of the DNA at that very site. These enzymes are mainly found in bacteria and protect against foreign DNA.

Mode of action—

  1. The restriction endonuclease recognizes a definite recognition sequence (such as a palindromic sequence) on the DNA.
  2. On finding the recognition site, it breaks the phosphodiester bond of the DNA.
  3. As a result, fragments of DNA are formed, whose ends may be sticky or identical (blunt).

Thus, restriction endonucleases play an important role in genetic engineering by cutting DNA at specific sites.

Q: Are restriction endonucleases present in eukaryotic cells?
Answer: No.

Restriction endonucleases are not present in eukaryotic cells. These enzymes are found only in bacteria (prokaryotic cells). Restriction enzymes can recognize and cut a specific sequence in viral DNA. Bacteria protect their own cells from these enzymes by methylating their DNA. Eukaryotic cells use other means to protect themselves from viral invasion.

Q: Clarify the difference between exonuclease and endonuclease. (2024)
Answer:
Point of difference Exonuclease Endonuclease
1. Site Nuclease enzymes that cut at the ends of DNA. Nuclease enzymes that cut in the middle of DNA.
2. Requirement for action A free 3′ or 5′ end is required for action; cannot cut closed (circular) DNA. A 3′ or 5′ end is not required; they can also cut closed (circular) DNA.
3. Product after action Release single nucleotides after action. Release oligonucleotides after action.
4. Type General enzymes. Specific enzymes.
5. Example Exonuclease EcoRI Endonuclease
Q: What is a palindrome?
Answer:
It is a group of letters or characters that forms the same word whether read forwards or backwards. For example, "madam", "level", "rotator", etc.
Q: What is a DNA palindrome?
Answer:

A DNA palindrome refers to a sequence of base pairs on both strands of DNA such that if the reading orientation is kept the same (for example 5'→3' or 3'→5'), the same sequence is read on both DNA strands.

Example—

5' G-A-A-T-T-C 3'
3' C-T-T-A-A-G 5'
Q: Naming of restriction enzymes
Answer:

The first capital letter of the restriction enzyme's name is the first letter of the genus of the prokaryotic cell from which it has been obtained. Likewise, the second and third letters are taken from the species.

For example— the enzyme EcoRI has been obtained from Escherichia coli.

The letter E is chosen from the genus and the letters "co" from the species, forming the word "Eco". Here R represents the strain.

Thus E = Escherichia, co = coli, R = strain. The enzyme was named EcoRI using the Roman numeral I.

Agarose is a polysaccharide obtained from marine algae (seaweed).

In biotechnology it is used in the separation of DNA molecules.

Q: What is polymerase enzyme?
Answer:

Polymerase enzymes are enzymes that join nucleotides together to form nucleic acids (DNA or RNA). These enzymes work according to a template and synthesize a new strand.

Examples—

  • DNA polymerase → makes a copy (replicates) of DNA.
  • RNA polymerase → synthesizes RNA from DNA.
Q: What is C-DNA?
Answer:
With the help of DNA polymerase enzyme, many copies of recombinant DNA can be prepared by amplification; these are called C-DNA (copy DNA).
Q: What is ligase enzyme?
Answer:

Ligase enzymes are enzymes that join two different DNA segments together. This enzyme joins the broken ends by forming the phosphodiester bond present in the DNA strand.

Example: DNA ligase.

Q: What is cloning? (2023)
Answer:
The asexual progeny of a particular organism or cell are called clones, and the method of obtaining them is called cloning.
Q: What is meant by gene cloning? Write its importance. (2019)(2023)
Answer:

The method of preparing many copies of a specific gene is called gene cloning.

Gene cloning is also called recombinant DNA technology. It is a method of preparing recombinant DNA segments in which a cleaved (cut) DNA molecule is joined with a vector DNA (such as a plasmid), and then the virus or bacteria are made to multiply. In this way, copies of the related DNA are prepared. The obtained copy of the related DNA is called cloned DNA.

This process was discovered by Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer.

Importance (uses) of gene cloning—

  1. Many useful genetic traits can be obtained by this method.
  2. This technique can be used to prevent many diseases.
  3. Many kinds of medicines can also be synthesized by this method.
  4. This technique is also used in eugenics.
  5. Through this technique, desired traits can be incorporated into plants and animals.

A DNA molecule that has the ability to replicate within a host cell, and which allows binding for cloning a foreign or alien DNA, is called a vector (cloning vector).

Q: What is a vector?
Answer:

A vector is a DNA molecule that carries a foreign gene (DNA segment) joined to itself into the host cell.

Examples: Plasmid, bacteriophage, cosmid, Yeast Artificial Chromosome (YAC), etc.

Q: Describe four properties of a good vector. (2022)
Answer: Properties (characteristics) of a vector—
  1. The size of the vector should be small, and it should have an origin of replication, a restriction endonuclease site and one or two selectable markers, so that the transformed host cell can be easily identified.
  2. Its isolation and purification should be simple and convenient.
  3. The vector should be such that it can be easily inserted into the host cell, i.e., the transformation or transfection of the vector into the host cell should be easy.
  4. Its replication should be independent of the replication of the host cell.
Q: What is a plasmid? (2021)
Answer:

A plasmid is a small, circular, double-stranded DNA molecule found separate from the main chromosomal DNA of bacteria. It is capable of replicating independently.

Characteristics—

  1. It is generally found in bacteria and some yeasts.
  2. It often carries useful genes such as antibiotic-resistance genes.
  3. It is used as a vector in genetic engineering.
Q: What is a bacteriophage? (Question bank)
Answer:
Bacteriophages, also commonly known as phages, are actually viruses that replicate inside bacteria; that is, viruses that infect bacteria are called bacteriophages.
Q: Why is Agrobacterium tumefaciens called a natural genetic engineer?
Answer:

Agrobacterium tumefaciens is called a natural genetic engineer because this bacterium naturally transfers a part of its own DNA into plant cells and also gets it expressed there.

Reasons:

  1. This bacterium contains the Ti-plasmid.
  2. The T-DNA part of the Ti-plasmid integrates into the genome of the infected plant cell.
  3. This T-DNA produces crown gall disease in plants.
  4. Scientists use this very natural ability to transfer foreign genes into plants.

Q: Write the process of recombinant DNA technology.
Answer: Its main steps are as follows—
  1. Isolation of the desired gene: The gene having the desired trait is extracted from the donor DNA.
  2. Cutting of DNA: The donor DNA and the vector (such as a plasmid) are cut using a restriction endonuclease.
  3. Formation of recombinant DNA: The cut desired gene is joined to the vector DNA with the help of the DNA ligase enzyme.
  4. Entry into the host cell: The recombinant DNA is introduced into a suitable host cell (such as E. coli).
  5. Selection and multiplication: Cells containing the correct recombinant DNA are selected and grown.
  6. Expression of the gene: The desired gene is expressed and produces a useful protein or product.

In this way, medicines, hormones (such as insulin), vaccines, etc., are made using recombinant DNA technology.

Q: What is PCR?
Answer:

The full form of PCR is Polymerase Chain Reaction. It is a modern biotechnological technique by which the amount of a specific portion of DNA is amplified many times in a very short time in the laboratory.

Importance of PCR—

  1. Helps amplify a very small amount of available DNA millions of times.
  2. Useful in forensic science for identifying a criminal.
  3. Helpful in the medical field in diagnosing genetic disorders and infections.
  4. Extremely important in genetic research and biotechnology.
  5. Useful in evolutionary studies and DNA fingerprinting.
Q: Name the DNA polymerase used in the PCR technique. Explain why this enzyme is used in this technique.
Answer:

The DNA polymerase used in the PCR technique is called Taq DNA Polymerase.

The reasons for using this enzyme are as follows—

  1. In PCR, DNA is repeatedly heated to a high temperature (about 94–95°C).
  2. Ordinary DNA polymerases are destroyed at this temperature, but Taq DNA polymerase is heat-tolerant.
  3. This enzyme is obtained from the bacterium Thermus aquaticus, which lives in hot springs.
  4. Therefore, it keeps synthesizing DNA in every cycle without being destroyed.
Q: Can you recall meiosis and state at which stage recombinant DNA is formed?
Answer:
In meiosis, the formation of recombinant DNA takes place during the exchange of genetic material, that is, during crossing over between the non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes at the chiasmata sites, from the pachytene to the diplotene stage of prophase I of meiosis I.

Q: Write a short note on gel electrophoresis.
Answer:

Gel electrophoresis is a laboratory technique used to separate fragments of DNA, RNA or protein on the basis of their size and electric charge.

In this method, the samples are placed in an agarose or polyacrylamide gel and an electric current is passed through it. Since DNA is negatively charged, it moves towards the positive electrode (anode). Smaller DNA fragments move faster through the gel, while larger fragments move more slowly.

The separated DNA fragments are visualized under UV light with the help of ethidium bromide or another dye.

Importance—

  1. Determination of the size of DNA fragments.
  2. Use in recombinant DNA technology.
  3. Helpful in DNA fingerprinting and genetic studies.
Q: What is a bioreactor? Explain.
Answer:

A bioreactor is a specially designed large vessel in which microbes, plant or animal cells, or enzymes are kept under controlled conditions so that they can produce useful bio-products (such as antibiotics, enzymes, hormones, vaccines, etc.) on a large scale.

Conditions controlled in a bioreactor—

  1. Temperature
  2. pH
  3. Oxygen supply
  4. Nutrients
  5. Stirring and aeration

Importance: A bioreactor makes it possible to scale up production in industrial biotechnology from the laboratory scale to the industrial scale.

Answer:

Industrial use: Through this, the genes for vitamins, antibiotics or hormones of higher organisms are coded, and the DNA synthesized from these is inserted into bacteria for the industrial-scale synthesis of vitamins, hormones, etc. Using this method, biosynthesis of human insulin has also been done under the name Humulin.
