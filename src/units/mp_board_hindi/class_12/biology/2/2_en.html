What is human reproduction?

In humans, the fertilisation of the male and female gametes (sperm and ovum) leads to the formation of a new human baby. This is called human reproduction.

What is the human reproductive system?

The organs that assist in human reproduction are called the human reproductive system.

It is divided into the following two parts.

  1. Male reproductive system
  2. Female reproductive system

What is the testis? Give its structure.

The testis is the main or primary reproductive organ in the male.

In men, a pair of testes is present outside the abdominal cavity, in a sac-like structure called the scrotum.

Each testis is oval, 4-5 cm long and 2-3 cm wide, and is covered externally by two membranes. The inner membrane extends into the testis, dividing it into 250-300 compartments, called testicular lobules.

Write two main functions of the testis.

Main functions of the testis

  1. The male gametes, sperm, are formed in the testes; this process is called spermatogenesis.
  2. The testis secretes the hormone testosterone, which helps in the development of secondary sexual characters in males (beard and moustache, deep voice, etc.).
Why are the testes located outside the abdominal cavity in men? (2016/20/21/24)

Answer - Sperm are formed in the testes. This requires a temperature 2-3°C lower than the normal body temperature (about 37°C).
The temperature inside the abdominal cavity is higher, which is not favourable for sperm formation. Therefore, the testes are located outside the body so that a suitably cool temperature is maintained.

The testes are located in a skin sac called the scrotum.

  1. It provides protection to the testes.
  2. It helps keep the temperature of the testes low, which is necessary for spermatogenesis.

What is the ovary? Give its structure.

The ovary is the main organ of the female reproductive system, where the ovum is formed. The female has a pair of ovaries. These are suspended in the abdominal cavity by ligaments from the pelvic wall.

Structure:

Each ovary is an almond-shaped structure, 2 to 4 cm long, 1.5 cm wide and about 1 cm thick. It has the following parts.

  1. Outer layer – the outermost thin layer of the ovary.
  2. Cortex – contains follicles. The ovum is formed within the follicle.
  3. Medulla – contains blood vessels, fibres (connective tissue), and lymph vessels. It provides nourishment to the ovary.
Write the main functions of the ovary.

Main functions of the ovary

  1. The female gamete, the ovum, is formed in the ovary.
  2. The ovary secretes the hormones oestrogen and progesterone, which regulate the menstrual cycle and the development of secondary sexual characters in the female.

Functions of the corpus luteum

  1. Secretion of the hormone progesterone – this hormone prepares the inner lining of the uterus for pregnancy.
  2. Maintaining pregnancy – progesterone helps in the development of the embryo in the uterus and prevents menstruation.

What is the uterus?

The uterus is a hollow, muscular organ located in the female reproductive system.

Its upper part opens into the fallopian tubes, and its lower, narrow part is called the cervix.

Its main functions are as follows.

  1. The fertilised ovum (embryo) develops in the uterus.
  2. If pregnancy does not occur, the inner lining of the uterus breaks down and is expelled as menstruation.

Its main functions are as follows.

  1. It receives semen from the penis during sexual intercourse.
  2. At the time of childbirth, the baby comes out through this passage.
  3. During menstruation, blood and tissue are expelled through the vagina.

What is gametogenesis?

The process by which gametes are formed from the cells of the germinal epithelium in humans is called gametogenesis. It occurs in the following two ways.

  1. Spermatogenesis
  2. Oogenesis
Question - What is spermatogenesis? Explain it.

Answer -

Spermatogenesis: The process of formation of haploid and functional sperm from diploid spermatogonia cells in the seminiferous tubules of the testes is called spermatogenesis.

This process is completed through the following stages—

(1) Multiplication Phase: The cells of the germinal epithelium undergo repeated mitotic divisions, resulting in an increase in the number of spermatogonia cells.

(2) Growth Phase: The spermatogonia cells increase in size and develop into primary spermatocytes.

(3) Maturation Phase: The primary spermatocyte undergoes meiosis. The first meiotic division produces two haploid secondary spermatocytes. These then undergo the second meiotic division to form four haploid spermatids.

(4) Transformation Phase: The spherical and non-motile spermatids transform into active and motile sperm.

Remember:

Spermatogonium → Primary spermatocyte → Secondary spermatocyte → Spermatid → Sperm

Question - How many sperm are formed from one primary spermatocyte?

Answer -

Answer: One primary spermatocyte produces four haploid sperm through meiotic division.

Sequence: Primary spermatocyte → 2 secondary spermatocytes → 4 spermatids → 4 sperm

What is the acrosome? Write its functions.

Answer –
The acrosome is a cap-like structure located at the tip of the head of the sperm.

Function - the main function of the acrosome is to make fertilisation possible by helping the sperm enter the ovum with the help of enzymes.

Question - Where is the acrosome formed?

Answer -

The acrosome is formed from the Golgi apparatus of the spermatid and is located at the anterior part of the mature sperm.

Question - What is a sperm? Describe its structure.

Answer -

Sperm: The male gamete that takes part in fertilization is called a sperm. Sperm are very small, thin and elongated and can be seen only with the help of a microscope.

Structure of Sperm: A typical sperm consists of four main parts—

(1) Head: It is the anterior part of the sperm. It contains a haploid nucleus. A cap-like structure called the acrosome is present at the front of the nucleus. It contains enzymes that help the sperm penetrate the outer layer of the egg during fertilization.

(2) Neck: It is a small part between the head and the middle piece. It contains two centrioles, which help in the formation of structures required for the movement of the sperm.

(3) Middle Piece: It is the energy-producing part of the sperm. It contains many mitochondria that provide the energy required for sperm movement.

(4) Tail: It is the long posterior part of the sperm. It helps the sperm to move and reach the egg.

Remember: Sperm → Head + Neck + Middle Piece + Tail

Question - What is oogenesis? Explain it.

Answer -

Oogenesis: The process of formation of a haploid ovum from diploid oogonia cells in the ovary through meiotic division is called oogenesis.

In females, the process of oogenesis begins during the embryonic stage. It is mainly completed through three stages—

(1) Multiplication Phase: In this phase, the cells of the germinal epithelium of the ovary undergo repeated mitotic divisions. This produces oogonia and increases their number.

(2) Growth Phase: In this phase, the oogonia absorb nutrients and increase in size. Their nucleus also becomes larger. Later, they develop into primary oocytes.

(3) Maturation Phase: The primary oocyte undergoes the first meiotic division, producing one large secondary oocyte and one small polar body. The second meiotic division results in the formation of one mature ovum and polar bodies.

Remember: Oogonium → Primary oocyte → Secondary oocyte → Mature ovum

Question - How many ova are formed from one primary oocyte?

Answer -

Answer: One mature ovum is formed from one primary oocyte through meiotic division.

Remember: 1 Primary oocyte → 1 Ovum + Polar bodies

Question - What is an ovum? Describe its structure.

Answer -

Ovum: The female gamete that takes part in fertilization is called an ovum.

Structure:

(1) Nucleus: A haploid nucleus (n) is present in the centre of the ovum.

(2) Zona Pellucida: A protective layer called the zona pellucida surrounds the ovum.

(3) Corona Radiata: An outer layer of cells called the corona radiata surrounds the zona pellucida.

Question - Name the hormones involved in oogenesis.

Answer -

Main Hormones: The following hormones mainly participate in oogenesis and the female reproductive cycle—

(1) FSH: It helps in the growth and development of ovarian follicles and supports the growth of the oocyte.

(2) LH: It triggers ovulation.

(3) Estrogen: It plays an important role in the development of ovarian follicles and the female reproductive cycle.

(4) Progesterone: It prepares the uterus for implantation of the fertilized ovum.

This process generally occurs around the middle of the menstrual cycle, approximately on the 14th day.

What is puberty?

Puberty is the stage at which the onset of sexual maturity occurs in a girl's body and reproductive capacity begins to develop. At this stage, the ovaries become active and the formation of ova begins.

Puberty usually begins at 12–13 years of age and continues up to 45–50 years of age.

What is the menstrual cycle?

The menstrual cycle is the regular biological process in which sequential changes occur in the ovary and uterus every month. If fertilisation does not occur, the inner lining of the uterus breaks down.

It is usually 28 days long and reflects the reproductive capability of the female.

What is menstruation?

When the ovum released into the female's fallopian tube is not fertilised, the thickened inner lining of the uterus separates. As a result, blood, mucus and the unfertilised, degenerated ovum are expelled through the vaginal passage. This process is called menstruation.

Bleeding usually lasts 3 to 5 days.

What is menarche?

The first occurrence of menstruation in a girl's life is called menarche.

This event indicates that reproductive capability has begun in the girl.

What is menopause?

The stage at which the menstrual cycle and menstruation permanently stop in a female is called menopause.

This usually occurs at 45–50 years of age, after which reproductive capability ends.

Name the hormones that regulate the menstrual cycle and all the processes related to it.

The menstrual cycle and all the processes related to it are controlled by hormones secreted by the pituitary gland—

  1. FSH (Follicle Stimulating Hormone) — helps in the development of the ovum
  2. LH (Luteinizing Hormone) — helps in ovulation
What is fertilisation?

The process by which the fusion of the male gamete (sperm) and the female gamete (ovum) in the fallopian tube of the female forms a diploid zygote (2n) is called fertilisation.

Process of fertilisation -

  1. In humans, for fertilisation, sperm are ejaculated into the female reproductive tract during copulation.
  2. The sperm travel by their own movement to reach the ovum.
  3. The actual process of fertilisation takes place in the fallopian tube. Hence, internal fertilisation occurs in humans.
  4. The fusion of the haploid (n) nucleus present in the sperm with the haploid (n) nucleus present in the cytoplasm of the ovum forms the diploid (2n) zygote.

Importance of fertilisation

  1. Due to fertilisation, development begins in the zygote.
  2. As a result, the number of chromosomes in the zygote becomes doubled (diploid).
  3. Changes occur again in the periphery of the egg, so that other sperm are prevented from entering the fertilised egg.
  4. As a result of fertilisation, the vitelline membrane separates from the egg, allowing the egg to rotate inside it.
  5. There is an increase in the rate of metabolic activities.
What is the zygote? The first single-celled structure formed by the fertilisation of the male gamete (sperm) and the female gamete (ovum) is called the zygote.

Sperm + Ovum → Zygote

Characteristics -

  1. The zygote is the first cell of the new organism.
  2. It contains the genetic characters of both the mother and the father.
  3. This zygote subsequently divides to form the embryo.
What is implantation? Define it.

Answer –
When the embryo formed after fertilisation, at the blastocyst stage, attaches to the wall of the uterus (endometrium) and embeds itself in it, this process is called implantation.

The blastocyst attaches near the fundus of the posterior wall of the uterus on about the 7th day after ovulation, and this process is completed by the 8th day.

Question – What is embryonic development? What are its different stages? Write their names.

Embryonic Development: The process of growth and changes that takes place from the zygote to the formation of a fully developed embryo is called embryonic development.

Main Stages:

  1. Zygote Stage
  2. Cleavage Stage
  3. Morula Stage
  4. Blastula/Blastocyst Stage
  5. Gastrula Stage
  6. Organogenesis Stage
Question - What is cleavage?

Answer -

Cleavage: The process of continuous and rapid mitotic cell division in the fertilized egg during embryonic development is called cleavage. During this process, the egg divides into many small cells without any increase in its overall size.

These small cells are called blastomeres. As a result of cleavage, morula is formed first, followed by the formation of a blastula.

Question – What is a morula?

The solid, ball-like structure of cells formed by the successive cleavage of the zygote is called a morula. It consists of approximately 8–16 cells.

Question – What is a blastula?

The hollow, ball-like structure of cells formed by the further development of the morula is called a blastula. The cavity present inside it is called the blastocoel.

Question - What is a gastrula? Write the names of its different parts and the organs formed from them.

Answer -

Gastrula: The multilayered embryonic stage formed by the transformation of the blastula during embryonic development is called a gastrula. It consists of three primary germ layers.

1. Ectoderm: It forms the outer layer of the skin, hair, nails and nervous system.

2. Mesoderm: It forms muscles, bones, blood, heart and reproductive organs.

3. Endoderm: It forms the inner lining of the digestive tract, liver, pancreas and the inner lining of the respiratory tract.

Remember:

Ectoderm → Skin and nervous system.

Mesoderm → Muscles, bones and blood.

Endoderm → Inner lining of digestive and respiratory tracts.

What is the placenta? Write its functions. (M.P. 2018/23 )

The composite structure formed by the foetal membranes of the embryo together with the uterine mucosa of the mother, through which various substances are exchanged between the blood of the mother and the foetus, is called the placenta.

The main functions of the placenta are as follows.

  1. Delivering nutrients and oxygen from the mother to the foetus, and carbon dioxide back from the foetus to the mother.
  2. Sending the waste products of the foetus into the mother's blood.
  3. Storing fat, glycogen and iron.
What is parturition (childbirth)?

Parturition is the biological process in which, on completion of the full term of pregnancy (about 9 months / 280 days), the mature foetus is expelled from the uterus through the vaginal passage and is born as a baby.

Process of parturition

At the time of parturition, regular and intense contractions begin in the muscles of the uterus.

These contractions cause the cervix to dilate, and the foetus moves downward.

Eventually, the foetus comes out through the vaginal passage; this is called parturition.

Which hormones are involved in inducing parturition?

Parturition is controlled by the following hormones.

  1. Oxytocin — intensifies uterine contractions
  2. Relaxin — helps in widening (relaxing) the pelvis.

Question: What is the mammary gland?

Answer –
The mammary gland is a well-developed gland located in the breast of the female, which produces and secretes milk.

Main function - it produces milk for the nourishment of the newborn baby.

What is lactation?

Lactation is the biological process in which, after parturition, the mammary glands of the female become active and produce and secrete milk, so that the newborn baby can be nourished.

Process of lactation

After parturition, the alveolar cells present in the mammary glands produce milk.

This milk reaches the nipple through the ducts and is released when the baby suckles.

Which hormones are involved in inducing lactation?

Lactation is mainly controlled by the following hormones.

  1. Prolactin — stimulates the production of milk
  2. Oxytocin — helps in the release of milk (let-down reflex)
Write the importance of lactation.

Lactation has the following importance.

  1. It provides complete and balanced nutrition to the newborn baby.
  2. The antibodies present in mother's milk protect the baby from diseases.
  3. It strengthens the emotional bond between mother and baby.
What is colostrum?

(M.P. 2023/25)

Answer- the milk that is released during the initial few days of lactation is called colostrum.

It contains many types of antibodies (immune factors) that are extremely necessary for developing immunity in the newborn baby.

Why is mother's milk considered the best food for a newborn baby?

Mother's milk is the best food for the baby because it is clean (germ-free) and easily digestible. It contains the nutrients necessary for the baby's growth, which help in the development of its body and brain.

Mother's milk also contains protective factors against disease, which keep the baby safe from infection.

For this reason, mother's milk is considered the best diet for a newborn baby.
