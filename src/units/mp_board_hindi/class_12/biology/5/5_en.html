Q1. What are the different experiments involved in the discovery of genetic material?
Answer:

The discovery of genetic material became clear gradually through the experiments of many scientists—

  1. Frederick Griffith's experiment (1928): Experiment on the pneumonia-causing bacterium Streptococcus pneumoniae. S-type (disease-causing) and R-type (non disease-causing). Dead S + live R killed the mouse → this proved the presence of a transforming factor.
  2. Avery, MacLeod and McCarty's experiment (1944): Proved that the transforming factor is DNA.
  3. Hershey and Chase's experiment (1952): Showed that in the bacteriophage virus, only DNA enters the bacterium. This proved that DNA alone is the genetic material.
Q2. How did Hershey and Chase establish the difference between DNA and protein?
Answer:
  • DNA was labelled with P-32 and protein with S-35.
  • After infection, only P-32 (DNA) enters the bacterium.
  • This proved that DNA alone is the genetic material.
Q3. What is DNA? 2025
Answer:

The full form of DNA is Deoxyribonucleic Acid. It is the genetic material in all living organisms. It transfers traits from generation to generation. It is called the blueprint of life.

Q4. Write the chemical composition of DNA. 2025
Answer:

DNA is a polynucleotide chain. Each nucleotide contains:

  1. Phosphoric acid
  2. Deoxyribose sugar
  3. Nitrogenous base

Nitrogenous bases are of two types—

  • Purines (A, G)
  • Pyrimidines (C, T)

These units together form the double helix structure of DNA.

Q: What is Chargaff's rule? 2022
Answer: According to Erwin Chargaff's rule,
  1. The amount of A, T, G and C in DNA varies from species to species.
  2. In every species, the molar amount of A is equal to the molar amount of T, and the molar amount of G is equal to the molar amount of C, i.e.—
    A = T
    G = C
  3. Total purine amount = Total pyrimidine amount, i.e.—
    A + G = T + C

Q6. Explain the structure of the Watson and Crick model of DNA. 2022
Answer:

Watson and Crick proposed the double helix structure of DNA in 1953.

DNA is made up of two polynucleotide chains running parallel but in opposite directions.

The chains are joined by hydrogen bonds.

A pairs with T (two bonds) and G pairs with C (three bonds).

The diameter of each helix is about 20 Å, and the length of one turn is 34 Å, containing 10 base pairs.

This structure is known as the Watson and Crick model.

Q7. What is called packaging or supercoiling of DNA? 2025
Answer:

In a human cell, the length of DNA is about 2 metres, which cannot fit inside the nucleus. The DNA molecule wraps around histone proteins to form a nucleosome. These nucleosomes coil further to form chromosomes. This process of condensation of DNA is called the packaging or supercoiling of DNA.

Q9. What is DNA replication? Write its method.
Answer:

DNA replication is the process by which a DNA molecule makes an identical copy of itself so that during cell division each new cell can receive an equal amount of genetic material.

Site: This process occurs during the S-phase of the cell cycle.

Method of DNA replication—

  1. Initiation - The double helix of DNA starts to unwind at the origin. The enzyme DNA helicase breaks the hydrogen bonds and separates the two strands, while SSBPs (single-strand binding proteins) keep the separated strands stable.
  2. Primer formation - The enzyme RNA primase lays down a short RNA primer so that the enzyme DNA polymerase can begin synthesising new DNA.
  3. Elongation - The enzyme DNA polymerase synthesises the new complementary strand. Continuous synthesis of one strand occurs steadily in the 5′→3′ direction, while synthesis on the other strand occurs in short discontinuous segments called Okazaki fragments.
  4. Joining - The enzyme polymerase-I removes the RNA primer and replaces it with DNA, and the enzyme DNA ligase joins the Okazaki fragments to form a continuous strand.
  5. Termination - The two new DNA strands join with the original strands to form two identical double helix DNA molecules.

Each new DNA molecule has one old (parental) strand and one new (daughter) strand. This is called semi-conservative replication.

Q10. Which experiment proved that DNA replication is semi-conservative? VIMP
Answer:

Watson and Crick (1953) suggested that DNA replication is semi-conservative, i.e., when new DNA forms, one strand is old and one strand is new. This was proved by the experiment of Meselson and Stahl.

Method of the experiment—

E. coli was grown for several generations in a medium containing 15N (heavy nitrogen), due to which all its DNA became heavy. These bacteria were then placed in a medium containing 14N (light nitrogen), where the newly formed DNA began to become light.

DNA was extracted at different times (generations), and then separated on the basis of density by CsCl density gradient centrifugation.

Results—

  • Generation 0 (only in 15N) - only heavy DNA was found.
  • Generation 1 (in 14N) - one band of DNA was found at an intermediate density (because one strand was of 15N and the other of 14N).
  • Generation 2 (in 14N) - two bands were found - one intermediate (old + new) and one light (both new 14N strands).
  • Generation 3 and beyond - the light DNA band kept increasing while the intermediate DNA kept decreasing.

These results made it clear that DNA replication is semi-conservative, i.e., in every new DNA molecule one strand remains old while the other is newly formed.

Q: What are called repetitive DNA and unique (non-repetitive) DNA?
Answer:

Repetitive DNA - Sequences of DNA that do not code for protein and are repeated again and again are called repetitive DNA. It is found mainly in the centromere and telomere regions of chromosomes. Its function is structural and supportive.

Unique (non-repetitive) DNA - This is the part of DNA in which the nucleotide sequences are unique. It is found mainly in genes. Its function is to carry genetic information and to take part in protein synthesis.

Q: Write the functions of RNA.
Answer: Main functions of RNA—
  1. Acting as genetic material in some viruses.
  2. Carrying out protein synthesis in eukaryotes.
Q: How many types of RNA are there? Explain.
Answer: Types of RNA—
  1. Genetic RNA: RNA that itself acts as genetic material.
    Example - some viruses such as TMV.
  2. Non-genetic RNA: RNA that does not function as genetic material. It only assists in protein synthesis. It is found in eukaryotes. There are three main types of non-genetic RNA—

    mRNA - It is called messenger RNA.
    Function:

    1. It carries genetic information from DNA to the ribosome.
    2. It acts as a template for protein synthesis.

    tRNA - It is also called transfer RNA or adaptor molecule.
    Function:

    1. It recognises specific amino acids and binds to them.
    2. It brings amino acids to the ribosome and binds through anticodons complementary to the codons of mRNA.
    3. It helps to add amino acids in the correct order during protein synthesis.

    rRNA - It is called ribosomal RNA.
    Function:

    1. It is the major component of the ribosome.
    2. It provides the laboratory for protein synthesis.
Q: How are capping and tailing of mRNA important?
Answer:

Capping: A methylated guanosine (m⁷G-cap) is added at the 5′ end of mRNA.
Importance:

  1. It protects mRNA from being degraded by nuclease enzymes.
  2. It helps in the recognition and attachment of mRNA at the ribosome.
  3. It helps in the initiation of translation.

Tailing: A chain of adenine nucleotides (poly-A tail) is added at the 3′ end of mRNA.
Importance:

  1. It increases the stability of mRNA.
  2. It helps mRNA move out of the nucleus into the cytoplasm.
  3. It assists in protein synthesis.
Q: Why is tRNA called an adaptor molecule? VImp
Answer:

The anticodon present in tRNA translates the genetic language (codon) of DNA present in mRNA into the language (sequence) of amino acids in protein. In this way, it acts as a bridge (adaptor) between the codon of mRNA and the amino acid. Hence tRNA is called an adaptor molecule.

Q: What is called the central dogma? Explain.
Answer:

The central dogma is a principle of molecular biology, proposed by Crick in 1958. According to the central dogma, the flow of genetic information occurs from DNA to RNA and then from RNA to protein.

DNA —(Transcription)→ mRNA —(Translation)→ polypeptide / protein
(In some RNA viruses, reverse transcription from mRNA to DNA is also possible.)

This principle explains that the genetic information stored in DNA is used, via RNA, to build proteins.

Q: Which properties of DNA make it more stable than RNA?
Answer:

DNA is more stable than RNA, mainly for the following reasons—

  1. Deoxyribose sugar - The 2′-OH group is absent in DNA, whereas it is present in RNA. Because of this, DNA is less susceptible to hydrolysis and remains more stable.
  2. Double helix structure - The two complementary strands of DNA are joined by hydrogen bonds, which make it more stable both mechanically and chemically.
  3. Use of thymine (T) - DNA contains thymine, whereas RNA contains uracil. Thymine is chemically more stable and makes the repair of damaged bases easier.
  4. Long lifespan - DNA is a permanent genetic material that stores information across generations, whereas RNA works temporarily in protein synthesis.

Q: What is the genetic code? Write its features. 2023/25
Answer:

The genetic code is the system by which the genetic information stored in DNA or mRNA is translated into a chain of amino acids in a protein. In simple words, it is a kind of language code that indicates which triplet codes for which amino acid.

Features of the genetic code:

  1. Triplet nature: There is a group of three nucleotides (codon) for each amino acid.
    Example: AUG, GAA, UUU, etc.
  2. Specificity: Each codon codes for only one amino acid.
  3. Universality: The genetic code is found to be the same in almost all organisms (from bacteria to humans).
  4. Commaless (non-punctuated): There is no punctuation (comma) between codons; they are read continuously.
  5. Degeneracy: There can be more than one codon for the same amino acid.
    Example: There are 6 codons for leucine.
  6. Presence of start and stop codons:
    AUG: start codon (codes for methionine).
    UAA, UAG, UGA: stop codons (stop translation).
Q: What is a codon? Write its features. 2022
Answer:

A group of nucleotides (a triplet) that codes for a particular amino acid is called a codon.

Example:
AUG → methionine (start codon)
UAA, UAG, UGA → stop codons

Main features—

  1. Each codon consists of three bases.
  2. A codon is always read in the 5′ → 3′ direction.
  3. A codon codes for only one amino acid.
  4. There are 64 codons, of which 61 code for amino acids and 3 (UAA, UAG, UGA) are stop signals.
  5. The genetic code is universal - almost the same in all organisms.
Q: What is called transcription? 2025
Answer:

The process by which mRNA is formed from DNA is called transcription. In this process, one strand of DNA is used as a template.

Mechanism of mRNA formation from DNA—

  1. Initiation - The enzyme RNA polymerase attaches to a specific region of DNA (the promoter site). One of the two strands of DNA (the template strand) opens up.
  2. Elongation - RNA polymerase adds nucleotides complementary to the template strand of DNA.
    Example: If the DNA sequence is A–T–G, then U–A–C will be added respectively on RNA.
  3. Termination - When RNA polymerase reaches the terminator sequence, synthesis of mRNA stops. The newly formed mRNA separates from DNA.
  4. Processing - The mRNA undergoes capping and tailing. Introns (non-coding sequences) are cut out and removed, and exons (coding sequences) are joined together.
Q: What is called an enhancer or promoter?
Answer:

A special nucleotide sequence is found on DNA where RNA polymerase and other helper proteins come and attach, and the process of transcription begins - this is called the promoter.

Features:

  1. It is located at the beginning portion of the gene.
  2. It contains special sequences such as the TATA box.
  3. The promoter determines where transcription of the gene will begin.
  4. It is part of the gene's control system, as it determines when and how much a gene will be active.
  5. Without the promoter, RNA polymerase cannot properly bind to DNA and begin forming mRNA.
Q: What is an exon? 2022/23
Answer:

The part of DNA or a gene that actually carries information for protein synthesis and is expressed in mRNA is called an exon.

In simple words - exon = coding sequence.

Q: Why are both strands of DNA not transcribed during transcription?
Answer:

If both strands were transcribed, two different mRNAs would be formed. These two mRNAs would be complementary to each other and would bind together. In this situation, their protein synthesis would not be possible. Therefore, only one strand is transcribed, and the other strand is merely a helper.

Q: What is translation? Write its mechanism.
Answer:

After mRNA is formed from DNA, the process of protein synthesis by reading the genetic code present on mRNA is called translation.

Mechanism—

  1. Initiation - mRNA binds to the ribosome, and the first tRNA (Met-tRNA) attaches to begin protein synthesis.
  2. Elongation - tRNAs bring amino acids and peptide bonds keep forming.
  3. Termination - When a stop codon arrives, protein synthesis stops and the polypeptide chain is released.
Q: Write the two main roles of the ribosome during translation.
Answer:
  1. It reads the codons present on mRNA.
  2. It joins together the amino acids brought by tRNA to form the polypeptide chain.

Q: What is DNA fingerprinting?
Answer:

The technique of identifying a person on the basis of DNA is called DNA fingerprinting.

Method:

  1. DNA is isolated.
  2. DNA is cut with specific enzymes.
  3. The fragments of DNA are separated using electrophoresis and hybridisation.
  4. The DNA pattern is obtained using radioactive or fluorescent probes.

Importance:

  1. Identification of criminals
  2. Paternity testing
  3. Study of biodiversity and lineage
  4. Identification of missing persons
