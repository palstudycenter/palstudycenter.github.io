13 - Biodiversity
Q: What is meant by biodiversity?
Answer:

Biodiversity refers to the diversity of all types of organisms found on Earth, their genetic variations, and the different ecosystems formed by them. In other words, the diversity of organisms, their species, and ecosystems found in a given region is called biodiversity.

Q: Write the major benefits of biodiversity.
Answer:

The major benefits of biodiversity are as follows—

  1. It helps in maintaining ecological balance.
  2. It provides food, medicine, fuel, and other natural resources.
  3. It provides useful genetic resources for agriculture and animal husbandry.
  4. It helps in keeping the climate and environment balanced.
  5. It makes an important contribution to scientific research, education, and economic development.
Q: How many types of biodiversity are there?
Or
Name the three essential components of biodiversity.
Or
Name the levels of biological organization of biodiversity.
Answer:

Biodiversity is mainly of three types—

  1. Genetic Diversity: The diversity of genes and genetic traits found among organisms of the same species.
  2. Species Diversity: The diversity of different species found in a given region.
  3. Ecosystem Diversity: The diversity of different types of ecosystems and their habitats.
Q: What is meant by genetic diversity?
Answer:

Genetic Diversity refers to the difference in genes and genetic traits found among different organisms of the same species. This diversity helps organisms adapt to a changing environment.

Example: Different varieties/strains of rice, wheat, mango, and humans.

Q: What is meant by species diversity?
Answer:

Species Diversity refers to the number and diversity of different biological species found in a given region.

Example: Finding many species of trees, birds, mammals, reptiles, and insects in a forest.

Q: What are community and ecological diversity?
Answer:
  1. Community Diversity: The diversity of different biotic communities found in a given region is called community diversity.
  2. Ecological/Ecosystem Diversity: The diversity of different types of ecosystems (such as forests, grasslands, deserts, rivers, lakes, etc.) found in a given region is called ecological diversity.
Q: Why is biodiversity greater in tropical regions?
Answer:

Biodiversity is greater in tropical regions for the following reasons—

  1. Favourable climate: Here, the temperature and rainfall remain favourable throughout the year, so the growth and reproduction of organisms continue uninterrupted.
  2. More solar energy: Abundant sunlight results in greater photosynthesis, which increases primary productivity.
  3. Long evolutionary history: These regions have been less affected by major climatic changes such as ice ages, so sufficient time has been available for the formation of new species (speciation).
  4. Diverse habitats: Because various types of habitats are available here, many kinds of organisms live in these regions.
  5. Less seasonal variation: Because the climate remains relatively stable, the existence and diversity of organisms are maintained.
Q: What is meant by loss of biodiversity?
Answer:

Loss of Biodiversity refers to the decrease or destruction of species of organisms, their genetic diversity, and the diversity of ecosystems. As a result, many species become endangered or extinct, and ecological balance is affected.

Q: Write the causes of loss of biodiversity.
Answer:

The major causes of loss of biodiversity are as follows—

  1. Destruction and fragmentation of habitat: Natural habitats of organisms are being destroyed due to deforestation, urbanization, and industrialization.
  2. Excessive exploitation of natural resources: Uncontrolled use of timber, wildlife, and other natural resources.
  3. Entry of foreign (invasive) species: Foreign species become a threat to the existence of local species.
  4. Pollution: Air, water, and soil pollution affect the life of many organisms.
  5. Climate change: Global warming and climate change create a threat to the existence of many species.
  6. Illegal hunting and smuggling: Illegal hunting and trade of wildlife rapidly reduce their numbers.
Q: What are the major causes of species loss in a geographical region?
Answer:

The major causes of species loss in a geographical region are as follows—

  1. Destruction and fragmentation of habitat: Natural habitats are destroyed due to deforestation, agricultural expansion, urbanization, and industrialization.
  2. Excessive exploitation of natural resources: Excessive hunting and indiscriminate use of wildlife and plants.
  3. Entry of foreign (invasive) species: Foreign species compete with and destroy local species.
  4. Co-extinction: When one species becomes extinct, other species associated with it also become extinct.
Q: What do you understand by an extinct animal?
Answer:

Extinct Animals are animals of which no living population remains anywhere on Earth. That is, they have completely disappeared both from their natural habitat and from captivity.

Example: Dodo, Dinosaurs, Passenger Pigeon, etc.

Q: What do you understand by an endangered species? Write examples of these.
Answer:

Endangered Species are species whose numbers have become extremely low, and if they are not conserved, there is a possibility of their becoming extinct in the near future.

Example: Tiger, Asiatic Lion, Indian One-horned Rhinoceros, Gharial, Snow Leopard, etc.

Q: What are rare species?
Answer:

Rare Species are species whose numbers are very small and which are found only in limited areas. At present, there is no immediate threat of their becoming extinct, but if they are not conserved, they may become endangered in the future.

Example: Himalayan Brown Bear, Desert Fox, Wild Buffalo, etc.

Q: What are threatened species?
Or
Q: What are endangered species (संकटग्रस्त)?
Answer:

Threatened Species are species whose numbers are continuously declining and which are facing the risk of extinction in the near future. If they are not conserved in time, they may become extinct.

Example: Tiger, Asiatic Lion, Gharial, Indian One-horned Rhinoceros, etc.

Q: What are vulnerable species?
Answer:

Vulnerable Species are species whose numbers are declining, and if unfavourable conditions continue, they may enter the endangered category in the near future.

Example: Cheetah, Sloth Bear, Nilgiri Tahr, etc.

Q: Write any four reasons for the need to conserve wildlife.
Answer:
  1. To maintain ecological balance.
  2. To save rare and endangered species from extinction.
  3. To conserve biodiversity and natural resources.
  4. To keep wildlife and their natural habitats safe for future generations.
Q: What is meant by biodiversity conservation?
Answer:

Biodiversity Conservation refers to the conservation and proper management of different species of organisms, their genetic diversity, and ecosystems, so that their existence is maintained and they remain safe for present and future generations.

Q: Write the reasons for biodiversity conservation.
Answer:

The major reasons for biodiversity conservation are as follows—

  1. To maintain ecological balance.
  2. To protect endangered and threatened species.
  3. To maintain a continuous supply of food, medicine, fuel, and other natural resources.
  4. To keep genetic resources and natural heritage safe.
  5. To conserve biodiversity for future generations.
Q: How can biodiversity be conserved?
Or
Q: Write the efforts made for the conservation of biodiversity.
Answer:

The following efforts can be made for the conservation of biodiversity—

  1. Establishing national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, and biosphere reserves.
  2. Banning deforestation and carrying out large-scale afforestation.
  3. Controlling illegal hunting and smuggling of wildlife.
  4. Carrying out In-situ and Ex-situ conservation of rare and endangered species.
  5. Increasing public awareness and effectively enforcing laws related to environmental protection.
Q: What is in-situ conservation?
Answer:

In-situ Conservation is the method of conservation in which animals and plants are conserved within their own Natural Habitat, so that they can live and reproduce safely under natural conditions.

Example: National Park, Wildlife Sanctuary, and Biosphere Reserve.

Q: What is a biodiversity hotspot?
Answer:

A Biodiversity Hotspot is a geographical region where the number of endemic species is very high, but which is under severe threat due to the destruction of natural habitat. Such regions are considered extremely important from the point of view of biodiversity conservation.

Q: Write the names of the biodiversity hotspots of India.
Answer:

India has four biodiversity hotspots—

  1. Himalaya
  2. Indo-Burma
  3. Western Ghats and Sri Lanka
  4. Sundaland
    (The Nicobar Islands of India fall under this.)
Q: What is a national park?
Answer:

A National Park is a protected area notified by the government for the conservation of wildlife, plants, and their natural habitat. Hunting, tree-felling, and other human activities are prohibited here.

Q: Which is the first national park in the world?
Answer:

Yellowstone National Park, United States of America (USA), which was established in 1872.

Q: Which is the first national park of India?
Answer:

Jim Corbett National Park, Uttarakhand. It was established in 1936.

Q: What is a wildlife sanctuary?
Answer:

A Wildlife Sanctuary is a protected area where wildlife and their natural habitat are conserved. Hunting of wildlife is prohibited here, but certain limited human activities may be carried out with government permission.

Q: What is a biosphere reserve?
Answer:

A Biosphere Reserve is a large protected area where natural ecosystems, plants, wildlife, and genetic diversity are conserved. Its objective is to ensure the conservation of biodiversity and the sustainable use of natural resources.

Q: Write the features of a biosphere reserve.
Answer:

The major features of a biosphere reserve are as follows—

  1. It is a large protected area established for the conservation of biodiversity.
  2. Plants, wildlife, and their natural habitat are conserved in it.
  3. It is divided into three parts— Core Zone, Buffer Zone, and Transition Zone.
  4. It is important for research, education, and environmental conservation.
  5. It promotes the sustainable use of natural resources.
Q: Write the objectives of a biosphere reserve.
Answer:
  1. To conserve biodiversity and natural ecosystems.
  2. To protect rare, endemic, and endangered species.
  3. To ensure the sustainable use of natural resources.
  4. To promote research, education, and training related to the environment.
  5. To make local communities participants in conservation activities.
Q: What is Project Tiger?
Answer:

Project Tiger is an important wildlife conservation scheme launched by the Government of India on 1 April 1973. Its main objective is the conservation of tigers and their natural habitat, increasing their numbers, and saving them from extinction.

Q: How do cultural and religious beliefs help conserve biodiversity?
Answer:

Cultural and religious beliefs play an important role in the conservation of biodiversity. Through these, people hold a sense of respect and protection towards nature and living organisms.

Role of cultural and religious beliefs in biodiversity conservation—

  1. Many trees (such as peepal, banyan, tulsi, etc.) are considered sacred and are protected accordingly.
  2. Many animals and birds (such as cow, cobra, peacock, etc.) are conserved because they hold religious significance.
  3. Sacred groves and the forests around religious sites are conserved, which keeps many rare species safe.
  4. Religious and cultural traditions generate awareness among people about nature conservation and the environment.
Q: What is ex-situ conservation?
Answer:

Ex-situ Conservation is the method of conservation in which animals and plants are conserved outside their Natural Habitat, under artificial or controlled conditions. Its objective is to save rare and endangered species from extinction and to increase their numbers.

Example: Zoo, Botanical Garden, Seed Bank, Gene Bank, and Tissue Culture.

Q: What is cryopreservation?
Answer:

Cryopreservation is a technique in which cells, tissues, seeds, embryos, or reproductive cells are preserved at an extremely low temperature (−196°C, liquid nitrogen), so that their biological activities almost stop and they can be kept safe for a long time.

Q: Write the uses of cryopreservation.
Or
Q: How is cryopreservation helpful in the conservation of biodiversity?
Answer:
  1. The seeds, embryos, and reproductive cells of rare and endangered species are conserved.
  2. New offspring can be produced from these under favourable conditions in the future.
  3. Genetic Diversity is kept safe.
  4. It is widely used in gene banks and biodiversity conservation programmes.
Q: What is a gene bank?
Answer:

A Gene Bank is a scientific facility in which the seeds, genes, pollen, tissues, embryos, or reproductive cells of plants, animals, and microorganisms are kept safe, so that their genetic diversity can be conserved.

Q: What is a semen bank?
Answer:

A Semen Bank is a facility where the sperm (semen) of humans or animals is kept safe at an extremely low temperature.

Uses—

  1. In the breeding of superior-breed animals.
  2. In the conservation of endangered species and in artificial insemination.
Q: What is in vitro fertilization (IVF)?
Answer:

In Vitro Fertilization (IVF) is a process in which the ovum and sperm are fertilized outside the body, in a special vessel in a laboratory. After the embryo is formed, it is implanted into the woman's uterus.

Use: In the treatment of infertility.

Q: What is meant by the Red Data Book?
Answer:

The Red Data Book is a book published by the IUCN, which gives details of rare, endangered, vulnerable, and threatened species.

Q: Write the major measures taken by the Government of India for environmental protection.
Answer:
  1. Scientific and balanced use of natural resources.
  2. Promoting forest conservation and afforestation.
  3. Control of air, water, and soil pollution.
  4. Control of pollution-causing industries and vehicles.
  5. Control on the use of chemical pesticides.

Major Acts—

  1. Indian Forest Act, 1927
  2. Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980
  3. Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981
  4. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986
Q: Which are the international conservation organizations?
Answer:
  1. CITES – An international agreement that regulates the international trade in endangered wildlife and plants.
  2. TRAFFIC – An organization that monitors the illegal trade in wildlife and plants.
  3. IUCN – An organization that works for the conservation of nature and natural resources. It is this organization that publishes the Red Data Book.
  4. WWF (World Wide Fund for Nature) – An organization that provides financial and technical assistance for biodiversity and wildlife conservation.

Q: What was called for at the biodiversity conference held in Rio de Janeiro in 1992?
Answer:

At the Earth Summit held in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, in 1992, all countries were called upon to adopt effective measures for the conservation of biodiversity, its sustainable use, and the equitable distribution of the benefits derived from it.
