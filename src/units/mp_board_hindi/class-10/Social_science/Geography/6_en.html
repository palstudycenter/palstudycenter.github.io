Unit 6- Manufacturing Industries
Manufacturing Industries

Manufacturing Industries: The process of converting raw materials into more useful and valuable products with the help of machines and labour is called manufacturing. For example, steel is made from iron ore, cloth from cotton, and sugar from sugarcane. Manufacturing industries are an important basis of the country's economic development.

Importance of manufacturing industries: Manufacturing industries help in the modernisation of agriculture, create employment opportunities and increase the income of the country. They provide agriculture with machines, fertilisers, pesticides and other necessary goods. Foreign exchange is earned by exporting finished goods, which strengthens the country's economy.

Relationship between agriculture and industry: Agriculture and industry depend on each other. Agriculture provides industries with raw materials such as cotton, jute, sugarcane, tobacco and vegetable oils. On the other hand, industries provide agriculture with tractors, pumps, fertilisers, pesticides and agricultural equipment. Thus the development of both sectors strengthens each other.

Factors affecting the location of industries: Facilities such as raw material, labour, capital, energy, transport, market and water are necessary for the establishment of industries. Industries are established more in places where these facilities are easily available. Some industries are set up near the source of raw material, while some are established in areas with market and transport facilities.

Classification of industries: Industries are classified on the basis of raw material, size, ownership and product. On the basis of raw material, there are agro-based, mineral-based, marine-based and forest-based industries. On the basis of size, there are small-scale and large-scale industries, and on the basis of ownership, there are public sector, private sector, joint sector and cooperative sector industries.

Agro-based industries: Industries that use raw materials obtained from agriculture are called agro-based industries. Textile, sugar, vegetable oil and jute industries are its main examples. The cotton textile industry is one of the most important agro-based industries in India.

Cotton textile industry: The cotton textile industry is a major industry of India. It provides employment to a large number of people and also contributes to the country's exports. Cities like Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Surat, Coimbatore, Kanpur and Indore are important centres of the cotton textile industry. This industry includes processes such as spinning, weaving, dyeing and printing of cloth.

Jute industry: The jute industry is mainly concentrated along the banks of the Hugli river in West Bengal. Facilities such as raw jute, water, cheap labour, transport and market are available here. Sacks, ropes, mats and other goods are made from jute. The jute industry is also important for exports.

Sugar industry: The sugar industry is an important agro-based industry of India. It uses sugarcane as raw material. Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Bihar are its main producing areas. Sugarcane is a heavy and quickly perishable raw material, so sugar mills are generally established near sugarcane-producing areas.

Iron and steel industry: The iron and steel industry is called a basic industry because steel is required to make machines and tools for many other industries. This industry depends on raw materials such as iron ore, coal, limestone and manganese. Jamshedpur, Bhilai, Bokaro, Durgapur, Rourkela and Visakhapatnam are the major iron and steel centres of India.

Aluminium industry: Bauxite is used in the aluminium industry. Aluminium is a light, strong and rust-resistant metal. It is used in aircraft, vehicles, electric wires, packaging and many industrial goods. This industry is suitable for areas with the availability of electricity because it requires a large amount of energy.

Chemical industry: The chemical industry is one of the fastest-growing industries in India. It produces inorganic and organic chemicals. Fertilisers, plastics, dyes, paints, medicines, soap and synthetic fibres are among its products. This industry is also important for agriculture and other manufacturing industries.

Fertiliser industry: The fertiliser industry provides nitrogen, phosphate and potash-based fertilisers necessary for agriculture. This industry depends on natural gas and other raw materials. The fertiliser industry plays an important role in increasing agricultural production in India.

Cement industry: The cement industry provides materials necessary for buildings, roads, bridges, dams and other construction works. It requires raw materials such as limestone, silica, alumina and gypsum. Cement is produced in many states in India and is used in the development of the country's infrastructure.

Automobile industry: Cars, buses, trucks, motorcycles and other vehicles are manufactured in the automobile industry. This industry creates employment and promotes many ancillary industries. Areas like Delhi, Gurugram, Pune, Chennai, Jamshedpur and Lucknow are major centres of the automobile industry.

Electronics industry: Computers, mobile devices, televisions, communication equipment and other electronic goods are manufactured in the electronics industry. This industry plays an important role in the development of the modern economy and information technology. It requires high technology and skilled workers.

Industrial pollution: Smoke, gases, chemical substances and waste released from industries can pollute air, water and land. Hot water and chemical waste discharged from factories can affect rivers and other water sources. Industrial smoke causes air pollution and the noise of machines can cause noise pollution.

Measures to control industrial pollution: Pollution control equipment should be used in industries. Wastewater should be treated before being released into water sources. Saving energy, using clean fuel, recycling waste, rainwater harvesting and developing green belts also help in reducing pollution.

Role of industries in the national economy: Manufacturing industries help in reducing dependence on agriculture, increasing employment, boosting exports and developing modern technology in the country. Industrial development also expands transport, banking, trade and other service sectors. Therefore, the manufacturing sector is considered an important basis of economic development.

Conclusion: Manufacturing industries are extremely important for the economic and social development of the country. They strengthen agriculture, provide employment, convert raw materials into valuable products and promote exports. At the same time, it is also necessary to control the pollution caused by industrial development. Keeping environmental protection and sustainable development in mind, industrial development is beneficial for the country in the long run.

Question 1. What is meant by manufacturing industries?
Answer: The economic activity of processing raw materials and converting them into higher-value finished goods is called manufacturing industry.
Question 2. What is the importance of manufacturing industries?
Answer: Manufacturing industries provide employment, help modernise agriculture, increase exports and make an important contribution to the country's economic development.
Question 3. What role do manufacturing industries play in India's economic development?
Answer: Manufacturing industries increase employment, production, exports and income, and also promote the development of the agriculture and service sectors.
Question 4. How are agriculture and industry dependent on each other?
Answer: Agriculture provides industries with raw materials such as cotton, jute, sugarcane and oilseeds, while industries provide agriculture with machines, fertilisers, pesticides and other equipment.
Question 5. What are agro-based industries?
Answer: Industries that use raw materials obtained from agriculture are called agro-based industries, such as the cotton textile, jute, sugar and food processing industries.
Question 6. What are mineral-based industries?
Answer: Industries that use minerals and metals as raw material are called mineral-based industries, such as the iron and steel and cement industries.
Question 7. What are the major manufacturing industries of India?
Answer: The cotton textile industry, jute industry, sugar industry, iron and steel industry, cement industry, chemical industry and automobile industry are the major manufacturing industries of India.
Question 8. What is the importance of the cotton textile industry?
Answer: The cotton textile industry provides employment, increases the demand for cotton, contributes to exports and plays an important role in the country's economy.
Question 9. In which areas has the cotton textile industry developed the most in India?
Answer: Maharashtra and Gujarat are the major centres of the cotton textile industry. Besides these, this industry is also important in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Telangana and Uttar Pradesh.
Question 10. What are the major problems of the cotton textile industry?
Answer: This industry faces problems such as old machinery, irregular power supply, variation in the quality of raw material, global competition and production cost.
Question 11. What is the importance of the jute industry?
Answer: The jute industry provides employment and is useful for making sacks, ropes, mats and other goods. It also contributes to exports.
Question 12. Which state is the major centre of the jute industry in India?
Answer: West Bengal is the major centre of the jute industry in India.
Question 13. Why is the jute industry concentrated in West Bengal?
Answer: Facilities such as sufficient raw jute, availability of water, cheap labour, transport facilities and the port of Kolkata are available here.
Question 14. What is the importance of the sugar industry?
Answer: The sugar industry uses sugarcane, provides a market for farmers and gives employment to a large number of people.
Question 15. Which are the major sugar-producing states of India?
Answer: Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra are the major sugar-producing states of India. Besides these, the sugar industry is also important in Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat and Bihar.
Question 16. What are the main reasons for the shift in the location of the sugar industry?
Answer: The centres of the sugar industry have changed due to factors such as availability of sugarcane, transport, market, labour, climate and modern technology.
Question 17. Why is the iron and steel industry called a basic industry?
Answer: The iron and steel industry provides essential material for machine manufacturing, transport, construction, defence and many other industries. Therefore, it is called a basic industry.
Question 18. What are the major centres of the iron and steel industry in India?
Answer: Jamshedpur, Bhilai, Bokaro, Durgapur, Rourkela and Visakhapatnam are the major iron and steel centres of India.
Question 19. Which raw materials are necessary for the iron and steel industry?
Answer: Iron ore, coking coal, limestone, manganese and other supporting materials are the major raw materials of the iron and steel industry.
Question 20. What is the importance of Jharkhand and Odisha in the development of the iron and steel industry in India?
Answer: Mineral resources such as iron ore, coal, manganese and limestone are found in sufficient quantity in these states, hence the iron and steel industry has developed here.
Question 21. What is the importance of the aluminium industry?
Answer: Aluminium is a light, strong metal and a good conductor of electricity. It is used in aircraft, electrical equipment, utensils, transport and the construction industry.
Question 22. What is the importance of the cement industry?
Answer: The cement industry provides material necessary for buildings, roads, bridges, dams and other construction works.
Question 23. Which raw materials are necessary for the development of the cement industry in India?
Answer: Limestone, silica, alumina and gypsum are the major raw materials of the cement industry.
Question 24. What is the importance of the chemical industry?
Answer: The chemical industry provides fertilisers, medicines, plastics, dyes, soap, synthetic rubber and many other industrial chemicals.
Question 25. Why is the chemical industry called a fast-growing industry?
Answer: The demand for the products of the chemical industry is continuously increasing in agriculture, industry, medicine and various fields of daily life. Therefore, it is a rapidly developing industry.
Question 26. What is the importance of the fertiliser industry?
Answer: The fertiliser industry provides farmers with nitrogen, phosphorus and potash-based fertilisers, which helps in increasing agricultural production.
Question 27. What is the automobile industry?
Answer: The industry related to the manufacture of cars, buses, trucks, motorcycles and other motor vehicles is called the automobile industry.
Question 28. What are the major centres of the automobile industry?
Answer: The Delhi-Gurugram region, the Mumbai-Pune region, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad and Kolkata are among the major automobile centres of India.
Question 29. What is the importance of the electronics industry?
Answer: The electronics industry plays an important role in the manufacture of computers, mobile phones, telecommunication equipment, electronic machines and other technical devices.
Question 30. What are the major factors that affect the establishment of manufacturing industries?
Answer:
  • Availability of raw material
  • Availability of energy
  • Labour
  • Capital
  • Transport
  • Market
  • Availability of water
  • Government policies
Question 31. What is air pollution caused by industries?
Answer: Dust, smoke and harmful gases released from the chimneys of industries pollute the atmosphere. This is called industrial air pollution.
Question 32. How does industrial water pollution occur?
Answer: Water pollution occurs when chemical waste, hot water and other dirty substances released from industries mix into rivers, ponds or groundwater.
Question 33. What is thermal pollution caused by industries?
Answer: The temperature of water rises because of hot water released from industries into water sources. This is called thermal pollution.
Question 34. How can industrial pollution be controlled?
Answer:
  • Proper treatment of waste.
  • Installing equipment to control smoke and gases.
  • Reuse and recycling of water.
  • Using clean technology.
  • Safe disposal of industrial waste.
Question 35. How do manufacturing industries affect the environment?
Answer: Manufacturing industries can cause air, water and land pollution. Because of them, noise pollution and pressure on natural resources can also increase.
Question 36. What is industrial waste?
Answer: Solid, liquid or gaseous waste released during the production process in industries is called industrial waste.
Question 37. What is sustainable industrial development?
Answer: Industrial development in which the environment, natural resources and future needs are also kept in mind along with production and economic progress is called sustainable industrial development.
Question 38. What is the importance of employment in manufacturing industries?
Answer: Manufacturing industries provide employment directly and indirectly to a large number of people and help increase people's income and standard of living.
Question 39. What benefit does agriculture get from industrial development?
Answer: Industrial development provides agriculture with machines, irrigation equipment, fertilisers, pesticides and processing facilities, which can increase agricultural productivity.
Question 40. Why is the development of manufacturing industries necessary in India?
Answer: The development of manufacturing industries is necessary to increase employment, reduce dependence on agriculture, boost exports, increase income and make the country economically strong.
Question 1. What is a mineral?
Answer: A mineral is a natural substance found in the rocks of the earth that has a definite chemical composition.
Question 2. What is the importance of mineral resources?
Answer: Minerals provide raw material for industries and play an important role in the development of areas such as machines, buildings, transport and energy production.
Question 3. How many types of minerals are there?
Answer: Minerals are mainly of two types—metallic minerals and non-metallic minerals.
Question 4. What are metallic minerals?
Answer: Minerals from which metal is obtained are called metallic minerals, such as iron, copper, bauxite and manganese.
Question 5. What are non-metallic minerals?
Answer: Minerals from which metal is not obtained are called non-metallic minerals, such as mica, limestone and salt.
Question 6. What are ferrous minerals?
Answer: Metallic minerals in which iron content is dominant are called ferrous minerals. Iron ore, manganese and chromite are its main examples.
Question 7. What is the importance of iron ore?
Answer: Iron ore is the main raw material of the iron and steel industry. It is used in the manufacture of machines, rail tracks, vehicles and various industrial goods.
Question 8. Which are the major iron ore-producing states in India?
Answer: Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka and Goa are the major iron ore-producing states.
Question 9. What is the importance of manganese?
Answer: Manganese is mainly used in the manufacture of steel and ferro-alloys. It is also used in batteries, paints and other industries.
Question 10. Which are the major manganese-producing states in India?
Answer: Odisha, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh and Goa are the major manganese-producing states.
Question 11. What is bauxite?
Answer: Bauxite is a major mineral ore from which aluminium is obtained.
Question 12. Which are the major bauxite-producing states in India?
Answer: Odisha, Gujarat, Jharkhand, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh are the major bauxite-producing states.
Question 13. What is the importance of mica?
Answer: Mica is useful in the electrical and electronics industries because it is a poor conductor of electricity and resistant to heat.
Question 14. Which are the major mica-producing regions in India?
Answer: Jharkhand, Andhra Pradesh and Rajasthan are the major mica-producing regions.
Question 15. What is limestone used for?
Answer: Limestone is mainly used in the cement industry. It is also used in the iron and steel industry and construction works.
Question 16. How are minerals formed?
Answer: Minerals are formed by various geological processes. Minerals are formed through the formation of rocks, deposition by hot water, cooling of magma and other processes.
Question 17. Why is it necessary to conserve minerals?
Answer: Most minerals are limited and non-renewable resources. It takes millions of years for them to form, so their judicious use and conservation is necessary.
Question 18. What are energy resources?
Answer: Natural resources from which energy is obtained are called energy resources, such as coal, petroleum, natural gas, water and sunlight.
Question 19. How many types of energy resources are there?
Answer: Energy resources are mainly of two types—conventional energy resources and non-conventional energy resources.
Question 20. What are conventional energy resources?
Answer: Energy sources that have been commonly used for a long time are called conventional energy resources, such as coal, petroleum, natural gas and hydroelectricity.
Question 21. What are non-conventional energy resources?
Answer: Sources of energy that are used in a relatively new or alternative form are called non-conventional energy resources, such as solar energy, wind energy, tidal energy, bio-energy and geothermal energy.
Question 22. What is coal?
Answer: Coal is a fossil fuel formed from ancient vegetation under the effect of pressure and heat over a long period of time. It is an important energy source of India.
Question 23. What are the major types of coal?
Answer: The major types of coal are peat, lignite, bituminous and anthracite.
Question 24. Which are the major coal-producing states in India?
Answer: Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal, Madhya Pradesh and Telangana are the major coal-producing states of India.
Question 25. What is lignite?
Answer: Lignite is a low-grade brown coal. In India, the Neyveli region of Tamil Nadu is famous for lignite.
Question 26. What is petroleum?
Answer: Petroleum is a fossil fuel found between underground rocks. It is also called mineral oil.
Question 27. What is the importance of petroleum?
Answer: Petrol, diesel, kerosene, lubricants, petrochemical substances and many other useful products are obtained from petroleum. It is an important energy source for transport and industries.
Question 28. Which are the major petroleum-producing regions in India?
Answer: Mumbai High, the Ankleshwar region of Gujarat, and Digboi and other oil fields of Assam are among the major petroleum-producing regions of India.
Question 29. What is natural gas?
Answer: Natural gas is a fossil fuel that is mainly composed of methane and is found along with petroleum in many places.
Question 30. What are the advantages of natural gas?
Answer: Natural gas is a relatively clean fuel. It is used in electricity generation, industries, fertiliser manufacturing, domestic fuel and vehicles.
Question 31. What is electricity?
Answer: Electricity is an important energy source that is used in various activities of homes, agriculture, industry, transport and communication.
Question 32. What is thermal electricity?
Answer: Electricity generated from the heat obtained by burning fuels such as coal, petroleum or natural gas is called thermal electricity.
Question 33. What is hydroelectricity?
Answer: Electricity generated by running turbines using the energy of flowing or falling water is called hydroelectricity.
Question 34. What are the major hydroelectric projects in India?
Answer: Projects such as Bhakra-Nangal, Tehri, Sardar Sarovar, Koyna and Hirakud are important for hydroelectricity generation.
Question 35. What is nuclear energy?
Answer: The energy obtained from processes occurring in the nucleus of an atom is called nuclear energy. It is used in electricity generation.
Question 36. Which minerals are used for nuclear energy in India?
Answer: Minerals such as uranium and thorium are mainly used for nuclear energy production in India.
Question 37. What is solar energy?
Answer: The energy obtained from the light and heat received from the sun is called solar energy. It is a renewable and clean source of energy.
Question 38. Why are the prospects for solar energy greater in India?
Answer: India is a tropical country and receives sufficient sunlight for most of the year. Therefore, the prospects for solar energy are very high.
Question 39. What is wind energy?
Answer: The kinetic energy of fast-moving air is called wind energy. Electricity is generated from it with the help of wind turbines.
Question 40. Which are the major wind energy regions in India?
Answer: Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Rajasthan and Andhra Pradesh are among the major wind energy regions.
Question 41. What is bio-energy?
Answer: Energy obtained from agricultural residue, animal waste, wood and other organic matter is called bio-energy.
Question 42. What is biogas?
Answer: The gas produced from the decomposition of cow dung and other organic matter is called biogas. It mainly contains methane gas.
Question 43. What is tidal energy?
Answer: The energy obtained from the motion of tides in the sea and the difference in water levels is called tidal energy.
Question 44. What is geothermal energy?
Answer: Energy produced from the heat obtained from the interior of the earth is called geothermal energy.
Question 45. What is the importance of non-conventional energy sources?
Answer: These energy sources are renewable and reduce pollution. They help in reducing dependence on fossil fuels.
Question 46. Write the measures for the conservation of mineral resources.
Answer:
  • Judicious use of minerals.
  • Preventing the wastage of minerals.
  • Recycling of metals.
  • Using substitutes for minerals.
  • Using modern and efficient technologies.
Question 47. Why is the conservation of energy resources necessary?
Answer: Conventional energy sources such as coal, petroleum and natural gas are limited and non-renewable. Their conservation will help meet future energy requirements.
Question 48. Write the major measures for energy conservation.
Answer:
  • Not using energy unnecessarily.
  • Using energy-efficient equipment.
  • Promoting public transport.
  • Increasing the use of solar and wind energy.
  • Saving fossil fuels.
Question 49. What is the importance of recycling in the conservation of mineral and energy resources?
Answer: Through recycling, old metals and other materials can be reused. This reduces the need for mining new minerals and reduces energy consumption.
Question 50. Why is the development of renewable energy necessary for India?
Answer: Renewable energy sources remain available for a long time, reduce pollution and reduce dependence on limited resources such as coal, petroleum and natural gas.
