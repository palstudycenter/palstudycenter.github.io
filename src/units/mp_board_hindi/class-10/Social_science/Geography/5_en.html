Minerals and Energy Resources

Mineral Resources: Minerals are naturally occurring substances that have a definite chemical composition and physical properties. Minerals are very important for our daily life and economic development. Various minerals are used in industry, transport, construction, agriculture and energy production. Mineral resources are limited and non-renewable, so their judicious use and conservation are necessary.

Classification of Minerals: Minerals are mainly divided into metallic and non-metallic minerals. Metallic minerals include iron ore, manganese, chromite and bauxite etc. Non-metallic minerals mainly include mica, limestone and salt etc. Metallic minerals also include ferrous minerals and non-ferrous minerals.

Ferrous Minerals: Iron ore is one of the most important minerals of India. It is the basis of the iron and steel industry. The major reserves of iron ore in India are found in Odisha, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Karnataka and Goa. High quality iron ore is especially useful for industry.

Manganese: Manganese is mainly used in the manufacture of iron and steel. It is also used in batteries, paints, glass and chemical industries. Odisha, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Goa are its major producing states in India.

Non-ferrous Minerals: Minerals that do not contain iron are called non-ferrous minerals. Copper, bauxite, lead and zinc are its major examples. Copper is used in electrical equipment, wires and the electronics industry. Aluminium is obtained from bauxite, which is used in the aircraft, vehicle, electrical and packaging industries.

Mica: Mica is an important non-metallic mineral. It is useful in the electrical and electronics industries due to its excellent insulating properties. Jharkhand, Andhra Pradesh and Rajasthan are the major producing regions of mica in India.

Limestone: Limestone is the main raw material of the cement industry. It is also used in the iron and steel industry and other industries. Reserves of limestone are found in many states of India.

Conservation of Minerals: Minerals take millions of years to form and their reserves are limited. Therefore, uncontrolled exploitation of minerals can create problems in the future. Scientific and planned use of minerals, recycling, reuse of metals and development of substitutes are important measures for mineral conservation.

Energy Resources: Energy is essential for modern life and economic development. Energy is used in industry, agriculture, transport and household activities. Energy resources are mainly divided into conventional and non-conventional energy resources. Coal, petroleum, natural gas and electricity are the major sources of conventional energy, while solar energy, wind energy, tidal energy, biogas energy and geothermal energy are non-conventional sources.

Coal: Coal is India's major fossil fuel and an important source of energy. It is used in electricity generation, industries and household activities. The major reserves of coal in India are found in Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal, Madhya Pradesh and Telangana. Coal is a non-renewable resource and its burning also causes pollution.

Petroleum: Petroleum is an important source of energy and is also called mineral oil. Petrol, diesel, kerosene, petroleum gas and many petrochemical products are obtained from it. It is used in transport, industry and household activities. Its major fields in India are Assam, Gujarat and the Mumbai High and Krishna-Godavari basin.

Natural Gas: Natural gas is a clean fossil fuel. It is used for electricity generation, the fertilizer industry, the petrochemical industry and as a domestic fuel. It is found along with petroleum or in separate gas fields. The Krishna-Godavari basin, Mumbai High, Gujarat and Assam are its major fields.

Electricity: Electricity is the basis of the modern economy. In India, thermal power, hydel power and atomic energy are used for electricity generation. Thermal power is generated from coal, petroleum and natural gas, while hydel power is generated from the water of rivers.

Hydel Power: Electricity generated from the power of water of rivers and dams is called hydel power. It is a renewable source of energy. In India, hydel power is generated from many multipurpose river valley projects. Other benefits such as irrigation and flood control are also obtained from it.

Atomic Energy: Atomic energy is obtained from the fission of atoms. In India, minerals like uranium and thorium are important for atomic energy. India has established various atomic power stations for the development of atomic energy.

Non-conventional Energy Sources: Due to the increasing demand for energy and the limited reserves of fossil fuels, the importance of renewable energy sources is increasing. Solar energy, wind energy, tidal energy, biogas and geothermal energy are its major examples. These sources are relatively clean and can be used for a long time.

Solar Energy: Energy obtained from the sun is called solar energy. Due to the availability of sufficient sunshine throughout the year in India, there are great possibilities for solar energy. Solar energy can be used for electricity generation, heating water and other activities. It is also helpful in reducing pollution.

Wind Energy: Energy obtained from the speed of wind is called wind energy. States like Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka and Rajasthan have good possibilities for wind energy in India. Wind energy is a pollution-free and renewable source of energy.

Biogas Energy: Energy obtained from agricultural waste, animal dung and other organic materials is called biogas energy. Biogas can be made from dung and organic waste. This can fulfil the need for cooking and small-scale energy in rural areas.

Energy Conservation: Considering the limited reserves of energy resources, saving energy is necessary. Use of public transport, use of energy-efficient equipment, preventing unnecessary consumption of electricity and promoting renewable energy sources are important measures of energy conservation.

Conclusion: Minerals and energy resources are extremely important for the industrial and economic development of the country, but most of these resources are limited and non-renewable. Therefore, it is necessary to stop their indiscriminate exploitation, conserve minerals, save energy and make greater use of solar, wind and other renewable energy sources. Sustainable and judicious use of resources is the basis of safe development for present and future generations.
