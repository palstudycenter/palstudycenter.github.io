Forest and Wildlife Resources

Forest and Wildlife Resources: Forests and wildlife are important resources of nature. Forests provide us with wood, fuel, medicines, fruits, gum and many other useful things. Wildlife plays an important role in maintaining ecological balance. Plants, animals and micro-organisms together create biodiversity. India is included among the world's important countries in terms of biodiversity.

Biodiversity: The variety of different types of plants, animals and micro-organisms found in an area is called biodiversity. Many types of forests, flora and wildlife are found in India. But due to deforestation, expansion of agriculture, industrialisation, urbanisation, mining, hunting and pollution, many species have come under threat.

Different categories of flora and fauna: Generally, wildlife and flora are placed in different categories based on their status. Normal species are those whose numbers are sufficient. The numbers of endangered species are declining rapidly and they are in danger of disappearing. Vulnerable species may become endangered in the future. Rare species are found in small numbers. Endemic species are found only in a particular area, while extinct species are no longer found anywhere.

Causes of destruction of wildlife: During the colonial period, large-scale deforestation took place for railways, agriculture, commercial forestry and industries. Even after independence, forest areas were affected due to agricultural expansion, development projects, mining, dam construction, roads and industries. Illegal hunting of wildlife and destruction of their natural habitat are also major reasons for the decline in their numbers.

Conservation of forests: The Government of India has taken many steps for the conservation of forests and wildlife. The Wildlife Protection Act was implemented in 1972. Under this, national parks, wildlife sanctuaries and biosphere reserves were created. Special projects have also been run for the conservation of important wildlife such as tigers, elephants and rhinoceroses. Project Tiger was launched in 1973, with the aim of protecting tigers and their natural habitat.

Major types of forests: According to government records, forests in India are divided into reserved forests, protected forests and unclassed forests. Reserved forests are considered the most important, and special attention is given to their conservation. Wildlife resources are also protected in protected forests, whereas unclassed forests have relatively less control over some areas.

Role of local communities in forest conservation: Forest conservation is not the responsibility of the government alone. Local communities have also made important contributions to the protection of forests and wildlife. The Bishnoi community of Rajasthan has made long-standing efforts to protect trees and wildlife. In the Chipko movement, local people played an important role in stopping the felling of trees. This makes it clear that forest conservation can be more effective with the participation of local people.

Joint Forest Management: In India, a system of Joint Forest Management was developed to involve local communities in forest conservation. Under this, the forest department and local people work together to protect and manage forests. Through this, people are given a share in the benefits obtained from forest resources, and their responsibility towards the conservation of forests increases.

Importance of forest and wildlife conservation: The conservation of forests and wildlife is essential for environmental balance, biodiversity, soil and water conservation, and human life. If forests and wildlife disappear, the ecosystem may become unbalanced. Therefore, along with development, the conservation and sustainable use of natural resources is necessary.

Conclusion: Forests and wildlife are a natural heritage. The government, local communities and every citizen should work together for their conservation. It is necessary to stop deforestation, stop the hunting of wildlife, plant more and more trees, and protect natural habitats. Only through sustainable development can we meet present needs while keeping forests and wildlife safe for future generations.

Question 1. What is meant by forest and wildlife resources?
Forest and wildlife resources refer to the flora, plants, animals and their habitats that are found in nature and play an important role in maintaining ecological balance.
Question 2. What is biodiversity?
The variety of different types of plants, animals and micro-organisms found in an area is called biodiversity.
Question 3. Why is the conservation of biodiversity necessary?
Biodiversity is necessary for maintaining ecological balance, the availability of natural resources and human life. Therefore, the conservation of biodiversity is necessary.
Question 4. Into how many categories have the various species of flora and fauna been divided?
Species of flora and fauna are mainly divided into the following categories—
  1. Normal species
  2. Endangered species
  3. Vulnerable species
  4. Rare species
  5. Endemic species
  6. Extinct species
Question 5. What are normal species?
Species whose numbers are so large that there is no immediate threat to their existence are called normal species.
Question 6. What are endangered species?
Species whose numbers have become so low that there is a risk of their becoming extinct are called endangered species.
Question 7. What are vulnerable species?
Species whose numbers are continuously declining and which are likely to become endangered in the near future are called vulnerable species.
Question 8. What are rare species?
Species whose numbers are very small, and which may become endangered if unfavourable conditions continue, are called rare species.
Question 9. What are endemic species?
Species that are found only in a particular geographical area and are not naturally found in other areas are called endemic species.
Question 10. What are extinct species?
Species that have not been seen in their natural habitat for a long time and whose existence has ended are called extinct species.
Question 11. What are the main causes of the decline in the number of wildlife?
  • Deforestation
  • Destruction of natural habitat
  • Illegal hunting
  • Trade in wildlife
  • Pollution
  • Industrialisation and urbanisation
  • Expansion of agriculture
Question 12. What problems arise due to deforestation?
Deforestation causes loss of biodiversity, soil erosion, climate change, changes in rainfall patterns and destruction of the natural habitat of wildlife.
Question 13. Why is the conservation of wildlife necessary?
Wildlife is an important part of the ecosystem. It helps maintain the food chain and ecological balance. Therefore, the conservation of wildlife is necessary to save it from extinction.
Question 14. What measures have been taken for the conservation of forests and wildlife in India?
  • Establishment of national parks.
  • Establishment of wildlife sanctuaries.
  • Establishment of biosphere reserves.
  • Formulation of wildlife conservation laws.
  • Control of illegal hunting.
  • Conservation and afforestation of forests.
Question 15. What is a national park?
A protected area where natural vegetation, wildlife and their habitats are given protection is called a national park.
Question 16. What is a wildlife sanctuary?
A protected area where wildlife and their natural habitat are given protection is called a wildlife sanctuary.
Question 17. What is a biosphere reserve?
Large protected areas designated for the conservation of biodiversity and the natural ecosystem are called biosphere reserves.
Question 18. What is Project Tiger?
Project Tiger was launched in 1973 in India to stop the declining number of tigers and for their conservation. Its aim was to conserve tigers and their natural habitat.
Question 19. When was the Wildlife Protection Act enacted?
The Wildlife Protection Act was enacted in India in 1972 for the conservation of wildlife.
Question 20. Into how many major categories have forests in India been divided?
Forests in India are mainly divided into the following three categories—
  1. Reserved forests
  2. Protected forests
  3. Unclassed forests
Question 21. What are reserved forests?
Forests that are considered the most important from a conservation point of view, and in which human activities are strictly restricted, are called reserved forests.
Question 22. What are protected forests?
Forests in which natural vegetation and wildlife are given protection, but some controlled human activities may be permitted, are called protected forests.
Question 23. What are unclassed forests?
Forests and wastelands that do not fall under the category of reserved or protected forests are called unclassed forests.
Question 24. In which state is the largest area of reserved forests found in India?
Madhya Pradesh has a large share of the country's total reserved forests.
Question 25. What is Joint Forest Management?
The participation of local communities in the conservation and management of forests is called Joint Forest Management. Under this, local people cooperate with the forest department in protecting and developing forests.
Question 26. When was the Joint Forest Management programme started in India?
In India, the Joint Forest Management programme was formally implemented in various states following the new forest policy of 1988.
Question 27. What was the Chipko movement?
The Chipko movement was a forest conservation movement in which local people protested by hugging trees to save them from being cut down. This movement promoted forest conservation and environmental awareness.
Question 28. What was the main feature of the Chipko movement?
The main feature of this movement was to protest against the felling of trees in a peaceful manner and to have active participation of local communities in forest conservation.
Question 29. How did people in the Sariska Tiger Project area contribute to forest conservation?
Local villagers protested against illegal hunting and felling of trees, and cooperated in protecting wildlife and forests.
Question 30. What are sacred groves?
Forest areas that local communities consider sacred due to religious or cultural beliefs and keep protected are called sacred groves. In these areas, harming trees and wildlife is generally forbidden.
Question 31. What is the contribution of the Bishnoi community of Rajasthan to forest conservation?
The Bishnoi community has played an important role for a long time in protecting trees and wildlife. Their religious and cultural rules emphasise nature and wildlife conservation.
Question 32. Explain the role of local communities in the conservation of forest and wildlife resources.
Local communities live close to forests and wildlife, so they can play an important role in their conservation. They can help prevent illegal felling and hunting, protect forests, and cooperate in the sustainable use of natural resources.
Question 33. How are human activities harmful to wildlife?
Human activities such as deforestation, expansion of agriculture, establishment of industries, road construction, mining, hunting and pollution destroy the natural habitat of wildlife and cause a decline in their numbers.
Question 34. Why is a joint role of the government and local communities necessary in the conservation of forest and wildlife?
The government carries out conservation through laws, institutional arrangements and protected areas, while local communities, living close to the forests, can cooperate in their daily protection and proper use. Conservation is more effective with the participation of both.
Question 35. Write the main measures for wildlife conservation.
  1. Establishing national parks and sanctuaries.
  2. Banning illegal hunting.
  3. Controlling deforestation.
  4. Conserving the natural habitats of wildlife.
  5. Effectively enforcing forest conservation laws.
  6. Involving local communities in conservation activities.
Question 36. Write the main measures for forest conservation.
  • Carrying out afforestation and reforestation.
  • Banning illegal felling.
  • Protecting forests from forest fires.
  • Promoting controlled grazing.
  • Ensuring the participation of local people.
  • Following forest conservation laws.
Question 37. Explain the main causes of loss of biodiversity.
The main causes of loss of biodiversity are—destruction of natural habitat, deforestation, agricultural and industrial expansion, mining, pollution, hunting of wildlife and illegal trade.
Question 38. Why is the conservation of forest and wildlife resources necessary?
Forests and wildlife are important in maintaining ecological balance. Forests affect soil, climate and the water cycle, and provide habitat to many organisms. Therefore, their conservation is necessary for environmental balance and for future generations.
Question 39. Write the main objectives of Project Tiger.
  • To stop the decline in the number of tigers.
  • To conserve the natural habitat of tigers.
  • To control illegal hunting.
  • To develop safe protected areas for tigers.
Question 40. Explain the importance of public participation in forest and wildlife conservation.
Local people living near forests have direct knowledge about forests and wildlife. If they are involved in conservation activities, it helps in preventing illegal felling and hunting. This makes both the sustainable use and conservation of natural resources possible.
Question 41. What are the main institutional measures created for wildlife conservation in India?
For wildlife conservation in India, the Wildlife Protection Act, national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves and various species conservation projects have been implemented.
Question 42. Give an example of the participation of local people in the conservation of forest resources.
Joint Forest Management is a major example of this. Under it, local communities and the forest department together protect and manage forest areas.
Question 43. Why is it necessary to stop the illegal hunting of wildlife?
Illegal hunting rapidly reduces the number of wildlife, and many species may reach the verge of extinction. This also affects ecological balance. Therefore, it is necessary to stop illegal hunting.
Question 44. Why is the conservation of natural habitat important for wildlife?
Natural habitat provides wildlife with the necessary conditions for food, water, shelter and reproduction. When the habitat is destroyed, the existence of wildlife is threatened.
Question 45. What is the importance of laws in the conservation of forest and wildlife resources?
Laws control the illegal felling of forests, the hunting of wildlife and their illegal trade. Through these, legal protection is provided to protected areas and endangered species.
