Agriculture

Importance of Agriculture: Agriculture is an important base of India's economy. A large part of India's population is directly or indirectly dependent on agriculture. Agriculture provides people with food, raw materials for industries and employment. Agricultural products such as tea, coffee, spices, cotton, jute, etc. are also exported.

Types of Agriculture in India: In India, different types of agriculture are practised based on the climate, soil, rainfall and irrigation facilities of various regions. In India, primitive subsistence farming, intensive subsistence farming, commercial farming and plantation agriculture are mainly found.

Primitive Subsistence Farming: This type of farming is carried out on small plots of land using traditional methods and simple tools. In this, farmers mainly grow crops to meet the needs of their family. Farming by changing location is called shifting cultivation. In north-eastern India, it is known as jhum cultivation. In this, the land is prepared for farming by cutting and burning vegetation. After a few years, when the fertility of the land decreases, farmers move to another piece of land.

Intensive Subsistence Farming: This type of farming is practised in areas where population pressure is high and land is limited. Farmers try to obtain higher output by applying more labour and capital on small plots of land. Irrigation, fertilisers and high-yielding variety seeds are also used.

Commercial Farming: In this type of farming, crops are produced mainly for selling in the market. Modern agricultural technology, improved seeds, chemical fertilisers, pesticides and irrigation are used extensively. Many crops such as wheat, cotton, sugarcane and oilseeds are grown under commercial farming.

Plantation Agriculture: Plantation agriculture is commercial farming carried out on a large scale. In this, a single crop is produced over a large area. Tea, coffee, rubber, sugarcane and banana are its major examples. It requires more capital, labour, technology and processing.

Major Food Crops: Rice, wheat, coarse grains and pulses are the major food crops of India. Rice is mainly grown in areas with high temperature and heavy rainfall. West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Bihar, Assam, Odisha and Chhattisgarh are among its major producing states. Wheat is mainly grown in the cool season, and the north-western region and the Ganga plains are its main areas.

Coarse Grains: Jowar, bajra and ragi are called coarse grains. These can also be grown in areas with less rainfall and contain higher amounts of nutrients. Bajra is grown extensively in states like Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Gujarat and Haryana. Ragi is grown in areas like Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand.

Pulses: Pulses are an important part of India's food and are rich in protein. Arhar, moong, urad, masur and gram are the major pulses. Pulses also help increase soil fertility because bacteria present in the roots of leguminous plants fix atmospheric nitrogen in the soil.

Food and Non-Food Crops: Food crops include rice, wheat, maize, pulses and coarse grains. Non-food crops include crops such as cotton, jute, sugarcane, tea, coffee and rubber. These crops are used as raw material in industries or are traded and exported.

Cotton: Cotton is an important fibre and commercial crop. High temperature, light rainfall and a frost-free period are suitable for growing it. Black soil is particularly suitable for cotton cultivation. Maharashtra, Gujarat, Telangana, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh are among its major producing regions.

Jute: Jute is called the golden fibre. It is mainly grown in hot and humid climate and on flood plains. West Bengal is the leading producer state of jute. Jute is used to make sacks, ropes, mats and other items.

Sugarcane: Sugarcane is an important commercial crop. It is used to make sugar, jaggery (gur), khandsari and molasses. Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Bihar are the major sugarcane producing states in India. Sugarcane cultivation requires a hot climate and sufficient water.

Tea: Tea is a major beverage crop and an important export item. It requires a hot and humid climate, sufficient rainfall throughout the year and well-drained soil. Assam, the Darjeeling hills of West Bengal, Tamil Nadu and Kerala are the major tea producing regions of India.

Coffee: In India, coffee is mainly grown on the hill slopes of South India. Karnataka is India's leading coffee producing state. Coffee is also produced in Kerala and Tamil Nadu. Indian coffee is famous for its quality and is also exported.

Rubber: Rubber grows well in tropical and humid climate. It requires heavy rainfall and high temperature. Kerala is India's leading natural rubber producing state. Rubber is also produced in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and the Andaman-Nicobar Islands.

Agricultural Reforms: After independence, many reforms were carried out in India to increase agricultural production. Land reforms, expansion of irrigation facilities, high-yielding variety seeds, chemical fertilisers, pesticides and modern agricultural techniques were promoted. Due to the Green Revolution, there was a significant increase, particularly in the production of wheat and rice.

Problems of Farmers: Indian agriculture is still affected by problems such as dependence on monsoon, small and fragmented holdings, lack of irrigation, expensive agricultural inputs, debt, market uncertainty and natural calamities. To stabilise farmers' income and agricultural production, it is necessary to expand facilities for irrigation, storage, transport, proper markets and modern technology.

Conclusion: Agriculture is an important base of India's economic and social life. Besides providing food grains, agriculture provides raw materials to industries and employment to millions of people. For the sustainable development of agriculture, it is necessary to conserve water and soil, make proper use of modern technology, diversify crops, and provide farmers with better markets and fair prices.

Question 1. What is meant by agriculture?
Answer: Growing crops on land, animal husbandry and other related activities are together called agriculture.
Question 2. What is the importance of agriculture in India?
Answer: Agriculture is an important base of India's economy. It provides employment to people, supplies food grains and provides raw materials for industries.
Question 3. How many major types of farming systems are found in India?
Answer: In India, primitive subsistence farming, intensive subsistence farming, commercial farming and plantation agriculture are mainly found.
Question 4. What is primitive subsistence farming?
Answer: Farming carried out on small pieces of land using traditional tools and family labour is called primitive subsistence farming. Its main aim is to fulfil the needs of the family.
Question 5. What is shifting cultivation?
Answer: Clearing a part of the forest, farming it for a few years, and then moving to another place to farm when the fertility of the land decreases is called shifting cultivation.
Question 6. By what name is shifting cultivation known in north-eastern India?
Answer: In north-eastern India, shifting cultivation is called 'jhum' cultivation.
Question 7. What is intensive subsistence farming?
Answer: Farming in which higher output is obtained on small plots of land by using more labour and other resources in densely populated areas is called intensive subsistence farming.
Question 8. What is commercial farming?
Answer: Farming carried out with the aim of selling produce in the market is called commercial farming. It uses modern agricultural equipment, improved seeds, chemical fertilisers and pesticides.
Question 9. What is plantation agriculture?
Answer: Farming of a single major crop carried out commercially on a large scale over a large area is called plantation agriculture.
Question 10. What is the major food crop of India?
Answer: The major food crop of India is rice.
Question 11. What kind of climate is required for the cultivation of rice?
Answer: Rice requires high temperature, high humidity and sufficient rainfall. Generally, rainfall of more than 100 cm is considered suitable for its cultivation.
Question 12. Which are the major rice growing areas in India?
Answer: West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Bihar, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Tamil Nadu and Assam are the major rice producing areas.
Question 13. What kind of climate is required for the cultivation of wheat?
Answer: Wheat crop requires a cool growing period and bright sunshine at the time of ripening. Generally, moderate rainfall is favourable for it.
Question 14. Which are the major wheat producing states of India?
Answer: Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan and Bihar are the major wheat producing states.
Question 15. What are coarse grains?
Answer: Grains such as jowar, bajra and ragi are called coarse grains. They contain sufficient amounts of nutrients and minerals.
Question 16. Which are the major jowar growing areas?
Answer: Maharashtra, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu are the major jowar producing states.
Question 17. Which conditions are suitable for the cultivation of bajra?
Answer: Bajra can also be grown in areas with less rainfall. Light soil and a hot climate are suitable for it.
Question 18. What is the importance of ragi?
Answer: Ragi is a good source of nutrients, especially calcium and iron. It is an important drought-resistant crop.
Question 19. What kind of climate is required for the cultivation of maize?
Answer: Maize requires a climate with moderate temperature and sufficient rainfall. It can be grown in various types of soils.
Question 20. Which are the major pulse crops of India?
Answer: Gram, arhar, moong, urad, masur and peas are the major pulse crops of India.
Question 21. What is the importance of pulse crops?
Answer: Pulses are an important source of protein. They also help increase soil fertility by fixing atmospheric nitrogen in the soil.
Question 22. What are oilseeds?
Answer: Crops from which edible oil is obtained are called oilseed crops, such as groundnut, mustard, soybean, sunflower and sesame.
Question 23. Which are the major oilseed crops of India?
Answer: Groundnut, mustard, soybean, sunflower, sesame, castor and linseed are the major oilseed crops.
Question 24. Which are the major groundnut growing states?
Answer: Gujarat, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Rajasthan and Maharashtra are the major groundnut producing states.
Question 25. What kind of climate is required for the cultivation of sugarcane?
Answer: Sugarcane requires a hot and humid climate and sufficient rainfall or irrigation. It has a long growing period.
Question 26. Which are the major sugarcane producing states in India?
Answer: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Bihar, Gujarat and Haryana are the major sugarcane producing states.
Question 27. What kind of climate is required for the cultivation of tea?
Answer: Tea requires a hot and humid climate, sufficient rainfall and well-drained fertile soil.
Question 28. Which are the major tea producing areas in India?
Answer: Assam, the Darjeeling hills of West Bengal, Tamil Nadu and Kerala are the major tea producing areas.
Question 29. Which are the major coffee growing states?
Answer: Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu are the major coffee producing states in India.
Question 30. What kind of climate is required for the cultivation of rubber?
Answer: Rubber requires a hot and humid climate and sufficient rainfall throughout the year.
Question 31. Which is the major rubber producing state in India?
Answer: Kerala is India's leading natural rubber producing state.
Question 32. Which soil is most suitable for the cultivation of cotton?
Answer: Black soil is considered most suitable for the cultivation of cotton.
Question 33. By what name is cotton known in India?
Answer: Cotton is called white gold in India because it is an important raw material for the textile industry.
Question 34. Which are the major cotton producing states in India?
Answer: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Punjab and Haryana are the major cotton producing states.
Question 35. What kind of climate is required for the cultivation of jute?
Answer: Jute requires a hot and humid climate and sufficient rainfall. Alluvial soil is suitable for it.
Question 36. Which are the major jute producing states in India?
Answer: West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Odisha and Meghalaya are the major jute producing states.
Question 37. What are Rabi crops?
Answer: Rabi crops are generally sown in winter and harvested in spring. Wheat, barley, gram, peas and mustard are the major Rabi crops.
Question 38. What are Kharif crops?
Answer: Kharif crops are generally sown at the onset of the monsoon and harvested after the monsoon. Rice, maize, jowar, bajra, cotton and groundnut are the major Kharif crops.
Question 39. What are Zaid crops?
Answer: Crops grown during the summer season between the Rabi and Kharif crops are called Zaid crops. Watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber and some vegetables are its major crops.
Question 40. What are the major problems of agriculture in India?
Answer: Small and fragmented holdings, dependence on the monsoon, lack of irrigation, farmers' economic problems, decrease in soil fertility and market-related problems are the major problems of agriculture.
Question 41. Why is Indian agriculture called a gamble with the monsoon?
Answer: In many regions of India, agriculture depends on monsoon rains due to the lack of adequate irrigation facilities. When rainfall is low or irregular, the crop is affected; hence it is called a gamble with the monsoon.
Question 42. What is the Green Revolution?
Answer: The rapid increase in food grain production due to the use of improved seeds, irrigation, chemical fertilisers, pesticides and modern agricultural techniques is called the Green Revolution.
Question 43. What is agricultural diversification?
Answer: Adopting various crops and other activities related to agriculture instead of depending on only one or a few crops is called agricultural diversification.
Question 44. What is organic farming?
Answer: Farming carried out using organic manure, natural resources and environment-friendly methods, with reduced dependence on chemical fertilisers and pesticides, is called organic farming.
Question 45. What is the importance of irrigation in Indian agriculture?
Answer: Irrigation provides timely water to crops, reduces dependence on the monsoon and increases agricultural production.
Question 46. How are agriculture and industry dependent on each other?
Answer: Agriculture provides raw materials such as cotton, jute, sugarcane and oilseeds to industries, while industries provide farmers with machines, fertilisers, pesticides and other agricultural inputs.
Question 47. What are the major means of modernisation of agriculture?
Answer: Improved seeds, modern agricultural machinery, irrigation, chemical and organic fertilisers, pesticides, agricultural research and better agricultural techniques are the major means of modernisation of agriculture.
Question 48. What is sustainable agriculture?
Answer: Agriculture that meets present needs while conserving soil, water and the environment, and keeps agricultural resources safe for the future, is called sustainable agriculture.
Question 49. Why is the conservation of agriculture necessary?
Answer: Agriculture provides the basis for food security, employment, raw materials for industries and the rural economy. Therefore, conservation of agricultural land and resources is necessary.
Question 50. What measures are necessary for the development of Indian agriculture?
Answer:
  • Expanding irrigation facilities.
  • Promoting modern agricultural techniques.
  • Using improved seeds.
  • Paying attention to soil conservation.
  • Providing farmers with proper markets and storage facilities.
  • Promoting organic and sustainable agriculture.
