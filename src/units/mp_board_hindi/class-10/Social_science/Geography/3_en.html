Unit 3- Water Resources
Water Resources

Water Resources: Water is one of the most important natural resources found on Earth. Human life, agriculture, industry, power generation and other economic activities depend on water. The amount of water on Earth is very large, but most of it exists as saline water in seas and oceans. Fresh and usable water is available in a limited quantity. Therefore, conservation and proper management of water are essential.

Availability of Water in India: The availability of water in India mainly depends on monsoon rainfall. The distribution of rainfall is not uniform across the country. Some regions receive very heavy rainfall, while others receive very little. Because rainfall is seasonal, a large amount of water is received only in a few months of the year. This creates the problem of floods in many regions and water scarcity in others.

Water Crisis: When the demand for water in a region becomes much greater than the water available there, a water crisis arises. Population growth, urbanisation, industrialisation, expansion of agriculture, excessive exploitation of water and increasing domestic needs are the major causes of the water crisis. Excessive use of groundwater has also caused the water table to fall in many regions.

Multi-purpose River Valley Projects: By building dams on rivers, water is used for various purposes. Such projects are called multi-purpose river valley projects. They provide water for irrigation, hydel power generation, flood control, drinking water supply, fish breeding and industries. After independence, many large dams and river valley projects were built in India for development.

Importance of Dams: Dams collect the water of rainfall and rivers so that it can be used when needed. They provide irrigation facilities in dry regions and are used to generate hydel power. Projects such as Bhakra-Nangal, Tehri, Sardar Sarovar and Hirakud are among India's major multi-purpose projects.

Problems Related to Dams: The construction of large dams also gives rise to many social and environmental problems. Building reservoirs submerges large areas of land, which can affect forests, agricultural land and the habitats of wildlife. Many people are forced to leave their homes and land and move to other places. It can also affect the natural flow of rivers and aquatic ecology. For this reason, large dams have been opposed in many places.

Narmada Bachao Andolan: The people affected by the construction of the Sardar Sarovar Project on the Narmada river, especially the displaced people and environmental groups, launched a movement against it. This was called the Narmada Bachao Andolan (Save the Narmada Movement). This movement prominently raised issues related to displacement, rehabilitation and environmental impacts.

Rainwater Harvesting: An important measure to reduce the water crisis is rainwater harvesting. In rainwater harvesting, rainwater is collected for future use, or it is directed underground to raise the groundwater level. This increases the availability of water and reduces pressure on groundwater.

Traditional Water Conservation in India: Various traditional systems of water conservation have been developed in India since ancient times. In Rajasthan, systems such as khadin, tanka and johad were used to collect rainwater. In South India, temple tanks and, in other regions, ponds and lakes have also been important means of water conservation.

Rainwater Harvesting in Tamil Nadu: In Tamil Nadu, rainwater harvesting was made compulsory to promote it. Rainwater from the roofs of houses and buildings is collected through pipes into tanks or underground structures. This helps maintain the groundwater level and reduce the water crisis.

Need for Water Conservation: Water is a limited and extremely valuable resource. Therefore, it is necessary to use water judiciously. Rainwater harvesting, reuse of water, controlled exploitation of groundwater, conservation of ponds and lakes, and preventing water pollution are important measures of water conservation. The use of water-saving irrigation techniques in agriculture is also useful.

Conclusion: Water resources are extremely essential for human life and economic development, but the availability of water is limited and unequal. Due to the growing population and increasing demand for water, the water crisis can become a serious problem. Therefore, conservation, equal distribution and sustainable management of water are essential. Saving water for future generations while meeting the needs of the present generation should be the main objective of proper management of water resources.

Question 1. What is meant by water resources?
Answer: The water available on Earth that humans use for their various needs such as drinking, irrigation, industry, domestic work and power generation is called water resources.
Question 2. Why is water important for us?
Answer: Water is the basis of life. It is used for drinking, agriculture, animal husbandry, industry, domestic work, transportation and hydel power generation.
Question 3. What fraction of the Earth's total water is fresh water?
Answer: Only about 2.5 percent of the Earth's total water is fresh water.
Question 4. What is meant by water scarcity?
Answer: When the amount of water available in a region becomes less than the needs there, it is called water scarcity or water crisis.
Question 5. What are the major causes of water scarcity in India?
Answer: Population growth, urbanisation, industrialisation, excessive irrigation in agriculture, excessive exploitation of groundwater, water pollution and uneven distribution of rainfall are the major causes of water scarcity.
Question 6. Why is the distribution of rainfall uneven in India?
Answer: In India, the distribution of rainfall is uneven due to monsoon winds, topography and geographical conditions. Some regions receive very heavy rainfall while others receive very little.
Question 7. Why are water resources being used excessively?
Answer: Water resources are being used excessively due to the growing population, increasing agricultural needs, industrial development, urbanisation and domestic requirements.
Question 8. What is a multi-purpose river valley project?
Answer: A river valley project in which water is used for irrigation, flood control, hydel power generation, drinking water, fish breeding and several other purposes is called a multi-purpose river valley project.
Question 9. What are the major objectives of multi-purpose river valley projects?
Answer: Their major objectives are irrigation, hydel power generation, flood control, providing drinking water, industrial use, fish breeding and water conservation.
Question 10. On which river is the Bhakra-Nangal project located?
Answer: The Bhakra-Nangal project is located on the Sutlej river.
Question 11. On which river is the Tehri Dam project located?
Answer: The Tehri Dam project is located on the Bhagirathi river.
Question 12. On which river is the Sardar Sarovar project located?
Answer: The Sardar Sarovar project is located on the Narmada river.
Question 13. What is a dam?
Answer: A large structure built to hold back and store the water of a river is called a dam.
Question 14. Write the major benefits of dams.
Answer: Dams provide water for irrigation, hydel power generation, flood control, drinking water supply and industrial use.
Question 15. Why are large dams opposed?
Answer: Large dams can cause problems such as displacement of people, submergence of agricultural land and forests, environmental problems and changes in the natural flow of rivers.
Question 16. What is the Narmada Bachao Andolan?
Answer: The people's movement carried out over the problems faced by people and the environment due to large dams being built on the Narmada river is called the Narmada Bachao Andolan.
Question 17. Who was prominently associated with the Narmada Bachao Andolan?
Answer: Medha Patkar was prominently associated with the Narmada Bachao Andolan.
Question 18. What is meant by water conservation?
Answer: Using water judiciously, preventing it from being wasted, and keeping it safe for the future is called water conservation.
Question 19. What is rainwater harvesting?
Answer: Collecting rainwater for use, or directing it into the ground to raise the groundwater level, is called rainwater harvesting.
Question 20. What is the main objective of rainwater harvesting?
Answer: Its main objective is to conserve rainwater, raise the groundwater level and reduce the water crisis.
Question 21. What is the traditional system of rainwater harvesting in Rajasthan called?
Answer: In Rajasthan, a traditional system such as the tanka is used for rainwater harvesting.
Question 22. What is a tanka?
Answer: A tanka is a traditional underground water-storage structure in which rainwater is collected and kept safe.
Question 23. What is rooftop rainwater harvesting?
Answer: Collecting rainwater falling on the roof of houses or buildings through pipes into a tank, or directing it into groundwater, is called rooftop rainwater harvesting.
Question 24. Why was rainwater harvesting promoted in Tamil Nadu?
Answer: Rainwater harvesting was widely promoted in Tamil Nadu to maintain the groundwater level and reduce the water crisis.
Question 25. What is water pollution?
Answer: When the quality of water deteriorates due to the mixing of harmful substances and it becomes unusable or harmful for humans and other living beings, it is called water pollution.
Question 26. Write the major causes of water pollution.
Answer: Industrial waste, domestic sewage, agricultural chemicals, plastic, garbage and other pollutants are the major causes of water pollution.
Question 27. How can water pollution be prevented?
Answer: Water pollution can be reduced by treating industrial and domestic waste, preventing garbage from being dumped into rivers, limiting the use of chemical fertilisers and pesticides, and through water conservation.
Question 28. What are the major causes of the falling groundwater level?
Answer: Excessive exploitation of groundwater, excessive irrigation, urbanisation, low rainfall and excessive use of water are the major causes of the falling groundwater level.
Question 29. What role do forests play in water conservation?
Answer: Forests help direct rainwater into the ground, prevent soil erosion and maintain the groundwater level.
Question 30. What measures can be taken to conserve water resources?
Answer:
  • Carrying out rainwater harvesting.
  • Using water in a limited and judicious manner.
  • Preventing excessive exploitation of groundwater.
  • Controlling water pollution.
  • Conserving trees, plants and forests.
  • Using modern irrigation techniques.
Question 31. How does rainwater harvesting help in removing the water crisis?
Answer: Rainwater harvesting conserves rainwater and recharges groundwater. This increases the availability of water and reduces the water crisis.
Question 32. Why is the need for water for irrigation increasing in India?
Answer: The need for irrigation is increasing due to population growth, expansion of the agricultural sector, uncertainty of rainfall, and the need to obtain higher production.
Question 33. What is hydel power?
Answer: Electricity generated from the energy of flowing or falling water is called hydel power.
Question 34. Why were multi-purpose projects called the temples of modern India?
Answer: Pandit Jawaharlal Nehru called multi-purpose river valley projects the temples of modern India because they promote agriculture, industry, power generation and economic development.
Question 35. Write the social problems caused by large dams.
Answer: Large dams can cause social problems such as displacement of people, the problem of rehabilitation, loss of livelihood and changes in the lives of local communities.
Question 36. Write the environmental problems caused by large dams.
Answer: Problems such as submergence of forests and agricultural land, loss of biodiversity, changes in soil and the natural flow of rivers, and ecological imbalance can arise.
Question 37. Why is public participation necessary in the conservation of water resources?
Answer: Water conservation is not possible through government efforts alone. With people's participation, the proper use of water, rainwater harvesting and protection of water sources can be carried out more effectively.
Question 38. How can water be used judiciously?
Answer: Water can be used judiciously by not leaving taps running unnecessarily, fixing leaks, harvesting rainwater and using water-saving techniques in irrigation.
Question 39. Why is traditional knowledge important for water conservation?
Answer: Traditional water-conservation systems have developed according to local conditions. They have the ability to collect rainwater and recharge groundwater at low cost.
Question 40. What is the most important measure for the conservation of water resources?
Answer: Proper use of water, rainwater harvesting, groundwater recharge and control of water pollution are important measures for the conservation of water resources.
