Unit 1: Resource and Development

The chapter Resource and Development deals with the importance of natural and human resources, their classification, planning, conservation and sustainable development. Things available in our environment that satisfy human needs, are technologically accessible, economically feasible and culturally acceptable are called resources. Resources are classified on the basis of their origin, exhaustibility, ownership and status of development. On the basis of origin, resources are classified as biotic and abiotic; on the basis of exhaustibility as renewable and non-renewable; on the basis of ownership as individual, community, national and international; and on the basis of the status of development as potential, developed, stock and reserve.

The distribution of resources in India is not uniform. Some regions are rich in a particular resource, while some regions are deficient in that resource. Hence, resource planning is essential for the proper utilisation of resources. Resource planning involves identification and surveying of resources, preparing suitable techniques and plans for their use, and matching resource development plans with overall national development plans. Indiscriminate use of resources creates problems such as environmental pollution, depletion of resources, land degradation and ecological imbalance. Therefore, meeting the needs of the present while safeguarding resources for future generations is called sustainable development.

Land is an important natural resource. It is used for agriculture, forests, pastures, industries, transport and human settlements. Land use is influenced by physical factors such as topography, climate, soil and water, as well as human factors such as population, technology and economic activities. Land degradation occurs due to excessive use of land, deforestation, overgrazing, mining, industrial waste and faulty agricultural practices. Land degradation can be reduced through afforestation, controlled grazing, proper irrigation, rehabilitation of mining areas and proper disposal of industrial waste.

Soil is the thin top fertile layer of the earth's surface, which contains minerals, organic matter, water and air. Parent rock, climate, topography, vegetation, fauna and time play an important role in soil formation. In India, mainly alluvial, black, red and yellow, laterite, arid and forest and mountain soils are found. Alluvial soil is found in the northern plains and river valleys and is extremely fertile for agriculture. Black soil retains moisture for a long time and is suitable for cotton cultivation. Red and yellow soil is found in peninsular India. Laterite soil forms in areas of high temperature and heavy rainfall. Arid soil is mainly found in Rajasthan and dry regions, while forest and mountain soil is found in hilly and forested areas.

The removal of the upper fertile layer of soil by water, wind and other natural forces is called soil erosion. Deforestation, overgrazing, faulty agricultural practices and fast-flowing water and wind are its main causes. Measures such as contour ploughing, strip cropping, terrace farming, afforestation and controlled grazing are adopted to check soil erosion. Thus, the main message of the chapter is that resources are extremely essential for human life and economic development, but only their judicious use, proper planning and conservation can ensure safe and balanced development for present and future generations.

Question 1. What is a resource?
All those things, substances, forces and means available in nature which human beings use to satisfy their needs are called resources.
Question 2. On what basis are resources classified?
Resources are classified on the following bases—
  1. On the basis of origin
  2. On the basis of exhaustibility
  3. On the basis of ownership
  4. On the basis of status of development
Question 1. Classify resources on the basis of origin.
Answer: On the basis of origin, resources are of two types—
  1. Biotic resources: Resources obtained from the biosphere, such as flora, fauna and human beings.
  2. Abiotic resources: Resources obtained from non-living things, such as water, minerals and rocks.
Question 3. What are biotic and abiotic resources?
Biotic resources: Resources obtained from the biosphere are called biotic resources. For example—flora, fauna and human beings.

Abiotic resources: Resources obtained from non-living things are called abiotic resources. For example—water, air, rocks and minerals.
Question 2. Classify resources on the basis of exhaustibility.
Answer: On the basis of exhaustibility, resources are of two types—
  1. Renewable resources: Resources which can be replenished by natural processes, such as solar energy, wind energy and water.
  2. Non-renewable resources: Resources which occur in a limited quantity and take a very long time to form, such as coal, petroleum and minerals.
Question 4. Differentiate between renewable and non-renewable resources.
Renewable resources: Resources which can be regenerated by natural processes in a relatively short time. For example—solar energy, wind energy and water.

Non-renewable resources: Resources which take a very long time to form and whose stock is limited. For example—coal, petroleum and natural gas.
Question 3. Classify resources on the basis of ownership.
Answer: On the basis of ownership, resources are of four types—
  1. Individual resources: Resources owned by an individual, such as farms and houses.
  2. Community resources: Resources used by all members of a community, such as public parks and playgrounds.
  3. National resources: Resources over which the nation has rights, such as forests, rivers and minerals.
  4. International resources: Resources which are not owned by any single country and are used according to international rules.
Question 5. What are individual and community resources?
Individual resources: Resources owned by an individual are called individual resources. For example—houses, farms and wells.

Community resources: Resources which can be used by all members of a community are called community resources. For example—pastures, public parks and ponds.
Question 4. Classify resources on the basis of status of development.
Answer: On the basis of status of development, resources are of four types—
  1. Potential resources: Resources whose presence is known, but which have not yet been fully utilised.
  2. Developed resources: Resources which have been surveyed and whose quantity and quality have been determined.
  3. Stock: Resources which can be used with the present technology, but are kept in reserve for the future.
  4. Reserve: Resources which can be used with present technology, but their use has been kept aside for future needs.
Question 6. What are national resources?
All resources found within the political boundary of a country are called national resources. For example—forests, minerals, rivers and roads.
Question 7. What are potential resources?
Resources whose availability is known in a region but which are not currently being fully utilised are called potential resources.
Question 8. What are developed resources?
Resources which have been surveyed, whose quantity and quality have been determined, and which are being used with present technology, are called developed resources.
Question 9. What is resource planning?
The planned process of finding out the availability of resources and then their proper utilisation, development and conservation is called resource planning.
Question 10. Why is resource planning necessary in India?
The distribution of resources in India is uneven. Some regions have abundant minerals, while others have more water or forest resources. Therefore, resource planning is necessary for the proper utilisation of resources and balanced regional development.
Question 11. Write the main stages of resource planning.
  1. Identifying and surveying resources.
  2. Preparing a map of the availability of resources.
  3. Formulating plans for the development of resources.
  4. Arranging technological, economic and institutional means.
  5. Implementing the plan at the appropriate level.
Question 12. What is sustainable development?
Development that meets the needs of the present generation without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs is called sustainable development.
Question 13. What problems arise from the indiscriminate use of resources?
  • Rapid depletion of resources.
  • Increase in environmental pollution.
  • Land degradation.
  • Loss of biodiversity.
  • Increase in climate-related problems.
  • Shortage of resources for future generations.
Question 14. Why is land an important resource?
Land is an important natural resource because many human activities such as agriculture, forestry, industry, housing, roads and transport depend on land.
Question 15. What are the main factors affecting land use?
The main factors affecting land use are—
  • Topography
  • Climate
  • Soil
  • Population
  • Technology
  • Economic development
Question 16. What is land degradation?
The process by which the quality and productive capacity of land decreases is called land degradation.
Question 17. Write the main causes of land degradation.
  • Deforestation
  • Overgrazing
  • Mining
  • Over-irrigation
  • Industrial waste
  • Excessive use of chemical substances
Question 18. Write the measures to check land degradation.
  • Afforestation.
  • Controlled grazing.
  • Adopting scientific irrigation.
  • Proper disposal of industrial waste.
  • Reforestation of mining areas.
  • Adopting land conservation techniques.
Question 19. What is soil?
The thin layer found on the upper surface of the earth in which the growth of plants is possible is called soil.
Question 20. What are the factors affecting soil formation?
  • Parent rock
  • Climate
  • Topography
  • Vegetation and fauna
  • Time
Question 21. Name the major soils of India.
  1. Alluvial soil
  2. Black soil
  3. Red and yellow soil
  4. Laterite soil
  5. Arid and desert soil
  6. Forest and mountain soil
Question 22. Write the main characteristics of alluvial soil.
  • It is formed from the silt brought by rivers.
  • It is mainly found in the northern plains and river delta regions.
  • It is extremely suitable for agriculture.
  • It contains adequate amounts of potash and lime.
  • It is useful for crops such as wheat, rice and sugarcane.
Question 23. Differentiate between Khadar and Bangar.
Khadar Bangar
This is new alluvial soil. This is old alluvial soil.
It is found in flood plains. It is found in relatively higher areas.
It is more fertile. Pebbles may be found in it.
Question 24. Write the main characteristics of black soil.
  • Black soil is also called Regur soil.
  • It is very suitable for cotton cultivation.
  • It has a high moisture-retaining capacity.
  • It is found in many parts of the Deccan Plateau.
  • It contains lime, iron and magnesium.
Question 25. Where is red and yellow soil found?
Red and yellow soil is mainly found in the eastern and southern parts of the Deccan Plateau. It is found in regions such as Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha and Jharkhand.
Question 26. Write the main characteristics of laterite soil.
  • It forms in areas of heavy rainfall and high temperature.
  • It undergoes the process of leaching.
  • It generally has a low humus content.
  • With proper manure and fertiliser use, it can become useful for agriculture.
Question 27. What is soil erosion?
The process by which the fertile upper layer of soil is removed by natural forces such as water and wind is called soil erosion.
Question 29. What is gully erosion?
When flowing water cuts into the soil and forms deep channels, it is called gully erosion.
Question 30. What is sheet erosion?
When rainwater washes away the upper fertile soil of the land as a thin layer, it is called sheet erosion.
Question 28. Write the main causes of soil erosion.
  • Deforestation
  • Overgrazing
  • Faulty agricultural practices
  • Fast-flowing water
  • Strong winds
  • Mining and construction work
Question 1. What are the harmful effects of soil erosion? (What are the harmful effects of soil erosion?)
Answer: The main harmful effects of soil erosion are as follows—
  1. The fertile upper layer of soil is destroyed.
  2. The fertility of land and agricultural production decrease.
  3. The deposition of soil in rivers and reservoirs reduces their water-holding capacity.
  4. The extent of wasteland may increase.
  5. Soil erosion affects the balance of the environment.
Question 31. Write the main measures for soil conservation.
  1. Afforestation.
  2. Contour ploughing.
  3. Terrace farming.
  4. Strip cropping.
  5. Controlled grazing.
  6. Building shelter belts.
  7. Controlling the flow of water.
Question 32. What is terrace farming?
The method of dividing land on hill slopes into small flat sections like steps for cultivation is called terrace farming. This reduces soil erosion.
Question 33. What is contour ploughing?
The method of ploughing along contour lines on sloping land is called contour ploughing. It slows down the flow of water and reduces soil erosion.
Question 34. Why is the conservation of resources necessary?
Resources are limited and their distribution is also uneven. Their excessive use creates environmental problems. Therefore, conservation of resources is necessary to meet the needs of both present and future generations.
Question 35. Where was the 1992 Earth Summit held?
The 1992 Earth Summit was held in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.
Question 36. What is Agenda 21?
Agenda 21 is a comprehensive action plan adopted for sustainable development, aimed at promoting economic and social development along with environmental protection.
Question 37. Explain the measures to check soil erosion.
To check soil erosion, measures such as afforestation, contour ploughing, terrace farming, strip cropping, controlled grazing and the construction of shelter belts are adopted.
Question 38. Explain the causes of land degradation and the measures for its conservation.
Causes: Deforestation, overgrazing, mining, over-irrigation and industrial waste are the main causes of land degradation.

Measures: Land degradation can be reduced through afforestation, controlled grazing, scientific irrigation, proper management of industrial waste and reforestation of mining areas.
Question 39. Why is a balance between resource development and environmental conservation necessary?
Resources are necessary for human development, but their excessive exploitation can harm the environment. Therefore, it is necessary to use resources judiciously while conserving the environment, so that the needs of both present and future generations can be met.
