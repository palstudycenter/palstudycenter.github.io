The Making of a Global World

The Making of a Global World: Globalisation means the growing contact between trade, people, capital, goods, technology and ideas among different countries and regions of the world. Modern globalisation did not happen suddenly; rather, its beginnings go back a long way. Since ancient times, trade and cultural contacts have kept being established between different regions.

Ancient trade and the Silk Route: In ancient times, trade routes developed between different regions of Asia, Europe and Africa. The Silk Route was a network of such important routes that connected different parts of Asia with Europe and other regions. Silk, spices, textiles, pottery and other goods were traded through these routes. Religion, culture and ideas also travelled from one place to another through them.

Food and global contact: Food also played an important role in connecting different parts of the world. Many crops such as potatoes, maize, tomatoes, soya and chillies travelled from America to other parts of the world. These food items changed people's diet and agriculture. Crops like the potato also helped improve the diet of the poor in Europe.

Discovery of America: At the end of the fifteenth century, contact between Europe and America increased. After the discovery of America, gold, silver and other resources reached Europe from there. European powers established colonies in America. This led to the expansion of world trade and economic ties between different continents grew rapidly.

The world economy in the nineteenth century: In the nineteenth century, the flow of trade, capital and labour played an important role in the development of the world economy. Industrialisation increased in Europe, raising the need for raw materials and new markets for industries. The development of railways, steamships and communication made long-distance trade faster and cheaper.

Corn Laws and Britain: Because of the Corn Laws in Britain, restrictions and duties were imposed on the import of grain. As industrial and urban areas grew, the need for cheap food grains increased. After the Corn Laws were abolished, the import of cheap food grains into Britain increased. This brought about important changes in the British economy and world agricultural trade.

Migration and the flow of labour: In the nineteenth century, millions of people moved from one country to another in search of employment. Large numbers of people from Europe went and settled in regions such as America and Australia. Labourers from India and China were also taken to work in various colonies. In this way, the international flow of labour became an important part of the world economy.

Indentured labour: Indentured labourers were workers who were sent to work in other countries and colonies on the basis of a contract for a fixed period. Indian indentured labourers were sent to Mauritius, Fiji, the Caribbean islands, Sri Lanka, Malaya and other regions. They mainly came from Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, central India and some regions of south India.

Condition of indentured labourers: These labourers often had to work in difficult conditions. They faced many hardships due to low wages, long working hours and poor living conditions. After the contract ended, some labourers settled there permanently and became part of the local society and culture.

Colonialism and the world economy: European powers established their control over many regions of Asia and Africa. Raw materials were obtained from the colonies, and markets were created there for goods manufactured in Europe. This had a deep impact on the traditional economies and industries of the colonies.

First World War: The First World War began in 1914. It was different from earlier wars because modern industry and technology were used on a massive scale in it. Machine guns, tanks, aircraft, submarines and other modern weapons made the war extremely destructive. During the war, a large number of soldiers and civilians were affected, and the world economy suffered a serious impact.

Economic effects of the war: The First World War affected the production and trade of European countries. Governments spent huge amounts of money and took loans for the war. After the war, America emerged as a major lender and economic power. New economic relationships developed in the world economy.

The Great Depression: In 1929, a severe economic crisis began in the world economy, which is called the Great Depression. There was a sharp fall in industrial production and agricultural prices, unemployment increased and trade declined. Many banks and businesses closed down. Its effect was felt in America, Europe and other parts of the world.

Effect of the Great Depression on India: In India, the Great Depression caused a sharp fall in the prices of agricultural products. Farmers' incomes fell, but the burden of their debts and land revenue continued. People dependent on agriculture especially faced hardships. It also affected export and import trade.

Second World War: The Second World War began in 1939. This war once again affected the world economy. During the war, the demand for industrial production and military supplies increased. After the war, the balance of political and economic power in the world changed, and the position of European countries weakened.

Bretton Woods system: After the Second World War, the Bretton Woods conference was held in 1944 to stabilise and organise the world economy. As a result, institutions such as the International Monetary Fund (IMF) and the World Bank were established. The purpose of these institutions was to help with economic stability and reconstruction.

The new international economy after the war: After the Second World War, new institutions and systems were developed to increase world trade and economic cooperation. America emerged as a leading economic power. Many countries in Asia and Africa gradually gained independence from colonial rule and moved forward in the direction of developing their own economies.

Modern globalisation: In the final decades of the twentieth century, the process of globalisation became very fast. Rapid development took place in transport and communication technology. Computers, the internet, mobile communication, faster transport and modern production techniques connected different parts of the world more closely. Multinational companies increased production and investment in various countries.

Multinational companies: Multinational companies are companies that carry out production or trade in more than one country. They set up factories, offices and investments in different countries. Cheap labour, raw materials, markets and favourable government policies attract them to set up production in different countries.

Importance of globalisation: Globalisation has increased trade, investment, technology and contact between people across countries. This has increased the availability of goods, spread new technologies and created new opportunities for employment and production in many countries. But its benefits have not reached all countries and all sections of society equally.

Conclusion: The making of a global world is the result of a long historical process. Starting from ancient trade routes to industrialisation, colonialism, migration, world wars, the Great Depression and the Bretton Woods system, many events have influenced the world economy. In modern times, technology, communication, transport and multinational companies have further accelerated globalisation. Therefore, to understand the present global economy, it is necessary to understand its historical development.

Question 1. How was global contact established in ancient times?
Answer: Contact between different regions was established through trade, travel, sea routes, migration and cultural exchange.
Question 2. What was the Silk Route?
Answer: The Silk Route was a network of trade routes through which goods and ideas were exchanged between Asia, Europe and other regions.
Question 3. What is meant by the Silk Route?
Answer: The Silk Route was a network of ancient trade routes that connected Asia, Europe and Africa. It was mainly used to carry Chinese silk to Europe.
Question 4. What goods were traded through the Silk Route?
Answer: Pottery and silk were traded from China, while textiles and spices were traded from India and Southeast Asia. In return, gold and silver reached Asia from Europe.
Question 5. How did food contribute to connecting the world?
Answer: Food items and crops from different regions travelled from one place to another. This brought about major changes in people's diet and agriculture.
Question 6. Which food items did our ancestors not have 500 years ago?
Answer: Many food items such as potatoes, soya, groundnuts, maize, tomatoes, chillies and sweet potatoes were unknown to our ancestors about 500 years ago.
Question 7. What is meant by the "shrinking" of the world in the sixteenth century?
Answer: In the sixteenth century, European sailors discovered sea routes to Asia and crossed the Atlantic Ocean to reach America. This increased trade and contact between countries, and the world came closer together.
Question 8. What effect did the discovery of America have on the world economy?
Answer: Gold, silver and new crops reached Europe from America. This led to the expansion of international trade and economic activities.
Question 9. What was the Corn Law?
Answer: The Corn Laws were import duties and other trade restrictions enforced in Britain from 1815 to 1846, aimed at controlling the import of foreign food grains.
Question 10. Why did the world economy grow in the 19th century?
Answer: The world economy expanded rapidly due to industrialisation, the expansion of trade, railways, steamships and new technologies.
Question 11. What is meant by Import Duty?
Answer: The tax levied by customs authorities on goods imported into a country is called import duty.
Question 12. Who are called Indentured Labourers?
Answer: Workers who worked for an employer on the basis of a contract for a fixed period were called indentured labourers.
Question 13. Which regions did most Indian indentured labourers come from?
Answer: Most Indian indentured labourers came from present-day eastern Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, central India and the dry districts of Tamil Nadu.
Question 14. To which regions did Indian labourers go?
Answer: Indian labourers went to the Caribbean islands, Mauritius, Fiji and other regions to work on plantations, in mines and in construction work.
Question 15. What is Hosay?
Answer: Hosay is a festive form of the annual Muharram procession in Trinidad. It was linked to the memory of Imam Hussain.
Question 16. What is meant by Flow of Labour?
Answer: In the 19th century, large numbers of labourers moved from one country to another in search of work. This international movement of labourers for work on plantations, in mines, and in the construction of roads and railways is called the flow of labour.
Question 17. What were Canal Colonies?
Answer: The settlements established on fertile land in Punjab irrigated by new canals were called Canal Colonies.
Question 18. What is the Exchange Rate System?
Answer: The rate at which one currency is converted into another is called the exchange rate. The system based on this rate between different currencies is called the exchange rate system.
Question 19. What is meant by the Hire-Purchase System?
Answer: This was a system in which the buyer received the goods immediately and paid for them in weekly or monthly instalments.
Question 20. Who was the first car manufacturer and what was the model of the car?
Answer: The first car manufacturer was Karl Benz, and his first car model was the Benz Patent Motorwagen.
Question 21. What are Multinational Companies?
Answer: Large companies that carry out trade and production in several countries at the same time are called multinational companies.
Question 22. What was the Great Depression?
Answer: The severe global economic slowdown that began in 1929 is called the Great Depression. It caused a sharp fall in production, trade and employment.
Question 23. When did the Great Depression begin and how long did it last?
Answer: The Great Depression began around 1929 and lasted till the mid-1930s.
Question 24. What effect did the Great Depression have on India?
Answer: The prices of agricultural products fell and farmers' incomes declined. The export of agricultural products was also adversely affected.
Question 25. Who were the Allied Nations in the First World War?
Answer: The Allied Nations in the First World War mainly included Britain, France and Russia. Later, America also joined the Allied Nations.
Question 26. Who were the Central Powers in the First World War?
Answer: The Central Powers mainly included Germany, Austria-Hungary and Ottoman Turkey.
Question 27. "The First World War was the first modern industrial war." Explain.
Answer: In the First World War, modern industrial weapons such as machine guns, tanks, aircraft and chemical weapons were used on a massive scale. Millions of soldiers and huge amounts of industrial resources were used. Hence, it is called the first modern industrial war.
Question 28. "The First World War was completely different from wars fought before it." Explain.
Answer: In this war, modern weapons and technology were used on a massive scale. Millions of soldiers were transported to the battlefields by rail and ships, and there was a massive loss of life and property.
Question 29. Why was the First World War called an industrial war?
Answer: Because weapons made using industrial technology, such as machine guns, tanks, aircraft and chemical weapons, were used on a massive scale, and industries and transport systems were extensively used for the war.
Question 30. Who were the Allied Powers and the Axis Powers in the Second World War?
Answer: The Axis Powers mainly consisted of Germany, Japan and Italy. The main Allied Powers were Britain, France, the Soviet Union and America.
Question 31. Who was Sir Henry Morton Stanley and why was he famous?
Answer: Sir Henry Morton Stanley was a journalist and explorer. He became famous for his expeditions and explorations in Africa. His explorations and mapping work later helped in the expansion of European control over Africa.
Question 32. What was the Bretton Woods Agreement?
Answer: The Bretton Woods system was a system created after the Second World War to establish international economic stability and economic cooperation. Its purpose was to stabilise international trade and the financial system.
Question 33. What changes took place in the world economy after the Second World War?
Answer: International economic cooperation increased, and new institutions and systems were developed to organise world trade.
Question 34. What is Veto Power?
Answer: The permanent members of the United Nations Security Council have a special right to block an important resolution, which is called veto power.
Question 35. Which technologies promoted the modern form of globalisation?
Answer: The internet, modern communication, faster transport and information technology promoted modern globalisation.
Question 36. What is meant by Globalisation?
Answer: The process of growing exchange of trade, capital, technology, people and goods between different countries is called globalisation.
