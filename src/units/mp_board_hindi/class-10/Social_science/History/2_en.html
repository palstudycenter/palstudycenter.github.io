Nationalism in India

Rise of Nationalism in India: Modern nationalism in India developed during the struggle against colonial rule. After the First World War, problems such as economic hardship, rising prices, increased taxes, forced military recruitment and epidemics increased discontent among the people. At this time, Mahatma Gandhi tried to bring together different classes into the freedom movement through Satyagraha and non-violence.

Satyagraha: Satyagraha means opposing injustice on the basis of truth and non-violence. According to Mahatma Gandhi, a Satyagrahi should face injustice without violence. In India, Gandhiji first practised Satyagraha in 1917 in Champaran in favour of peasants, in 1917 in Kheda for peasants, and in 1918 in Ahmedabad in support of mill workers.

Rowlatt Act: In 1919, the British government passed the Rowlatt Act. This law gave the government the power to arrest and detain people without trial. Indians considered it an unjust law and there was widespread protest against it across the country. Gandhiji began a Satyagraha against it.

Jallianwala Bagh Massacre: On 13 April 1919, a large number of people gathered at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar on the occasion of Baisakhi. General Dyer ordered the crowd present there to be fired upon. This incident shook the whole country and increased people's opposition to British rule even further.

Khilafat Movement: After the First World War, Indian Muslims were discontented with British policy regarding the Khalifa of Turkey. The Ali brothers led the Khilafat Movement forward. With the aim of strengthening Hindu-Muslim unity, Mahatma Gandhi tried to link the Khilafat Movement with the Non-Cooperation Movement.

Non-Cooperation Movement: In 1920, Gandhiji started the Non-Cooperation Movement against British rule. Its aim was to end cooperation with the government by boycotting British institutions and goods. Government schools, colleges, courts, government jobs, foreign goods and other institutions were boycotted. People were called upon to use swadeshi (indigenous) goods.

Withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement: In February 1922, a violent incident took place between protesters and the police at Chauri Chaura in Uttar Pradesh. Gandhiji was deeply saddened by this incident and withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement because he wanted to keep the movement non-violent.

Different Forms of the Non-Cooperation Movement: In cities, students boycotted government schools and colleges, lawyers boycotted courts, and people boycotted foreign goods. In rural areas, peasants launched movements against zamindars and colonial rule. In tribal areas too, people protested against forest laws and colonial policies.

Simon Commission: In 1928, the Simon Commission came to India. It did not have a single Indian member, so Indians opposed it widely and raised slogans of 'Simon Go Back'. Lala Lajpat Rai also led the protest against it. He died after being injured in a police lathi-charge.

Demand for Purna Swaraj: In December 1929, a session of the Congress was held in Lahore under the presidency of Jawaharlal Nehru. In this session, Purna Swaraj (complete independence) was declared as the goal of the Congress. 26 January 1930 was observed as Independence Day, and Indians took a pledge to achieve complete independence.

Civil Disobedience Movement: In 1930, Mahatma Gandhi started the Civil Disobedience Movement. Its purpose was to peacefully violate unjust colonial laws. Gandhiji decided to break the Salt Law because salt was an essential item for every person, and government control over it was considered unjust.

Dandi March and Salt Satyagraha: Gandhiji began his journey from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi on 12 March 1930. He reached Dandi with his companions and broke the Salt Law on 6 April 1930. This event played an important role in spreading the Civil Disobedience Movement widely across the country.

Participation of Different Classes in the Civil Disobedience Movement: Peasants, merchants, women, workers and various social groups took part in the movement. Peasants raised their voice against problems related to rent and revenue. Merchants supported the boycott of foreign goods. Women played an active role in processions, pickets and the Salt Satyagraha.

Dalits and the Nationalist Movement: The question of the political and social rights of Dalits was also important in the national movement. Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar was demanding political representation and social equality for Dalits. In 1932, an agreement between Gandhiji and Dr. Ambedkar led to the Poona Pact, under which reserved seats were provided for the Depressed Classes in legislative assemblies.

Hindu-Muslim Unity and the National Movement: Hindu-Muslim unity was considered important in the early national movements. During the Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movements, both communities opposed British rule together. In later years, this unity weakened due to communal differences and differing political interests.

The Spirit of Nationalism in India: Nationalism developed not only through political movements but also through cultural symbols, folk songs, folk tales, history and images. The image of Bharat Mata, national songs, and glorious events of Indian history strengthened the feeling of national unity among the people.

The Image of Bharat Mata: The famous painting of Bharat Mata was created by Abanindranath Tagore in 1905. Bharat Mata was depicted as an ascetic figure. This symbol played an important role in enabling people to see India as a mother and in increasing emotional attachment towards the country.

Folk Songs and Folk Tales: Nationalist leaders and writers tried to revive Indian culture and traditions by collecting Indian folk songs and folk tales. This created a sense of pride in their cultural heritage among the people and promoted nationalist ideas.

The Broad Significance of the National Movement: The development of nationalism in India occurred through the participation of various social and economic groups. Peasants, workers, merchants, students, women, tribal people and other groups contributed to the freedom struggle. Although the demands of different groups were different, the struggle against colonial rule still strengthened the feeling of national unity among them.

Conclusion: The development of nationalism in India came about through opposition to British colonial rule and the struggle to achieve independence. Mahatma Gandhi united the people through movements such as Satyagraha, non-violence, Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience. The participation of various social classes, national symbols, folk culture and the spirit of freedom strengthened Indian nationalism and ultimately laid an important foundation towards India's independence.

Question 1. What impact did the First World War have on the development of nationalism in India?
Answer: Because of the war, taxes increased, prices rose, forced military recruitment took place and economic hardships increased. This increased discontent among the people against British rule.
Question 2. What is meant by Satyagraha?
Answer: Satyagraha means opposing injustice on the basis of truth and non-violence.
Question 3. When did Mahatma Gandhi return to India?
Answer: Mahatma Gandhi returned to India from South Africa in January 1915.
Question 4. When did the Champaran Satyagraha take place?
Answer: The Champaran Satyagraha took place in 1917 in Bihar. It was a movement against the problems of peasants related to indigo cultivation.
Question 5. Why did the Kheda Satyagraha take place?
Answer: Due to crop failure, the peasants were not in a position to pay the rent. They demanded relief in the rent.
Question 6. What was the Rowlatt Act?
Answer: The Rowlatt Act of 1919 gave the British government the power to arrest and detain people without trial.
Question 7. When did the Jallianwala Bagh massacre take place?
Answer: The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place on 13 April 1919 in Amritsar.
Question 8. When did the Non-Cooperation Movement begin?
Answer: The Non-Cooperation Movement began in 1920 under the leadership of Mahatma Gandhi.
Question 9. Why was the Non-Cooperation Movement withdrawn?
Answer: After the violent incident at Chauri Chaura in February 1922, Mahatma Gandhi withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement.
Question 10. When did the Civil Disobedience Movement begin?
Answer: The Civil Disobedience Movement began in 1930 with the Dandi March and the breaking of the Salt Law.
Question 11. What was the Dandi March?
Answer: Mahatma Gandhi protested against the Salt Law by marching from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi. This is called the Dandi March or the Salt March.
Question 12. Which were the major symbols that strengthened the spirit of nationalism in India?
Answer: The image of Bharat Mata, folk songs, folk tales, flags, national history and Indian culture strengthened the spirit of nationalism.
Question 13. Describe some of the Satyagrahas started by Gandhiji.
Answer: Gandhiji conducted Satyagraha in 1917 in Champaran in support of peasants engaged in indigo cultivation, in 1917 in Kheda on the issue of rent faced by peasants, and in 1918 in Ahmedabad in support of textile mill workers.
Question 14. Why were Indians angry with the Rowlatt Act?
Answer: The Rowlatt Act gave the government the power to suppress political activities and detain political prisoners without trial. Therefore, Indians were opposing it.
Question 15. Write a brief note on the Jallianwala Bagh massacre.
Answer: On 13 April 1919, General Dyer ordered firing on the crowd gathered at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar. A large number of people were killed in this incident, and anger against British rule increased throughout the country.
Question 16. Write a brief note on the Simon Commission.
Answer: The Simon Commission was formed by the British government to review the constitutional system of India. It did not have a single Indian member. When it arrived in India in 1928, it was opposed with the slogan 'Simon Go Back'.
Question 17. What was the Khilafat Movement?
Answer: The Khilafat Movement was a movement launched by Indian Muslims after the First World War to protect the position and powers of the Khalifa of Turkey. Gandhiji tried to strengthen Hindu-Muslim unity by linking it with the Non-Cooperation Movement.
Question 18. Write any four reasons for starting the Non-Cooperation Movement.
Answer: The main reasons for the Non-Cooperation Movement were opposition to the Rowlatt Act, the Jallianwala Bagh massacre, support for the Khilafat Movement, and the desire to attain Swaraj.
Question 19. Which goods and institutions were instructed to be boycotted during the Non-Cooperation Movement?
Answer: Instructions were given to boycott government posts and jobs, the army, police, courts, legislative councils, government schools and foreign goods.
Question 20. Define Boycott and Picketing.
Answer: Boycott means deliberately refusing to have any connection with a person, activity or item, or not buying it. Picketing means demonstrating or protesting at the entrance of a shop, factory or office.
Question 21. What role did rural and tribal areas play in the Non-Cooperation Movement?
Answer: In rural areas, peasants demanded a reduction in rent, the abolition of begar (forced labour) and the social boycott of oppressive zamindars. Tribal people took part in the movement by opposing forest laws and forced labour.
Question 22. List the various social groups involved in the Non-Cooperation Movement and describe the hopes and struggles of any three groups.
Answer: The urban middle class, peasants, tribal people, peasants of Awadh, members of the Khilafat committee, plantation workers of Assam, and Congress leaders and members were involved in the Non-Cooperation Movement. The urban middle class boycotted government schools, colleges and courts. Peasants opposed rent and begar. Plantation workers of Assam tried to leave the plantations and return to their villages.
Question 23. Why was the Inland Emigration Act of 1859 a cause of trouble for the plantation workers of Assam?
Answer: Due to this Act, plantation workers could not leave the tea plantations without permission. Such permission was generally not granted, so there was a restriction on their free movement.
Question 24. Who formed the Swaraj Party and why?
Answer: Chittaranjan Das and Motilal Nehru formed the Swaraj Party within the Congress. Its aim was to oppose British rule by returning to council politics.
Question 25. What was the Bardoli Satyagraha?
Answer: In 1928, Vallabhbhai Patel led the peasants in the Bardoli taluka of Gujarat. This movement was launched against the increase made in land revenue. It was called the Bardoli Satyagraha.
Question 26. In 'Hind Swaraj', what means did Gandhiji describe for attaining Swaraj?
Answer: Gandhiji explained that if Indians stopped cooperating with the British, British rule in India would weaken and end, and Swaraj could be attained.
Question 27. When was the Hindustan Socialist Republican Army (HSRA) established? How did its leaders express their opposition to the British government?
Answer: The HSRA was established in 1928 at the Firozshah Kotla Ground in Delhi. In 1929, Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt opposed British rule by throwing a bomb in the Central Legislative Assembly. The organisation carried out revolutionary activities targeting symbols of British power.
Question 28. Due to which incident did Lala Lajpat Rai die?
Answer: In 1928, during a demonstration against the Simon Commission, the British police lathi-charged Lala Lajpat Rai. He later died due to the injuries suffered during this.
Question 29. What was the significance of 26 January 1930?
Answer: In December 1929, the demand for Purna Swaraj was accepted at the Lahore Congress session. It was decided to observe 26 January 1930 as Independence Day throughout India.
Question 30. Write four reasons for the rise of modern nationalism in India.
Answer: The joint struggle, cultural processes, the image of Bharat Mata, and the revival of Indian folk tales and folk culture played an important role in the development of modern nationalism.
Question 31. Why were there sharp differences among political leaders over the question of Separate Electorates?
Answer: Different political leaders represented different social groups and their views differed. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar supported separate electorates for the political representation of Dalits, while Gandhiji believed that this would increase division within society.
Question 32. When was the Poona Pact between Gandhiji and Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar signed? What were its main terms?
Answer: The Poona Pact took place in September 1932. Under it, reserved seats were provided for the Depressed Classes (later Scheduled Castes) in provincial and central legislative councils, but these representatives were to be elected by the general electorate.
Question 33. Describe the Civil Disobedience Movement.
Answer: In 1930, Gandhiji started the Civil Disobedience Movement by breaking the Salt Law. People broke the Salt Law, boycotted foreign goods, protested against government institutions, and in many places tried not to pay taxes.
Question 34. Write the significance of the Civil Disobedience Movement.
Answer: The Civil Disobedience Movement brought a large number of people into the national movement. Opposition to the Salt Law gave rise to widespread popular resistance against British rule and strengthened the demand for independence.
Question 35. Explain the Civil Disobedience Movement in sequence.
Answer: Gandhiji began his journey from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi on 12 March 1930. Reaching Dandi on 6 April 1930, he broke the Salt Law. After this, the violation of the Salt Law, the boycott of foreign goods, and opposition to other colonial laws began across the country.
Question 36. Compare the image of Bharat Mata with the image of Germania.
Answer: Bharat Mata was a symbol of the Indian nation and was shown in a calm, spiritual and ascetic form. Germania was a symbol of the German nation and was depicted with symbols of bravery and strength.
Question 37. Write a note on the Salt March/Dandi March. How did it become an effective symbol of resistance against colonialism?
Answer: Gandhiji marched from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi in protest against the Salt Law and broke the Salt Law there. Salt was a necessity for every person, so this movement became an effective symbol that united different sections of society.
Question 38. How did the Salt Movement help strengthen the unity of the country?
Answer: The Salt Movement connected ordinary people with the freedom struggle. Peasants, workers, women and other social classes took part in it, which strengthened national unity against British rule.
Question 39. Who was Alluri Sitarama Raju? How did he inspire the rebels with Gandhiji's ideas?
Answer: Alluri Sitarama Raju was a leader of the tribal people of the Gudem hills in Andhra Pradesh. He was influenced by Gandhiji's ideas and inspired people to wear khadi and give up alcohol. However, he also considered armed struggle necessary for attaining freedom.
