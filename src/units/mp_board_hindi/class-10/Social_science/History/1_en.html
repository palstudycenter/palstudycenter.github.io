Chapter 1 — The Rise of Nationalism in Europe

1. The Rise of Nationalism

During the 18th and 19th centuries, the spirit of nationalism developed rapidly in Europe. At that time, Europe was not divided into separate nation-states as it is today. Many regions were split into small states, principalities and monarchies.

2. The French Revolution and Nationalism

The French Revolution of 1789 popularised the ideas of liberty, equality and fraternity. This revolution strengthened the sense of unity and nationhood among citizens and influenced the growth of nationalism throughout Europe.

3. Napoleon and His Reforms

Napoleon carried out administrative and legal reforms in many regions of Europe. He took steps to abolish feudal privileges, establish equality before the law, and make administration more systematic.

4. Liberalism and Nationalism

Liberalism laid emphasis on individual freedom, equality before the law, government by consent, and the right to private property. Liberals opposed autocratic rule.

5. Conservatism

Conservative ideology emphasised the importance of traditions, established institutions and old social customs. Conservatives preferred gradual change over sudden transformation.

6. Congress of Vienna

The Congress of Vienna was held in 1815. Its main objective was to restore the old monarchies in Europe after Napoleon and to reorganise the political system of Europe.

7. Unification of Germany

Germany was divided into many small states. Prussia led the movement for German unification. Otto von Bismarck united the German states with the help of war and diplomacy, and the German Empire was established in 1871.

8. Unification of Italy

Italy too was divided into many states. Giuseppe Mazzini inspired the nationalist movement. Count Cavour, through diplomacy, and Giuseppe Garibaldi, through military efforts, made important contributions to the unification of Italy.

9. Culture and Nationalism

Art, literature, music and folk culture played an important role in strengthening the spirit of nationalism. Romantic artists and writers promoted national identity through shared culture, history and traditions.

10. Frederic Sorrieu

Frederic Sorrieu was a French artist. In 1848, he created a series of four prints in which he presented his vision of an ideal world made up of democratic and social republics.

11. Zollverein

In 1834, a customs union called the Zollverein was formed at the initiative of Prussia. Its purpose was to reduce trade barriers among the German states and bring them economically closer to one another.

12. The Balkan Region

Many ethnic and cultural groups lived in the Balkan region. National consciousness grew among these groups and they began to demand independent nations. This led to increasing political conflict and tension in the region.

13. Consequences of Nationalism

The growth of nationalism in Europe led to the creation of many modern nation-states. The unification of Germany and Italy is a major example of this. Nationalism also deeply influenced the politics and international relations of Europe.

  • 1789 — French Revolution
  • 1815 — Congress of Vienna

  • Unification of Germany — Bismarck and Prussia

  • 1848 — Frederic Sorrieu's prints
  • Balkans — a major region of nationalism and political conflict
Q1. What is meant by nationalism?
Ans: Nationalism is the feeling in which people hold a sense of unity, loyalty and pride towards their nation.
Q2. When did nationalism arise in Europe?
Ans: Modern nationalism developed in Europe mainly during the 18th and 19th centuries.
Q3. What impact did the French Revolution have on nationalism?
Ans: The French Revolution spread the ideas of liberty, equality and fraternity and strengthened the sense of national unity among the people.
Q4. What reforms did Napoleon carry out in Europe?
Ans: Napoleon carried out administrative reforms, established equality in laws, and took steps towards abolishing feudal privileges.
Q5. When was the Congress of Vienna held?
Ans: The Congress of Vienna was held in 1815.
Q6. What was the main objective of the Congress of Vienna?
Ans: Its objective was to restore the old monarchies in Europe and to reorganise the political system of Europe after Napoleon.
Q7. Who was Giuseppe Mazzini?
Ans: Giuseppe Mazzini was a famous Italian revolutionary and nationalist leader. He worked for the unification of Italy.
Q8. Under whose leadership was Germany unified?
Ans: The unification of Germany took place under the leadership of Prussia and through the important role played by Otto von Bismarck.
Q9. Which leaders played a role in the unification of Italy?
Ans: Mazzini, Count Cavour and Giuseppe Garibaldi played important roles in the unification of Italy.
Q10. Why did nationalism grow in the Balkan region?
Ans: National consciousness grew among the various ethnic groups in the Balkan region, and they demanded the formation of independent nations. This increased political tension.
Q11. What is meant by Autocratism?
Ans: Autocratism is a system of government in which unlimited power and authority rest in the hands of a single person.
Q12. What was created between England and Scotland by the Act of Union of 1707?
Ans: The Act of Union of 1707 created a union between England and Scotland, bringing into existence the United Kingdom of Great Britain.
Q13. Explain the ideology of Conservatism.
Ans: Conservatism was a political ideology that emphasised the importance of traditions, established institutions and customs, and preferred gradual development over rapid change.
Q14. Who was Frederic Sorrieu?
Ans: Frederic Sorrieu was a French artist. In 1848, he prepared a series of four prints in which he presented his vision of a world made up of democratic and social republics.
Q15. What is meant by Feminism?
Ans: Feminism is an awareness of women's rights and interests, based on the belief in social, economic and political equality between women and men.
Q16. Explain the meaning of Liberalism.
Ans: Liberalism emphasised government by consent, individual freedom, equality for all, and an end to autocracy. Liberals also considered the protection of private property to be important.
Q17. Name the countries included in the Balkan region.
Ans: The Balkan region included countries such as Romania, Bulgaria, Albania, Greece, Macedonia, Croatia, Bosnia-Herzegovina, Slovenia, Serbia and Montenegro.
Q18. What is meant by Absolutist?
Ans: Absolutist refers to a monarchical system of government that is centralised, militaristic and repressive.
Q19. What is Utopian?
Ans: Utopian refers to the imagination or vision of an ideal society, one that is considered very difficult or impossible to bring into actual existence.
Q20. Define Plebiscite.
Ans: A plebiscite is a direct vote in which all the people of a region are asked to accept or reject a proposal.
Q21. What was the Jacobin Club?
Ans: The Jacobin Club was a political club active in France during and after the French Revolution.
Q22. What do you understand by Ideology?
Ans: Ideology is a systematic set of social or political ideas and beliefs that provides a basis for thinking and acting about society and politics.
Q23. Define Ethnicism.
Ans: Ethnicism is an ideology based on the shared racial, tribal or cultural origin and identity of a community. For example, the Scottish and Irish people had their own distinct cultural and political traditions.
Q24. What was the Zollverein? Why was it formed?
Ans: The Zollverein was a customs union formed in 1834 at the initiative of Prussia. Its purpose was to bring the German states together economically as a nation.
Q25. What was Young Italy?
Ans: Young Italy was a secret organisation established by Giuseppe Mazzini for the unification of Italy.
Q26. Who were the Slavs?
Ans: The Slavs were people belonging to various Slavic ethnic groups living in and around the Balkan region, including Bulgarians, Serbs and others.
