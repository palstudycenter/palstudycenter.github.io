📚4-Political Parties
Q1. What is a political party?
Answer: A political party is an organised group of people who contest elections on the basis of a common ideology and policies in order to form the government and run the administration of the country.
Q2. What are the major components of a political party?
Answer: A political party has three major components—(1) the leaders, (2) the active members, and (3) the followers or supporters.
Q3. Write any four features / characteristics of a political party.
Answer: The four major features of a political party are—
1. It is an organised group of people.
2. Its members share a common ideology and policies.
3. It takes part in elections.
4. Its aim is to gain power and form the government.
Q4. Explain the meaning of a political party and its uses.
Answer: A political party is an organised group of people with common ideas and objectives. It is useful for contesting elections, forming the government, making policies, raising people's problems, and making the government accountable.
Q5. Why are political parties necessary for democracy?
Answer: In a democracy, political parties contest elections, form the government, make policies, and act as a link between the people and the government. Without them, running a representative democracy becomes difficult.
Q6. What are the major functions of a political party?
Answer: The major functions of a political party are—contesting elections, making policies and programmes, participating in law-making, forming and running the government, playing the role of the opposition, and shaping public opinion.
Q7. What role do political parties play in elections?
Answer: Political parties select candidates, campaign for elections, present their policies and programmes to the people, and form the government when they gain a majority.
Q8. What is partisanship?
Answer: Having a special inclination towards a particular person, group, or idea without a proper reason is called partisanship.
Q9. How many types of party systems are there?
Answer: There are mainly three types of party systems—(1) one-party system, (2) two-party system, and (3) multi-party system.
Q10. Explain the one-party system at the national level with an example.
Answer: In a one-party system, only one political party is allowed to form the government and rule. Other parties do not get a real chance to come to power. For example, in China, the Communist Party has dominance.
Q11. What is a multi-party system?
Answer: When more than two political parties in a country contest elections and get the opportunity to form the government, it is called a multi-party system.
Q12. What kind of party system exists in India?
Answer: India has a multi-party system. Here, many political parties at the national and regional (state) levels take part in elections.
Q13. Why did India adopt a multi-party system?
Answer: India is a vast and diverse country. It has different languages, religions, regions, cultures, and social groups. To give political representation to these diversities, India adopted a multi-party system.
Q14. "The multi-party system has strengthened democracy in India." Comment.
Answer: The multi-party system has given political representation to different classes, regions, and communities. This has given the people more choices and increased control over and accountability of the government. Therefore, it has contributed to strengthening Indian democracy.
Q15. What is a national party?
Answer: A political party whose political activities and influence extend to several states, and which fulfils the conditions laid down by the Election Commission, is called a national party.
Q16. What is a regional (state) party?
Answer: A political party whose influence is mainly limited to one state or a few particular states, and which is recognised by the Election Commission as a state party, is called a regional (state) party.
Q17. What is the difference between national and regional (state) parties?
Answer: National parties have influence and organisation across several states, while regional (state) parties' influence is mainly limited to a particular state or region. National parties focus more on national issues, while regional parties generally focus more on regional issues.
Q18. How does a political party get recognition as a national party?
Answer: A political party gets recognition as a national party when it fulfils the conditions laid down by the Election Commission. The percentage of votes and the performance in terms of seats won in Lok Sabha or Vidhan Sabha elections are among the main bases for this.
Q19. What are recognised political parties?
Answer: Political parties that receive official recognition as a national or state party after fulfilling the necessary conditions laid down by the Election Commission are called recognised political parties.
Q20. How many national political parties are there in India at present? Write their names.
Answer: At present, there are 6 recognised national political parties in India—Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), Indian National Congress (INC), Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP), Aam Aadmi Party (AAP), Communist Party of India (Marxist) [CPI(M)], and National People's Party (NPP).
Q21. Write the names of India's six national political parties in chronological order.
Answer: The major national parties in order of their formation are—
1. Indian National Congress (1885)
2. Communist Party of India (1925)
3. Bharatiya Janata Party (1980)
4. Bahujan Samaj Party (1984)
5. National People's Party (2013)
6. Aam Aadmi Party (2012)
Q22. When was the Indian National Congress founded? What is its ideology?
Answer: The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. Its ideology is based on democracy, secularism, social justice, national unity, and economic development.
Q23. When was the Bharatiya Janata Party founded? What are its ideologies?
Answer: The Bharatiya Janata Party was founded in 1980. Its ideology emphasises cultural nationalism, national unity, democracy, and a strong nation-state.
Q24. When was the Bahujan Samaj Party founded? What are its ideologies?
Answer: The Bahujan Samaj Party was founded in 1984 by Kanshi Ram. It mainly emphasises the interests and social equality of the Bahujan Samaj, especially Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, Other Backward Classes, and minorities.
Q25. What role does the opposition play in a democracy?
Answer: Opposition parties criticise the policies and actions of the government, hold the government accountable, raise issues of public interest, and oppose the government's wrong policies.
Q26. Write short notes on: 1. Opposition party, 2. Alliance or front, 3. Coalition government, 4. Ruling party.
Answer:
1. Opposition party: Parties that are not part of the government and monitor the actions of the government.
2. Alliance or front: When two or more political parties work together for a common purpose, it is called an alliance or front.
3. Coalition government: When no single party gets a clear majority and several parties come together to form the government, it is called a coalition government.
4. Ruling party: The party or alliance of parties that forms the government and governs is called the ruling party.
Q27. What is defection?
Answer: When an elected representative leaves their political party after winning the election and joins another party, it is called defection.
Q28. What is an affidavit?
Answer: An affidavit is a legal declaration in which a candidate contesting an election provides information about their property, liabilities, criminal cases, and other necessary personal details.
Q29. What are the major challenges faced by political parties?
Answer: The major challenges faced by political parties are—lack of internal democracy, dynastic succession, the influence of money and muscle power, and the lack of meaningful choices among political parties.
Q30. Why is internal democracy necessary in political parties?
Answer: Internal democracy gives party members the opportunity to take part in decision-making. It promotes transparency, accountability, and capable leadership.
Q31. What is dynastic succession in political parties?
Answer: When leadership and important positions in political parties continue to be dominated by members of the same family, and family members are given preference over capable individuals, it is called dynastic succession.
Q32. How can reforms be made in political parties?
Answer: To bring about reforms in political parties, internal elections and transparency should be promoted, information about candidates should be made public, the influence of money and muscle power should be reduced, more opportunities should be given to women and youth, and the anti-defection law and other electoral reforms should be made effective.
