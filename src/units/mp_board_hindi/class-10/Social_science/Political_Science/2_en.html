Federalism

Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between two or more levels of government. The main objective of a federal system is to respect the diversity of different regions and communities while maintaining the unity of the country. A federal system generally has a central government and state governments, and in India, local governments have also been given constitutional recognition.

The main features of a federal system are—two or more levels of government, a clear division of powers by the constitution, each level having its own jurisdiction, supremacy of the constitution, an independent judiciary, and arrangements for sources of revenue for the different levels of government. In a federal system, changes to the constitution are generally made only with the consent of all the levels concerned. There are two major types of federalism—Coming Together Federation, in which independent states come together to form a union, as in the USA, Switzerland and Australia; and Holding Together Federation, in which power is divided between the centre and the states within one large country, as in India, Belgium and Spain.

India is a country with a federal system. The constitution calls India a "Union of States". There are three levels of government in India—the Central government, State government and Local government. The constitution has divided powers between the centre and the states. For this purpose there are three lists—the Union List, which includes subjects like defence, foreign policy and currency; the State List, which includes subjects like police, agriculture and public order; and the Concurrent list, which includes subjects like education, forests and marriage. Both the centre and the states can make laws on the Concurrent list. The power to make laws on residuary subjects lies with the Central government.

The language policy has played an important role in strengthening federalism in India. The constitution has not declared any one language as the "national language". Hindi is the official language of the Union, and English also continues to be used for official work. 22 scheduled languages have been given recognition in the constitution. States have been given the freedom to adopt their own official languages. Instead of forcefully imposing any one language, India accepted linguistic diversity, which strengthened the unity of the country.

Centre-State relations are also important for the success of federalism in India. For a long time, the influence of the centre remained greater because the same political party was in power at both the centre and in the states. After 1990, during the period of coalition governments, the role of the states increased, and the centre had to cooperate and consult with the states more. This made Indian federalism stronger.

Decentralisation means transferring power from the centre and the states down to the local level. After the 1992 constitutional amendments, local governments were given constitutional status. Panchayati Raj was established in rural areas and municipalities in urban areas. The Gram Sabha is an important institution of local democracy. Reservation has been provided for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and women in local bodies. A State Election Commission has been set up to hold regular elections, and a State Finance Commission to improve the financial condition of local bodies.

Thus, federalism is very useful for a diverse country like India. It gives different regions, languages and communities a share in governance, respects regional diversities, reduces conflict and strengthens democracy. The basis of the success of federalism in India is the sharing of power, respect for linguistic diversity, centre-state cooperation and local self-government.

Q1. What is federalism?
Answer: Federalism is a system of government in which power is divided between the Central government and State governments by the constitution.
Q2. What are the main features of federal government?
Answer: The main features of federal government are—two or more levels of government, constitutional division of powers, supremacy of the constitution, an independent judiciary and financial autonomy for each level of government.
Q3. What is the difference between a unitary and a federal system of government?
Basis Unitary government Federal government
Division of power Power is mainly concentrated at the centre. Power is divided between the centre and the states.
Levels of government Generally there is one main level. There are two or more levels of government.
Constitution The central government may get more power. The division of powers is fixed by the constitution.
Example Sri Lanka India and the USA
Q4. What are the two major types of federalism?
Answer: The two major types of federalism are—(1) Coming Together Federation and (2) Holding Together Federation.
Q5. What is Coming Together Federation?
Answer: When independent states come together of their own will to form a larger union, it is called Coming Together Federation. Example—the USA, Switzerland and Australia.
Q6. What is Holding Together Federation?
Answer: When a large country divides its power between the centre and the states in order to maintain its unity, it is called Holding Together Federation. Example—India, Belgium and Spain.
Q7. What is the difference between Coming Together and Holding Together Federation?
Basis Coming Together Federation Holding Together Federation
Formation Formed by independent states coming together. Formed by dividing a large country into states.
Objective To achieve security and unity. To maintain the unity of the country.
Distribution of power Generally all units get equal power. The central government may be more powerful.
Example USA, Switzerland, Australia India, Belgium, Spain
Q8. India is a federal country, explain with examples / What are the features that make India a federal country?
Answer: In India there are governments at the central, state and local levels. The constitution has divided powers between them. The constitution is supreme, and an independent judiciary resolves disputes. Therefore, India is a federal country.
Q9. How many levels of government are there in India?
Answer: There are three levels of government in India—the Central government, State government and Local government.
Q10. How has the division of powers been done in India?
Answer: In India the division of powers has been done through the Union List, State List and Concurrent list.
Q11. What is the Union List?
Answer: The Union List contains subjects on which only the Central government can make laws. For example—defence, foreign policy, currency and communication.
Q12. What is the State List?
Answer: The State List contains subjects on which generally the State government can make laws. For example—police, agriculture and irrigation.
Q13. What is the Concurrent list?
Answer: The Concurrent list contains subjects on which both the Central and State governments can make laws. For example—education, forests and laws relating to marriage.
Q14. Why were linguistic states created in India?
Answer: Linguistic states were created to give respect to people's language and cultural identity and to make administration more convenient.
Q15. What are the reasons for the success of federalism in India?
Answer: The main reasons for the success of federalism in India are—the creation of linguistic states, flexibility in language policy, centre-state cooperation and the development of coalition politics.
Q16. What is decentralisation?
Answer: When power and responsibilities are transferred from the central and state governments to local governments, it is called decentralisation.
Q17. What are the benefits of decentralisation? / Write four benefits of decentralisation of power.
Answer: The four benefits of decentralisation are—
1. Local problems are solved quickly.
2. People's political participation increases.
3. Administration becomes more effective.
4. Local people get the right to make decisions.
Q18. What major step was taken towards decentralisation in 1992?
Answer: In 1992, the constitution was amended to give constitutional status to local self-government, and regular elections, reservation and a five-year term were ensured for panchayats and municipalities.
Q19. What are the benefits of local government?
Answer: Local government understands local problems better and resolves them. This increases people's participation, accountability and the effectiveness of administration.
Q20. State any two differences between local government before and after the 1992 constitutional amendment.
Basis Before 1992 After 1992
Elections There was no constitutional guarantee of regular elections. Regular elections were made mandatory.
Reservation There was no constitutional provision for reservation. Reservation was provided for SC, ST and women.
Term The term of local bodies was not fixed. A fixed five-year term was set.
Q21. Explain the three-tier system of Panchayati Raj.
Answer: In the three-tier system of Panchayati Raj, there is the Gram Panchayat at the village level, the Panchayat Samiti/Block Panchayat at the intermediate level, and the Zila Parishad at the district level.
Q22. What is local self-government in India?
Answer: Local self-government is a system in which local people make decisions themselves, through local institutions, regarding the problems and development work of their area.
Q23. Describe the functions of the Gram Panchayat.
Answer: The main functions of the Gram Panchayat are—arranging for roads, drains, drinking water, sanitation and lighting in the village, carrying out development work and implementing government schemes.
