Power Sharing

Power Sharing: Power sharing is an important basis of democracy. It means that the power of governance should be divided among different institutions, different levels of government, social groups and political parties. This ensures that all power is not concentrated in the hands of one person or group, and every section gets an opportunity to participate in governance.

Why power sharing is necessary: The first major reason for power sharing is that it reduces the possibility of social conflict. When different communities and groups get appropriate participation in power, trust and cooperation increase among them. The second reason is that power sharing is in keeping with the spirit of democracy, because it gives the people affected by governance an opportunity to take part in the decision-making process.

The example of Belgium: Belgium is a small country in Europe where Dutch, French and German speaking communities live. About 59 percent of the country's population speaks Dutch, about 40 percent speaks French and about 1 percent speaks German. The situation in the capital Brussels is different, where the French speaking people are in the majority and the Dutch speaking people are in the minority.

Power sharing in Belgium: Considering the linguistic and social diversity, the leaders of Belgium adopted a system of power sharing. The number of Dutch and French speaking ministers in the central government was kept equal. State governments were also given important powers. Both major communities were given equal representation in Brussels and a separate community government was created. This arrangement helped reduce tension and conflict in the country.

Community government: In Belgium, the community government is a government that is elected not on the basis of any particular area, but on the basis of the language community. It includes representatives of the Dutch, French and German speaking communities. This government takes decisions on matters related to culture, education and language.

The example of Sri Lanka: In Sri Lanka, the Sinhalese and Tamils are the major linguistic communities. Here the Sinhalese are the majority community and the Tamils are an important minority community. After independence, the leaders of the majority community in Sri Lanka adopted policies that increased discontent among the Tamil community and created social tension.

Majoritarianism: When a majority community, on the basis of its numbers, disregards the wishes and rights of minority communities in governance, it is called majoritarianism. In Sri Lanka, the policies of Sinhalese majoritarianism increased political and social tension with the Tamil community.

Centralisation of power in Sri Lanka: In 1956, a law made Sinhala the official language of Sri Lanka. The government also adopted policies that gave the Sinhalese community special opportunities in education and employment, and gave special protection to Buddhism. The Tamil community opposed these policies and demanded equal rights and a share in power.

Conflict in Sri Lanka: Due to majoritarian policies, tension increased between the Sinhalese and Tamil communities. The Tamils demanded equal rights, regional autonomy and political representation. The long-drawn-out conflict created serious political and social problems in Sri Lanka and eventually led to a civil war.

Four major forms of power sharing: Power sharing is mainly seen in four forms. First, the sharing of power among different organs of government—the legislature, the executive and the judiciary. Second, the sharing of power among central, state and local governments. Third, power sharing among different social groups. Fourth, power sharing among political parties, pressure groups and movements.

Power sharing among organs of government: The legislature makes laws, the executive implements the laws, and the judiciary interprets the laws and delivers justice. Each organ exercises checks and balances on the other organs. This arrangement is called separation of powers or horizontal power sharing.

Power sharing among different levels of government: The distribution of powers among governments at the central, state and local levels is called vertical power sharing. In India, there is a constitutional division of powers between the central and state governments. Local self-government institutions have also been given rights.

Power sharing among social groups: Giving representation in governance to various religious, linguistic, ethnic and weaker sections of society is one form of power sharing. In India, seats are reserved for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in the legislative assemblies and Parliament. There is also a provision for reservation for women in local bodies.

Power sharing among political parties: In a democracy, different political parties try to gain power through elections. When no single party gets a clear majority, several parties may come together to form a coalition government. In this way, power is shared among different political parties.

Pressure groups and movements: Pressure groups and social movements also play an important role in power sharing. They work to influence government policies and to convey people's demands to the government. This gives space to different interests and views in governance.

Benefits of power sharing: Power sharing reduces social conflict, increases political stability and builds trust among different communities. It strengthens democracy and helps protect the rights of minorities. It makes the government more accountable and participatory.

A cautious conclusion: Power sharing is not necessary merely because it reduces conflict, but it is also the basic spirit of democracy. In a democracy, every citizen and social group should have the right to respect and participation in governance. Belgium tried to manage its diversity through power sharing, while the experience of Sri Lanka shows that majoritarianism can increase social conflict. Therefore, a fair and just distribution of power is necessary in a democracy.

Question 1. What is meant by power sharing?
Answer: When the power of governance is divided among different organs, levels, social groups and political parties, it is called power sharing.
Question 2. Why is power sharing necessary?
Answer: Power sharing reduces social conflict, increases political stability and gives different groups an opportunity to participate in governance.
Question 3. What is the moral reason for power sharing?
Answer: The moral reason for power sharing is that in a democracy, all citizens and social groups should have an equal right to take part in governance.
Question 4. What are the major forms of power sharing?
Answer: The major forms of power sharing are—(1) among different organs of government, (2) among central and state governments, (3) among different social groups, and (4) among political parties and pressure groups.
Question 5. What is horizontal distribution of power?
Answer: When power is divided among different organs of government such as the legislature, the executive and the judiciary, it is called horizontal distribution of power.
Question 6. What is vertical division of power?
Answer: When power is divided among different levels of government such as the central, state and local governments, it is called vertical division of power.
Question 7. What is the difference between horizontal and vertical power sharing?
Answer: Horizontal power sharing takes place among different organs of government at the same level, whereas vertical power sharing takes place among different levels of government.
Question 8. What is the system of checks and balances in power sharing?
Answer: In the system of checks and balances, each organ of government exercises control over the powers of the other organs. This prevents excessive power from being concentrated in the hands of any one organ.
Question 9. What is a community government?
Answer: In Belgium, a special government formed by the people of the language communities is called a community government. It is elected by the people of the language community and takes decisions on cultural, educational and language-related matters.
Question 10. What is a coalition government?
Answer: When two or more political parties come together to form a government, it is called a coalition government.
Question 11. Why was a system of power sharing set up in Belgium?
Answer: In Belgium, there was a possibility of differences and conflict among the Dutch, French and German speaking communities. Therefore, a system of power sharing was set up to reduce social and political tension and to maintain the unity of the country.
Question 12. Which linguistic community is in the majority in Brussels, the capital of Belgium?
Answer: The French speaking community is in the majority in Brussels, the capital of Belgium, whereas the Dutch speaking people are in the majority in the whole of Belgium.
Question 13. How is the community government elected in Belgium?
Answer: The community government is elected by the people of each language community. This government manages the cultural, educational and language-related matters of the concerned language community.
Question 14. How were the Dutch and French speaking people accommodated in the community government in Belgium?
Answer: In Belgium, the Dutch and French speaking communities were given equal political representation, and through community governments they were given the right to take decisions on matters related to their language, culture and education.
Question 15. What is majoritarianism?
Answer: When a majority community imposes its wishes on a minority community and disregards the interests and rights of the minorities, it is called majoritarianism.
Question 16. Because of majoritarianism, peace was disturbed in which country?
Answer: Because of majoritarianism, peace was disturbed in Sri Lanka, and conflict increased between the Sinhalese and Tamil communities there.
Question 17. What was the main reason for the tense relations between the Sinhalese and Tamil communities?
Answer: In Sri Lanka, the Sinhalese majority government gave special importance to the Sinhala language and Buddhism, and disregarded the interests of the Tamils. This increased tension between the two communities.
Question 18. What was the result of the lack of power sharing in Sri Lanka?
Answer: Due to the lack of power sharing, conflict increased between the Sinhalese and Tamil communities, and eventually a long civil war took place in Sri Lanka.
Question 19. Why are some Tamils in Sri Lanka called 'Indian Tamils'?
Answer: During British rule, labourers were taken from India to work on the tea and coffee plantations of Sri Lanka. Their descendants are called Indian Tamils.
Question 20. What is a civil war?
Answer: When there is a prolonged armed conflict between different groups or communities within a country, it is called a civil war.
Question 21. What is meant by ethnic?
Answer: Ethnic relates to a group of people who share similarities in language, culture, traditions, descent or social identity.
