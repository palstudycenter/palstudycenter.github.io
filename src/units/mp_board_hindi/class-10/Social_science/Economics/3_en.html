3-Money and Credit
Q1. What is money? / What is money (currency)?
Answer: Money is the medium that is used for the purchase and sale of goods and services and for making payments.
Q2. What is the barter system? / What is called the barter system?
Answer: When goods and services are exchanged without money, in the form of goods for goods, it is called the barter system.
Q3. What are the limitations of the barter system? / Explain the problem of double coincidence of wants with an example.
Answer: In this system, it is difficult to determine the value of goods, and the needs of both parties must match each other. This is called the double coincidence of wants. For example, a person offering wheat must need cloth, and the person offering cloth must need wheat, only then will the exchange be possible.
Q4. How did money remove the difficulty of the barter system? / How does the use of money make the exchange of goods convenient?
Answer: Money is a universally accepted medium of exchange. It removes the need to give goods directly in exchange for goods, and the problem of double coincidence of wants is eliminated.
Q5. Write four functions of money. / What are the main functions of money?
Answer: (1) Medium of exchange, (2) Measure of value, (3) Store of value, (4) Basis for future (deferred) payments.
Q6. What are the main forms of modern money?
Answer: The main forms of modern money are paper notes and coins. Demand deposits in banks are also an important form of money.
Q7. What is a bank? / What are the benefits of depositing money in banks?
Answer: A bank is an institution that accepts deposits from people and gives loans to people in need. Deposited money remains safe and also earns interest.
Q8. Why do people deposit money in banks?
Answer: People deposit money in banks to keep it safe, to earn interest, and to be able to withdraw it easily when needed.
Q9. What are the main sources of income of banks?
Answer: The interest received on loans given by banks and the fees received from various banking services are their main sources of income.
Q10. What is a demand deposit?
Answer: Money deposited in a bank that the depositor can withdraw at any time on demand is called a demand deposit.
Q11. Why is a demand deposit considered money?
Answer: Demand deposits can be used directly for payment through cheques, ATMs, or other means, so they are considered money.
Q12. Why is a bank deposit considered money?
Answer: A bank deposit can be used to make payment directly and can be withdrawn on demand, so it functions as money.
Q13. What is a cheque? / What are the benefits of a cheque?
Answer: A cheque is a written order given to a bank, by which a fixed amount is paid to a person from the account holder's account. It reduces the need to keep cash and keeps payment secure.
Q14. How is payment made through a cheque?
Answer: The account holder writes the amount and the name of the recipient on the cheque and signs it. The recipient deposits the cheque in the bank, and the amount is transferred into their account.
Q15. How can bank deposits, as a form of money, be beneficial for individuals and the nation?
Answer: Bank deposits provide safe savings, interest, and easy payment facilities. Banks lend a part of these deposits to promote economic activities and development.
Q16. How do banks mediate between people with surplus money and people in need?
Answer: Banks take deposits from people who save and lend a part of that money to people in need in the form of loans. In this way, banks act as an intermediary between the two.
Q17. What is demonetisation?
Answer: The process by which the government legally declares a prevailing currency invalid is called demonetisation.
Q18. What is loan or credit? / What is a loan?
Answer: Money taken by a person or institution on the condition of returning it in the future along with interest is called a loan or credit.
Q19. What are the terms of credit? / What is collateral? Explain.
Answer: The terms of credit may include the interest rate, the loan period, the method of payment, and collateral. Collateral is an asset that is kept as security against a loan.
Q20. What is interest?
Answer: The amount of money given to the lender in addition to the amount of the loan taken is called interest.
Q21. Why do banks give loans?
Answer: Banks give loans to meet the money-related needs of people and businesses, and to earn interest on the loans.
Q22. Why are banks not willing to give loans to some borrowers? / Why are banks not willing to give loans to small farmers?
Answer: Banks consider borrowers who do not have adequate collateral, a regular income, or a certain ability to repay the loan to be risky. Therefore, banks often exercise caution in giving loans to small farmers.
Q23. What are the sources of formal sector credit?
Answer: Institutions such as banks and cooperative societies are the main sources of formal sector credit.
Q24. What are the sources of informal sector credit?
Answer: Moneylenders, traders, employers, relatives, and friends are the main sources of informal sector credit.
Q25. What is the difference between formal and informal sources of credit?

Q26. What are the main sources of credit for the poor? Give reasons.
Answer: Poor people mostly take credit from moneylenders, traders, relatives, and Self Help Groups. The reason for this is the difficulty of collateral and other conditions in taking loans from banks.
Q27. Write any three reasons for the dependence of rural people on informal sources.
Answer: (1) Limited availability of banks, (2) The complex process of taking a loan, (3) Difficulty in fulfilling collateral and other conditions.
Q28. What are formal financial institutions?
Answer: Financial institutions that operate under government regulations are called formal financial institutions. For example — banks and cooperative societies.
Q29. What is meant by a commercial bank?
Answer: A bank that accepts deposits from the public and provides loans to individuals and businesses is called a commercial bank.
Q30. Describe the credit-related activities of banks.
Answer: Banks accept deposits from people and, while keeping a part of the deposit amount secure, use the remaining amount to give loans. Banks earn income by receiving interest on the loans.
Q31. Why is there a need to expand formal sources of credit in India?
Answer: So that people can get safe and regulated loans at a lower rate of interest and can avoid the high interest and exploitation imposed by moneylenders.
Q32. What is a debt trap?
Answer: When a person has to keep taking a new loan to repay a loan and keeps getting trapped in debt continuously, it is called a debt trap.
Q33. How can credit create more problems for a borrower in high-risk situations? Explain.
Answer: If the expected income from the investment does not materialise, the borrower is unable to repay the loan and interest. Because of this, the borrower may have to sell property or may get trapped in a debt trap.
Q34. Analyse the role of credit for development. / Write the role of loans for development.
Answer: With credit, farmers can buy seeds and equipment, businesspeople can expand trade, and people can create new opportunities for production and employment. Therefore, credit on reasonable terms is helpful for economic development.
Q35. Write three functions of the Reserve Bank of India. / How does the Reserve Bank of India monitor the functioning of banks? Why is this necessary?
Answer: (1) Supervising and regulating banks, (2) Issuing currency, (3) Controlling the monetary system. Supervision of banks is necessary so that they follow the rules and the interests of depositors remain protected.
Q36. What is a Self Help Group?
Answer: A Self Help Group (SHG) is generally a small group of 15–20 people, who save regularly and provide loans to members in need.
Q37. How does a Self Help Group work? / Write three functions of a Self Help Group.
Answer: Members save regularly, small loans are given to members from the group's savings, and later the group helps in obtaining loans from banks.
Q38. What is the basic idea behind organising Self Help Groups for the poor?
Answer: Organising the poor into small groups to develop the habit of saving and to provide cheap credit without much collateral is its basic idea.
Q39. State any two benefits of Self Help Groups.
Answer: (1) The poor easily get small loans. (2) They get relief from dependence on moneylenders and high interest rates.
Q40. Who is the founder of Grameen Bank? With which award was he honoured?
Answer: The founder of Grameen Bank is Muhammad Yunus. He was honoured with the Nobel Peace Prize in the year 2006.
