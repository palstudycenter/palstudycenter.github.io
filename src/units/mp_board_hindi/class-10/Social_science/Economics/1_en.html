1-Development
Q1. What is meant by development?
Answer: Development means an improvement in people's lives in terms of income, education, health, security, freedom and other facilities.
Q2. Why do different people have different developmental goals? / How can the developmental goals of two people be different?
Answer: People's needs, circumstances and priorities differ. Therefore, higher income may be more important for one person, while education, health, security or respect may be more important for another.
Q3. What is national development?
Answer: An improvement in the standard of living, income, education, health and facilities of all citizens of a country, along with the country's economic and social progress, is called national development.
Q4. Why is income an important criterion of development?
Answer: With income, people can buy the goods and services they need. Higher income makes it easier to obtain many essential facilities of life.
Q5. What is average income or per capita income?
Answer: The income obtained by dividing the total income of a country by its total population is called average income or per capita income.
Q6. How are different countries compared on the basis of income?
Answer: Different countries are compared on the basis of their per capita income. A country with a higher per capita income is generally considered more developed.
Q7. On what basis does the World Bank compare countries?
Answer: The World Bank mainly compares countries on the basis of per capita income.
Q8. Besides income, what else do the people of a country want?
Answer: People also want good education, better health, security, equality, freedom, respect, a clean environment and employment opportunities.
Q9. Why is average income considered an important criterion of development? Explain.
Answer: Average income makes it easy to compare the incomes of different countries. It reduces the effect of population size and indicates the general income status of a country.
Q10. What is human development? / What is meant by human development?
Answer: Human development means improving the quality of people's lives. It includes, along with income, facilities such as health, education and a dignified life.
Q11. Who publishes the Human Development Report?
Answer: The Human Development Report is published by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP).
Q12. On what basis does the Human Development Report compare different countries?
Answer: The Human Development Report compares countries on major criteria such as health, education and per capita income.
Q13. What is the Human Development Index?
Answer: The Human Development Index (HDI) is a measure used to assess the status of people's health, education and standard of living in countries.
Q14. Why is the infant mortality rate lowest in Kerala? / What are the reasons for the low infant mortality rate in Kerala?
Answer: The infant mortality rate is low in Kerala due to better health facilities, higher literacy, good education, sanitation and public health services.
Q15. What is Body Mass Index (B.M.I.)?
Answer: Body Mass Index (BMI) is a measure that estimates nutritional status on the basis of a person's body weight and height.
Q16. What are public facilities?
Answer: Facilities that are made available by the government for the collective use of people are called public facilities. For example—schools, hospitals, clean water and public transport.
Q17. What is the Public Distribution System?
Answer: The Public Distribution System (PDS) is the arrangement through which the government, through fair price shops, provides food grains and other essential goods to poor and eligible people.
Q18. What is sustainable development?
Answer: Development that meets the needs of the present generation without affecting the ability to meet the needs of future generations is called sustainable development.
