2-Sectors of the Indian Economy
Q1. What are economic activities?
Ans: Activities carried out to earn income are called economic activities.
Q2. What is division of labour?
Ans: Dividing the work of production among different people or groups is called division of labour.
Q3. What are the three sectors of the economy?
Ans: Primary sector, Secondary sector and Tertiary sector.
Q4. What is the primary sector? / What are primary activities? / What comes under the primary sector?
Ans: The sector in which goods are produced directly from natural resources is called the primary sector. It includes agriculture, fishing, forestry, animal husbandry and mining, etc.
Q5. What is the secondary sector? / What comes under the secondary sector?
Ans: The sector that converts raw materials obtained from the primary sector into finished goods is called the secondary sector. It includes factories, construction and manufacturing industries.
Q6. What is the tertiary sector? / What is meant by the tertiary sector?
Ans: The sector that provides services instead of producing goods is called the tertiary sector. For example—banking, transport, education, health and communication.
Q7. What is GDP? / What is meant by Gross Domestic Product?
Ans: The total monetary value of all final goods and services produced in a country in one year is called the Gross Domestic Product (GDP).
Q8. Why is the importance of the service sector increasing in India?
Ans: The importance of the service sector is increasing because of the rising demand for services such as education, health, banking, transport, communication, information technology and trade.
Q9. Do you agree that the tertiary sector is not playing any important role in the development of the Indian economy? Give reasons.
Ans: I do not agree with this statement. The tertiary sector provides employment, offers essential services such as transport and communication, and helps in the development of the primary and secondary sectors.
Q10. What is meant by public facilities?
Ans: The essential facilities made available to the public by the government are called public facilities. For example—education, health, clean water and electricity.
Q11. What is disguised (hidden) unemployment? / Explain disguised unemployment with an example.
Ans: When more people than necessary are engaged in a job and removing some of them would not affect production, it is called disguised unemployment. Example: A farm needs 5 people, but 8 people are working on it.
Q12. Where is disguised unemployment found in India? Give one example each from urban and rural areas.
Ans: In rural areas, it is mainly found in agriculture. In urban areas, it can be seen when more people than necessary work in small shops or small family businesses.
Q13. What is the difference between open unemployment and disguised unemployment?
Open Unemployment Disguised Unemployment
The person is able and willing to work, but does not get work. The person is engaged in work, but is not actually needed.
The person appears completely unemployed. The person appears to be employed, but their contribution is very little or zero.
Q14. What is the organised sector? / Write two features of the organised sector.
Ans: The sector that works under rules and laws laid down by the government is called the organised sector. It has facilities such as regular employment and fixed working hours.
Q15. Why is the organised sector called a welfare sector?
Ans: Because workers here get regular salary, fixed working hours, paid leave, social security and other legal benefits.
Q16. What is paid leave? / What do you understand by the provision of paid leave?
Ans: Leave in which an employee continues to receive salary while on leave is called paid leave.
Q17. What is the unorganised sector?
Ans: A sector where the terms of employment are not regular and government rules are not fully followed is called the unorganised sector.
Q18. Compare the employment conditions prevailing in the organised and unorganised sectors.
Organised Sector Unorganised Sector
Terms of employment are regular and protected by law. Terms of employment are uncertain and less secure.
Regular salary, leave and social security are provided. Wages are low and social security benefits are limited.
Q19. Workers in the unorganised sector are exploited, do you agree with this view? Give reasons. / What difficulties do workers in the unorganised sector face?
Ans: Yes, workers in the unorganised sector are often exploited. They face difficulties such as low wages, uncertain employment, long working hours and lack of social security.
Q20. How is unemployment affected by rapidly increasing population? Give two reasons.
Ans: (1) The number of people looking for work increases. (2) When employment opportunities do not increase at the same rate, unemployment increases.
Q21. How can employment be increased in rural areas? Explain with an example.
Ans: Employment can be increased by expanding irrigation, roads, storage, agriculture-based industries and rural construction works. For example—setting up food processing industries.
Q22. How can employment be increased in urban areas? / Write two measures for employment generation in urban areas.
Ans: (1) By promoting small and medium industries. (2) By expanding service activities such as education, health, transport and information technology.
Q23. Write two measures to improve the primary sector.
Ans: (1) Expanding modern agricultural techniques and irrigation facilities. (2) Providing farmers with cheap credit facilities, better seeds and proper markets.
Q24. What is the public sector?
Ans: The sector in which the ownership and operation of production and services rest in the hands of the government is called the public sector.
Q25. What are the main features of the public sector?
Ans: It is owned by the government, its objective is public welfare, and it plays an important role in providing essential services.
Q26. How does the public sector contribute to the economic development of the nation?
Ans: It provides employment, develops infrastructure facilities, provides essential services and helps in the welfare of economically weaker sections.
Q27. What is the private sector? Explain with any two examples.
Ans: The sector in which the ownership and operation of production and services rest in the hands of private individuals or companies is called the private sector. Examples—Tata Motors and Reliance Industries.
Q28. What is the full form of MGNREGA?
Ans: The full form of MGNREGA is the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act).
Q29. Why is MGNREGA called the '100 Days Employment Scheme'?
Ans: Because it provides a legal guarantee of at least 100 days of unskilled wage employment to rural households in a financial year.
Q30. Write the main provisions of MGNREGA (any two).
Ans: (1) Guarantee of 100 days of employment to rural households in a year. (2) Providing employment within a stipulated time when work is demanded; provision for paying an unemployment allowance if employment is not provided.
