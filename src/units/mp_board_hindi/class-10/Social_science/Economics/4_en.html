4-Globalisation and the Indian Economy)
Q1. What was the main medium of connecting countries in the past?
Ans: In ancient times, land and sea trade routes were the main medium of connecting countries.
Q2. What are the main links connecting different countries?
Ans: Trade, investment, transport, communication and technology are the main links connecting different countries.
Q3. What is Globalisation?
Ans: The connecting of the economies of different countries with each other through trade, investment, technology and production is called Globalisation.
Q4. What do you understand by the rationale of Globalisation / the reasoning behind Globalisation?
Ans: The main reasoning behind Globalisation is that an increase in trade and investment between countries increases opportunities for production, markets, employment and technological development.
Q5. What are the main factors promoting Globalisation? / What are the factors that have promoted the process of Globalisation?
Ans: Technological development, improvements in transport and communication, foreign trade, foreign investment and the liberalisation of trade policies are the main factors of Globalisation.
Q6. Write about the role of transport among the factors that have made Globalisation possible.
Ans: Faster and cheaper transport made it easier to move goods and raw materials from one country to another, which increased international trade.
Q7. What kind of role has communication technology played in Globalisation?
Ans: The internet, telephone and satellite communication have made it easier to transmit information quickly from one country to another. This has increased contact between trade and companies.
Q8. What is the advantage of using containers in the transport of goods?
Ans: Goods can be transported from one place to another safely and at a lower cost using containers. This also makes the loading and unloading of goods faster.
Q9. What is meant by investment?
Ans: Putting money into a business, machinery, building or any other means of production with the aim of earning profit is called investment.
Q10. What is a Multinational Corporation (MNC)? How does it operate? / What is meant by Multinational Corporations?
Ans: A company that carries out production or services in more than one country is called a Multinational Corporation (MNC). It operates by setting up production units, offices or partner companies in different countries.
Q11. Name two Multinational Corporations.
Ans: (1) Samsung, (2) Toyota.
Q12. Where do Multinational Corporations set up production?
Ans: Multinational Corporations set up production units in those countries or regions where markets, cheap labour, raw material and other facilities are available.
Q13. How is a Multinational Corporation different from other companies?
Ans: A Multinational Corporation carries out production and trade in many countries, whereas an ordinary company mainly operates in one country or a limited region.
Q14. How do Multinational Corporations keep the production cost of their products low?
Ans: They keep the production cost low by using cheap labour, raw material, modern technology and large-scale production.
Q15. In which sectors have Multinational Corporations invested the most?
Ans: Multinational Corporations have invested mainly in sectors such as automobiles, electronics, telecommunications, food processing and information technology.
Q16. Who has played an important role in organising production across countries?
Ans: Multinational Corporations have played an important role in organising production across countries.
Q17. What is the role of Multinational Corporations in the process of Globalisation?
Ans: Multinational Corporations make foreign investments, set up production units in different countries, and connect the economies of countries through technology and trade.
Q18. What is foreign investment?
Ans: When a company or a person invests money for production or business in another country, it is called foreign investment.
Q19. What is foreign trade?
Ans: The buying and selling of goods and services between two or more countries is called foreign trade.
Q20. What is the difference between foreign trade and foreign investment?
Foreign Trade Foreign Investment
Buying and selling of goods and services takes place between countries. Money is invested for production or business in another country.
Its main purpose is to carry out trade. Its main purpose is to earn profit from investment and increase production.
Q21. How does foreign trade help in the integration of markets of different countries?
Ans: Through foreign trade, the products of one country reach the markets of another country. This connects the markets of different countries with each other for both producers and consumers.
Q22. What is a trade barrier? Give an example.
Ans: Restrictions imposed by the government to limit foreign trade are called trade barriers. Example - import duty or quota on imports.
Q23. What were the reasons for the Indian government imposing barriers on foreign trade and foreign investment?
Ans: The government imposed barriers on trade and investment to protect domestic industries and producers from foreign competition and to develop new industries in the country.
Q24. What do you understand by the liberalisation of foreign trade?
Ans: The process of reducing or removing the government restrictions and barriers imposed on foreign trade is called the liberalisation of foreign trade.
Q25. What is Liberalisation?
Ans: The process of reducing government restrictions imposed on trade and economic activities is called Liberalisation.
Q26. When did economic liberalisation begin in India?
Ans: The process of economic liberalisation began in India in 1991.
Q27. How has the liberalisation of trade and investment policies helped the process of Globalisation?
Ans: This reduced restrictions on imports-exports and foreign investment. As a result, the entry of foreign companies into India and international trade increased.
Q28. Explain three steps taken by the Indian government to encourage foreign investment.
Ans: (1) Restrictions on foreign investment were reduced. (2) Foreign companies were allowed to invest in many sectors. (3) Rules related to trade and industry were simplified.
Q29. How will flexibility in labour laws help companies?
Ans: This will allow companies to change the number of workers and working hours according to their needs, and reduce production costs.
Q30. What is meant by cooperative marketing?
Ans: When small producers come together to carry out the sale and marketing of their goods, it is called cooperative marketing.
Q31. State three benefits that local companies get by setting up production units in collaboration with Multinational Corporations.
Ans: (1) New technology is obtained. (2) Capital and investment are received. (3) Production and market expand.
Q32. What happened to the sales of Indian toys when Chinese toys invaded the domestic market?
Ans: Due to cheap Chinese toys, the sales of Indian toys declined, and Indian toy producers had to face tough competition.
Q33. Write any two advantages of Liberalisation.
Ans: (1) Foreign trade and investment increased. (2) Consumers began to get more choices and better quality products.
Q34. What is the WTO?
Ans: The full form of WTO is World Trade Organisation. It makes rules for international trade and promotes trade between countries.
Q35. What benefits did consumers get from Globalisation?
Ans: Consumers got more choices, better quality, products with new technology, and competitive prices for many goods.
Q36. What impact did Globalisation have on Indian producers?
Ans: Large Indian producers got opportunities for new markets and technology, but small producers had to face tough competition from foreign companies and cheap imports.
Q37. What impact did Globalisation have on the Indian economy? / Write the effects of Globalisation in India.
Ans: Foreign investment, trade and production increased in India, and new technology and employment opportunities came along. At the same time, the pressure of competition on small producers also increased.
Q38. What are the effects of Globalisation on small producers?
Ans: Small producers have to compete fiercely with foreign companies and cheap imports. Some producers benefit by adopting new technology, while others have to bear losses.
Q39. Which class of consumers has benefited the most from Globalisation?
Ans: Consumers with high and middle incomes have benefited more from Globalisation, as a wider variety of goods and products with new technology became easily available to them.
Q40. Write the benefits and drawbacks of Globalisation.
Benefits Drawbacks
Increase in foreign investment and trade. Foreign competition increased on small producers.
New technology and better products became available. Some small industries and workers had to face job insecurity.
Choices increased for consumers. The distribution of benefits was not equal among all sections.
Q41. "The impact of Globalisation has not been uniform." Explain this statement.
Ans: Large companies and high-income consumers benefited more from Globalisation, while many small producers had to face foreign competition. Therefore, its impact has not been uniform on all people and regions.
