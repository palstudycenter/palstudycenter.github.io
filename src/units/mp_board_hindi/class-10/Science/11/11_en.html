What is Electricity?

Electricity is the flow of electric charge through a conductor.

It is a form of energy that can be used to run various devices like bulbs, fans, motors, and machines.

What is Electric Charge?

Electric charge is a basic property of matter due to which it experiences a force in an electric field.

Charge is measured in coulomb (C).

How many kinds of electric charges?

There are two kinds of electric charges:

  1. Positive charge (+)
  2. Negative charge (–) Like charges repel.

unlike charges attract.

What type of charge do electrons and protons have?

Electrons have negative charge, and protons have positive charge.

How does an object get charged by friction?

When two neutral objects are rubbed together then due to friction some electrons get transferred from one object to another object.

Thus, an object which loses electrons becomes positively charged and which receives electrons becomes negatively charged.

What are Conductors?

Those substances through which electricity can flow are called conductors.

Example – All metals like silver, copper and aluminium, etc are conductors.

What are Insulators?

Those substances through which electricity cannot flow are called insulators.

Example – Glass, ebonite, rubber, most plastic, paper, dry wood, etc are insulators.

What is Ohm's law?(2023)

Ohm's law states that the electric current flowing in a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across the ends of the conductor, provided the physical conditions (e.g., temperature) of the conductor remains the same.

If I is the current flowing through a conductor and V is the potential difference across its ends then according to Ohm's law, V ∝ I V = RI Where R is a constant called resistance of the conductor

What is resistance? Give its unit

Resistance is the property of a conductor that opposes the flow of electric current through it.

The SI Unit of resistance is Ohm (Ω).

Define Ohm.

The resistance of a conductor when a potential difference of 1 volt produces a current of 1 ampere through it is called one ohm.

What are the factors affecting the resistance of the conductor?

The resistance of any conducting wire depends on the following factors -

  1. Length of the wire - The resistance of a uniform conducting wire is directly proportional to its length i.e., R ∝ L
  2. Cross-section of the wire -  The resistance of a uniform conducting wire is inversely proportional to its Cross-section of the wire i.e., R ∝ 1/A
  3. Nature of conducting wire-The resistance of the wire depends upon the nature of the substance (matter) of the conducting wire.
  4. Temperature of wire - Resistance of a metallic wire of uniform thickness increases with the increase of temperature and decreases with the decrease in temperature.
Will current flow more easily through a thick wire or a thin wire of the same material, when connected to the same source? Why? (Q. Bank)

The current will flow more easily through a thick wire as compared to thin wire because the resistance of thick wire is less than that of thin wire. Less resistance means more current because I = V/R

What happens to an electric current if resistance is increased?

According to Ohm's law, current and resistance are inversely proportional. Hence, when resistance increases, current is decreased in the circuit.

What is the thermal effect of electric current?

When an electric current flows through a conductor, it produces heat.This heating effect of electric current is called the thermal effect of electric current.

What is Joule’s law of heating?

Joule’s law of heating states that:

The heat produced (H) in a conductor by an electric current is directly proportional to:

  1. Square of the current (I²)
  2. Resistance of the conductor (R)

Why are coils of electric toasters and electric irons made of an alloy rather than a pure metal? (Q. Bank)

Or, Why are the conductors of electric heating devices, such as bread toasters and electric irons, made of an alloy rather than a pure metal? (2022)

Why does the cord of an electric heater not glow while the heating element does? Write cause.(2024)

The resistance of the cord is extremely small as compared to that of the heating element, so the heat produced in cord is less as compared to heating element. So, the heating element begins to glow but the cord does not glow.

Why is tungsten used almost exclusively for filament of electric lamps? (2020/22)
  1. It has a special property that it glows on heating.
  2. It has a high melting point.
  3. It does not react with the gases present in air and does not get oxidised.
  4. It has low resistance.
What is a fuse wire?

A fuse is a metallic wire of low melting point. It is connected in the live line of every section of the building's wiring.

How does fuse wire prevent electrical devices from damage?

If there is any overheating in the circuit the fuse wire melts immediately, thus preventing the accidents and electrical devices from damage
