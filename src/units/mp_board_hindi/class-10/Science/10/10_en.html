The Various parts of eye and their functions

  1. Cornea : It is a thin membrane through which light enters. It forms the transparent bulge on the front of the eyeball. Most of the refraction occurs at the outer surface of the cornea.
  2. Eyeball : it is approximately spherical in shape, with a diameter of about 2.3cm.
  3. Iris : It is a dark muscular diaphragm that controls the size of the pupil. It is behind the cornea.
  4. Pupil : It regulates and controls the amount of light entering the eye. It is the black opening between aqueous humour & lens.
  5. Crystalline eye lens : Provide the focussed real & inverted image of the object on the retina. It is composed of a fibrous, jelly-like material. This is a convex lens that converges light at the retina.
  6. Ciliary muscles : It helps to change the curvature of eyelens and hence changes its focal length so that we can see the object clearly placed at different positions.
  7. Retina : Thin membrane with large no. of sensitive cells.

How does a pupil work ?

The pupil of an eye provides a variable aperture, whose size is controlled by iris.

What is Iris?

A structure behind the cornea is called iris. It controls the size of the pupil.

  • When the light is bright : Iris contracts the pupil, so that less light enters the eye.
  • When the light is dim : Iris expands the pupil, so that more light enters the eye.
What is the effect on pupils in dim light? (Q. Bank)

The pupils dilate (open wider), in dim light.

What is the Near point of the Eye ?

The minimum distance at which object can be seen most distinctly without strain is the Near point of the Eye. It is 25cm for a normal eye.

What is the for point of the Eye ?

The farthest point upto which the eye can see objects clearly is the For point of the Eye. It is infinity for normal eye

Why is a normal eye not able to see clearly the objects placed closer than 25 cm?

Ciliary muscles can contract the lens of the human eye to a certain limit because of which a person with normal vision can see the nearby objects clearly only if placed at 25 cm but if the object is placed closer than 25 cm, then the person will not be able to see the objects clearly.

How can we see the nearby and far-off objects clearly?

The eye lens is composed of a fibrous, jelly-like material. Its curvature can be modified to some extent by the ciliary muscles. The change in the curvature of the eye lens can thus change its focal length.

When the muscles are relaxed, the lens becomes thin. Thus, its focal length increases. This enables us to see distant objects clearly.

When you are looking at objects closer to the eye, the ciliary muscles contract. This increases the curvature of the eye lens. The eye lens then becomes thicker. Consequently, the focal length of the eye lens decreases. This enables us to see nearby objects clearly.

What is meant by the power of accommodation of the eye?

The ability of the eye to focus the distant objects as well as the nearby objects on the retina by changing the focal length of the eye lens is called power of accommodation.

What is Presbyopia ? Write its causes and correction

As we become old, the power of accommodation of the eye usually decreases, the near point gradually recedes away.This defect is called Presbyopia.

People may suffer from both myopia and hypermetropia.

Reason of defect - Gradual weakening of ciliary muscles and decreasing the flexibility of the eye lens.

Correction- Using of Bifocal lens with appropriate power. Bifocal lenses consist of both concave and convex lenses, upper position consist of concave lens and lower portion consist of convex lens.

What is Dispersion ?

The splitting of light into its component colours is called Dispersion.

The different component colour of light bends at different angles with respect to incident angle; the red light bends the least while the violet bends most.

Why does dispersion of light occur?

Or, Why does red light deviate the least and violet light deviates the most?

Dispersion of light occurs due to following reasons

  1. Light of different colours has different wavelengths and on its basis, light of different colours bend by different amounts of refraction. In this bend, one can distinguish seven prominent colours. Wavelength of the light is inversely proportional to its angle of deviation Since the wavelength of red light is most, it deviates the least whereas the wavelength violet light is the least and thus, it deviates the most.
  2. Due to different refractive index for different colours.

What is internal reflection?

When a light ray enters from one medium to another (i.c., rarer to denser e.g., air to water droplet) then a ray of light instead of passing through it reflects in the second medium. This is known as internal reflection of light.

The star appears higher than its actual position. Why?

When the starlight enters the earth’s atmosphere it undergoes refraction continuously, due to changing refractive index i.e. from Rarer to denser. It bends towards the normal.

Due to this the apparent position of the star is different from actual position.

The star appear higher than its actual position.

Why do stars twinkle? (M. P. 2020 Suppl., 22 Suppl., Q. Bank)

Due to the presence of various gases having different densities in the earth's atmosphere, its refractive index gradually changes. The starlight, when entering this atmosphere, has to undergo continuous refraction. Thus, the path of rays of light coming from the star goes on varying slightly. As a result, the apparent position of the star fluctuates and the amount of starlight entering the observer's eye flickers, which gives the twinkling effect to the star.

This is the “Twinkling effect of star”

Why do the planets not twinkle?

Planets are closer to earth and are seen as extended source of light i.e. the collection of large no: of point sized sources of light. Therefore the total amount of light entering our eyes from all individual point source will nullify the twinkling effect.

Explain with the help of a diagram that how we can see the sun about two minutes before actual sunrise.

The sun is visible to us 2 minutes before the actual sunrise because of atmospheric refraction. When the sun is below the horizon, the light travelling from rarer to denser medium bends and reaches our eyes creating an impression that it is above the horizon.

Why the duration of a day is 4 minutes longer than the normal time?

Due to atmospheric refraction, at the time of sunrise and sunset, Sun is visible to us about 2 minutes before the actual sunrise and 2 minutes after the actual sunset. Therefore, the duration of day is 4 minutes longer than normal time.

What is scattering of light?

The scattering of light is the phenomenon in which light rays deviate from their original path when they strike small particles like dust, water droplets, or air molecules.

The colour of the scattered light depends on the size of the scattering particles

  1. Very fine particle - Scatter light of short wave length i.e. blue
  2. Large size particle - Scatter light of longer wave length i.e. red
  3. Very large particle enough - Scattered all wavelength of light almost equally.So,The sky appearwhite.
Why colour of sky is blue?

The molecules of air and other fine particles in the atmosphere have size smaller than the wavelength of visible light. Since the blue has shorter wavelength than red, hence it will scattered the most. So,colour of sky is blue.

Why the danger signal or sign are made of red colour?

Red colour scattered the least when strikes the small particle of fog and smoke because in has the maximum wavelength (visible spectrum). Hence at large distance also, we can see the red colour clearly.

Why sun appear red in

Sunrise and Sunset?

During sunset and sunrise, the sun is near horizon, and therefore the sunlight has to travel larger distance in atmosphere. Due to this most of the blue light (shorter wavelength) is scattered away by the particles. The light of longer wavelength (red colour) will reach our eye. This is why sun appear red in colour.

Why cloud Appear white?

The size of water droplet (scattering particle) is very large, hence scattered all wavelength of light almost equally.So,cloud Appear white.

: Why does the Sun appear white at noon?

At noon, the Sun is almost overhead. At this time, sunlight travels the shortest distance through the atmosphere, so only a small amount of blue and violet light is scattered. As a result, most of the colors of sunlight reach our eyes, and the Sun appears white.

If there is no earth’s atmosphere? What will happen to the scattering phenomenon?

There will be no scattering and the sky will appear dark.

What is the Tyndall Effect?

When a beam of light strikes the minute particle of earth’s atmosphere suspended particles of dust and molecule of air the path of beam become visible. The phenomenon is called the Tyndall Effect.

When is the Tyndall effect observed?

It can be observed when sunlight passes through a canopy of a dense forest.

Write the role of scattering of light in Tyndall effect.

The Tyndall effect occurs due to the scattering of light by fine particles present in a colloidal solution. It makes the path of light visible when a beam passes through such a mixture.
