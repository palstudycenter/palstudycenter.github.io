🧬 Class 10 Science — Unit 8: Heredity
Question 1. What is heredity?
Answer: Heredity is the transmission of characteristics or traits from parents to their offspring through genetic material. For example, certain characteristics such as blood group and some physical features can be inherited from parents.
Important: Heredity is based on genetic information carried by DNA and genes.
Question 2. What is variation?
Answer: Variation refers to the differences in characteristics or traits among individuals of the same species. For example, individuals of the same family may differ in height, hair characteristics, or other traits.
Question 3. Why are variations important for organisms?
Answer: Variations help organisms adapt to changing environmental conditions. Some variations may provide an advantage to an organism and increase its chances of survival and reproduction.
Variation is one of the important bases of evolution.
Question 6. What is the role of DNA in heredity?
Answer: DNA is the molecule that stores genetic information in living organisms. Genes present in DNA contain information needed for the formation of proteins and play an important role in determining characteristics.
Question 4. What is a gene?
Answer: A gene is a functional segment of DNA that contains information related to a particular characteristic or the production of a protein. Genes are located on chromosomes.
Question 11. What is an allele?
Answer: An allele is one of the alternative forms of a gene. For example, T and t may represent two different alleles controlling the height of a pea plant.
Question 10. What are Homozygous and Heterozygous?
Answer:

Homozygous: An organism is called homozygous when it has two identical alleles for a particular trait.

Examples: TT or tt

Heterozygous: An organism is called heterozygous when it has two different alleles for a particular trait.

Example: Tt

Question 5. What are chromosomes?
Answer: Chromosomes are thread-like structures made mainly of DNA and proteins. They are present in the nucleus of a cell. Genes are arranged on chromosomes and carry hereditary information.
Question 8. What are dominant and recessive traits?
Answer:

Dominant trait: A trait that is expressed even when only one copy of the corresponding dominant allele is present is called a dominant trait.

Recessive trait: A trait that is generally expressed only when both alleles are recessive is called a recessive trait. For example, in Mendel's pea plant experiment, tallness was dominant over dwarfness.

Question 9. Differentiate between genotype and phenotype.
Genotype Phenotype
The genetic constitution of an organism. The observable characteristics of an organism.
It is related to the genes and alleles present in an organism. It may be influenced by genes as well as environmental factors.
Examples: TT, Tt and tt. Examples: Tall plant or dwarf plant.
Question 7. Why did Mendel choose pea plants for his experiments?
Answer: Gregor Mendel selected pea plants because:
  • They have several clearly distinguishable contrasting traits.
  • They can undergo both self-pollination and cross-pollination.
  • They have a relatively short life cycle.
  • Large numbers of offspring can be obtained.
  • The inheritance of traits can be easily observed over generations.
Question 12. Explain Mendel's monohybrid cross.
Answer: A cross involving one pair of contrasting traits is called a monohybrid cross. For example, Mendel crossed a pure tall pea plant with a pure dwarf pea plant:
TT × tt
The F₁ generation contains:
All Tt
All F₁ plants are tall because T is dominant. When F₁ plants are crossed:
Tt × Tt
The possible genotypes in the F₂ generation are:
TT : Tt : Tt : tt
Therefore, the genotypic ratio is: 1 : 2 : 1 The phenotypic ratio is: 3 : 1
Question 13. Why is the phenotypic ratio 3:1 obtained in a monohybrid cross?
Answer: In the F₁ generation, the plants are heterozygous:
Tt × Tt
The possible combinations are:
T t
T TT Tt
t Tt tt
Three offspring have the dominant tall phenotype and one has the recessive dwarf phenotype. Therefore:
Phenotypic ratio = 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf
Question 14. What is a dihybrid cross?
Answer: A cross involving two pairs of contrasting traits at the same time is called a dihybrid cross. For example, Mendel studied seed shape and seed colour together. The typical F₂ phenotypic ratio in a dihybrid cross is:
9 : 3 : 3 : 1
Question 15. What is the law of independent assortment?
Answer: The law of independent assortment states that during gamete formation, the alleles of different genes can assort independently of one another, producing different combinations of traits. This principle is important in understanding inheritance of two or more different traits.
Question 16. How is sex determined in human beings?
Answer: In humans, females generally have XX sex chromosomes, while males generally have XY sex chromosomes. All eggs produced by a female carry an X chromosome. The sperm produced by a male may carry either X or Y chromosome. If an X-carrying sperm fertilises the egg:
X + X = XX → Female
If a Y-carrying sperm fertilises the egg:
X + Y = XY → Male
Thus, the sex chromosome carried by the fertilising sperm determines whether the zygote has an XX or XY combination.
Question 17. Is it correct to say that the mother is responsible for the sex of the child?
Answer: No. This statement is scientifically incorrect. The mother's egg always carries an X chromosome, whereas the father's sperm may carry either an X or a Y chromosome. Therefore, the sex chromosome contributed by the father's sperm determines whether the resulting combination is XX or XY.
The mother should not be blamed for the sex of a child. It is not scientifically justified.
Question 18. Differentiate between acquired traits and inherited traits.
Acquired Traits Inherited Traits
They may develop during the lifetime of an organism due to environmental conditions or experiences. They are passed from parents to offspring through genetic information.
They generally do not directly change the DNA of reproductive cells. They are associated with genetic information present in DNA.
Example: A skill developed through practice. Example: Blood group.
Question 19. How do genes and environment affect the characteristics of an organism?
Answer: The observable characteristics of an organism are not always determined by genes alone. Environmental factors can also affect many characteristics. For example, plant growth depends not only on its genetic potential but also on factors such as light, water, nutrients and temperature. Therefore, phenotype may be influenced by both genes and the environment.
Question 20. What are the major causes of variations?
Answer: Major causes of variation include:
  • Small errors during DNA copying.
  • Recombination of genetic material during sexual reproduction.
  • New combinations of genes and chromosomes.
  • Mutations in genetic material.
These processes can produce differences among offspring.
Question 21. What is mutation?
Answer: A mutation is a change in the genetic material, usually DNA. Some mutations can produce new characteristics. If a mutation occurs in reproductive cells and is inherited, it may be passed to the next generation.
Question 22. What is natural selection?
Answer: Natural selection is a process in which organisms having variations that are better suited to their environment are more likely to survive and reproduce. Over many generations, favourable variations may become more common in a population.
Question 23. What is the importance of variation in evolution?
Answer: Variation provides the basic material for evolution. When environmental conditions change, some variations may help organisms survive and reproduce more successfully. Over many generations, such favourable variations may become more common, contributing to evolutionary change.
Question 24. Why is a pedigree used in the study of human heredity?
Answer: A pedigree is a diagram that represents the inheritance of a particular trait through generations of a family. It helps scientists study how a trait is transmitted from parents to offspring and can help determine whether a trait may show dominant or recessive inheritance.
Question 25. Give examples of inherited traits in human beings.
Answer: Examples of traits having a genetic basis include:
  • Blood group
  • Some inherited characteristics of eye appearance
  • Some characteristics of hair
  • Some genetic disorders
  • Several other inherited physical characteristics
However, many observable characteristics can also be influenced by environmental factors.
Question 26. What is the relationship between heredity and variation?
Answer: Heredity transfers characteristics from parents to offspring, whereas variation refers to differences in characteristics among individuals. Therefore:
Heredity → Transmission of traits
Variation → Differences in traits
Both processes are important for understanding changes occurring in populations over generations.
Question 27. If the genotype of a tall pea plant is Tt, what will be its phenotype?
Answer: If T represents the dominant allele for tallness and t represents the recessive allele for dwarfness:
Tt → Tall plant
The dominant T allele is expressed even though the plant has one t allele.
Question 28. If the genotype of a pea plant is tt, what will be its phenotype?
Answer: If T represents tallness and t represents dwarfness:
tt → Dwarf plant
Both alleles are recessive, so the recessive dwarf phenotype is expressed.
