Question 1 (i): Find the nature of the roots of the quadratic equation \(2x^2-3x+5=0\). If the real roots exist, find them.

Solution -

Given equation:

\[ 2x^2-3x+5=0 \]

Here, \(a=2,\; b=-3,\; c=5\)

Discriminant,

\[ D=b^2-4ac \]

\[ D = (-3)^2-4(2)(5) \]

\[ D =9-40=-31 \]

\[ D =-31 \]

Since \(D<0\), the equation has no real roots.

Question 1 (ii): Find the nature of the roots of the quadratic equation \(3x^2-4\sqrt3x+4=0\). If the real roots exist, find them.

Solution -

Given equation:

\[ 3x^2-4\sqrt3x+4=0 \]

Here, \(a=3,\; b=-4\sqrt3,\; c=4\)

Discriminant,

\[ D=b^2-4ac \]

\[ D =(-4\sqrt3)^2-4(3)(4) \]

\[ D = 48-48 \]

\[ D = 0 \]

Since \(D=0\), the equation has two equal real roots.

Using the formula,

\[ x=\frac{-b}{2a} \]

\[ =\frac{4\sqrt3}{6} =\frac{2\sqrt3}{3} \]

Hence, the roots are

\[ \boxed{x=\frac{2\sqrt3}{3},\;\frac{2\sqrt3}{3}} \]

Question 1 (iii): Find the nature of the roots of the quadratic equation \(2x^2-6x+3=0\). If the real roots exist, find them.

Solution -

Given equation:

\[ 2x^2-6x+3=0 \]

Here, \(a=2,\; b=-6,\; c=3\)

Discriminant,

\[ D = b^2-4ac \]

\[ D = (-6)^2-4(2)(3) \]

\[ D = 36-24=12 \]

\[ D =12 \]

Since \(D>0\), the equation has two distinct real roots.

Using the quadratic formula,

\[ x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt D}{2a} \]

\[ =\frac{6\pm\sqrt{12}}{4} =\frac{6\pm2\sqrt3}{4} \]

\[ =\frac{3\pm\sqrt3}{2} \]

Hence, the roots are

\[ \boxed{x=\frac{3+\sqrt3}{2},\;\frac{3-\sqrt3}{2}} \]

Question 2 (i): Find the value of \(k\) so that the quadratic equation \(2x^2+kx+3=0\) has two equal roots.

Solution -

Given equation:

\[ 2x^2+kx+3=0 \]

Here, \(a=2,\; b=k,\; c=3\)

For equal roots,

\[ D = 0 \]

\[ b^2-4ac=0 \]

\[ k^2-4(2)(3)=0 \]

\[ k^2-24=0 \]

\[ k^2=24 \]

\[ k=\pm2\sqrt6 \]

Question 2 (ii): Find the value of \(k\) so that the quadratic equation \(kx(x-2)+6=0\) has two equal roots.

Solution -

Given equation:

\[ kx(x-2)+6=0 \]

\[ kx^2-2kx+6=0 \]

Here,\(a=k,\; b=-2k,\; c=6\)

For equal roots,

\[ D = 0 \]

\[ b^2-4ac=0 \]

\[ (-2k)^2-4(k)(6)=0 \]

\[ 4k^2-24k=0 \]

\[ 4k(k-6)=0 \]

Since the equation must remain quadratic, \(k\neq0\).

Therefore,

\[ k=6 \]

Question 3: Is it possible to design a rectangular mango grove whose length is twice its breadth, and the area is 800 m2? If so, find its length and breadth.

Solution -

Let the breadth of the mango grove be \(x\) m.

Then, its length is \(2x\) m.

According to the question,

Area of the rectangular grove = 800

\[ \text{Length} \times \text{Breadth} = 800\]

\[ 2x \times x = 800 \]

\[ 2x^2 = 800 \]

\[ x^2 = 400 \]

\[ x = \pm 20 \]

Since the breadth cannot be negative,

Breadth = 20 m

Length = \(2 \times 20 = 40\) m

Therefore,

Yes, it is possible.

Breadth = 20 m and Length = 40 m.

Question 4: Is the following situation possible? If so, determine their present ages. The sum of the ages of two friends is 20 years. Four years ago, the product of their ages was 48.

Solution -

Let the present age of one friend be \(x\) years.

Then, the present age of the other friend is \((20-x)\) years.

Four years ago, their ages were \((x-4)\) years and \((16-x)\) years.

According to the question,

\[ (x-4)(16-x)=48 \]

\[ 16x-x^2-64+4x=48 \]

\[ -x^2+20x-112=48 \]

\[ x^2-20x+160=0 \]

Here,

\(a=1,\; b=-20,\; c=160\)

Discriminant,

\[ D=b^2-4ac \]

\[ =(-20)^2-4(1)(160) \]

\[ =400-640=-240 \]

Since \(D<0\), the quadratic equation has no real roots.

Therefore,

No, this situation is not possible.

Question 5: Is it possible to design a rectangular park of perimeter 80 m and area 400 m2? If so, find its length and breadth.

Solution -

Let the length of the rectangular park be \(x\) m.

Let the breadth of the park be \(y\) m.

Perimeter of the park = 80 m

\[ 2(x+y)=80 \]

\[ x+y=40 \]

\[ y=40-x \]

Area of the park = 400 m2

\[ x(40-x)=400 \]

\[ 40x-x^2=400 \]

\[ x^2-40x+400=0 \]

\[ (x-20)^2=0 \]

\[ x-20=0 \]

\[ x=20 \]

Hence,

\[ y=40-20=20 \]

Therefore,

Yes, it is possible.

Length = 20 m and Breadth = 20 m.
