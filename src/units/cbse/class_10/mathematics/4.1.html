📘 Unit-4 Quadratic Equations
Question 1 (i): Check whether \((x+1)^2=2(x-3)\) is a quadratic equation.

Solution -

Given equation:

\[ (x+1)^2=2(x-3) \]

Expanding both sides,

\[ x^2+2x+1=2x-6 \]

Bringing all terms to one side,

\[ x^2+7=0 \]

The highest power of \(x\) is 2.

Hence, it is a quadratic equation.

Question 1 (ii): Check whether \(x^2-2x=(-2)(3-x)\) is a quadratic equation.

Solution -

Given equation:

\[ x^2-2x=(-2)(3-x) \]

Expanding the right-hand side,

\[ x^2-2x=-6+2x \]

Bringing all terms to one side,

\[ x^2-4x+6=0 \]

The highest power of \(x\) is 2.

Hence, it is a quadratic equation.

Question 1 (iii): Check whether \((x-2)(x+1)=(x-1)(x+3)\) is a quadratic equation.

Solution -

Given equation:

\[ (x-2)(x+1)=(x-1)(x+3) \]

Expanding both sides,

\[ x^2-x-2=x^2+2x-3 \]

Bringing all terms to one side,

\[ -3x+1=0 \]

The highest power of \(x\) is 1.

Hence, it is not a quadratic equation.

Question 1 (iv): Check whether \((x-3)(2x+1)=x(x+5)\) is a quadratic equation.

Solution -

Given equation:

\[ (x-3)(2x+1)=x(x+5) \]

Expanding both sides,

\[ 2x^2-5x-3=x^2+5x \]

Bringing all terms to one side,

\[ x^2-10x-3=0 \]

The highest power of \(x\) is 2.

Hence, it is a quadratic equation.

Question 2 (i): The area of a rectangular plot is 528 m2. The length of the plot (in metres) is one more than twice its breadth. Represent the situation in the form of a quadratic equation.

Solution -

Let the breadth of the rectangular plot be \(x\) m.

Then, the length of the plot is \((2x+1)\) m.

Area of the plot = Length × Breadth

\[ x(2x+1)=528 \]

Bringing all the terms to one side,

\[ 2x^2+x-528=0 \]

Hence, the required quadratic equation is

\[ 2x^2+x-528=0 \]

Question 2 (ii): The product of two consecutive positive integers is 306. Represent the situation in the form of a quadratic equation.

Solution -

Let the first positive integer be \(x\).

Then, the next consecutive integer is \((x+1)\).

According to the question,

\[ x(x+1)=306 \]

Bringing all the terms to one side,

\[ x^2+x-306=0 \]

Hence, the required quadratic equation is

\[ x^2+x-306=0 \]

Question 2 (iii): Rohan's mother is 26 years older than him. The product of their ages (in years) 3 years from now will be 360. Represent the situation in the form of a quadratic equation.

Solution -

Let Rohan's present age be \(x\) years.

Then, his mother's present age is \((x+26)\) years.

After 3 years,

Rohan's age = \((x+3)\) years

Mother's age = \((x+29)\) years

According to the question,

\[ (x+3)(x+29)=360 \]

Expanding,

\[ x^2+32x+87=360 \]

Bringing all the terms to one side,

\[ x^2+32x-273=0 \]

Hence, the required quadratic equation is

\[ x^2+32x-273=0 \]

Question 2 (iv): A train travels a distance of 480 km at a uniform speed. If the speed had been 8 km/h less, then it would have taken 3 hours more to cover the same distance. Represent the situation in the form of a quadratic equation.

Solution -

Let the speed of the train be \(x\) km/h.

Time taken to travel 480 km = \(\dfrac{480}{x}\) hours.

If the speed were \((x-8)\) km/h, then the time taken would be \(\dfrac{480}{x-8}\) hours.

According to the question,

\[ \frac{480}{x-8}=\frac{480}{x}+3 \]

Multiplying both sides by \(x(x-8)\),

\[ 480x=480(x-8)+3x(x-8) \]

Simplifying,

\[ 3x^2-24x-3840=0 \]

Dividing throughout by 3,

\[ x^2-8x-1280=0 \]

Hence, the required quadratic equation is

\[ x^2-8x-1280=0 \]
