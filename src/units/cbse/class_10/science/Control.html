Control and Coordination
Q1. What is control and coordination?

Answer: Control and coordination is the process by which different parts of an organism work together and respond properly to internal and external stimuli.

Q2. What is a stimulus?

Answer: A stimulus is any change in the surroundings of an organism that produces a response.

Q18. What are receptors?

Answer: Receptors are specialised cells or groups of cells that detect stimuli such as light, sound, heat, pressure, smell and taste.

Q3. What is the nervous system?

Answer: The nervous system is a system of specialised cells and organs that receives information, processes it and controls the activities of the body.

Q4. What is a neuron?

Answer: A neuron is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system. It is specialised to receive and transmit electrical impulses.

Q5. Name the main parts of a neuron.

Answer: The main parts of a neuron are:

  1. Cell body or cyton
  2. Dendrites
  3. Axon
Q6. What is the function of dendrites?

Answer: Dendrites receive information or nerve impulses from receptors or other neurons and carry them towards the cell body.

Q7. What is the function of the axon?

Answer: The axon carries nerve impulses away from the cell body towards another neuron, muscle or gland.

Q8. What is a synapse?

Answer: A synapse is the small junction or gap between two neurons through which nerve impulses are transmitted with the help of chemicals.

Q9. What is a reflex action?

Answer: A reflex action is a quick, automatic and involuntary response to a stimulus. For example, withdrawing the hand immediately after touching a hot object.

Q10. What is a reflex arc?

Answer: The pathway followed by nerve impulses during a reflex action is called a reflex arc.

A reflex arc generally involves a receptor, sensory neuron, spinal cord, motor neuron and effector.

Q11. Why are reflex actions important?

Answer: Reflex actions protect the body from sudden harmful stimuli. They are very quick because the response is mainly coordinated through the spinal cord.

Q12. What is the role of the brain in coordination?

Answer: The brain receives information from sense organs, interprets it and coordinates suitable responses. It also controls many voluntary and involuntary activities of the body.

Q13. Name the three major parts of the human brain.

Answer: The three major parts of the human brain are:

  1. Forebrain
  2. Midbrain
  3. Hindbrain
Q14. What are the functions of the forebrain?

Answer: The forebrain is involved in thinking, memory, intelligence, reasoning, interpreting sensory information and controlling voluntary actions.

Q15. What is the function of the cerebellum?

Answer: The cerebellum coordinates muscular movements and helps maintain posture and balance of the body.

Q16. What is the function of the medulla?

Answer: The medulla controls involuntary activities such as breathing, heartbeat, blood pressure and swallowing.

Q17. What is the function of the spinal cord?

Answer: The spinal cord carries nerve impulses between the brain and the rest of the body. It also coordinates many reflex actions.

Q19. What are hormones?

Answer: Hormones are chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands. They are transported through the blood and regulate various functions of the body.

Q20. What is the endocrine system?

Answer: The endocrine system consists of glands that release hormones directly into the bloodstream to control and coordinate various activities of the body.

Q21. Name some important endocrine glands in humans.

Answer: Important endocrine glands include the pituitary gland, thyroid gland, adrenal glands, pancreas, testes and ovaries.

Q22. What is the function of the pituitary gland?

Answer: The pituitary gland secretes several hormones and controls the activities of other endocrine glands. It also produces growth hormone.

Q23. What is the function of thyroxine?

Answer: Thyroxine regulates metabolism and helps in the proper growth and development of the body.

Q24. What is the function of insulin?

Answer: Insulin helps regulate the level of glucose in the blood. It is secreted by the pancreas.

Q25. What is the function of adrenaline?

Answer: Adrenaline prepares the body to deal with emergency situations. It increases heart rate, breathing rate and blood flow to the muscles.

Q26. What is tropism?

Answer: Tropism is the directional growth movement of a plant part in response to an external stimulus.

Q27. What is phototropism?

Answer: Phototropism is the growth movement of a plant part in response to light. Shoots generally show positive phototropism.

Q28. What is geotropism?

Answer: Geotropism is the growth movement of a plant part in response to gravity. Roots generally show positive geotropism, while shoots show negative geotropism.

Q29. What is hydrotropism?

Answer: Hydrotropism is the growth movement of a plant part in response to water. Roots generally grow towards water and therefore show positive hydrotropism.

Q30. What is chemotropism?

Answer: Chemotropism is the growth movement of a plant part in response to a chemical stimulus. Growth of the pollen tube towards the ovule is an example of chemotropism.

Q31. What is the role of auxin in plants?

Answer: Auxin promotes cell growth and helps in the growth of shoots. It also plays an important role in phototropic movement.

Q32. What is the role of gibberellins?

Answer: Gibberellins promote stem growth and help in the growth and development of plants.

Q33. What is the role of cytokinins?

Answer: Cytokinins promote cell division and help in the growth of plant tissues.

Q34. What is the role of abscisic acid?

Answer: Abscisic acid generally inhibits plant growth and helps plants respond to stress. It also promotes dormancy and stomatal closure during water stress.

Q37. How does the movement of a shoot towards light occur?

Answer: Auxin produced near the shoot tip moves towards the shaded side of the shoot. It promotes cell elongation on that side, causing the shoot to bend towards the light.

Q38. Why is the use of iodised salt advisable?

Answer: Iodine is required by the thyroid gland to produce thyroxine. Iodised salt helps prevent iodine deficiency and disorders related to thyroid function.

Q39. What happens when the body produces insufficient insulin?

Answer: Insufficient insulin can cause the blood glucose level to rise and may lead to diabetes.

Q40. Why is the coordination of the nervous system important?

Answer: Nervous coordination enables the body to detect changes, process information and produce quick and appropriate responses. It helps different organs work together efficiently.
