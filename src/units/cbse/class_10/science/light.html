📘 Unit 9- Light
Q. What is Light?

Answer -

Light is a form of energy that enables us to see objects.

Q. What is the Nature of Light?

Answer -

Light has a dual nature. It behaves as both a wave and a particle.

Q. What do you mean by the Reflection of Light?

Answer -

The reflection of light is the bouncing back of light into the same medium after striking a smooth surface.

Q. What is the Angle of Incidence?

Answer -

The angle of incidence is the angle between the incident ray and the normal at the point of incidence.

Q. What is the Angle of Reflection?

Answer -

The angle of reflection is the angle between the reflected ray and the normal at the point of incidence.

Q. Write the Laws of Reflection of Light.

Answer -

  1. The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
  2. The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection (∠i = ∠r).
Question - What is an Object?

Answer -

An object is anything that emits light or reflects light and can be seen by our eyes.

Question - What is an Image? How many types of images are there?

Answer -

An image is the optical appearance formed when light rays coming from an object are reflected from a mirror or refracted through a lens.

There are two types of images:

  1. Real Image
  2. Virtual Image
Question - What is a Real Image?

Answer -

A real image is formed when light rays actually meet after reflection or refraction. It can be obtained on a screen and is always inverted.

Example: Image formed on a cinema screen.

Question - What is a Virtual Image?

Answer -

A virtual image is formed when light rays only appear to meet. It cannot be obtained on a screen and is always erect.

Example: Image formed by a plane mirror.

Question - Write the difference between Real Image and Virtual Image.

Answer -

Real Image Virtual Image
It is formed by the actual meeting of light rays. It is formed by the apparent meeting of light rays.
It can be obtained on a screen. It cannot be obtained on a screen.
It is always inverted. It is always erect.
Example: Cinema screen. Example: Plane mirror.
Question - What is Lateral Inversion?

Answer -

The phenomenon in which the left side of an object appears as the right side and the right side appears as the left side in a plane mirror is called lateral inversion.

Question - What are the properties of an image formed by a Plane Mirror?

Answer -

  1. The image is virtual.
  2. The image is erect.
  3. The image is of the same size as the object.
  4. The image is formed at the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front of it.
  5. The image is laterally inverted.
Question - Write the uses of a Plane Mirror.

Answer -

  1. Used as dressing mirrors.
  2. Used in periscopes.
  3. Used in shops to make rooms appear bigger.
  4. Used at blind turns on roads to avoid accidents.
Question - What is a Spherical Mirror?

Answer -

A spherical mirror is a mirror whose reflecting surface is a part of a hollow sphere.

Types of Spherical Mirrors:

  1. Concave Mirror
  2. Convex Mirror
Question - What is a Concave Mirror?

Answer -

A concave mirror is a spherical mirror whose reflecting surface is curved inward. It is also called a converging mirror.

Question - What is a Convex Mirror?

Answer -

A convex mirror is a spherical mirror whose reflecting surface is curved outward. It is also called a diverging mirror.

Question - Define the following terms related to a Spherical Mirror.

Answer -

  1. Pole (P): The pole is the centre of the reflecting surface of a spherical mirror.
  2. Centre of Curvature (C): The centre of curvature is the centre of the hollow sphere of which the spherical mirror is a part.
  3. Radius of Curvature (R): The radius of curvature is the distance between the pole (P) and the centre of curvature (C) of a spherical mirror.
  4. Principal Axis: The principal axis is the straight line passing through the pole (P) and the centre of curvature (C) of a spherical mirror.
  5. Aperture: The aperture is the diameter of the reflecting surface of a spherical mirror through which reflection takes place.
  6. Principal Focus (Concave Mirror): The principal focus (F) of a concave mirror is the point on its principal axis where rays parallel to the principal axis meet after reflection.
  7. Principal Focus (Convex Mirror): The principal focus (F) of a convex mirror is the point on its principal axis from which rays parallel to the principal axis appear to diverge after reflection.
  8. Focal Length (f): The focal length is the distance between the pole (P) and the principal focus (F) of a spherical mirror.
Question - Write the Ray Diagram Rules for Image Formation by a Concave Mirror.

Answer -

  1. A ray of light parallel to the principal axis passes through the principal focus (F) after reflection.
  2. A ray of light passing through the principal focus (F) is reflected parallel to the principal axis.
  3. A ray of light passing through the centre of curvature (C) is reflected back along the same path.
  4. A ray of light striking the pole (P) of the mirror is reflected according to the laws of reflection.

Question - Write the uses of a Concave Mirror.

Answer -

  1. Used as a shaving mirror to obtain a magnified image of the face.
  2. Used by dentists to see an enlarged image of teeth.
  3. Used as reflectors in torches, searchlights, and vehicle headlights.
  4. Used in solar furnaces to concentrate sunlight.
  5. Used by doctors to focus light on the part being examined.
Question - Write the uses of a Convex Mirror.

Answer -

  1. Used as rear-view mirrors in vehicles.
  2. Used as security mirrors in shops and supermarkets.
  3. Used at road intersections to provide a wider field of view.
Question - Which mirror is used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles? Why?

Answer -

A convex mirror is used as a rear-view mirror because it forms an erect, diminished image and provides a wider field of view.

Question - Write the Mirror Formula.

Answer -

The mirror formula gives the relationship between the object distance (u), image distance (v), and focal length (f) of a spherical mirror.

\[ \frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{u}+\frac{1}{v} \]

Question - What is Linear Magnification of a Mirror?

Answer -

Linear magnification is the ratio of the height of the image to the height of the object.

\[ m=\frac{h_i}{h_o}=-\frac{v}{u} \]

Question - The magnification produced by a plane mirror is +1. What does it mean?

Answer -

The sign (+) indicates that the image is virtual and erect, while 1 indicates that the image is of the same size as the object.

Question - Write the Sign Convention for Mirrors.

Answer -

  • Object distance (u) is always negative.
  • Image distance (v) is negative for a real image and positive for a virtual image.
  • Focal length (f) is negative for a concave mirror and positive for a convex mirror.
  • Magnification (m) is negative for a real image and positive for a virtual image.
