📘Unit 5 - Life Processes
Question - What are Life Processes?

Answer -

Life processes are the basic biological activities that are essential for maintaining life in living organisms. These processes help organisms to obtain energy, grow, repair tissues, and survive.

The four main life processes are:

  1. Nutrition
  2. Respiration
  3. Transportation
  4. Excretion
Question - What is Nutrition?

Answer -

Nutrition is the process by which living organisms obtain food and utilize it for energy, growth, repair, and maintenance of the body.

Question - How many types of Nutrition are there?

Answer -

There are two main types of nutrition:

  1. Autotrophic Nutrition
  2. Heterotrophic Nutrition
Question - What is Autotrophic Nutrition?

Answer -

Autotrophic nutrition is the mode of nutrition in which organisms prepare their own food from carbon dioxide and water using sunlight in the presence of chlorophyll.

Examples: Green plants and algae.

Question - What is Photosynthesis?

Answer -

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants prepare their own food from carbon dioxide and water in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.

Chemical Equation:

\[ 6CO_2 + 6H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{Sunlight, Chlorophyll}} C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \]

Question - What are the essential requirements for Photosynthesis?

Answer -

  1. Sunlight
  2. Chlorophyll
  3. Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
  4. Water (H₂O)
Question - What is the role of Chlorophyll in Photosynthesis?

Answer -

Chlorophyll is the green pigment present in leaves. It absorbs sunlight and provides the energy required for photosynthesis.

Question - What is Heterotrophic Nutrition?

Answer -

Heterotrophic nutrition is the mode of nutrition in which organisms depend on other organisms for their food because they cannot prepare it themselves.

Examples: Humans, animals, and fungi.

Question - What are the types of Heterotrophic Nutrition?

Answer -

There are three main types of heterotrophic nutrition:

  1. Holozoic Nutrition
  2. Saprophytic Nutrition
  3. Parasitic Nutrition

Question - What is Holozoic Nutrition?

Answer -

Holozoic nutrition is a mode of nutrition in which organisms ingest, digest, absorb, assimilate, and egest food.

Examples: Human beings, Amoeba, and most animals.

Question - What is Nutrition in Amoeba?

Answer -

Amoeba shows holozoic nutrition. It engulfs food with the help of temporary finger-like projections called pseudopodia. The food is digested inside a food vacuole, absorbed, assimilated, and the undigested food is expelled outside the body.

Question - What are the steps of Holozoic Nutrition in Amoeba?

Answer -

  1. Ingestion
  2. Digestion
  3. Absorption
  4. Assimilation
  5. Egestion
Question - What is Nutrition in Human Beings?

Answer -

Human beings show holozoic nutrition. Food is ingested through the mouth, digested in the alimentary canal, absorbed into the blood, assimilated by body cells, and the undigested food is eliminated through the anus.

Question - What is the Human Digestive System?

Answer -

The human digestive system is a group of organs that helps in the digestion, absorption, and assimilation of food and the removal of undigested waste.

Question - Name the parts of the Human Alimentary Canal.

Answer -

  1. Mouth (Buccal Cavity)
  2. Oesophagus (Food Pipe)
  3. Stomach
  4. Small Intestine
  5. Large Intestine
  6. Rectum
  7. Anus
Question - Name the Digestive Glands in Human Beings.

Answer -

  1. Salivary Glands
  2. Liver
  3. Pancreas
Question - What is Digestion?

Answer -

Digestion is the process of breaking down complex food into simple, soluble substances that can be absorbed by the body.

Question - What is the role of Saliva in Digestion?

Answer -

Saliva is secreted by the salivary glands. It moistens the food and contains the enzyme salivary amylase (ptyalin), which begins the digestion of starch into simple sugars.

Question - What is the role of Hydrochloric Acid (HCl) in the Stomach?

Answer -

Hydrochloric acid (HCl) creates an acidic medium in the stomach, activates the enzyme pepsin, and kills harmful microorganisms present in food.

Question - What is the role of Pepsin?

Answer -

Pepsin is a digestive enzyme present in the stomach. It digests proteins into simpler substances called peptones.

Question - What is the role of Bile Juice?

Answer -

Bile juice is produced by the liver and stored in the gall bladder. It neutralizes the acidic food coming from the stomach and helps in the digestion of fats by breaking them into tiny droplets (emulsification).

Question - What is the role of Pancreatic Juice?

Answer -

Pancreatic juice contains enzymes that digest carbohydrates, proteins, and fats into simpler substances.

Question - What is the role of the Small Intestine?

Answer -

The small intestine completes the digestion of food and absorbs the digested nutrients into the bloodstream through the villi.

Question - What are Villi?

Answer -

Villi are tiny finger-like projections present on the inner lining of the small intestine. They increase the surface area for the absorption of digested food.

Question - What is Assimilation?

Answer -

Assimilation is the process by which absorbed nutrients are utilized by the body cells for energy, growth, and repair.

Question - What is Egestion?

Answer -

Egestion is the process of removing undigested and unabsorbed food from the body through the anus.

Question - What is Saprophytic Nutrition?

Answer -

Saprophytic nutrition is a mode of nutrition in which organisms obtain food from dead and decaying organic matter by secreting digestive enzymes outside the body.

Examples: Mushroom, Bread mould, and Fungi.

Question - What is Parasitic Nutrition?

Answer -

Parasitic nutrition is a mode of nutrition in which an organism lives on or inside another living organism (host) and obtains food from it.

Examples: Cuscuta (Amarbel), Tapeworm, and Leech.

Question - What is Respiration?

Answer -

Respiration is the process of breaking down food (glucose) to release energy for carrying out various life processes.

Question - How many types of Respiration are there?

Answer -

There are two types of respiration:

  1. Aerobic Respiration
  2. Anaerobic Respiration
Question - What is Aerobic Respiration?

Answer -

Aerobic respiration is the breakdown of glucose in the presence of oxygen to release energy.

Chemical Equation:

\[ C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \rightarrow 6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{Energy (ATP)} \]

Question - What is Anaerobic Respiration?

Answer -

Anaerobic respiration is the breakdown of glucose in the absence of oxygen to release a small amount of energy.

In Yeast:

\[ C_6H_{12}O_6 \rightarrow 2C_2H_5OH + 2CO_2 + \text{Energy} \]

In Human Muscles:

\[ C_6H_{12}O_6 \rightarrow 2C_3H_6O_3 + \text{Energy} \]

Question - Write the differences between Aerobic and Anaerobic Respiration.

Answer -

Aerobic Respiration Anaerobic Respiration
Occurs in the presence of oxygen. Occurs in the absence of oxygen.
Releases a large amount of energy. Releases a small amount of energy.
End products are carbon dioxide and water. End products are ethanol and carbon dioxide (in yeast) or lactic acid (in muscles).
Question - What is Breathing?

Answer -

Breathing is the physical process of inhaling oxygen-rich air and exhaling carbon dioxide-rich air.

Question - What is the Human Respiratory System?

Answer -

The human respiratory system is a group of organs that helps in breathing and the exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide.

Main Parts:

  1. Nostrils
  2. Nasal Cavity
  3. Pharynx
  4. Larynx
  5. Trachea
  6. Bronchi
  7. Bronchioles
  8. Alveoli
  9. Lungs

Question - What are Alveoli?

Answer -

Alveoli are tiny, balloon-like air sacs present in the lungs. They provide a large surface area for the exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide between the air and the blood.

Question - What is Transportation?

Answer -

Transportation is the process of carrying food, oxygen, water, hormones, and waste materials from one part of the body to another.

Question - What are the components of Blood?

Answer -

Blood consists of four main components:

  1. Plasma
  2. Red Blood Cells (RBCs)
  3. White Blood Cells (WBCs)
  4. Platelets
Question - What is Plasma?

Answer -

Plasma is the yellow-coloured liquid part of blood. It transports digested food, hormones, carbon dioxide, and other dissolved substances throughout the body.

Question - What are Red Blood Cells (RBCs)?

Answer -

Red Blood Cells (RBCs) contain haemoglobin, which carries oxygen from the lungs to all parts of the body.

Question - What are White Blood Cells (WBCs)?

Answer -

White Blood Cells (WBCs) protect the body from diseases by destroying harmful microorganisms and providing immunity.

Question - What are Platelets?

Answer -

Platelets are tiny blood cells that help in the clotting of blood and prevent excessive bleeding during an injury.

Question - What is the Human Heart?

Answer -

The heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood to all parts of the body. It has four chambers: two atria and two ventricles.

Question - What is Double Circulation?

Answer -

Double circulation is the process in which blood passes through the heart twice during one complete circulation. It consists of pulmonary circulation and systemic circulation.

Question - What are Arteries?

Answer -

Arteries are blood vessels that carry oxygenated blood away from the heart to different parts of the body. The pulmonary artery is an exception as it carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs.

Question - What are Veins?

Answer -

Veins are blood vessels that carry deoxygenated blood from different parts of the body back to the heart. The pulmonary vein is an exception as it carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart.

Question - What are Capillaries?

Answer -

Capillaries are the smallest blood vessels that connect arteries and veins. They allow the exchange of oxygen, nutrients, and waste materials between the blood and body tissues.

Question - What is Lymph?

Answer -

Lymph is a colourless tissue fluid that transports absorbed fats from the small intestine and helps in the body's immune system.

Question - What is Transportation in Plants?

Answer -

Transportation in plants is the process of moving water, minerals, and food from one part of the plant to another through specialized tissues.

Question - What is Xylem?

Answer -

Xylem is a vascular tissue that transports water and minerals from the roots to the stem and leaves.

Question - What is Phloem?

Answer -

Phloem is a vascular tissue that transports prepared food from the leaves to all parts of the plant.

Question - What is Transpiration?

Answer -

Transpiration is the process of losing water in the form of water vapour from the aerial parts of plants, mainly through the stomata.

Question - What is Excretion?

Answer -

Excretion is the process of removing metabolic waste products and toxic substances from the body.

Question - What is the Human Excretory System?

Answer -

The human excretory system is the organ system responsible for removing nitrogenous wastes and maintaining the water and salt balance of the body.

Main parts of the human excretory system:

  1. Kidneys
  2. Ureters
  3. Urinary Bladder
  4. Urethra
Question - What are Kidneys?

Answer -

Kidneys are bean-shaped organs that filter blood, remove nitrogenous wastes, and produce urine.

Question - What is a Nephron?

Answer -

A nephron is the structural and functional unit of the kidney. It filters blood, reabsorbs useful substances, and forms urine.

Question - What is Urine?

Answer -

Urine is a liquid waste product formed in the kidneys. It contains water, urea, salts, and other dissolved waste substances.

Question - What is Dialysis?

Answer -

Dialysis is an artificial process of filtering blood when the kidneys fail to function properly.

Question - How does Excretion take place in Plants?

Answer -

Plants remove waste products through stomata, lenticels, and by storing wastes in leaves, bark, fruits, and old xylem tissues. They also release excess water by transpiration.

Question - Why are Life Processes Important?

Answer -

Life processes are essential because they provide energy, support growth and repair, transport materials, remove wastes, and help living organisms survive.

Answer -

Breathing Respiration
It is the process of inhaling oxygen and exhaling carbon dioxide. It is the process of breaking down food to release energy.
It is a physical process. It is a biochemical process.
It takes place in the lungs. It takes place inside the cells.
No energy is released. Energy (ATP) is released.
Question - What is ATP?

Answer -

ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate) is known as the energy currency of the cell. It stores and supplies energy for all life processes.

Question - Why is the small intestine the main site of absorption?

Answer -

The small intestine is the main site of absorption because its inner lining has millions of finger-like projections called villi, which greatly increase the surface area for absorbing digested nutrients.

Question - Why do arteries have thick and elastic walls?

Answer -

Arteries have thick and elastic walls because blood flows through them at high pressure from the heart.

Question - Why do veins have valves?

Answer -

Veins have valves to prevent the backflow of blood and ensure that blood flows only towards the heart.

Question - What is the function of Stomata?

Answer -

Stomata are tiny pores present on the surface of leaves. They help in the exchange of gases and the loss of water vapour during transpiration.

Question - What is Urea?

Answer -

Urea is the main nitrogenous waste product formed in the liver during protein metabolism. It is filtered by the kidneys and excreted through urine.

Question - What is the function of the Liver?

Answer -

The liver is the largest gland in the human body. It produces bile juice, which helps in the digestion of fats. It also stores glycogen, detoxifies harmful substances, and regulates metabolism.

Question - What is the function of the Pancreas?

Answer -

The pancreas secretes pancreatic juice containing enzymes that digest carbohydrates, proteins, and fats. It also produces the hormones insulin and glucagon to regulate blood sugar levels.

Question - What is the function of the Large Intestine?

Answer -

The large intestine absorbs water and salts from undigested food and stores the remaining waste as faeces before egestion.

Question - What is Haemoglobin?

Answer -

Haemoglobin is an iron-containing red pigment present in red blood cells (RBCs). It transports oxygen from the lungs to all parts of the body.

Question - What is Pulse?

Answer -

Pulse is the rhythmic expansion and contraction of arteries caused by the pumping action of the heart. It indicates the heartbeat rate.

Question - What is Blood Pressure?

Answer -

Blood pressure is the force exerted by flowing blood on the walls of arteries. The normal blood pressure of a healthy adult is about 120/80 mm Hg.

Question - What is the function of the Kidneys?

Answer -

The kidneys filter blood, remove nitrogenous wastes, regulate water and salt balance, and produce urine.

Question - What are the functions of Blood?

Answer -

  1. Transports oxygen and carbon dioxide.
  2. Transports digested food and hormones.
  3. Carries waste products to excretory organs.
  4. Protects the body from infections.
  5. Helps in blood clotting.
Question - Why is energy required by living organisms?

Answer -

Living organisms require energy for growth, movement, repair of tissues, reproduction, transport of substances, and all other life processes.
