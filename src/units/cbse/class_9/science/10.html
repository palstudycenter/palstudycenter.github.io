📘 Chapter 10 – Gravitation
Q1. What is gravitation?

Answer: Gravitation is the force of attraction between any two objects in the universe due to their masses.

Q2. State the universal law of gravitation.

Answer: Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force which is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres.

The mathematical expression is:

F = Gm₁m₂ / r²

Where F is the gravitational force, m₁ and m₂ are the masses of the two objects, r is the distance between their centres and G is the universal gravitational constant.

Q3. What is the SI unit of the universal gravitational constant G?

Answer: The SI unit of the universal gravitational constant is N m² kg⁻².

Its approximate value is:

G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²

Q4. What is free fall?

Answer: The motion of an object towards the Earth under the influence of Earth's gravitational force alone is called free fall.

Q5. What is acceleration due to gravity?

Answer: The acceleration produced in an object due to the gravitational force of the Earth is called acceleration due to gravity. It is represented by g.

Near the surface of the Earth:

g ≈ 9.8 m/s²

Q6. Write the relation between G and g.

Answer: The relation between the universal gravitational constant G and acceleration due to gravity g is:

g = GM / R²

Where M is the mass of the Earth and R is the radius of the Earth.

Q7. What is the difference between mass and weight?
Mass Weight
Mass is the amount of matter contained in an object. Weight is the gravitational force acting on an object.
Its SI unit is kilogram (kg). Its SI unit is newton (N).
Mass remains constant at different places. Weight may change from place to place.
Mass is measured using a beam balance. Weight is measured using a spring balance.
Q8. Write the formula for weight of an object.

Answer:

W = mg

Where W is weight, m is mass and g is acceleration due to gravity.

Q9. Why is the weight of an object on the Moon about one-sixth of its weight on the Earth?

Answer: The Moon has much less mass than the Earth. Therefore, the gravitational acceleration on the Moon is about one-sixth of that on Earth. Since W = mg, the weight of an object on the Moon is about one-sixth of its weight on Earth.

Q10. What is thrust?

Answer: The force acting normally on a surface is called thrust.

Q11. What is pressure?

Answer: Pressure is the force acting normally on a unit area of a surface.

Pressure = Force / Area

Its SI unit is pascal (Pa).

Q12. Why does a sharp knife cut better than a blunt knife?

Answer: A sharp knife has a smaller area of contact. For the same force, it produces greater pressure. Therefore, it cuts objects more easily than a blunt knife.

Q13. Why are school bags provided with broad straps?

Answer: Broad straps increase the area of contact with the shoulders. Therefore, the pressure exerted by the bag on the shoulders decreases, making the bag more comfortable to carry.

Q14. What is buoyancy?

Answer: The upward force exerted by a fluid on an object immersed in it is called buoyant force or buoyancy.

Q15. Why does an object float or sink in water?

Answer: An object floats when the buoyant force acting on it is equal to or greater than its weight. An object sinks when its weight is greater than the buoyant force.

Q16. State Archimedes' principle.

Answer: When an object is completely or partially immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the object.

Q17. Why does a ship made of iron float on water?

Answer: A ship is hollow and contains a large volume of air. Its average density becomes less than the density of water. Therefore, the buoyant force can balance its weight and the ship floats.

Q18. Why does a person feel lighter while standing in water?

Answer: Water exerts an upward buoyant force on the person. This upward force reduces the apparent weight of the person, so the person feels lighter in water.

Q19. What is relative density?

Answer: Relative density is the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water at 4°C.

Relative density = Density of substance / Density of water

Relative density has no unit.

Q20. What is the density of a substance?

Answer: Density is the mass contained in a unit volume of a substance.

Density = Mass / Volume

The SI unit of density is kg/m³.

Q21. Why do objects fall towards the Earth?

Answer: Objects fall towards the Earth because the Earth attracts objects towards its centre due to gravitational force.

Q22. What happens to the value of gravitational force if the distance between two objects is doubled?

Answer: According to the universal law of gravitation:

F ∝ 1/r²

If the distance is doubled, the gravitational force becomes:

F' = F/4

Therefore, the gravitational force becomes one-fourth of its original value.

Q23. What happens to the gravitational force if the mass of one object is doubled?

Answer: Gravitational force is directly proportional to the mass of each object. Therefore, if the mass of one object is doubled, the gravitational force also becomes double.

Q24. Why do objects of different masses fall with the same acceleration in the absence of air resistance?

Answer: The acceleration due to gravity near the surface of the Earth does not depend on the mass of the falling object. Therefore, in the absence of air resistance, objects of different masses fall with the same acceleration g.

Q25. What is meant by acceleration due to gravity on the Moon?

Answer: The acceleration produced in an object due to the gravitational force of the Moon is called acceleration due to gravity on the Moon. Its value is approximately:

gmoon ≈ 1.62 m/s²

🧮 Important Numericals
Q26. Calculate the weight of a body of mass 10 kg on Earth. Take g = 9.8 m/s².

Given:

Mass, m = 10 kg
Acceleration due to gravity, g = 9.8 m/s²

Formula: W = mg

W = 10 × 9.8

W = 98 N

Q27. A body has a mass of 50 kg. Calculate its weight on Earth.

Given: m = 50 kg, g = 9.8 m/s²

W = mg

W = 50 × 9.8

W = 490 N

Q28. An object weighs 98 N on Earth. Find its mass.

Given: W = 98 N, g = 9.8 m/s²

W = mg

m = W/g

m = 98/9.8

m = 10 kg

Q29. A body of mass 6 kg is taken to the Moon. Find its mass and weight on the Moon. Take gmoon = 1.62 m/s².

Mass on Moon: Mass remains unchanged.

Mass = 6 kg

Weight on Moon:

W = mg

W = 6 × 1.62

W = 9.72 N

⭐ Important Formulae
  • F = Gm₁m₂ / r²
  • g = GM / R²
  • W = mg
  • Pressure = Force / Area
  • Density = Mass / Volume
  • Relative Density = Density of substance / Density of water
📌 Important Points for Examination
  • Universal gravitational constant: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²
  • Acceleration due to gravity on Earth: g ≈ 9.8 m/s²
  • Acceleration due to gravity on Moon: ≈ 1.62 m/s²
  • SI unit of force: newton (N)
  • SI unit of pressure: pascal (Pa)
  • SI unit of density: kg/m³
  • SI unit of mass: kilogram (kg)
  • SI unit of weight: newton (N)
  • Relative density has no unit.
  • Weight changes from place to place, while mass remains constant.
⭐ Chapter Summary:

Gravitation is the universal force of attraction between masses. The Earth attracts objects towards itself due to gravity. The weight of an object is given by W = mg. Pressure depends on force and area. Fluids exert buoyant force on immersed objects, and Archimedes' principle explains this buoyant force. Density and relative density help us compare substances and explain why objects float or sink.
