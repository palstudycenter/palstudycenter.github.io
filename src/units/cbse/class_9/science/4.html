📘 Chapter 4 – Structure of the Atom
Q1. What is an atom?

Answer: An atom is the smallest particle of an element that retains all the chemical properties of that element.

Q2. Name the three subatomic particles of an atom.

Answer: The three main subatomic particles are:

  1. Electron
  2. Proton
  3. Neutron
Q3. What are the charges of an electron, proton and neutron?
Particle Charge
Electron Negative (-1)
Proton Positive (+1)
Neutron No charge (0)
Q4. Describe Thomson's model of an atom.

Answer: According to Thomson's model, an atom is a positively charged sphere in which negatively charged electrons are embedded. The positive and negative charges are equal, so the atom as a whole is electrically neutral.

Q5. What were the observations of Rutherford's alpha-particle scattering experiment?

Answer: Rutherford observed that:

  1. Most of the fast-moving alpha particles passed straight through the gold foil.
  2. Some alpha particles were deflected through small angles.
  3. A very small number of alpha particles were deflected back.
Q6. What were the conclusions of Rutherford's model of the atom?

Answer:

  1. Most of the space inside an atom is empty.
  2. Almost all the positive charge and mass of the atom are concentrated in a very small nucleus.
  3. Electrons revolve around the nucleus.
Q7. What is a nucleus?

Answer: The nucleus is the small, dense and positively charged central part of an atom. It contains protons and neutrons and most of the mass of the atom.

Q8. What was the major drawback of Rutherford's atomic model?

Answer: According to classical theory, revolving electrons should continuously lose energy and eventually fall into the nucleus. Therefore, Rutherford's model could not explain the stability of an atom.

Q9. Explain Bohr's model of the atom.

Answer: According to Bohr's model:

  1. Electrons revolve around the nucleus in fixed circular paths called shells or orbits.
  2. These shells have fixed energy levels.
  3. Electrons do not radiate energy while moving in a fixed shell.
  4. Energy is absorbed or released when an electron moves from one shell to another.
Q10. Name the shells of an atom.

Answer: The shells of an atom are named:

  • K shell
  • L shell
  • M shell
  • N shell
Q11. What is the maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in a shell?

Answer: The maximum number of electrons in a shell is given by the formula:

2n²

where n is the shell number.

Shell n Maximum electrons
K 1 2
L 2 8
M 3 18
N 4 32
Q12. What is valency?

Answer: Valency is the combining capacity of an atom. It depends mainly on the number of electrons present in the outermost shell.

Q13. What is atomic number?

Answer: The atomic number of an element is the number of protons present in the nucleus of its atom.

Atomic number (Z) = Number of protons

Q14. What is mass number?

Answer: The mass number of an atom is the total number of protons and neutrons present in its nucleus.

Mass number (A) = Number of protons + Number of neutrons

Q15. How can you calculate the number of neutrons in an atom?

Answer:

Number of neutrons = Mass number − Atomic number

Q16. What are isotopes?

Answer: Isotopes are atoms of the same element having the same atomic number but different mass numbers.

Example: Hydrogen has three isotopes: Protium, Deuterium and Tritium.

Q17. What are isobars?

Answer: Isobars are atoms of different elements having the same mass number but different atomic numbers.

Example: Calcium-40 and Argon-40 are isobars.

Q18. Write the differences between isotopes and isobars.
Isotopes Isobars
They are atoms of the same element. They are atoms of different elements.
They have the same atomic number. They have different atomic numbers.
They have different mass numbers. They have the same mass number.
Q19. Why is an atom electrically neutral?

Answer: An atom is electrically neutral because the number of positively charged protons is equal to the number of negatively charged electrons.

Q20. Why did Rutherford select a gold foil for his experiment?

Answer: Rutherford used a very thin gold foil because gold can be beaten into extremely thin sheets, allowing alpha particles to pass through it.

Q21. What is the significance of the atomic number?

Answer: The atomic number identifies an element and is equal to the number of protons in its nucleus. In a neutral atom, it is also equal to the number of electrons.

Q24. An atom has atomic number 17 and mass number 35. Find the number of protons, electrons and neutrons.

Answer:

  • Atomic number = 17
  • Number of protons = 17
  • Number of electrons = 17
  • Number of neutrons = 35 − 17 = 18
Q25. An atom has 8 protons and 8 neutrons. Find its atomic number and mass number.

Answer:

  • Atomic number = Number of protons = 8
  • Mass number = Protons + Neutrons = 8 + 8 = 16
⭐ Important Exam Points
  • Electron has a negative charge.
  • Proton has a positive charge.
  • Neutron has no charge.
  • The nucleus contains protons and neutrons.
  • Atomic number is equal to the number of protons.
  • Mass number is equal to protons + neutrons.
  • Isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers.
  • Isobars have the same mass number but different atomic numbers.
  • Bohr's model explains electrons in fixed energy shells.
