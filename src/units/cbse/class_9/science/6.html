📘 Chapter 6 – Tissues
Q1. What is a tissue?

Answer: A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to perform a specific function.

Q2. Why are tissues important in multicellular organisms?

Answer: Tissues provide division of labour in multicellular organisms. Different tissues perform different functions efficiently.

Q3. What are the main types of plant tissues?

Answer: Plant tissues are mainly of two types:

  1. Meristematic tissue
  2. Permanent tissue
Q4. What is meristematic tissue?

Answer: Meristematic tissue consists of actively dividing cells. It is responsible for the growth of plants.

Q5. Name the different types of meristematic tissues.

Answer: The three main types of meristematic tissues are:

  1. Apical meristem
  2. Lateral meristem
  3. Intercalary meristem
Q6. What is the function of apical meristem?

Answer: Apical meristem is present at the growing tips of roots and shoots. It increases the length of the plant.

Q7. What is the function of lateral meristem?

Answer: Lateral meristem increases the girth or thickness of stems and roots.

Q8. What is the function of intercalary meristem?

Answer: Intercalary meristem helps in the growth of stems and leaves, especially at the bases of leaves or internodes.

Q9. What are permanent tissues?

Answer: Permanent tissues are tissues whose cells have lost the ability to divide and have acquired a specific shape and function.

Q10. Name the different types of simple permanent tissues.

Answer: The three types of simple permanent tissues are:

  1. Parenchyma
  2. Collenchyma
  3. Sclerenchyma
Q11. What is parenchyma? Write its functions.

Answer: Parenchyma is a simple permanent tissue made up of living cells with thin cell walls.

Functions:

  • It stores food.
  • It helps in photosynthesis when chloroplasts are present.
  • It provides support to the plant.
Q12. What is collenchyma? Write its functions.

Answer: Collenchyma is a living permanent tissue whose cells have unevenly thickened walls at the corners.

Functions:

  • It provides mechanical support.
  • It provides flexibility to young stems and leaves.
Q13. What is sclerenchyma?

Answer: Sclerenchyma is a permanent tissue made up of dead cells with thick, lignified cell walls. It provides strength and rigidity to the plant.

Q14. What are the main types of complex permanent tissues?

Answer: The two main types of complex permanent tissues are:

  1. Xylem
  2. Phloem
Q15. What is the function of xylem?

Answer: Xylem transports water and minerals from the roots to different parts of the plant. It also provides mechanical support.

Q16. Name the components of xylem.

Answer: The components of xylem are:

  1. Tracheids
  2. Vessels
  3. Xylem parenchyma
  4. Xylem fibres
Q17. What is the function of phloem?

Answer: Phloem transports food prepared by the leaves to different parts of the plant.

Q18. Name the components of phloem.

Answer: The components of phloem are:

  1. Sieve tubes
  2. Companion cells
  3. Phloem parenchyma
  4. Phloem fibres
🧬 Animal Tissues
Q19. Name the four main types of animal tissues.

Answer: The four main types of animal tissues are:

  1. Epithelial tissue
  2. Connective tissue
  3. Muscular tissue
  4. Nervous tissue
Q20. What is epithelial tissue?

Answer: Epithelial tissue forms the protective covering of the body and lines the internal organs and body cavities.

Q21. Name the different types of epithelial tissues.

Answer: The main types of epithelial tissues are:

  1. Squamous epithelium
  2. Stratified squamous epithelium
  3. Columnar epithelium
  4. Ciliated epithelium
  5. Cuboidal epithelium
  6. Glandular epithelium
Q22. What is connective tissue?

Answer: Connective tissue connects, supports, binds, or separates different tissues and organs of the body.

Q23. Name some important types of connective tissues.

Answer: Important types of connective tissues include:

  • Areolar tissue
  • Adipose tissue
  • Tendon
  • Ligament
  • Cartilage
  • Bone
  • Blood
Q24. What is the function of areolar tissue?

Answer: Areolar tissue fills spaces inside organs, supports internal organs and helps in the repair of tissues.

Q25. What is the difference between tendon and ligament?

Answer:

  • Tendon: It connects muscles to bones and is strong and less flexible.
  • Ligament: It connects bones to other bones and is strong and flexible.
Q26. What is the function of adipose tissue?

Answer: Adipose tissue stores fat and provides insulation against heat loss.

Q27. Why is blood called a connective tissue?

Answer: Blood is called a connective tissue because it connects different parts of the body by transporting oxygen, nutrients, hormones and waste materials.

Q28. What is muscular tissue?

Answer: Muscular tissue is made up of muscle cells that contract and relax to produce movement.

Q29. Name the three types of muscular tissue.

Answer: The three types of muscular tissue are:

  1. Striated muscle
  2. Smooth muscle
  3. Cardiac muscle
Q30. What are striated muscles?

Answer: Striated muscles are voluntary muscles that have light and dark bands or striations. They are mainly attached to bones.

Q31. What are smooth muscles?

Answer: Smooth muscles are involuntary muscles without striations. They are found in the walls of internal organs such as the stomach and intestine.

Q32. What are cardiac muscles?

Answer: Cardiac muscles are involuntary, branched muscles found only in the heart. They contract rhythmically throughout life.

Q33. What is nervous tissue?

Answer: Nervous tissue is specialised for receiving and transmitting electrical signals in the body.

Q34. What is a neuron?

Answer: A neuron is the structural and functional unit of nervous tissue. It receives and transmits nerve impulses.

Q35. Differentiate between meristematic tissue and permanent tissue.
Meristematic Tissue Permanent Tissue

Cells are usually small and have dense cytoplasm. Cells have a specific shape and function.

Q36. Differentiate between plant tissues and animal tissues.
Plant Tissues Animal Tissues
Many plant tissues provide mechanical support. Animal tissues are mainly specialised for movement and various body functions.
Plants have meristematic and permanent tissues. Animals have epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous tissues.
Most plant tissues have limited activity after growth. Animal tissues generally remain active throughout life.

⭐ Important Exam Points
  • Tissue is a group of similar cells performing a specific function.
  • Meristematic tissue is responsible for plant growth.
  • Xylem transports water and minerals.
  • Phloem transports food.
  • Blood is a connective tissue.
  • Muscular tissue helps in movement.
  • Nervous tissue transmits nerve impulses.
  • Neuron is the structural and functional unit of nervous tissue.
