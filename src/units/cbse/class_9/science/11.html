Chapter 11 – Work and Energy
Q1. What is work?

Answer: Work is said to be done when a force acting on an object produces displacement in the direction of the force.

The mathematical expression for work is:

W = F × s

where W is work, F is force and s is displacement.

Q2. What are the two conditions necessary for work to be done?

Answer: The two conditions necessary for work to be done are:

  1. A force must act on the object.
  2. The object must undergo displacement in the direction of the force or have a component of displacement along the force.
Q3. Write the SI unit of work.

Answer: The SI unit of work is the joule (J).

One joule is the work done when a force of one newton displaces an object through one metre in the direction of the force.

1 J = 1 N × 1 m

Q4. When is work said to be positive?

Answer: Work is positive when the force and displacement are in the same direction.

For example, when a person pushes an object forward and the object moves forward, the work done by the applied force is positive.

Q5. When is work said to be negative?

Answer: Work is negative when the force and displacement are in opposite directions.

For example, friction generally does negative work because it acts opposite to the direction of motion.

Q6. When is work done zero?

Answer: Work done is zero when there is no displacement of the object or when the force is perpendicular to the displacement.

For example, when a person carries a bag horizontally at constant height, the upward force applied by the person is perpendicular to the horizontal displacement. Therefore, the work done by the upward force is zero.

When force and displacement are in the same direction, work done is positive.

Q7. What is energy?

Answer: Energy is the capacity of an object to do work.

The SI unit of energy is joule (J).

Q8. What are the different forms of energy?

Answer: Energy exists in many forms, such as:

  • Kinetic energy
  • Potential energy
  • Heat energy
  • Light energy
  • Sound energy
  • Electrical energy
  • Chemical energy
  • Nuclear energy
Q9. What is kinetic energy?

Answer: The energy possessed by an object due to its motion is called kinetic energy.

The kinetic energy of an object is:

K.E. = 1/2 mv2

where m is mass and v is velocity.

Q10. What is potential energy?

Answer: The energy possessed by an object due to its position or configuration is called potential energy.

The gravitational potential energy of an object at height h is:

P.E. = mgh

Q11. Give two examples of potential energy.

Answer:

  1. Water stored in a dam possesses gravitational potential energy.
  2. A stretched rubber band possesses potential energy due to its configuration.
Q12. Give two examples of kinetic energy.

Answer:

  1. A moving car possesses kinetic energy.
  2. A moving ball possesses kinetic energy.
Q13. On what factors does the kinetic energy of an object depend?

Answer: Kinetic energy depends on:

  1. The mass of the object.
  2. The square of its velocity.

K.E. = 1/2 mv2

Q14. On what factors does gravitational potential energy depend?

Answer: Gravitational potential energy depends on:

  1. Mass of the object.
  2. Acceleration due to gravity.
  3. Height of the object above the reference level.

P.E. = mgh

Q15. What happens to the kinetic energy of an object if its velocity is doubled?

Answer:

Kinetic energy is proportional to the square of velocity:

K.E. ∝ v2

If velocity is doubled:

K.E. becomes 22 = 4 times.

Therefore, the kinetic energy becomes four times.

Q16. What is power?

Answer: Power is the rate of doing work.

Power = Work / Time

P = W/t

The SI unit of power is watt (W).

Q17. Define one watt.

Answer: One watt is the power of an object that does one joule of work in one second.

1 W = 1 J/s

Q19. State the law of conservation of energy.

Answer: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It can only be transformed from one form to another. The total energy of an isolated system always remains constant.

Q20. Explain the law of conservation of energy using a freely falling object.

Answer: Consider an object falling from a height. At the highest position, it has maximum potential energy and minimum kinetic energy.

As it falls, its potential energy decreases and kinetic energy increases. Just before reaching the ground, its kinetic energy is maximum and potential energy is minimum.

Thus, potential energy is continuously converted into kinetic energy, while the total mechanical energy remains constant.

Diagram: Conservation of Mechanical Energy

During free fall, potential energy is converted into kinetic energy.

Q21. What is mechanical energy?

Answer: The sum of the kinetic energy and potential energy of an object is called its mechanical energy.

Mechanical Energy = Kinetic Energy + Potential Energy

Q22. What is the commercial unit of electrical energy?

Answer: The commercial unit of electrical energy is kilowatt-hour (kWh).

It is commonly called one unit of electricity.

Q23. Define one kilowatt-hour.

Answer: One kilowatt-hour is the energy consumed by an appliance having a power of one kilowatt when it is used for one hour.

1 kWh = 3.6 × 106 J

Q24. Can an object have energy even when it is not in motion?

Answer: Yes. An object can possess potential energy even when it is at rest. For example, a stone placed at a height possesses gravitational potential energy.

Q25. Can there be displacement without work being done?

Answer: Yes. If the force acting on an object is perpendicular to its displacement, the work done by that force is zero.

For example, while carrying a bag horizontally, the upward force applied by the person is perpendicular to the horizontal displacement.

Q26. A person holds a heavy object stationary. Is any work done by the person?

Answer: No. The person applies a force on the object, but the object does not undergo displacement. Therefore, the work done on the object is zero.

Q27. A force of 10 N displaces an object through 5 m in the direction of the force. Calculate the work done.

Given:

Force, F = 10 N
Displacement, s = 5 m

Formula:

W = F × s

W = 10 × 5

W = 50 J

Q28. A force of 20 N acts on an object and moves it through 4 m. Find the work done.

Given: F = 20 N, s = 4 m

W = F × s

W = 20 × 4

W = 80 J

Q29. Calculate the kinetic energy of a body of mass 2 kg moving with a velocity of 5 m/s.

Given:

m = 2 kg
v = 5 m/s

Formula:

K.E. = 1/2 mv2

= 1/2 × 2 × 52

= 25

K.E. = 25 J

Q30. Find the kinetic energy of an object of mass 10 kg moving with a velocity of 2 m/s.

Given: m = 10 kg, v = 2 m/s

K.E. = 1/2 mv2

= 1/2 × 10 × 22

= 5 × 4

K.E. = 20 J

Q31. Calculate the potential energy of a body of mass 5 kg kept at a height of 10 m. Take g = 9.8 m/s².

Given:

m = 5 kg
h = 10 m
g = 9.8 m/s²

P.E. = mgh

= 5 × 9.8 × 10

P.E. = 490 J

Q32. A body does 600 J of work in 20 seconds. Calculate its power.

Given:

Work, W = 600 J
Time, t = 20 s

Power = Work / Time

P = 600 / 20

P = 30 W

Q33. A machine has a power of 100 W. How much work will it do in 5 seconds?

Given:

P = 100 W
t = 5 s

P = W/t

Therefore,

W = P × t

W = 100 × 5

W = 500 J

Q34. What happens to the potential energy of an object when its height is doubled?

Answer: Since:

P.E. = mgh

Potential energy is directly proportional to height. Therefore, if the height is doubled, the potential energy also becomes two times.

Q35. What happens to the kinetic energy if the mass of an object is doubled while its velocity remains the same?

Answer: Since:

K.E. = 1/2 mv2

Kinetic energy is directly proportional to mass. Therefore, if the mass is doubled, the kinetic energy also becomes two times.
