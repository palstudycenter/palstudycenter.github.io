📘 Chapter 1 – Matter in Our Surroundings
Q1. What is matter?

Answer: Matter is anything that occupies space and has mass.

Examples: Air, water, wood, stone and milk.

Q2. What are the characteristics of particles of matter?

Answer: The main characteristics of particles of matter are:

  1. Particles of matter are very small.
  2. There are spaces between the particles of matter.
  3. Particles of matter are continuously moving.
  4. Particles of matter attract one another.
Q3. Give an example to show that particles of matter have spaces between them.

Answer: When a spoonful of salt or sugar is dissolved in water, the water level does not increase noticeably. This shows that salt or sugar particles occupy the spaces between water particles.

Q4. Give an example to show that particles of matter are continuously moving.

Answer: The smell of perfume spreads throughout a room because perfume particles continuously move and mix with air particles.

Q5. Give an example to show that particles of matter attract one another.

Answer: It is difficult to break an iron rod or a solid object because its particles attract one another strongly.

Q6. Name the three states of matter.

Answer: The three common states of matter are:

  1. Solid
  2. Liquid
  3. Gas
Q7. Write the characteristics of solids.

Answer:

  • Solids have a fixed shape.
  • They have a fixed volume.
  • They are generally rigid and cannot be compressed easily.
  • The particles are closely packed.
  • The force of attraction between particles is strong.
Q8. Write the characteristics of liquids.

Answer:

  • Liquids have a fixed volume.
  • They do not have a fixed shape.
  • They take the shape of the container.
  • They can flow.
  • The force of attraction between particles is weaker than in solids.
Q9. Write the characteristics of gases.

Answer:

  • Gases have neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume.
  • They are highly compressible.
  • They fill the entire container.
  • Gas particles move freely and rapidly.
  • The force of attraction between gas particles is very weak.
Q10. Why can gases be compressed easily?

Answer: Gases can be compressed easily because there are large spaces between their particles.

Q11. Why does a gas exert pressure?

Answer: Gas particles are continuously moving and collide with the walls of the container. These collisions exert force on the walls, producing pressure.

Q12. What is diffusion?

Answer: Diffusion is the process of intermixing of particles of two different substances due to their continuous random motion.

Example: The smell of perfume spreads throughout a room by diffusion.

Q13. Why does diffusion become faster when temperature is increased?

Answer: On increasing temperature, the kinetic energy of particles increases. Therefore, particles move faster and diffusion takes place more rapidly.

Q14. What is the effect of change in temperature on the state of matter?

Answer: Increasing or decreasing temperature can change the state of matter.

For example, ice changes into water on heating, and water changes into steam on further heating. On cooling, steam changes into water and water changes into ice.

Q15. What is melting point?

Answer: The temperature at which a solid changes into a liquid at atmospheric pressure is called its melting point.

The melting point of ice is 0°C or 273.15 K.

Q16. What is boiling point?

Answer: The temperature at which a liquid changes into gas at atmospheric pressure is called its boiling point.

The boiling point of water is 100°C or 373 K.

Q17. What is latent heat of fusion?

Answer: The amount of heat energy required to change 1 kg of a solid into liquid at its melting point without any change in temperature is called the latent heat of fusion.

Q18. What is latent heat of vaporisation?

Answer: The amount of heat energy required to change 1 kg of a liquid into gas at its boiling point without any change in temperature is called the latent heat of vaporisation.

Q19. What is sublimation?

Answer: Sublimation is the change of state from solid directly to gas without passing through the liquid state.

Examples: Camphor, naphthalene and ammonium chloride.

Q20. What is evaporation?

Answer: Evaporation is the process of conversion of a liquid into vapour from its surface at temperatures below its boiling point.

Q21. What are the factors affecting evaporation?

Answer: The rate of evaporation depends on:

  1. Surface area
  2. Temperature
  3. Humidity
  4. Wind speed

Evaporation increases with increase in surface area, temperature and wind speed, but decreases with increase in humidity.

Q22. Why does evaporation cause cooling?

Answer: During evaporation, high-energy particles escape from the surface of the liquid. The remaining particles have lower average kinetic energy, so the liquid becomes cooler.

Q23. Why do we feel cool after coming out of a swimming pool?

Answer: Water present on our body evaporates by taking heat from our body. This causes cooling, so we feel cool after coming out of a swimming pool.

Q24. Why do we wear cotton clothes in summer?

Answer: Cotton absorbs sweat and provides a larger surface for evaporation. The evaporation of sweat cools our body, so cotton clothes are comfortable in summer.

Q25. Why does water kept in an earthen pot become cool?

Answer: An earthen pot has tiny pores through which water slowly evaporates from its outer surface. Evaporation takes heat from the water inside the pot, making it cool.

Q26. Why does a desert cooler work better on a hot and dry day?

Answer: On a hot and dry day, the temperature is high and humidity is low. Therefore, water evaporates faster and produces greater cooling.

Q27. Convert the following temperatures into Kelvin scale: 25°C and 100°C.

Answer:

Temperature in Kelvin = Temperature in Celsius + 273

25°C = 25 + 273 = 298 K

100°C = 100 + 273 = 373 K

Q28. Convert 300 K and 273 K into Celsius scale.

Answer:

Temperature in Celsius = Temperature in Kelvin − 273

300 K = 300 − 273 = 27°C

273 K = 273 − 273 = 0°C

Q30. Why can we smell perfume from a distance?

Answer: Perfume contains volatile particles that diffuse rapidly through the air. Therefore, its smell can reach us from a distance.
