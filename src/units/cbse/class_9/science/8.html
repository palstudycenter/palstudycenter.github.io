📘 Chapter 8 – Force and Laws of Motion
Q1. What is force?

Answer: Force is a push or a pull acting on an object. It can change the state of motion, direction, or shape of an object.

Q2. What are balanced and unbalanced forces?

Answer: Forces acting on an object are called balanced forces when their resultant force is zero. They do not change the state of motion of the object.

Unbalanced forces are forces whose resultant is not zero. They can change the state of motion of an object.

Q3. What is inertia?

Answer: Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist any change in its state of rest or motion.

Q4. What are the different types of inertia?

Answer: There are three types of inertia:

  1. Inertia of rest
  2. Inertia of motion
  3. Inertia of direction
Q5. What is inertia of rest? Give an example.

Answer: The tendency of an object at rest to remain at rest is called inertia of rest.

Example: When a bus starts suddenly, passengers tend to fall backwards because their bodies tend to remain at rest.

Q6. What is inertia of motion? Give an example.

Answer: The tendency of a moving object to continue moving with the same velocity is called inertia of motion.

Example: When a moving bus stops suddenly, passengers tend to fall forward because their bodies tend to remain in motion.

Q7. What is inertia of direction? Give an example.

Answer: The tendency of an object to resist a change in its direction of motion is called inertia of direction.

Example: Passengers in a turning bus tend to lean sideways because their bodies tend to continue moving in the original direction.

Q8. State Newton's First Law of Motion.

Answer: An object remains at rest or continues to move with uniform velocity in a straight line unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force.

Newton's First Law is also called the Law of Inertia.

Q9. Why is Newton's First Law called the Law of Inertia?

Answer: Newton's First Law describes the tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of rest or uniform motion. This tendency is called inertia. Therefore, it is called the Law of Inertia.

Q10. State Newton's Second Law of Motion.

Answer: The rate of change of momentum of an object is directly proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction of the force.

Mathematically,

F = ma

where F = force, m = mass and a = acceleration.

Q11. Define momentum. Write its formula and SI unit.

Answer: Momentum is the product of the mass and velocity of an object.

p = mv

The SI unit of momentum is kg m/s.

Q12. State Newton's Third Law of Motion.

Answer: To every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Q13. Give two examples of Newton's Third Law of Motion.

Answer:

  1. While walking, we push the ground backwards and the ground pushes us forwards.
  2. A swimmer pushes water backwards and the water pushes the swimmer forwards.
Q14. What is the relation between force, mass and acceleration?

Answer: According to Newton's Second Law:

F = ma

Thus, force is equal to the product of mass and acceleration.

Q15. What is the SI unit of force?

Answer: The SI unit of force is newton (N).

One newton is the force required to produce an acceleration of 1 m/s² in an object of mass 1 kg.

Q16. Why do passengers fall forward when a moving bus stops suddenly?

Answer: When a moving bus stops suddenly, the lower part of the body comes to rest with the bus, while the upper part tends to remain in motion due to inertia of motion. Therefore, passengers fall forward.

Q17. Why do passengers fall backwards when a bus starts suddenly?

Answer: When a bus starts suddenly, the lower part of the body moves with the bus while the upper part tends to remain at rest due to inertia of rest. Therefore, passengers fall backwards.

Q18. Why are seat belts used in cars?

Answer: Seat belts protect passengers from being thrown forward when a car stops suddenly. They reduce the effect of inertia of motion and help prevent injuries.

Q19. Explain the conservation of momentum.

Answer: The law of conservation of momentum states that when no external force acts on a system, the total momentum of the system remains constant.

Total momentum before collision = Total momentum after collision

Q20. Write the mathematical expression for conservation of momentum.

Answer: If two objects of masses m₁ and m₂ have initial velocities u₁ and u₂, and final velocities v₁ and v₂, then:

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

Q21. Why does a gun recoil when a bullet is fired?

Answer: When a bullet is fired, it moves forward with momentum. According to the law of conservation of momentum, the gun moves backwards with an equal and opposite momentum. This backward motion is called recoil.

Q22. Why does a person fall backwards when a gun is fired?

Answer: When the bullet moves forward, the gun experiences a backward reaction due to conservation of momentum. This backward recoil can cause the person holding the gun to move backwards.

Q23. Why does a cricket player move his hands backwards while catching a ball?

Answer: The player moves his hands backwards while catching the ball to increase the time taken to stop the ball. This decreases the rate of change of momentum and hence reduces the force on the hands.

Q24. Why does a karate player break a pile of tiles with a blow?

Answer: The karate player strikes the tiles with a high velocity. The sudden change in momentum produces a large force, which can break the tiles.

Q25. Why does dust come out of a carpet when it is beaten with a stick?

Answer: When the carpet is beaten, it moves suddenly but the dust particles tend to remain at rest due to inertia of rest. Therefore, the dust separates from the carpet.

Q26. Why are athletes advised to come to a stop slowly after finishing a race?

Answer: An athlete's body is in motion while running. If the athlete stops suddenly, the body tends to continue moving due to inertia of motion. Stopping gradually reduces the risk of falling.

Q27. A force of 10 N acts on an object of mass 2 kg. Find its acceleration.

Answer:

Given: F = 10 N, m = 2 kg

Using F = ma

a = F/m = 10/2 = 5 m/s²

Therefore, the acceleration is 5 m/s².

Q28. An object of mass 5 kg is moving with an acceleration of 2 m/s². Find the force acting on it.

Answer:

Given: m = 5 kg, a = 2 m/s²

F = ma

F = 5 × 2 = 10 N

Therefore, the force acting on the object is 10 N.

Q29. A body of mass 2 kg moves with a velocity of 5 m/s. Calculate its momentum.

Answer:

Given: m = 2 kg, v = 5 m/s

p = mv

p = 2 × 5 = 10 kg m/s

Therefore, the momentum is 10 kg m/s.

Q30. What is the difference between mass and inertia?

Answer: Mass is the amount of matter present in an object, while inertia is the tendency of an object to resist a change in its state of motion. Greater mass means greater inertia.

⭐ Important Formulae

Momentum: p = mv

Force: F = ma

Force in terms of momentum: F = (v - u)m/t

Conservation of momentum:

m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

  • The SI unit of force is newton (N).
  • The SI unit of momentum is kg m/s.
