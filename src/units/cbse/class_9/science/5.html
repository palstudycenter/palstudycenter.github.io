📘 The Fundamental Unit of Life
Q3. What is a cell?

Answer: A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of a living organism. It contains various structures called cell organelles that perform specific functions.

Q4. What are unicellular and multicellular organisms?

Answer: Organisms made up of a single cell are called unicellular organisms. For example, Amoeba and Paramecium.

Organisms made up of many cells are called multicellular organisms. For example, humans, plants and animals.

Q5. What do you mean by prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?

Answer:

Prokaryotic Cells: Cells that do not have a true nucleus surrounded by a nuclear membrane are called prokaryotic cells. Their genetic material is present in a region called the nucleoid. Example: Bacteria.

Eukaryotic Cells: Cells that have a well-defined nucleus surrounded by a nuclear membrane are called eukaryotic cells. They also contain membrane-bound cell organelles. Examples: Plant cells and animal cells.

Q1. Why is the cell called the fundamental unit of life?

Answer: The cell is called the fundamental unit of life because all living organisms are made up of cells. A cell performs all basic life processes and is the smallest unit capable of carrying out life activities.

Q2. Who discovered the cell?

Answer: Robert Hooke discovered cells in 1665 while observing a thin slice of cork under a microscope. He observed small empty compartments and called them "cells".

Q6. What is the plasma membrane? Write its functions.

Answer: The plasma membrane is the thin, living and selectively permeable membrane surrounding the cell.

Functions:

  1. It separates the cell from its surroundings.
  2. It controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell.
  3. It helps maintain the internal environment of the cell.
Q7. What is diffusion?

Answer: Diffusion is the movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration until they become evenly distributed.

Q8. What is osmosis?

Answer: Osmosis is the movement of water molecules through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration to a region of lower water concentration.

Q9. Explain osmosis with a diagram.

Answer: In osmosis, water moves through a selectively permeable membrane from a dilute solution towards a concentrated solution.

Q10. What happens to a cell when it is placed in a hypotonic solution?

Answer: Water enters the cell by osmosis. As a result, the cell may swell and, in some cases, may burst.

Q11. What happens to a cell when it is placed in a hypertonic solution?

Answer: Water moves out of the cell by osmosis. The cell shrinks. In a plant cell, the plasma membrane may pull away from the cell wall. This process is called plasmolysis.

Q12. What is the function of the cell wall?

Answer: The cell wall is a rigid, non-living outer covering found in plant cells. It provides shape, support and protection to the cell and prevents the cell from bursting when excess water enters it.

Q13. Why do plant cells not burst when placed in a hypotonic solution?

Answer: Water enters the plant cell by osmosis, but the rigid cell wall provides resistance to excessive expansion. Therefore, the plant cell does not burst easily.

Q. Name the cell organelles and write one function of each.

Answer:

  1. Nucleus: Controls all the activities of the cell and contains genetic material.
  2. Mitochondria: Produces energy in the form of ATP.
  3. Ribosomes: Help in protein synthesis.
  4. Endoplasmic Reticulum: Helps in the transport and synthesis of materials inside the cell.
  5. Golgi Apparatus: Modifies, packages and transports substances.
  6. Lysosomes: Digest waste materials and worn-out cell organelles.
  7. Vacuoles: Store water, food and other substances.
  8. Chloroplasts: Carry out photosynthesis in plant cells.
  9. Plastids: Help in photosynthesis, storage and giving colour to plant parts.
  10. Cell Wall: Provides protection, support and shape to plant cells.
Q14. What is the nucleus? Write its functions.

Answer: The nucleus is a membrane-bound organelle present in eukaryotic cells. It contains genetic material and controls many activities of the cell.

Functions:

  1. It controls cellular activities.
  2. It contains chromosomes and genetic information.
  3. It plays an important role in cell division.
Q15. What are chromosomes?

Answer: Chromosomes are thread-like structures present in the nucleus. They contain DNA and carry genes that transmit hereditary information from parents to offspring.

Q16. What are genes?

Answer: Genes are functional units of heredity present on chromosomes. They carry information responsible for particular characteristics of an organism.

Q17. What are mitochondria? Why are they called the powerhouse of the cell?

Answer: Mitochondria are membrane-bound organelles that carry out cellular respiration and release energy in the form of ATP.

They are called the powerhouse of the cell because they provide usable energy for various cellular activities.

Q18. What is the function of ribosomes?

Answer: Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis. They may occur freely in the cytoplasm or be attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum.

Q19. What is endoplasmic reticulum? Name its two types.

Answer: Endoplasmic reticulum is a network of membranous tubules and channels present in the cytoplasm.

Its two types are:

  1. Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER) – It has ribosomes and helps in protein synthesis.
  2. Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum (SER) – It lacks ribosomes and helps in lipid synthesis and other cellular functions.
Q20. What is the Golgi apparatus? Write its functions.

Answer: The Golgi apparatus is a system of flattened membranous sacs.

Functions:

  1. It modifies proteins and lipids.
  2. It packages materials for transport.
  3. It helps in the formation of lysosomes.
  4. It is involved in secretion and intracellular transport.
Q21. What are lysosomes? Why are they called suicidal bags?

Answer: Lysosomes are small membrane-bound organelles containing digestive enzymes. They help digest unwanted materials and worn-out cell parts.

They are sometimes called suicidal bags because, if their membrane breaks under certain conditions, their enzymes can digest cellular components.

Q22. What are vacuoles? Write their functions.

Answer: Vacuoles are membrane-bound storage structures.

They store water, food, minerals, pigments and waste materials. In plant cells, the large central vacuole also helps maintain the internal pressure of the cell.

Q23. What is the function of chloroplasts?

Answer: Chloroplasts are present mainly in plant cells. They contain chlorophyll and are the sites of photosynthesis.

Answer: Important parts to label include:

Cell Wall, Plasma Membrane, Cytoplasm, Nucleus, Chloroplast, Large Vacuole, Endoplasmic Reticulum, Golgi Apparatus and Mitochondria.

Q26. Draw a neat labelled diagram of an animal cell.

Answer: Important parts to label include:

Plasma Membrane, Cytoplasm, Nucleus, Mitochondria, Endoplasmic Reticulum, Golgi Apparatus and Ribosomes.

Q27. Why are lysosomes important for the cell?

Answer: Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes that break down unwanted materials, foreign substances and worn-out cell organelles. Thus, they help keep the cell clean and functioning properly.

Q28. Why are mitochondria called semi-autonomous organelles?

Answer: Mitochondria contain their own DNA and ribosomes and can make some of their proteins. Therefore, they are called semi-autonomous organelles.
