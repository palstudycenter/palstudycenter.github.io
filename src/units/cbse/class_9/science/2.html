📘 Chapter 2 – Is Matter Around Us Pure?
Q1. What is a pure substance?

Answer: A pure substance is a substance consisting of only one kind of particles and having a fixed composition and definite properties.

Examples: Gold, oxygen, distilled water and sodium chloride.

Q2. What is a mixture?

Answer: A mixture is a substance formed by physically combining two or more different substances in any proportion.

The components of a mixture retain their individual properties and can usually be separated by physical methods.

Q3. What are the two types of mixtures?

Answer: Mixtures are of two main types:

  1. Homogeneous mixture
  2. Heterogeneous mixture
Q4. What is a homogeneous mixture?

Answer: A homogeneous mixture has a uniform composition throughout and its components cannot be distinguished separately.

Examples: Salt solution, sugar solution and air.

Q5. What is a heterogeneous mixture?

Answer: A heterogeneous mixture does not have a uniform composition throughout. Its different components can often be distinguished.

Examples: Soil, oil and water, and sand in water.

Q6. What is a solution?

Answer: A solution is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances. The substance that is dissolved is called the solute and the substance in which it is dissolved is called the solvent.

Example: In a salt solution, salt is the solute and water is the solvent.

Q7. What are solute and solvent?

Solute: The component of a solution that is dissolved in the solvent is called the solute.

Solvent: The component of a solution that dissolves the solute is called the solvent.

Q8. What is a saturated solution?

Answer: A saturated solution is a solution that cannot dissolve any more solute at a given temperature.

Q9. What is an unsaturated solution?

Answer: An unsaturated solution is a solution that can dissolve more solute at a given temperature.

Q10. What is concentration of a solution?

Answer: The concentration of a solution is the amount of solute present in a given amount of solution.

One formula used to express concentration is:

Mass percentage = (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) × 100

Q11. What is a suspension?

Answer: A suspension is a heterogeneous mixture in which insoluble solid particles are dispersed in a liquid. The particles are large enough to be seen and settle down when the mixture is left undisturbed.

Example: Muddy water.

Q12. What is a colloidal solution?

Answer: A colloid is a heterogeneous mixture in which very small particles of one substance are dispersed in another substance. The particles do not settle down on standing.

Example: Milk.

Q13. What is the Tyndall effect?

Answer: The scattering of light by colloidal particles is called the Tyndall effect.

It makes the path of a beam of light visible when it passes through a colloid.

Q15. How can a mixture of salt and water be separated?

Answer: A mixture of salt and water can be separated by evaporation. On heating, water evaporates and salt is left behind.

Q16. How can a mixture of salt and water be separated by distillation?

Answer: The mixture is heated in a distillation apparatus. Water changes into vapour and is then cooled in a condenser to obtain pure water. Salt remains behind in the flask.

Q17. How can the components of a mixture of two miscible liquids be separated?

Answer: Two miscible liquids having different boiling points can be separated by distillation. If their boiling points are close, the mixture can be separated by fractional distillation.

Q18. What is filtration?

Answer: Filtration is a method used to separate an insoluble solid from a liquid by using a filter medium.

Example: Separating sand from water.

Q19. What is centrifugation?

Answer: Centrifugation is a method of separation in which components of a mixture are separated by rapidly rotating the mixture.

Example: Cream can be separated from milk by centrifugation.

Q20. What is chromatography?

Answer: Chromatography is a method used to separate different components of a mixture based on their different rates of movement through a medium.

It is commonly used to separate different colours in a dye or ink.

Q21. What is sublimation?

Answer: Sublimation is the process in which a solid changes directly into vapour without changing into a liquid.

Examples: Ammonium chloride, camphor and naphthalene.

Q22. How can a mixture of ammonium chloride and salt be separated?

Answer: The mixture can be separated by sublimation. Ammonium chloride sublimes on heating, while salt remains behind.

Q23. What is crystallisation?

Answer: Crystallisation is a process used to obtain pure crystals of a substance from its solution.

It is used to purify solids that may decompose or contain impurities when separated by simple evaporation.

Q24. Give examples of physical and chemical changes.

Physical changes: Melting of ice, boiling of water and cutting of paper.

Chemical changes: Rusting of iron, burning of paper and formation of curd from milk.

Q25. Differentiate between a mixture and a compound.
Mixture Compound
Substances are physically combined. Elements are chemically combined.
Components may be present in any proportion. Elements are present in a fixed ratio.
Components retain their individual properties. A compound has properties different from its constituent elements.
Components can generally be separated by physical methods. Components can be separated only by chemical methods.
Q26. Give two examples each of homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures.

Homogeneous mixtures: Air and salt solution.

Heterogeneous mixtures: Soil and oil in water.

Q27. Is air a mixture or a pure substance? Give reason.

Answer: Air is a mixture because it contains several gases such as nitrogen, oxygen, carbon dioxide and other gases. These gases are not chemically combined and their proportions can vary.

Q28. Why is water considered a compound and not a mixture?

Answer: Water is a compound because hydrogen and oxygen are chemically combined in a fixed ratio of 1:8 by mass. It has properties different from hydrogen and oxygen.

Q29. Which separation method would you use for the following mixtures?
  1. Salt from water – Evaporation
  2. Sand from water – Filtration
  3. Ammonium chloride from salt – Sublimation
  4. Cream from milk – Centrifugation
  5. Different colours from ink – Chromatography
  6. Two miscible liquids with different boiling points – Distillation
