📘 Chapter 12 – Patterns in Life: Diversity and Classification
Q1. What is biodiversity?

Answer: Biodiversity refers to the variety of living organisms found on Earth. It includes the diversity of plants, animals, microorganisms and their different forms and habitats.

Q2. Why is classification of organisms necessary?

Answer: Classification is necessary because there are millions of organisms on Earth. It helps us identify, study and understand organisms systematically and shows the relationships among them.

Q3. What is biological classification?

Answer: Biological classification is the process of grouping organisms into different categories on the basis of their similarities, differences and evolutionary relationships.

Q4. What are the main criteria used for classification of organisms?

Answer: Organisms may be classified on the basis of cell structure, number of cells, mode of nutrition, body organisation, reproduction and evolutionary relationships.

Q5. What is the difference between unicellular and multicellular organisms?

Answer:

  • Unicellular organisms: Their body consists of a single cell which performs all necessary life processes.
  • Multicellular organisms: Their body consists of many cells which usually perform specialised functions.
Q6. What are prokaryotic and eukaryotic organisms?

Answer: Prokaryotic organisms have cells without a true membrane-bound nucleus. Eukaryotic organisms have cells with a well-defined membrane-bound nucleus.

Q7. Give two examples of prokaryotic organisms.

Answer: Bacteria and cyanobacteria are examples of prokaryotic organisms.

Q8. Give examples of eukaryotic organisms.

Answer: Plants, animals, fungi and protists are examples of eukaryotic organisms.

Q9. What are the major groups of living organisms?

Answer: Living organisms can be grouped into major categories based on their cell type, organisation, nutrition and evolutionary relationships. Important groups include bacteria, protists, fungi, plants and animals.

Q10. What is the basis of classification of plants?

Answer: Plants are classified mainly on the basis of body organisation, presence or absence of vascular tissues, seed formation and whether seeds are enclosed within fruits.

Q11. What are flowering and non-flowering plants?

Answer: Flowering plants produce flowers and usually form seeds inside fruits. Non-flowering plants do not produce flowers and include groups such as ferns and mosses.

Q13. What is taxonomy?

Answer: Taxonomy is the branch of biology concerned with the identification, naming and classification of organisms.

Q14. What is binomial nomenclature?

Answer: Binomial nomenclature is the scientific system of naming an organism with two words. The first word represents the genus and the second word represents the species.

Example: Homo sapiens

Q15. Who introduced the binomial system of nomenclature?

Answer: Carolus Linnaeus is widely known for establishing the modern system of binomial nomenclature.

Q16. What is a species?

Answer: A species is a group of similar organisms that can generally reproduce among themselves and produce fertile offspring.

Q17. What is the importance of scientific names?

Answer: Scientific names provide a universal and standard way of identifying organisms. They avoid confusion caused by different common names used in different regions and languages.

Q18. What is the hierarchy of classification?

Answer: Classification is arranged in a hierarchical manner. The major taxonomic categories include:

Species → Genus → Family → Order → Class → Phylum/Division → Kingdom

Q19. How does classification help in studying biodiversity?

Answer: Classification organises the enormous variety of organisms into manageable groups. It helps scientists identify organisms, study their characteristics and understand their evolutionary relationships.

Q20. What is the relationship between classification and evolution?

Answer: Classification can reflect evolutionary relationships. Organisms having more similarities are often placed closer together because they may have a common evolutionary history.
